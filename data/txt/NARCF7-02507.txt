How “Safe Logistics” Gave Us the Ride of Our Lives (and Almost Our Sanity)

Picture this: October-end, Diwali around the corner, my husband (28M) and I (25F) packing up our lives in Hyderabad to move to Jaipur. After browsing countless options, we settled on “Safe Logistics Packers and Movers.” Legit website? Check. Lowest quote? Double check (₹25k + GST). They even asked for a modest ₹1k booking fee upfront. Perfect, right? WRONG.

The Packing Drama
Fast forward to moving day, a team of efficient packers showed up, wrapped up our entire world in neat bundles, and loaded it onto a truck. Signatures? Done. Photos? Clicked. Assurance? “Your goods will reach Jaipur in five business days.” What could go wrong?

Well, everything.

As soon as we boarded our flight to Jaipur, BOOM!—our own personal Diwali bomb exploded. The logistics guy sent us a bill for ₹51,000. Yes, you read that right—DOUBLE the original quote. And guess when we got the news? Right before takeoff. So, there we were, 30,000 feet in the air, spiraling into panic.

The First Showdown
When we landed, Jaipur was lit up with Diwali firecrackers, but our spirits were anything but festive. We called Mr. Logistics immediately, but he coolly told us to “call tomorrow” because he was celebrating Diwali. (Oh, the irony.)

The next day, he justified the inflated bill with a smorgasbord of bogus charges: Octroi (abolished since GST!), green tax (not our responsibility), huge labor charges, extra insurance, and—our favorite—“service charges.” Calmly, we pointed out that even with GST and tolls, ₹30k would’ve made sense—but ₹51k? Absolutely not. That’s when he dropped his ace card:
“Take legal action if you want. Let’s see what you can do.”
He even threatened to dump our goods in Nagpur if we didn’t cough up the cash. Nagpur?!

The Negotiation Saga
Backed into a corner, we grudgingly paid ₹28k upfront, hoping to salvage our sanity. After hours of back-and-forth, we haggled the bill down to ₹38k. Seven days later (not five, mind you), the truck driver called, claiming he’d be at our location in two hours. Relief? Not yet.

Cue the logistics guy’s final act. He demanded full payment before delivery. We refused, saying we needed to see the truck first. Once the truck arrived, we demanded to see our goods. Once we saw the goods, we demanded to count them. (Yes, we were playing chess at this point.) After hours of this exhausting drama, ALL our goods were finally delivered.

The Plot Twist
While battling this nonsense, we did some digging. Guess what? The company’s GST registration was cancelled suo moto. Armed with this juicy tidbit, we filed a consumer complaint and reported the scam to the GST grievance cell.

A few days later, Mr. Logistics called again, this time singing a different tune. He claimed he was the “owner,” that his father had passed away, and begged us to withdraw the complaint. (Spoiler alert: the guy we spoke to earlier was also the owner. Nice try, buddy.) His website had mysteriously vanished, but our complaint stayed put.

We told him the same thing we’d written in the complaint: we just want our ₹13k back (₹38k - ₹25k). After much groveling, he agreed and asked for time to return the money.

The Aftermath
This entire ordeal? An anxiety-ridden nightmare. Imagine shifting to a new city, starting afresh, with all your hard-earned goods—appliances worth ₹3 lacs—held hostage by a scammer. Today, as I write this, I feel relieved. Justice may be slow, but it’s sweet.

Lesson learned: Trust your instincts, do your homework, and never underestimate the power of legally calling someone’s bluff.
