The Indian Influencer Governance Council (IIGC), a self-regulatory body representing digital influencers, released a code of standards governing influencer content earlier this month. The code includes 20 sections, covering topics from transparent brand sponsorships to data privacy and AI influencers.

“This framework ensures authenticity in brand collaborations, responsible content creation, and consumer protection, fostering trust within the influencer marketing ecosystem,” said the body .

This section sets down definitions for a number of common terms like “reach”, “impressions”, and “engagement”. It also prohibits influencers from buying likes, followers or using bots to boost engagement. They also cannot guarantee specific sales, conversion rates, or traffic numbers unless backed by proven historical data.

Influencers are required to disclose “any type of material partnership” with a brand, including financial agreements, affiliate marketing commissions, sponsorships or receipt of free products. Creators also have to use labels like #Ad, #Sponsored and #PaidPartnership.

This follows previous guidelines from the Advertising Standards Council of India ( ASCI ) and the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, which required influencers to disclose promotional content.

On the flip side, influencers must also reveal if they’re engaging in unpaid promotions for brands they genuinely “admire”.

“When influencers genuinely like a product and choose to promote it without direct compensation, it is essential to distinguish this from paid endorsements to maintain audience trust,” said IIGC. The code instructed influencers to use #Admiration as a label for such content.

The code also requires AI influencers to adhere to the same guidelines, just like human ones, with an added disclaimer revealing their non-human nature. It also prohibits the use of deepfake technology to create AI influencers resembling real people. In addition, the code warns against the use of AI influencers to manipulate users into forming real relationships with them or specifically target minors.

Earlier in January this year, Meta tested and later removed AI influencers from its platforms following user backlash. Many critics pointed out that the influencers had poor-quality images and tended to provide inaccurate responses.

The code prohibits endorsing products influencers don’t genuinely like, working with competing brands simultaneously (unless specified in agreements), and misrepresenting brand images. It also requires maintaining professional communication, adhering to deadlines and contract terms, providing regular campaign updates, and following brand guidelines while preserving authenticity.

The code defines defluence as the act of publicly criticising or speaking negatively about a brand, product, or service. It requires influencers to ensure that their criticism is honest, accurate and free from personal attacks. They must also disclose potential conflicts of interest.

The Payment Compliance section of the Code of Standards establishes clear rules for influencer payment practices. It mandates written agreements or contracts before starting any work, detailed scope of work documentation and proof of work submission. It also prohibits delayed payments from brands and influencers from submitting false proofs of work and working without payment confirmation.

The code prohibits both explicit and implicit discriminatory content in influencer marketing, requiring content to be inclusive and respectful of all identities. It mandates respectful language, mindful representation, cultural sensitivity and accessibility practices. It also establishes accountability measures, including warnings, corrective actions, and potential bans for violations.

The code prohibits content that demeans individuals based on identity factors. It bans objectification, humiliation, sensationalism, and exploitation of vulnerable groups. Influencers must respect human dignity and get proper consent when featuring others. They should also avoid trauma exploitation and predatory marketing.

The code bans graphic depictions, hate-induced violence and dangerous activities. It also forbids animal cruelty and cyberbullying. Influencers must avoid trivialising violence. They should use trigger warnings for sensitive topics. They must promote non-violence and conflict resolution.

The code asks influencers to handle content related to sex with “responsibility and sensitivity”. “While discussions on these topics may be appropriate in educational, artistic, or health-related contexts, they should not be used in a way that is exploitative, inappropriate, or violates community standards,” it said.

The code also prohibits unsolicited sexualisation of minors or non-consenting individuals and the display of nudity outside of artistic or educational contexts.

The code prohibits hate speech, offensive language, excessive profanity, and vulgarity, as well as aggressive or harassing tones. It also prohibits spreading misinformation or conspiracy theories. Instead, the code encourages influencers to engage in constructive dialogue and promote positive speech.

The Health & Safety section prohibits promotion of dangerous activities and bans health misinformation without scientific backing. It also prohibits endorsing harmful products or unregulated supplements and requires influencers to consult experts before making health claims.

The code establishes a blanket prohibition on advertising gambling or alcoholic products in any form. They must not link alcohol or gambling with success, health, or lifestyle. Platforms must enforce age restrictions and include clear warnings. Violations can lead to content removal, demonetisation, account bans, or legal action.

Influencers must promote only those products they have used and verified. They should not make false claims or mislead audiences. Paid promotions must carry clear disclosures. Violating these rules can lead to content removal, loss of brand deals, legal action, or permanent bans.

Influencers must create safe, positive, and age-appropriate content for children. They cannot show violence, abuse, harmful stunts, or use bad language. They must avoid promoting junk food, unsafe behaviour, or adult products like alcohol or gambling. If they feature children, they must get parental consent and protect the childâs privacy. Influencers must be honest and responsible when creating financial content. They cannot promise guaranteed profits, promote get-rich-quick schemes, or give unlicensed financial advice. They must disclose risks clearly when talking about investments, crypto, or trading apps. Paid partnerships must be clearly labelled, and influencers should not promote financial products they have not used.

Influencers must share only accurate, verified, and transparent data in their content. They cannot use fake statistics, misleading graphs, or exaggerated claims about product performance. They must not share user data without consent or make false claims about audience reach or privacy.

The code also establishes a consumer complaint forum under the IIGC. The council will review these complaints and take action if needed. However, the code does not mention an enforcement mechanism for the forum to implement its findings.
