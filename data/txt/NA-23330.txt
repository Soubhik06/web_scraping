To my knowledge, every e-gold/gold ETF offering available in the market today holds an equivalent value in a combination of physical gold and cash (usually 95% gold, 5% cash for ETFs) or perhaps positions in Gold futures.

However this sector is not that hugely regulated. While ETFs are subject to more regulations because of the fund management structure, e-gold is just an OTC contract between the buyer and the vendor. For all you know, the vendor could make many claims, but I bet 99.999% investors are not in a position to privately audit or verify their claims, whether financially or logistically.

As has happened in the Kingold case, even the contracted insurer could be blindsided.

While the Kingold case is a case of defrauding the Chinese equivalents of NBFCs and investors, the same thing could likely be done with greater ease with retail investors in India.

Super-important to also point out that the mere presence of RBI/SEBI/ED/IT Dept/SFO does not guarantee that people will not attempt scams, because government agencies will step in post-facto to arrest the culprits. However for the gullible investor the money is down the drain.
