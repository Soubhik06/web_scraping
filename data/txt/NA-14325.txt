Australia’s online safety regulator, the eSafety Commission, has fined messaging platform Telegram nearly $610,000 (AUD 957,780) for failing to respond on time to inquiries regarding its efforts to check child sexual abuse material and extremist content on its platform, The Guardian reported .

In March 2024, the Commission had issued information requests to several social media platforms, including Telegram, YouTube, X (formerly Twitter), Facebook, and Reddit, asking for details on their strategies to prevent the spread of harmful material. While most platforms complied, Telegram took nearly 160 days – breaching the original October deadline – to submit its response.

“Timely transparency is not a voluntary requirement in Australia, and this action reinforces the importance of all companies complying with Australian law,” said eSafety Commissioner Julie Inman Grant . She emphasized that the delay obstructed the Commission from fulfilling its duties under the Online Safety Act for nearly half a year.

Telegram, however, defended its position, calling the penalty “unfair and disproportionate” and announcing plans to appeal the decision. The company added that it had fully responded to all of eSafety’s questions last year, with no outstanding issues.

In August 2024, French authorities placed Telegram CEO Pavel Durov under formal investigation , as he faced multiple charges, including complicity in the distribution of child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and failure to curb extremist and terrorist content on the platform. He has been released on bail and banned from leaving France until the case is heard.

A comprehensive four-month investigation by The New York Times , analyzing over 3.2 million messages across 16,000 channels, revealed that Telegram has become a hub for various illegal activities, including the dissemination of child sexual abuse material, terrorism, and illegal drug trade.

1. Child Sexual Abuse Material: The investigation uncovered numerous channels disseminating child sexual abuse material. Telegram’s minimal content moderation and refusal to cooperate with law enforcement have exacerbated the proliferation of such illicit content. French authorities have charged CEO Pavel Durov with complicity in the distribution of child sexual abuse material due to the platform’s inadequate response to these issues.

2. Terrorism and Extremism: Terrorist organizations, including Hamas and ISIS, have utilized Telegram to coordinate activities and disseminate propaganda. Notably, Hamas’ October 7 attack led to a surge in viewership across more than 40 related channels. The platform also hosts approximately 1,500 channels operated by white supremacists, coordinating activities among nearly one million followers worldwide.

3. Illegal Drug Trade: Telegram has become a marketplace for contraband items, with at least 22 channels advertising drugs such as MDMA, cocaine, and heroin for delivery to over 20 countries. The platform’s features, like channels and supergroups, combined with lax moderation, have facilitated the growth of these illegal markets.

The controversy surrounding Telegram highlights the growing tension between platform privacy and user safety. As messaging platforms continue to provide encrypted communication, they must also balance the responsibility of ensuring their platforms are not exploited for harmful activities. The Australian eSafety Commission’s actions underscore the need for timely transparency and adherence to regulatory frameworks.

This case also sets a precedent for other countries to enforce stricter compliance measures, pushing platforms like Telegram to prioritize safety without compromising user privacy.

Telegram now has 28 days to either pay the fine, request its withdrawal, or seek an extension. If the company fails to comply, the eSafety Commission could escalate the matter to the federal court and pursue civil penalties.
