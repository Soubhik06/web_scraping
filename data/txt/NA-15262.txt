Rapido is preparing to enter India’s food delivery market. According to a recent The Economic Times report, the company has finalised commercial terms with the National Restaurant Association of India (NRAI), under which restaurants would be charged a flat fee of Rs. 25 for orders below Rs. 400 and Rs. 50 for higher-value orders. ET notes that this structure translates to an effective commission of 8-15%, lower than the 16-30% typically charged by leading food delivery platforms, Zomato and Swiggy.

However, Business Standard and CNBC-TV18 , cited a Rapido proposal shared with restaurant partners, which showed a different structure for its food delivery platform. Both reports describe a platform named ‘Ownly,’ which does not charge commissions. Instead, it relies on flat fees and delivery subsidies. It remains unclear whether both pricing models will coexist or whether one has replaced the other.

According to CNBC-TV18 , Rapido says it will charge restaurants no commission on orders. Instead, it will levy a flat subscription fee, which would be Rs. 25 for orders worth Rs. 400 or less and Rs. 50 for orders above Rs. 400. The company also says it will subsidise delivery costs. For orders above Rs. 100, restaurants would pay Rs. 25 plus GST, while customers would pay Rs. 20. For orders under Rs. 100, restaurants would pay Rs. 10 in delivery fees. The proposal prohibits separate packaging charges and requires restaurants to maintain price parity between dine-in and delivery menus.

Business Standard also reported that the platform would charge restaurants a flat subscription fee, positioning Ownly as a zero-commission alternative to existing aggregators. The pilot is expected to begin in select areas of Bengaluru by the last week of June or the first week of July.

Restaurants may either use their own delivery fleet or opt into Rapido’s existing two-wheeler network without additional charges. The proposal also requires restaurants to list at least four meals priced under Rs. 150, prominently displayed on the app. Visibility will be based on customer ratings rather than promotional spending.

Rapido also said that restaurants will get access to customer data to support their external marketing efforts, but the nature of the specific data shared or the structure of its accessibility is unclear.

In its internal note, reviewed by CNBC-TV18, Rapido stated: “Offline price = Online price… We are committed to creating access to data that allows restaurants to drive marketing initiatives across their digital channels.”

Beyond commissions and data, pricing clarity for customers remains a concern. While food delivery platforms like Swiggy and Zomato advertise discounts, final costs often include multiple charges that are not visible upfront.

MediaNama reviewed a McDonald’s order’s pricing on Swiggy and Zomato:

Swiggy: Rs. 199 for the item, Rs. 45 for delivery, and Rs. 41.75 in taxes and fees. Total: Rs. 286

Zomato: Rs. 199 for the item, Rs. 26 for delivery, Rs. 31.75 in taxes, and Rs. 10 platform fee. Total: Rs. 266.75

In both cases, the final price increased due to bundled fees. Rapido’s proposal claims that customers will only pay the listed price, GST, and a delivery fee. However, whether this structure holds in practice remains to be seen.

A recent MediaNama investigation documented how restaurant owners experienced Zomato’s advertising system.

One restaurant reported that its weekly ad spending rose from Rs. 400 to over Rs. 18,000 in six months. Others said Zomato charged them per click rather than per completed order. Owners also said coupon discounts were deducted without consent and that they did not receive attribution data. Some noted that price parity obligations, combined with fees and promotions, reduced margins to between 5% and 10%.

One owner called the model a “honeytrap.” Another said, “There’s no formal audit or independent review if an ad campaign fails.”

These experiences may shape how many restaurant owners evaluate new platforms. Fee transparency, data access, and visibility terms have become critical concerns, not just commissions. In that context, Rapido’s proposal will be judged not only by what it promises but also by whether restaurants can hold the platform accountable if terms shift after onboarding.

Earlier in 2015, Ola launched Ola Café before acquiring Foodpanda India in 2017. It later exited the food delivery market and has since joined the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC). Uber launched Uber Eats India in 2017 and exited in 2020, transferring operations to Zomato. According to NDTV , Uber received a 10% stake in Zomato through that transaction.

Rapido has not indicated whether Ownly will integrate with ONDC or operate as a standalone food delivery platform.

Speaking to Business Standard , NRAI president Sagar Daryani described Rapido’s proposal as “economically viable and democratic.” He also said Rapido had agreed in principle to share customer data with restaurants.

Amit Bagga, CEO of Daryaganj Restaurants, told Business Standard that the model could work for smaller outlets if Rapido maintains reliable logistics, particularly during peak hours.

Restaurant owners interviewed by MediaNama earlier said food delivery platforms often introduce favourable terms during onboarding but change conditions later. Several noted they could not opt out of platform-led promotions or access clear breakdowns of deductions.

This raises key questions as Rapido prepares to launch:

Platform trust, restaurant owners previously said, rarely hinges on introductory offers. It depends on whether food delivery platforms allow opt-outs, uphold transparency, and give merchants a say when the rules shift. Rapido’s ability to distinguish itself may come down not just to pricing, but to how much power it gives back to its partners.

MediaNama has sent a detailed questionnaire to Rapido seeking clarity on its pricing model, data-sharing terms, contract enforcement mechanisms, and visibility algorithms. We have asked about subscription fee and per-order rates model of charging restaurants, the categories of customer data shared with partners, and the availability of redressal processes for merchants in case of disputes. This article will be updated if and when Rapido responds.
