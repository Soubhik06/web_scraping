Hey everyone,

I am moving out of Bangalore soon and am selling off most of my stuff. While doing this on OLX and Quikr, I came across fraudsters who employ a couple of different methods to try and defraud you. In general, the fraudsters seem to frequent OLX more since they dont have in-app payments. The fraudsters can also obtain your e-mail and try to defraud you there (see Mode 2 below). I also saw a couple of news items on folks getting defrauded so I thought I'd share my very recent experience with them. Thankfully, I was not in a hurry to sell off my items and was able to thoughtfully avoid these scammers. I hope this information helps you.

As in any transaction, a few simple rules of thumb will ensure you do not get defrauded

If you are a seller:

* Do not make any payments to anyone who wants to buy an item from you
* Be very wary of anyone who wants to buy a big ticket item from you without checking it physically
* Be very wary of anyone who does not bargain for a big ticket item
* Be very wary of giving out your phone number immediately

If you are a buyer:

* Do not make any payments until you have seen the product and are satisfied with its condition.
* Do not make any payment when you don't have the product right in front on you.
* Do not agree to meet in any isolated areas, always meet in a public place at a reasonable time.

Now, for the actual scams.

**Method 1: The Google Pay Scam**

**The Setup**

This method relies on creating a sense of urgency and trying to lure you via a promise of quick payment. As soon as you post a big ticket item on OLX, you will have multiple people messaging you with messages like "Contact Number send me", "Contact number send karo", "I am interested to buy this at xxxx" etc.

**The Scam**

As soon as you share your contact number, the fraudster will call you and say that they want to pick up the item immediately and want to give you an advance via GooglePay to lockdown the deal. They will then contact you on Whatsapp and send you an image that has a QR code and text that shows the advance amount. The scammer will then ask you to scan the image via Google Pay using a different phone and say that the amount will get credited to your account.

The scam of course is that the amount will get debited from your own account and get sent to the scammer. The scammer has sneakily edited out his name and account details from the image that has been sent to you. Once the amount is transferred, I am pretty sure it is close to impossible to get it back via legal means.

**Why it works**

The lure of a quick payoff is irresistible. The scammer also creates a sense of urgency since there are multiple accounts haranguing you on OLX asking you to close the deal quickly. It is also tempting to believe that such a transaction can be closed without having to haggle or have viewings of your product. This also preys on anyone who is not technologically savvy. The scammer will tell you that they have a merchant account which means they cannot send you money directly. They will also say that they will send 5 Rs as a test in case you doubt them. This is of course false again since they image they send will have the actual amount edited out and a fake amount edited in.

**Method 2: The Long Con via Email**

**The Setup**

This con relies on building your confidence via multiple emails and trying to exploit your sunk cost of the time you have already put into engaging with the scammer. It also tries to build legitimacy and exploit your desire to get a good price for your item

Once you have placed your ads, you will get an email - most likely from an ID that belongs to a woman. The email will be long and rambling and most likely mention that the person has absolutely fallen in love with the item you have advertised, that they are currently abroad and want to close the deal with you asap and also ask you to remove the ads for the product from all advertising platforms.

The emails will continue, long and rambling every time and ask very specific questions about your product to make it seem like there is a genuine interest. Once they have reeled you in into believing they are a genuine buyer, the next phase of the setup begins

In this phase, the scammer will mention that they are currently abroad and have made detailed and elaborate arrangements to make a payment to you.
The Scam

The scammer will now say that the only payment mode available to them since they are abroad is that of cheque which they will take the pains to laboriously air courier from a foreign country to you.

And then of course, there is the small matter of the import duty on the tiny piece of paper that is being sent to you. This will be to the tune of 10,000 and your kindly buyer will have included that amount in the cheque as well, its just that they need you to make an account transfer to the customs agent, who happens to have a very weird name and an account in some far flung area (mine was in Dimapur and the person was called TOHOSHE CHOPHY, I kid you not).

**Why it works**

It feels good to have someone agree to your listed price without haggling. You are also lulled into a false sense of security since they claim that you do not need to release the product till the cheque gets credited to your account. So if the cheque bounces, you're safe. This also tries to build legitimacy and authority with the scammer claiming to be abroad. After all, if they are abroad they must be rich enough to purchase your item right? Also, first class shipping on Lufthansa! Wow, that sounds legit indeed. And sure you have to pay 10,500 as "Customs fees" but thats included in the cheque too right? Also, if you fell into the trap of removing your ad, then this might be the only possible buyer and since you have emailed back and forth for a week, might as well pay and get it over with right?

**Fin**

So there you have it, some common scams. I hope this helps you or someone you know in not getting scammed!
