Per Mr. Rajendra Singh, Member: This complaint has been filed by the complainant under section 17 of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986. The brief facts of the complaint case are that, that the complainant is inter alia seeking damages from the respondent who has resorted to unfair trade practice and has rendered deficient services as a result of which the complainant has suffered. The respondent which is a construction company has delayed in handing over possession of the flat booked by the complainant. The delay has caused denial of services to the complainant and as such he is liable to pay compensation. The complainant is a taxpaying surgeon and the respondent is a construction company registered under the provisions of the Indian Companies Act 1956, having its Letter Office at 10, Local Shopping Centre, Kalkaji, Delhi. In or around 2006, the respondent construction company started a Housing Project in Noida, UP, after obtaining lease from New Okhla Industrial Development Authority, at sector 6 in respect of 1,20,000 m² land on 03.10.2006 for the purpose of developing Group Housing Scheme at GH-01, 02 and 03, sector 93 B, Noida and named it "GRANDSWOOD, Noida". The project was launched with great fanfare and a big advertisement blitz. As per the brochure, the "GRANDSWOOD, Noida" was a premium project and the builder had promised  world-class facilities. The construction company, its men and agents had promised that the possession of the flat would be given in 2009. This promise was also certified by means of email dated 11.03.2008 and 03.11.2008 where they asserted that possession would be given by June 2009. Another email dated 8.05.2009 was sent wherein the likely date for delivery opposition was mentioned as March 2010. Being swayed by the media blitzkrieg launched at the behest of the respondent construction company, the complainant booked a flat in their housing project  on 31.08.2006. The complainant was shown the model plans of the envisaged housing project and were shown depicting exotic locales and high-quality construction. He was assured that not only would "GRANDSWOOD, Noida" have the best class and quality of construction and amenities but also the project would be completed on time and the first possession would commence in 2009. On being assured about the timeframe for delivery of the flat, complainant paid the respondent company an advance of Rs.850,000/- on 31.08.2006 through cheque no.166095 and Rs.5,70,000/- on 26.12.2006 through cheque no.368134. Thereafter the complainant was informed that flat no. 703 has been booked for him on seventh floor of Alexandra-A Tower and he further deposited a sum of Rs.433,400/- vide cheque no.275519 drawn on Indian Overseas Bank, dated 17.08.2007. The cost of the flat was at the rate of Rs.3565/- square ft i.e. Rs.5,704,000/- + other charges. The complainant, thereafter deposited Rs.5,07,800/- on 12.10.2007 through cheque no.276647. As such from 31.08.2006 till 12.10.2007 complainant had paid Rs.2,361,600/-. Thereafter on further demand of the opposite parties, the complainant paid Rs.745,068/on 19.12.2007 through cheque no.401723 in which a sum of Rs.39,732/- was charged as the interest and Rs.4,04,647/- dated 25.01.2008 through cheque no.765997 in which a sum of Rs.23,153/- was illegally charged as the interest. Vide letter dated 04.03.2008 the respondent company sent two original copies of the allotment letter to the complainant in respect of flat no.703, seventh floor in Alexandra-A Tower at Omaxe Grandwoods, sector 93B, Noida and required the complainant to sign each and every page at the place marked allottee and send it back. The complainant objected to the unfair practice adopted by the respondent company of sending blank document for signature and requested them to send duly signed copy of the allotment letter and undertook to sign and return such a copy. The respondent company never sent the duly signed allotment letter however they continue to demand payment as per the construction linked  payment plan. The complainant made payment as per construction linked payment plan. The opposite parties at the time of booking the flat assured that the possession of the flat would be handed over to the complainant by June 2009 but the opposite parties instead of maintaining a time discipline regarding pace of construction demanded money from the complainant from time to time and charged interest at a rate of 18% over it in the head of delayed payment which comes under the defective as well as unfair trade practice. The letters and concerns raised by the complainant through his letters dated 22.09.2008  and  04.12.2009 were either totally ignored or brushed aside. Vide an email dated 03.11.2008 the opposite parties had assured the complainant that the possession of the flat would be given by June 2009 but thereafter the opposite parties themselves admitted in their email dated 18.05.2009 that the project was delayed and assured that up to March, 2010 the possession of the flat would be handed over. Needless to say that while there was an inordinate delay on the part of the respondent company in completing the construction and handing over possession the religiously charged interest at a rate of 18% on the delayed instalments. The complainant gave several request letters including letter dated 13.05.2009 for waiver of the interest charged illegally by the opposite party and also requested for possession of the flat but the opposite party neither waived off the illegal charges nor took steps to hand over the possession. On 04.12.2009 the complainant once again sent a letter to the opposite party for waiver of the illegal interest charged by them and further requested for possession of the flat but neither the illegal interest was waived of nor possession of the flat was offered while a considerable amount has been already given to the opposite party which shows the ill intention of the opposite party. On 15.03.2010 the respondents sent a letter and demanded service tax from the complainant which was not at all applicable in the case of the complainant and this letter was suitably replied by the complainant but the respondent company deliberately ignored the concerns of the complainant and did not give any reply to the objections of the complainant. Again on 29.11.2011 the complainant wrote to the respondent company for waiver of the penal interest charged by the opposite parties and further requested for handing over the possession of the flat after completion of the flat in question. The respondent vide their letter dated 01.02.2012 offered temporary possession and also demanded the balance due amount from the complainant while the respondents had not completed all works in the flat in question. The action of the respondent company in offering temporary possession is again an instance of illegality and is again an unfair trade practice. The complainant refused to be part of this illegal action and asserted that he would like proper registration of the property with complete and unfettered possession and thus refused to be part of any illegal exercise offered by the respondent company. On 19.03.2012 the respondent company by an omnibus decision informed the complainant that the name of the project had been changed and it was renamed as "Grand Omaxe" for reasons best known to them. The complainant vide letter dated 28.08.2012 and 24.09.2012 further demanded the possession of the flat in question. It is important to mention here that from 31 August 2006 to 8 August 2012 the complainant had paid Rs.7,088,396.15 to the respondent company as per their demand which was more than the actual cost of the flat. The fact about the payment made is also evidenced by the account statement given by the respondent company. Despite making all the payments as per the demand of the respondent company they were unable to start the registration process which they admitted in their email dated 1 October, 2012 which shows the deficiency on the part of the opposite party for which they are liable to be paralysed and the complainant be suitably compensated. Due to repeated reminders from the complainant, the respondent company started the registration process in 2013. The respondent company on 07.06 2013 sent a letter to the complainant requiring him to fulfil documentary formalities which included the signing of the allotment letter. The complainant replied to the letter dated 07.06.2013 sent by the respondent company vide his letter dated 26.06.2013 and asserted that when the entire payment in respect of the flat had been paid and receipts accordingly issued it was not at all relevant to sign on the allotment letter now. The respondent company with a view to harass the complainant insisted that unless the allotment letter was signed, the registration formalities would not be carried forward. On 26.06.2013 the officials of the company made a note on the letter dated 07.06.2013 that they had not received the signature of the complainant along with duly verified by bank and that NOC for maintenance of the flat was pending and insisted upon clearing the dues for facility plus management. The respondent company started creating petty fogging objections to thwart the registration process and as such the complainant was forced to comply with the illegal demands of the respondent company. The complainant on 28.06.2013 personally submitted a letter to them mentioning his objections while complying with the illegal demands. Under these compelling circumstances the complainant then signed the allotment letter on 29.07.2013 and personally handed over the allotment letter. Finally on 30.07.2013 after an inordinate delay of more than four years the respondent company gave the possession of the flat in question to the complainant and the registration of the flat in question took place on 31.07.2013. It is relevant to mention here that in June 2009 the circle rate of the area for the calculation of stamp duty is Rs.27,000 per square metre but due to the delay on the part of the respondent the registration of the flat was done at an enhanced rate of Rs.55,000/- per square metre therefore the complainant suffered a loss of Rs.174,240/-. The complainant feeling aggrieved by the deficiency in service and unfair trade practice of the respondent company submitted a legal notice dated 23.09.2013 upon them requiring them to pay Rs.55,340/- the illegal charge against the facility plus management, Rs.304,000/ the illegal charge against lease rent, Rs.720,000/- as loss of rent due to the delay in delivering possession, Rs.174,240/- against loss of stamp duty and Rs.44,17,740/- interest at a rate of 18% on payment made by the complainant i.e. total sum of Rs.5,671,350/- within a period of 15 days from the date of receipt of the notice failing which appropriate legal proceedings would be initiated. The respondent company however failed to comply with the notice and remained completely inert. The respondent company is liable to be penalysed and the complainant suitably be compensated for the delay in delivery of possession of the flat and the respondents are also liable to pay interest at a rate of 18% up on the deposited amount i.e. Rs.7,108,126/-. The complainant is advised to submit here that the action described in the preceding paragraphs on the part of the respondent construction company/its authorities/agents/implies is not only unfair but it is also a serious malpractice and grave deficiency in service. Likewise exorbitant rates of interest has been charged by the respondent construction company under various heads. The complainant has been forced to bear the brunt of unreasonable and access liabilities thrust upon him by the respondent construction company in an illegal and arbitrary manner. The respondent company enjoyed the interest on the amount deposited by the complainant and on the other hand deprived the complainant of the property booked by him and also deprive him of the interest he would have accrued if the same would have been invested in the bank. Had the flat in question been handed over on time the complainant could have received rental income. Additionally the complainant had to pay higher stamp duty at the time of registration in 2013 as compared to the circle rates are prevailing in 2009. In a catena of judgements the court has observed that where the builder was charging interest for delayed payment it was equitable that the consumer too be awarded interest for delay in handing over possession. As such the opposite party is also liable to provide interest at a rate of 18% up on the delayed possession from the date of deposit to the date of actual possession of the flat in question. It has also been consistent view of the courts that delay of possession amount to denial of service and therefore the complainant in this case is also liable to be compensated. It is undisputed that the complainant was ready and willing to take possession of the flat and had written several letters which were brushed aside by the company and that it was the intention on the part of the respondent company that losses were suffered by him.
