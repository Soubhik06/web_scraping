 reasonable doubt: (i) the identity of the perpetrators; (ii) house-
 breaking/trespass by night into the shop; and (iii) theft of the stated
 property by them. For Section 411, it must be proved that (i) the
 property recovered is "stolen property" from the subject theft; (ii) it
 was found in possession of the accused; and (iii) the accused knew or
 had reason to believe it to be stolen. 13. On identity from CCTV, PW-1, the complainant, unequivocally
 stated in Court that the pen drive video was blurred, contained stills,
 and that he could not identify any accused then or now. He also could
 not identify the currency or specify denominations. PW-2's supposed
 identification rests only on his claim that he once saw a video in the
 IO's laptop in PS and that the person near the shutter with a white
 cloth was Dharmender. The FSL report, placed through PW-5,
 expressly records that facial landmarks were not comparable due to
 low pixel resolution, and hence the requisite examination could not be
 carried out. In the face of an inconclusive FSL and the complainant's
 categorical inability to identify, the solitary police-station-based
 identification by PW-2--unsupported by a judicial TIP and not
 corroborated by the civilian witness--does not inspire confidence. It is
 unsafe to rest identity upon such evidence. 14. The prosecution relied on a "walking parade" video of the
 accused recorded inside the PS on 17.02.2018. This is not a TIP
 conducted under judicial supervision. It took place after arrests and
 custody, carries an inherent risk of suggestion, and, in any event, does
 not connect the faces in CCTV (which itself is blurred) to these
 accused. It therefore has no probative value on identity. 15. On recovery, the prosecution alleges recovery of ₹850/- from
 Dharmender, ₹600/- from Devender, and ₹380/- from Ashu. More
 importantly, PW-1 could neither identify the currency nor state the
 exact denominations allegedly stolen. There is no prior record of serial
 numbers. There is no special marking and nothing connects the seized
 notes specifically to the theft. In law, generic currency notes, absent
 identifying features or linkage, cannot by themselves constitute
 "stolen property" from the subject offence. 16. The manner of recovery also suffers from serious infirmities.
 Both PW-3 and PW-4 admitted that despite the spot/locality being
 crowded and public persons present, no independent public witness
 was joined either at the spot, at the time of arrests, or at the time of
 recoveries/searches at the accused persons' houses. PW-6 confirmed
 presence of 4-5 public persons during Ashu's apprehension/recovery
 but none was joined, and he could not even furnish basic address or
 household particulars. The failure to join available public witnesses,
 without any convincing explanation, casts a deep shadow on the
 fairness of the alleged recoveries. 17. Further, the record is silent that the police officers offered their
 own personal search prior to searching the accused persons/houses, a
 salutary safeguard the courts have consistently emphasized to enhance
 credibility in search/recovery proceedings. While not a statutory ritual
 in every theft case, its absence--when coupled with non-joining of
 public witnesses and other infirmities--adds to the cumulative doubt
 about the genuineness of recoveries. 18. PW-3's own testimony underscores the weakness. He was
 "unable to identify" any person from the CCTV. Yet, the same witness
