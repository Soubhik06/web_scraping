MediaNama’s Take: The fact that a scam app was so easily available to unsuspecting users on Google Play Store is a cause for concern and suggests deficiencies in Google’s screening protocols for apps. MediaNama had previously covered the extent of fraudulent and duplicitous apps on legitimate app stores on both Google and Apple.

At the same time, Google is planning to allow real-money games on its app store in India, accepting third-party certification from self-regulatory bodies as proof of an app’s legal status. Given the lack of a national-level statutory regulator for online real-money gaming, the tech giant could end up opening the gates for an even greater number of fake apps and scams.

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) has filed a prosecution complaint before the PMLA Special Court in Ahmedabad against the promoters of the Dani Data App, a scam that masqueraded as a betting platform. The app had allegedly duped 1,200 people of Rs 1,400 crore in only nine days. The investigative authority had previously frozen Rs 20 crore in bank accounts linked to the perpetrators.

The ED initiated an investigation into the app following multiple First Information Reports (FIRs) registered against the platform at the Cyber Crime Police Station in Banaskantha, Gujarat.

The investigation revealed that the mobile application, launched by a Chinese national, deceived investors by promising returns from betting and a football game on apps downloaded from the Google Play Store and through WhatsApp referral groups. The app lured investors with the false promise of a guaranteed return of 0.75% per game. For instance, an investment of Rs 1,000 was falsely claimed to yield Rs 1.95 lakh within 30 days. Such promises enticed thousands of users to invest.

The scammers collected the funds through multiple Merchant IDs with various payment aggregators, such as Agreepay, BharatPe, Cashfree, and Paytm, and routed them into connected bank accounts between December 2021 and June 2022.

After accruing significant amounts, the accused simply removed the app from the Google Play Store. According to the charge sheet filed by the Gujarat Police, the accused, including the Chinese national, had conspired to cheat and dishonestly induce thousands of investors to deposit crores of rupees through the app.

The perpetrators laundered their gains through a network of mule bank accounts before integrating them into the legal system through cash withdrawals and the purchase of foreign currencies or cryptocurrencies.

The scam first came to light in 2023, following an investigation carried out by a Special Investigation Team (SIT) of the Gujarat Police. According to the SIT, a Chinese national named Woo Uyanbe from Shenzhen teamed up with locals to run the scam in Gujarat’s Patan and Banaskantha municipalities. They launched the app in May 2022 and managed to steal Rs 200 crore per day from people aged 15 to 75, mostly through betting on football games.

The app was active for only nine days, and Uyanbe fled India before the Gujarat Police could arrest him. The authorities lacked concrete evidence against their main suspect, and the state is yet to begin extradition proceedings.

The ED conducted a number of search operations against the scammers as part of their investigation. Starting in November 2023, the authority searched 14 premises at different locations in Delhi and Gujarat, followed by search operations at six premises belonging to payment aggregators in March 2024. The latter yielded amounts totalling Rs 16.43 crore, which were frozen under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002.

Last year, the ED also arrested two Chinese nationals under the PMLA for running a fraudulent lending scheme.
