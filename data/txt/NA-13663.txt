You can access the proposed amendment here: Draft Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference (TCCCPR) Third Amendment, 2026

TRAI’s draft amendment proposes classifying senders based on economic importance and sector, potentially giving critical entities like banks lighter enforcement treatment than ordinary businesses or individuals for sending spam . The draft does not specify any minimum enforcement threshold for such classified entities.

Comments on the draft are open until April 12 , and counter-comments are due by April 27 .

Key takeaways :

The problem : According to Truecaller’s 2025 India Insights Report , 770 crore fraudulent calls in 2025 impersonated banks, government authorities, and payment platforms . Lighter enforcement for banks could further blur the line between legitimate bank communications and fraudulent calls impersonating them.

TRAI received 3.1 million spam complaints in 2025 , with registered entities, including banks, accounting for a significant share. While TRAI argues that disconnecting telecom resources of banks could disrupt OTPs and transaction alerts for millions of users, the absence of an enforcement floor and lack of transparency in classification decisions means the framework operates largely at TRAI’s discretion.

Questions this raises :

What this means for users : Users receiving spam from banks and financial institutions may find that their complaints lead to weaker action compared to complaints against smaller senders. The draft effectively creates a two-tier enforcement system, lighter treatment for economically important entities and stricter action for everyone else.
