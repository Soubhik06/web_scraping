A new LocalCircles survey based on over 124,000 responses from 302 districts finds that more than eight in ten airline passengers encountered dark patterns on airline websites and apps in the past year. Dark patterns refer to interface designs that steer users toward choices they may not have intended, often by hiding, delaying, or manipulating key information. IndiGo recorded the highest number of complaints, including forced action, confirm shaming, and bait and switch.

IndiGo has cancelled nearly 5,700 flights between November 21 and December 9, affecting 12.5 lakh passengers. The airline says it has issued Rs 1,158 crore in refunds and will offer Rs 10,000 vouchers to severely impacted passengers.

False urgency and nagging were also frequently reported, appearing as countdown timers, repetitive prompts, or persistent upsells that pressure users at key decision points.

The December disruption demonstrated how interface design can aggravate operational failures. As flights were delayed or cancelled at scale, many passengers reported that they could not cancel their tickets because the cancellation option was disabled. Others received partial refunds despite public commitments to full refunds. At the same time, some users reported that tickets continued to be sold for flights that were later cancelled or significantly delayed.

These design choices shaped how passengers experienced the crisis. Instead of enabling quick exits or clear refunds, booking and cancellation flows added friction at moments of peak disruption. The episode illustrates why interface-level practices require scrutiny in sectors where failures carry immediate and high financial consequences for users.

The CCPAâs June advisory required e-commerce, quick commerce, and online travel aggregators to conduct internal audits on dark pattern compliance. The authority has published 18 declarations , and it has said that 26 companies filed reports, though disclosures vary widely in depth and quality.

At the same time, the CCPA has stepped up enforcement in these sectors, issuing clarification notices to 15 platforms between November 24 and 28 over continued use of dark patterns.

Airlines, however, remain outside this framework. This is notable because the Consumer Affairs Ministry has previously flagged manipulative airline booking interfaces and, in 2023, asked IndiGo to rectify its website following consumer complaints. Those interventions, however, focused on specific practices rather than systemic, audit-led scrutiny.

Against that backdrop, the LocalCircles survey places airline platforms among the most frequently flagged for manipulative design. Yet aviation remains the only major digital consumer sector where interface-level risks have not been subjected to formal audit or disclosure, even though passenger losses can run into tens of thousands of rupees.
