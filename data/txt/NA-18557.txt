Hey Folks, I’m an Indian born American who visited India for the first time in years, and I just wanted to share my observations about India. I’m not going to harp on the usual things people complain about such as corruption, pollution or traffic- you know about that much more than I ever will. Instead, I’m going to talk about aspects of India I was supposed by, both the good and the bad.
Background about me: I moved from India when I was 12, having grown up in Bangalore. I moved just as India was starting to change in 2000. I’ve been back every 2-3 years since about 2010. My family moved for two reasons- my dad was training in a very obscure branch of medicine, and because my sister is mentally disabled, and resources were very bad at that time. Now I’m a soon-to-be-doctor, and I wanted to visit my aging grandparents as I will have little vacation time in the next few years. So I travelled South India, mainly visiting family, but also sightseeeing
So these are the things that surprised me

1)	**India’s cultural heritage**: I visited Hampi, Shravanabelagola, Belur- Hallebeedu on this vist. Holy shit these places are amazing- especially the temples at Belur Hallebeedu. Real kudos to the Indian Archaeological society for maintaining these so well! And I think this must be improving- I remember visiting Char Minar and Golkonda fort years ago and being horrified by the vandalism there. So real kudos to the organizations that maintain these. Honestly, these places should be advertised more- I only heard about them because my mom mentioned it in an offhand comment

2)	**Industrialization**- Holy shit India is changing. Every time I visit, it seems to have progressed a decade. Cell phones access, imo, is going to be one of the things that helps India’s poor the most. I was amazed to see people in slums have smartphones- in many ways, it is an equalizer

3)	**Food**- best food in the world. Enough said. Though my cousins are oddly fascinated by KFC when all I want is idiyappam and dosa. Though Indian KFC is wayyyyyy better than American KFC

4)	**My family’s tendency to overpraise and overshit on everything in India**- My uncles especially have this strange tendency to shit on India while also being weirdly proud of obviously untrue things. Like they’ll be complaining about traffic and corruption, calling politicians all crooks, calling everybody stupid or corrupt, while also proudly boasting that the Airport is the best in the world, or that the best cars in the world are Maruthi Suzukis. Also they have this need to compare everything to the West- which really does no good

5)	**Healthcare Beliefs**- I was dismayed by how common Ayurveda and Homeopathy are in India, even among the upper and middle classes. In addition, there is no regulation of the medical sector- leading to frauds.– I apologize if this part is preachy!

a.	My uncle is a former chain smoker who has never run in his life, and eats like shit. He’s had three stents to his coronary arteries- aka, he’s extremely high risk to have a heart attack. He should be on a strict diet, increasing exercise, and on a statin drug+good control of BP. Yet some ayurvedic doctor has convinced him that all he needs is turmeric and a yearly intravenous infusion of god knows what. If he continues on his current path, he will be dead within the next decade- and he’s just turned 50. 

b.	 My aunt has BPs in the 200s. Her homeopathic doctor convinced her to take amlodipine diluted in 200 parts water. THAT DOESN’T DO ANYTHING.

c.	My grandpa’s allopathic doctor was trying to convince him to have have his prostate removed. My aunt was suspicious so she had me sit in (I’m going into Urology). His explanation was that my grandpa had BPH, which is true, so he needed his ppprostate removed, which is false. Yes, surgery is an option, but not before trying several other less invasive methods, from a drug, to what’s called a TURP. As soon as he realized I knew what I was talking about, his demeanor changed. We went to a different urologist, who happily performed a TURP and my grandpa is significantly better, and avoided a surgery that may have made him incontinent

d.	Exercise- PEOPLE NEED TO EXERCISE. DO NOT FOLLOW AMERICA’s lead

6)	**Treatment of people in a lower social standing**- The thing that always makes me deeply uncomfortable when visiting India is how badly the poor are treated. It breaks my heart to see children begging or selling stuff on the street. I’d try to give them money and my cousins would chide me either of giving money, or that I gave too much. Maybe, but I’m not going to miss that 50 rupees. In addition, the women in my family (not my mom- she gives no fucks)- eat after the men, which is a horrible thing. And I know my family is not innocent of these things- when I was a kid, my mom hired a girl called Devi, who couldn’t have been over 15. She was paid, but was paid a pittance. I didn’t feel guilty then, but I often think of that girl and what became of her. 

7)	**Environmentalism among many of the Youth**- My cousins, I’m, ashamed to admit it it- are more conscious about the environment than I am. They recycle, reuse far more than I do. If this is widespread, that’s an amazing thing

8)	**Shorts**- To end on a humorous note, why the Hell do people not wear shorts? Like it’ll be hot as hell, with no mosquitos, and people still wear trousers and shirts. My grandma yelled at me for wearing shorts, saying it’s Indecent- these were knee length basketball shorts! What am I missing?

If anyone has any questions about the US, don’t hesitate to ask!
