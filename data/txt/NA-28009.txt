\# India's Cyber Complaint Scandal: A Web of Fraud, Police Inaction, and Bank Profiteering 

&amp;#x200B;

\## The New Norm of Cybercrime Complaints

&amp;#x200B;

It starts seemingly innocently enough. You sell a product on Instagram or trade cryptocurrency on Binance P2P. The buyer, for whatever reason—whether genuine dissatisfaction or malicious intent—files a complaint on India's cybercrime portal, cybercrime gov website. Immediately, the police, often without conducting a proper investigation, block the funds in your account.

&amp;#x200B;

\## A Nationwide Problem 

&amp;#x200B;

This issue isn't isolated to small-scale sellers on Instagram. Even transactions on Binance P2P, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, are affected. A Telegram group named \[Indiansp2p\] documents the ordeal of 1,500 individuals who've had their funds frozen. Extrapolating this figure suggests that lakhs (hundreds of thousands) of individuals are affected, not just in the cryptocurrency world but also among Instagram sellers and other online businesses.

&amp;#x200B;

\## Geographic Complexities

&amp;#x200B;

Now, consider the geographic complications. If you live in Kerala and the police who blocked your account are from Delhi, you're stuck in a bureaucratic maze. They require you to travel to Delhi to provide proof and statements, a trip that's not only inconvenient but often impractical. The frozen funds stay where they are, and your business grinds to a halt.

&amp;#x200B;

\## The Rise of Police Commissions 

&amp;#x200B;

This is where the situation takes a dark turn. The police, realizing the financial chokehold they've established, begin demanding a commission to unblock funds. Reports indicate that Gujarat police, in particular, are involved in this unethical practice. They even offer to "help" your friends whose accounts are also blocked, for a commission, of course.

&amp;#x200B;

\## Banking on Blocked Funds

&amp;#x200B;

Banks are quietly benefiting from this crisis. When police block an account, the money remains in the bank. While the account holder earns minimal interest at the savings account rate, the bank is free to use these funds. If you request the bank to convert your money into a fixed deposit to earn a better interest rate, they deny it, stating that the money is "blocked" and thus not eligible for a fixed deposit. Meanwhile, everyday, crores (tens of millions) of rupees are being blocked even in the remotest villages of India.

&amp;#x200B;

\## A Grim Outlook for Entrepreneurs 

&amp;#x200B;

In a country that already poses various challenges to starting a business, this scam amplifies the risk and uncertainty, making India an increasingly unfavorable ground for entrepreneurs.

&amp;#x200B;

\## The Takeaway

&amp;#x200B;

The freezing of accounts without proper investigation, demands for commission to unfreeze assets, and the banks' silent profiteering—all of these elements create a complex web of unethical practices that deeply affect the business landscape in India. While the figures might be hard to quantify exactly, the message is clear: a reform is urgently needed. 

&amp;#x200B;

\## Verification and Accountability 

&amp;#x200B;

While the full scope of this scandal is yet to be uncovered, the impact is undeniable. Calls for verification from RBI and banks could provide valuable insight into the extent of this national crisis, but what is needed most is immediate intervention and oversight to restore trust and justice to the business owners and entrepreneurs of India.

&amp;#x200B;

This isn't just a scandal; it's a national crisis affecting the very foundation of business and entrepreneurial spirit in the country.
