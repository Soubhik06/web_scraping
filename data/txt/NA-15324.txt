Commuters and ride-hailing platforms across Karnataka have found a workaround to the government’s ban on bike taxis. While Uber replaced its bike taxi service with a “moto courier,” Rapido introduced a “bike parcel” feature. Taking to X (formerly Twitter), users marvelled at this solution, calling it a classic example of Indian jugaad.

Following suit, users claimed to become the “parcel” and reach their destination.

Became a parcel today since Karnataka HC has banned bike taxis. @rapidobikeapp is offering you to become a parcel and get delivered. #bangalore #rapido pic.twitter.com/UeyEz05m7F

However, these ‘bike parcel’ offerings reportedly disappeared after attracting widespread public attention.

This alternative emerged after the Karnataka High Court ordered a statewide suspension of bike taxi operations on April 2, 2025. Justice B.M. Shyam Prasad directed the platforms to cease operations within six weeks. He also prohibited the operation of bike taxis unless the State Government notified relevant guidelines under Section 93 of the Motor Vehicles Act (MVA), 1988, and its subsequent rules.

On April 29, the Court permitted cab aggregators to continue bike taxi operations until June 15. During the hearing, the state government argued that deploying bikes registered for personal use for commercial purposes is illegal, reaffirming its stance on the ban. Following this, the aggregators appealed the suspension order, but the High Court refused to stay it, enforcing the ban from June 16.

The workaround of ‘bike parcels’ raises questions about legality, given the Court’s verdict. Taking to X, BJP MP P.C. Mohan voiced concerns over aggregators attempting to outsmart the law through such rebranding.

Karnataka High Court has banned bike taxis. But aggregators continue to operate under the guise of ‘Bike Parcel’. Citizens are now being reduced to mere parcels in this legal evasion. Karnataka Government has failed to implement the law. Are aggregators above the law? pic.twitter.com/cMl5GRK2kV

Speaking to MediaNama, Alay Razvi, Managing Partner at Accord Juris, called such rebranding tactics deliberate attempts to circumvent the Karnataka High Court’s directive. “The Court’s refusal to stay the previous directive banning bike taxis affirmed the legality and enforceability of the prohibition,” he explained.

Ajay Kumar, Partner at Triumvir Law, echoed this sentiment: “If Rapido and Uber are aware that individuals are booking themselves as parcels to undertake rides, they should cease such offerings immediately. If not, they could be in flagrant contempt of the Court’s order.”

According to Razvi and Kumar, companies could face adverse consequences if they violate the Court’s directive. “If found guilty, such companies could face fines, vehicle seizures, suspension of licenses and delivery partners, alongside criminal prosecution,” Razvi explained.

Since the ban came into effect, authorities have seized over 103 bike taxis across 11 RTO jurisdictions across Karnataka. Notably, this isn’t the first time the Karnataka Transport Authority has initiated such seizures and imposed fines during crackdowns on bike taxis.

Notably, as the alternative ‘bike parcel’ service gains popularity, the appeal by the cab aggregators is underway in the Karnataka High Court. The next date for the hearing is June 24. However, this draws attention to Rapido and Uber’s potential defences in response to such ‘bike parcels’ or ‘moto couriers’. Razvi explained that platforms could contend that while they offer legitimate logistic services like bike couriers, misuse occurs by individual delivery partners. Overall, they could seek regulatory clarity or interim permission while the policy evolves, he explained.

Passengers who facilitate such bookings may also be engaging in misuse or even fraud, Razvi stated. He cited Section 66 (requirement of a permit) and Section 192A (penalty for operating without a permit) of the Motor Vehicles Act, arguing that using two-wheelers for passenger transport without proper authorization remains illegal—disguising such travel as parcel delivery doesn’t change that.

Razvi also warned that passengers could be held liable in case of accidents. “Insurance claims may be denied due to unlawful usage, and passengers could even face contempt proceedings,” he explained. Extending this discussion, Kumar opined that there is a distinction between carrying freight and passengers. “The fact that you’ve booked yourself as freight creates risks which users aren’t aware of in terms of insurance coverage and similar aspects”, he noted.

Kumar also indicated other potentially risky situations. “What happens in case riders have not had proper security training for carrying passengers, but have instead had training for ferrying freight. Differently, what recourse would be available to a passenger who was mistreated by a rider but ideally should not have misused the freight service themself in the first place”, he questioned.

MediaNama has contacted Uber and Rapido for comments on this development and will update the story upon receiving a response.

Besides cab aggregators, the Court order troubled users of the bike service and the industry organisations. The Internet and Mobile Association of India (IAMAI) has sought clarity on the legal status of bike taxis and urged the state government to promptly notify the guidelines for its operations.

Gig worker unions have also requested more sustainable solutions instead of an overnight ban. In a letter to Karnataka Chief Minister Siddaramaiah and Congress Leader Rahul Gandhi, Mohammed Salim, President of the Namma Bike Taxi Association, warned that the suspension could dismantle the livelihood of over one lakh gig workers.

This sentiment was echoed by Shaik Salauddin, National General Secretary of the Indian Federation of App-Based Transport Workers (IFAT). While he appreciated the Court’s directive, he stressed the need for a clear legal framework. “Bike taxi drivers will be rendered unemployed due to the negligence of cab aggregators,” he told MediaNama.

“Despite the state government serving multiple notices to platforms like Ola, Uber, and Rapido, the continuation of their operations cheated workers and jeopardised their livelihood. Essentially, they kept workers in the dark”, Salauddin explained.

Meanwhile, autorickshaw unions have welcomed the move, following past tussles with bike taxi riders, The Indian Express reported .

Given the ban’s adverse impact on gig workers, Salauddin advocated that platforms should take responsibility. Similarly, Razvi explained that platforms’ responsibility depends on the terms defined under the workers’ contracts. He claimed that aggregators could offer alternative work streams like food or goods delivery, provide reskilling programs, or route existing workers into permitted service areas. Meanwhile, platforms could engage with the state government to formulate a regulated, safe bike taxi policy, Razvi concluded.

Further, Kumar opined that the issue is beyond a simple legal recourse. “If human beings are willing to be driven as cargo, to use a motorcycle taxi to travel, there’s a clear demand situation which the government and aggregators must solve”, he explained.

While similar crackdowns have occurred in other states, the Central Government introduced the Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines in 2020 to help states regulate bike taxis. However, Karnataka’s lack of a state-specific framework leaves such services in a regulatory limbo.

Given the state’s turbulent history with bike taxis, it remains to see how the dialogue around the regulation of bike taxis in the state continues. Currently, states like Goa, Telangana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, and most recently Maharashtra have permitted bike taxis.
