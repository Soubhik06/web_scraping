An anonymous source who owns a fuel station in Nagpur has confirmed to MediaNama that they will not accept digital payments from May 10 onwards. “We, the union, took the collective decision,” he states in a phone interview with MediaNama.

“We wish to inform you that a lien/hold has been placed on an amount in your account as per the notification/complaint received on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal,” said the email from HDFC Bank. The temporary block can only be lifted after police authorities or the court give a NOC (No Objection Certificate).

Amit Gupta, president of the Federation of All Maharashtra Petroleum Dealers Association (FAMPEDA), stated that the police block transactions when a cyber complaint is filed, as fraudsters hack card or net banking credentials to buy fuel. “As a result, a lien/hold has been marked on the bank accounts of nearly 30 petrol stations, and in two cases, the accounts were completely blocked,” he added.Â

Last year, to speed up the identification process, the Reserve Bank of India announced that it could temporarily block the accounts suspected of cybercrime without registering a police complaint, which can be a lengthy process.

At least 2,000 digital transactions occur daily, and 60% of total transactions are digital, said VPDA in its press release.Â

“A truck comes to a pump on a highway, and its owner pays for it from Rajasthan, Delhi, Punjab, or some other state. The official statement from VPDA declared that the Home Ministry of India is ordering banks to freeze many people’s accounts. However, one month ago, the Delhi High Court directed the Union Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) to draft guidelines for freezing accounts.

A petrol pump owner, who didn’t want to be named, stated that the strike against digital payments will happen all over Maharashtra. “We have posted posters and published news everywhere,” he assured regarding informing Nagpur’s residents.

Nagpur isn’t the only city that is concerned. Nashik’s Petrol Dealers’ Association also cautioned its members over digital payments. They also suggested being cautious while carrying out larger transactions.

Vijay Thakre, the president of Nashik’s Petrol Dealers’ Association, acknowledged the growing problem with digital payments as UPI has become a lifestyle now. “In such cases, if there is a rise in instances and larger sums are involved, our dealers will be in trouble. We will then take appropriate steps”, he added.

There is no official confirmation regarding the state-wide halting of digital payments at petrol pumps. We are trying to contact the All Maharashtra Petroleum Dealers Association (FAMPEDA). We will update the copy once we receive more information.

When asked about the potential losses they have to face if they do not accept the UPI payments, the owner of HP Petrol Pump, Lamba Traders, said, “Prevention is better than cure.”

The idea behind the detour of blocking “suspected accounts” was to hasten the process of following the money trail before the fraudster launders the entire money beyond traceability. However, the effort of “blanket freezing” seems to do a bit more harm than the benefit it reaps later.Â

“It’s a matter of speed versus accuracy and clarity,” said Nikhil Pahwa, MediaNama Founder-Editor, while calling for “guidelines to prevent erroneous harm to people’s accounts.” Pahwa was commenting on the vicious cycle of free criminals and restricted business people when the Delhi High Court asked the Union Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) to make a uniform policy, standard operating procedures (SOPs), and guidelines to tackle the issue of blanket freezing of bank accounts in cybercrime cases.
