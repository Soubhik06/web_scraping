A fake food delivery website called ‘Jio Eat’ is scamming users by pretending to be part of Reliance Retail. The site advertises “Rs. 500 meals for Rs. 99” with free delivery and has been promoted through ads running on YouTube.

The scam was first reported by Inc42 . In one case flagged on X , a user posted that after trying to pay Rs. 99 on the site, an OTP was triggered for Rs. 30,099. The user wrote, “A big scam has been operational in name of #Jioeat, where the site shows lucrative offers via @YouTube ads…OTP of INR 30,099 gets triggered.”

@JioCare A big scam has been operational in name of #Jioeat , where the site show lucrative offers via @YouTube ads, and payment of INR 99 through fake @Razorpay site sends OTP of INR 30099. One can find other similar cases on X. @Cyberdost Can you please take this site down? pic.twitter.com/6HpY93d7xb

The scam works by impersonating the Reliance Jio brand through a fake website. The site falsely presents itself as a Reliance service, although the company has not launched any food delivery platform.

Users encounter the fake site after clicking on YouTube ads. The website – which goes by the URL jioeat.in – has no mobile app and offers only card-based payments. Once users try to place an order, they are prompted for an OTP but for a much larger amount than what they originally intend to pay.

Similar experiences have been shared on Reddit. In a conversation thread , one user wrote, “100% fake…sketchy site asking for OTP”. Elsewhere, another user remarked, “Google has many fake ads these days”. Within the same thread, a person claimed that the website’s domain was only bought in May 2025.

Notably, Reliance Retail has not yet issued a public statement about the scam as of June 18. However, users on X have tagged @JioCare about the issue. Elsewhere, Reliance Jio had issued a general advisory in August 2024 , alerting customers about scammers committing cyber fraud by targeting unsuspecting users.

Google, which owns YouTube, says it has strong policies in place to block scams and impersonation. According to its 2023 Ads Safety Report , Google blocked or removed 206.5 million scam-related ads globally. Elsewhere, Business Standard reported that the tech giant removed 247 million ads within India in 2024 to tackle the scourge of scams. Furthermore, it suspended 2.9 million ad accounts.

Google’s misrepresentation policy bars ads that impersonate brands or promote misleading financial claims. Additionally, the tech giant employs AI to vet ads even before they make their way towards users’ eyeballs. Elsewhere, Google India’s Trust and Safety Head told the Indian Express in February 2025 that AI detects 96% of violating content on YouTube. For context, YouTube removed 4.1 million channels and 85,000 videos in Q3 2024 for scam-related violations, including festival season-related fraud. Further, Google has bolstered its advertisers’ identity checks, with 90% of YouTube ads now coming from verified advertisers.

Even so, ads for ‘Jio Eat’ ran on YouTube, leading users to a fraudulent site. How did these ads pass YouTube’s screening process? And what checks are in place to verify advertisers offering food delivery services in India?

Under the IT Rules 2021 , intermediaries like YouTube must remove misleading content within 36 hours of being notified by the Government. In particular, Rule 3(1)(b) covers such misleading or false content.

The Consumer Protection Act (CPA), 2019 classifies scams like ‘Jio Eat’ as unfair trade practices under Section 2(47), which covers false or misleading representations about the quality quantity, among other things, of goods or services. This provision is key when addressing fraudulent activities in e-commerce. Elsewhere, Section 94 empowers the Government to take measures to prevent such unfair practices on online platforms and to protect consumers’ rights and interests.

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) can also act against scams of this nature under Section 18 of CPA . However, enforcement remains difficult when scammers operate behind anonymous domains or take down sites quickly.

The ‘Jio Eat’ scam comes at a time when OTP and card frauds are on the rise in India. According to Finance Ministry data, as reported by Business Standard , digital payment frauds touched Rs. 4,245 crore in the first 10 months of FY2024-25, with 2.4 million incidents reported.

A 2022 joint report by think tanks Deepstrat and The Dialogue highlighted the lack of structured data on cyber payment frauds in India, calling for better tracking to understand the true scale of the problem. The report noted that social engineering and phishing remain two of the most common tactics behind ATM, online banking, UPI and OTP frauds. It urged the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to strengthen security features and improve coordination across the digital payments ecosystem to better protect users and assist law enforcement.

To tackle OTP fraud to some extent, the RBI mandated the additional factor of authentication (AFA) for online international transactions. In January 2025, it also issued a circular to banks for improving protection against OTP theft.

Further, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra asked banks to strengthen oversight of third-party service providers.

The ‘Jio Eat’ case shows that scam ads are still slipping through platforms like YouTube, even though these platforms claim to have strict enforcement in place. This incident raises concerns about the gap between ad policies and what users experience.

With OTP and card fraud increasing, scam ads that misuse trusted brand names pose growing risks for consumers. Are online platforms doing enough to review ads? And will regulators need to tighten rules to keep such scams from spreading?
