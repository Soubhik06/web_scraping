Indian singer Asha Bhosle has secured interim protection from the Bombay High Court in a commercial intellectual property (IP) suit concerning her personality rights. Justice Arif S. Doctor granted the ad-interim (temporary court protection pending full hearing) relief after noting that Bhosle had made out a strong prima facie case.

The case names several defendants: Mayk Inc. and its AI platform, Amazon Seller Services Pvt. Ltd., Flipkart Internet Pvt. Ltd., an independent sketch artist, and Google LLC. Bhosle’s suit alleges that these parties facilitated or directly engaged in the unauthorised cloning of her voice, the sale of merchandise using her image, and the distribution of AI-generated content misusing her persona.

Notably, the Court restrained the defendants from exploiting her name, voice, likeness, and other attributes without her consent, and directed platforms such as Amazon, Flipkart, and YouTube to remove infringing listings and videos. Furthermore, the order also required disclosure of seller information where available. While Google sought time to take instructions, it agreed to remove specific links identified in the plaint. The case will be heard next on October 13, 2025.

In her commercial IP suit, Asha Bhosle levelled allegations against six defendants, accusing them of exploiting her personality rights without consent. She asserted that Mayk Inc. (Defendant No. 1) and its AI platform (Defendant No. 2) created and offered tools that cloned her voice. According to her, these platforms enabled users to generate songs and recordings mimicking her style, thereby distorting and modifying her performances for commercial gain.

Furthermore, she alleged that Amazon Seller Services Pvt. Ltd. (Defendant No. 3) and Flipkart Internet Pvt. Ltd. (Defendant No. 4) hosted listings for posters, wallpapers, and caricatures of her likeness. She maintained that these online marketplaces facilitated unauthorised sellers in profiting from her image and goodwill.

Additionally, she claimed that the sketch artist (Defendant No. 5) promoted and sold T-shirts and hoodies featuring her photograph and name on his website, thereby commercially exploiting her identity.

Moreover, she argued that Google LLC (Defendant No. 6), through its platform YouTube, enabled the dissemination of videos using AI-generated versions of her voice. These clips, she emphasised, misled audiences by suggesting she had sung songs she never performed.

The judge held that the plaintiff had made out “a strong prima facie case for the grant of ad-interim relief.” He noted that Bhosle is “a pre-eminent personality in the field of music” and thus “entitled to protection of her personality rights, including the applicant/plaintiff’s name, voice, photograph/caricature, image, likeness, persona, and other attributes of her personality.”

Crucially, he remarked that “making AI tools available to enable the conversion of any voice into that of a celebrity without his/her permission would constitute a violation of the celebrity’s personality rights,” adding that such technological exploitation “facilitates unauthorised appropriation and manipulation of a celebrity’s voice, which is a key component of their personal identity and public persona.”

Consequently, the Court issued specific directions. Against Mayk Inc., its AI platform, and the sketch artist (Defendants 1, 2, and 5), the judge granted injunctions restraining them from using or misappropriating Bhosle’s name, voice, likeness, or any other attributes through technologies such as AI voice models or face morphing. He also ordered them to remove infringing content, block access to copies, and provide subscriber details linked to the identified material.

In addition, Justice Doctor directed Amazon and Flipkart (Defendants 3 and 4) to disable all listings selling merchandise with her image within one week. He further required them, along with Google (Defendant 6), to take down any future infringing videos on YouTube upon notification, subject to the right to raise objections. Moreover, he ordered them to disclose seller or uploader information, including contact and payment details.

Indian courts have increasingly sided with public figures seeking to block unauthorised AI use of their image, voice, and persona. In several recent cases, judges have granted interim injunctions ordering platforms to remove the offending content.

Recently, courts have restrained AI-generated deepfakes and ordered removals in several high-profile suits involving Karan Johar , Abhishek Bachchan , Sadhguru , Akkineni Nagarjuna , and Aishwarya Rai Bachchan .

Moreover, judges have emphasised the dual harms of financial loss and damage to dignity that unauthorised AI content can cause, framing reliefs both as privacy-protecting and as safeguards against deceptive commercial use.

Importantly, courts have increasingly underlined that while freedom of expression remains vital, it cannot extend to misappropriating a celebrity’s identity for profit. Judges have stressed that AI tools that mimic voices or likenesses without consent undermine both commercial rights and personal dignity, and therefore warrant strict restraint.

This case matters because it signals how Indian courts are beginning to draw firm boundaries around the unauthorised use of celebrity identities in the age of AI. By granting Asha Bhosle interim protection, the Bombay High Court reinforced the principle that names, voices, and likenesses remain legally protectable even when new technologies enable their replication.

Moreover, the order placed direct obligations on global platforms such as Google, Amazon, and Flipkart to remove infringing content and disclose uploader details, thereby widening the scope of accountability across digital intermediaries.

Additionally, the ruling reflects a broader judicial trend. In recent months, courts have granted similar protections to prominent figures, including Karan Johar, Abhishek Bachchan, Aishwarya Rai Bachchan, Sadhguru, and Nagarjuna.

Judges have consistently emphasised that unauthorised AI reproductions cause both economic harm and reputational injury, and thus must be restrained. They have also clarified that while free expression remains protected, it does not extend to the commercial misappropriation of a celebrity’s identity.

Therefore, the Bhosle order not only addresses a single dispute but also adds to an emerging body of precedent. It demonstrates that Indian jurisprudence is adapting quickly to AI’s risks, balancing technological innovation with the protection of personality rights.
