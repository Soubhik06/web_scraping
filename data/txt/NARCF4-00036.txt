--- POST TEXT ---

Bittihotra Mohanty, alias Bitti Mohanty, a convicted rapist and son of a former IPS officer, passed away due to stomach cancer at AIIMS Bhubaneswar. This has sparked a disturbing discussion on social media, with some people sympathizing with the perpetrator. I was young when the case occurred and only learned about it when Mohanty was arrested after evading parole, assuming a fake identity, and working at a bank - a string of sophisticated crimes. He was granted bail, practiced law, and only recently had his bail revoked by the Supreme Court. Ironically, he was diagnosed with cancer and died without serving his sentence. 

It's appalling to see the support he's receiving. Some people, especially from the older generation, are saying, "poor kid, minor mistake." In light of the Kolkata rape and murder case, which has outraged the nation, I'm forced to ask: "Does a rape victim need to die for people to stop defending the perpetrator?" Even in old news debates, some claimed, "maybe it was consensual" or "they were dating."

What does it take for a convicted rapist to receive no sympathy, and for us to not doubt the victim's integrity?
