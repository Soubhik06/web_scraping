In a recent case heard by the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission in Gurgaon, Haryana, concerning Asian Public School, a serious issue of misrepresentation came to light. The bench, led by Shri Sanjeev Jindal, President, along with Ms. Jyoti Siwach and Ms. Khushwinder Kaur, Members, found the academy guilty of misleading parents regarding admission and withdrawal dates to overcharge fees.

The complaint began when a parent felt unfairly treated by the academy's fee. It was revealed that the school claimed on charging extra fees for the period extending from April 2020 to January 2021. This disagreement raised suspicions about the accuracy of the school's records.

Upon investigation, the District Commission uncovered evidence supporting the parent's claim. The school had indeed manipulated the dates of admission and withdrawal to justify their fee demands. This deliberate misrepresentation was supposed a failure in the school's service provision.

Accordingly, the District Commission issued a series of directives to amend the situation and compensate the dissatisfied parent. First and foremost, the academy was ordered to refund the amount of Rs. 19,500, along with an additional 9% interest. This refund aimed to amend the financial harm caused by the school's unjustified charges.

Also, fetching the internal torture and mental harassment caused to the parent due to the academy's conduct, the District Commission awarded a compensation of Rs. 15,000. This amount aimed to help the emotional risk imposed on the parent as a result of the school's misleading practices.

Also, considering the expenditures incurred by the parent in pursuing legal recourse, the District Commission commanded the school to repay Rs. 11,000 as action costs. This measure aimed to insure that seeking justice would not put an overdue financial burden on the dissatisfied party.

The ruling by the District Commission sends a strong message about the significance of transparency and fairness in educational institutions. Schools are entrusted with the crucial task of nurturing and educating young minds, and any deviation from ethical conduct weaken this responsibility.

Misrepresenting data, especially regarding financial matters, erodes trust and weaken the integrity of educational institutions. Parents rely on schools to act in the best interests of their children, and any breach of this trust can not be tolerated.

The case serves as a memorial for schools to uphold the highest standards of integrity and honesty in their dealings with parents and students. Clear and transparent communication regarding fees, admission procedures, and other applicable matters is essential to maintaining trust and fostering a positive educational environment.

Also it shows the significance of consumer rights and the part of consumer dispute redressal mechanisms in insuring responsibility and fairness. The District Commission's swift and decisive action in this case demonstrates its commitment to protecting consumers from unjust practices and upholding their rights.
