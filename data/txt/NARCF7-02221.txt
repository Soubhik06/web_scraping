The terminated teachers as per the Honourable Supreme Court verdict, have raised the demand for Rs. 2 crore compensation per teacher. If the state agrees to the compensation, how will this be funded? From public money right? Else govt bonds which again will be funded from public money. The way it looks, if the state govt loses the review petition, then the state will most likely pay compensation to the terminated teachers to make up for the salary paid till date (to be refunded to the govt as per the Supreme Court order) and some additional compensation for the job loss. The state govt have a habit of offering Rs. 10 lakh compensation. 

We all know WB is a poor state. So, a sudden additional burden of Rs. 52,000 crore will come up for compensation which will be funded from public money. 

I fear this amount or any compensation amount paid to the terminated teachers will be recovered from the common man in the state via taxes. Under the GST regime, states have retained some powers to levy local taxes. WB govt did a similar thing on cigarettes during the Sarada Scam and God knows what they did with that money collected from the common man. What percentage of people in the state are govt employees and enjoy retirement benefits, and what percentage are employed in the private sector devoid of any retirement benefits? What is the median and average salary of a govt employee and those employed in the private sector. So, effectively, those working in the private sector will bear the brunt.

I see that WB is headed towards increasing taxes, resulting in higher prices of goods and commodities, thereby creating further hurdles for the common man.
