PhonePe Limited has filed its updated Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP-I) with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) for a proposed initial public offering (IPO) that will be conducted entirely through an offer for sale (OFS), with no fresh issue of shares. As a result, the company will not receive any proceeds from the IPO. Instead, up to 50.66 million equity shares will be sold by existing shareholders, making the listing a pure secondary sale, and a partial exit for current investors.

According to the updated DRHP, the selling shareholders include Walmart’s WM Digital Commerce Holdings Pte. Ltd., which will offer up to 45.94 million shares. In addition, Microsoft Global Finance Unlimited Company and Tiger Global PIP 9-1 Ltd. will sell up to 3.67 million shares and 1.04 million shares, respectively.

Consequently, the entire offer will comprise shares being offloaded by the promoter and investor groups. Furthermore, the company is reportedly looking to raise around $1.3 billion and is targeting a listing valuation of $15 billion.

PhonePe is an Indian financial technology company headquartered in Bengaluru that operates a digital payments platform and related services. Its core products include digital payments via the PhonePe app and a range of financial and consumer-tech businesses such as Share.Market (a wealth and investment platform), and Indus Appstore (a localised mobile app marketplace).

For the six months ended September 30, 2025, PhonePe reported revenue from operations of Rs 3,918.46 crore, compared with Rs 3,207.51 crore in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the company posted a net loss of Rs 1,444.42 crore between April and September 2025, widening from a net loss of Rs 1,203.20 crore in the corresponding period in 2024.

In addition, for the six months ended September 30, 2025, PhonePe’s net worth stood at Rs 9,539 crore, up from Rs 9,054.63 crore from the corresponding period from last year. However, the company’s return on net worth stood at -15.14%, widening from -13.29% a year earlier.

Operationally, PhonePe processed 9.15 billion Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions in August 2025 with a transaction value of nearly Rs 12 lakh crore. The platform held an estimated 46% share of UPI transaction volume during the month.

The company says its margins depend on payment processing charges payable to banks and financial institutions. If these charges rise materially and PhonePe is unable to pass them on to merchants or consumers, profitability could deteriorate sharply.

Speculation early last year claimed there would be a change to the zero-fee framework governing UPI and RuPay payments, which the government later denied in June 2025. However, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Governor suggested the end of free UPI transactions on UPI the next month. The company warns that even small changes in processing costs can have a significant impact due to the high-volume, low-margin nature of digital payments

Under the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) guidelines, PhonePe participates in the payments ecosystem through sponsor Payment Service Provider (PSP) banks and is consequently dependent on Yes Bank, Axis Bank and ICICI Bank.

Any operational disruption, regulatory action or termination of arrangements with these banks could affect transaction processing, settlements and customer access, directly impacting volumes and service reliability.

The company warns that the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) circular introducing a volume cap on UPI transactions for Third Party Application Providers (TPAPs) may adversely affect its business.

Notably, the NPCI first introduced this limit back in 2020 , stating that it would come into effect in 2021. However, in 2022, NPCI pushed the market share limit implementation to December 31, 2024, before it was deferred again to December 31, 2026. While NPCI said that it “considered various factors” for the 2026 deferral, MediaNama has previously reported that there were concerns about how PhonePe and Gpay would reduce their transaction volumes to this cap.

PhonePe says it has incurred losses since inception and expects losses to continue as it invests in scale, incentives and technology. As mentioned earlier, it reported a net loss of Rs 1,444.42 crore for the six months ended September 30, 2025, compared with a net loss of Rs 1,203.20 crore in the same period last year.

For FY25, the company posted a net loss of Rs 1,727.41 crore, after a net loss of Rs 1,996.17 crore in FY24. It also reported negative operating cash flows of Rs 117.27 crore in for FY26 upto September 30, 2025.

PhonePe warns that its Consumer Payments business remains its largest revenue driver. The segment contributed 56.14% of revenue in the six months ended September 30, 2025, compared with 68.84% in the same period last year.

For FY25, this segment accounted for 63.34% of operating revenue, following 71.56% in FY24 and 82.91% in FY23. As such, any disruption to this segment could materially impact revenue and cash flows.

PhonePe warns that unauthorised parties may attempt hacking, phishing and fraud against its systems, users and partners. While it reports no material breaches in recent periods, it notes that cyberattack methods evolve rapidly and vulnerabilities may exist.

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, penalties of up to Rs 250 crore may be imposed for failure to maintain reasonable security safeguards.

PhonePe says its business depends heavily on the strength of its brand and the trust of users, merchants and partners. Any failure to maintain service quality, data security or effective grievance redressal could erode confidence and harm engagement.

Although it owns 201 trademarks linked to the PhonePe brand, negative publicity, merchant misconduct or poor customer experiences could still damage reputation, reduce usage and adversely affect revenues and operations.

PhonePe states that it plans to initiate international operations, including in Singapore and the United Arab Emirates (UAE), which could lead to increased costs and management complexity.

While it has extended UPI services to several countries through NPCI International Payments Limited, mandatory licences in some markets remain pending. The company warns that regulatory, tax, foreign exchange and enforcement risks, as well as stronger local competition, could hinder expansion and adversely affect financial performance.

PhonePe discloses instances of non-conformance in certain Registrar of Companies (RoC) filings relating to share allotments and notes that adjudication proceedings are ongoing. PhonePe says it cannot assure that regulatory action or penalties will not be imposed. Any adverse order could lead to financial penalties, remediation costs and reputational impact.
