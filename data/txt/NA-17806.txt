--- POST TEXT ---

Some scams are getting more advanced. I want to tell you about what happened to my close friend. He's gay and uses an app called Grinder. He met a guy from America who claimed to be a neurosurgeon with a good physique. They exchanged numbers and talked on the phone for a week. The American guy, after just six days, started saying he couldn't sleep without hearing my friend's voice and used sweet words. He even claimed to be booking a flight to meet my friend in Delhi and sent pictures of tickets and selfies from the airport.

When my friend asked for a video call, the guy always had excuses, like no option or network problems. He also sent pictures of newly bought shirts and a Rolex watch he claimed to be gifting. My desperate friend believed it all and waited for him in Delhi. Then, he got a call from someone pretending to be airport staff, saying his friend Harry had 1 crore in cash from America but was stopped for money laundering. They asked for 55k in Indian currency to complete his documents.

My friend called me, and I warned him it was a scam. Harry called many times, asking for money, but my friend didn't fall for it. It's shocking how people can be so trusting, especially when scammers target those in need. Stay aware and don't fall for these traps.
