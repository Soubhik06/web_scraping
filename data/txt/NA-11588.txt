 7.0 The Termination

 7.1 Vide show cause notice dated 29.04.2004, the respondents

 alleged that the applicant was allowed to join at Lucknow Division on the

 basis of transfer order and associated documents which were found to be

 fake on enquiry as part of a larger scam and asked the applicant to

 produce inter alia his appointment order. It is noted that main

 controversy is whether the applicant was validly appointed to the post

 under the respondents. In our view, to settle this question, production of

 the appointment order becomes necessary. In the reply dated

 17.05.2004, the applicant did not produce the appointment order and

 instead asked the respondents to supply copies of the documents relied

 upon by them and some other documents. 7.2 In regard to the centrality of the appointment order to settle the

 controversy of fraudulent or fake appointments, we may make a

 reference to the following opening observations made by Hon'ble

 Supreme Court in Secretary, State of Karnataka & Ors vs Uma Devi

 & Ors AIR 2006 SC 1806: (emphasis supplied)

 7.3 In public employment, recruitment through an open and

 transparent process against regular vacancies in posts as per relevant

 recruitment rules is sine qua non in terms of the constitutional scheme.

 An appointment order issued after due process and by authority

 competent to make the appointment is integral to such constitutionally

 valid public employment. 7.4 Now, if any appointment order was issued in favour of the

 applicant, in the normal circumstances it should be available with the

 respondents as well as the applicant, and thus either side should be able

 to produce it. However, when the factum of appointment itself is

 disputed by the employer alleging that the appointment has been made

 through fraudulent means based on fake transfer order, it is obvious

 that the employer's claim is that no appointment order was issued in

 favour of the employee. In such circumstances, the employee is required

 to produce the appointment order through which he was appointed to

 the service to rebut the employer's claim. Relevance of other documents

 becomes material if the applicant is able to produce his appointment

 order so that the authenticity, or otherwise, of the appointment order

 can be tested. In other words, all other documents and records are Page 10 of 14 CAT,Lucknow Bench OA No. 332/00248/2012 VirendraKumar Vs. U.O.I. &Ors. ancillary to the central issue of the validity, or otherwise, of the

 applicant's appointment to be proved through the production of the

 appointment order which the applicant has failed to do. In our opinion,

 by not producing a valid appointment order, the applicant has failed to

 make out a case in his favour. Consequently, we do not find any infirmity

 in the order dated 27.07.2004 of the respondents terminating the

 applicant's service. 7.5 It is noted that in a similar case in OA No. 485 of 2014 Rajesh

 8.0 The Subsistence Allowance

 8.1 The impugned order dated 01.09.2011 issued by the respondents

 in compliance with this Tribunal's order dated 07.08.2009 in OA No. 130

 of 2009 was limited to the issue of subsistence allowance. We have gone

 through the impugned order and noted that the respondents have

 declined the applicant's request for payment of subsistence allowance

 from 27.07.2004. The respondents state that the applicant was placed

 under suspension with effect from 29.04.2004 and the applicant's

 service was terminated vide order dated 27.07.2004. It is noted that the

 applicant's termination order dated 27.07.2004 was not set aside in

 earlier litigation. In the termination order dated 27.07.2004, there is a

 reference to the subsistence allowance having been paid.

 8.2 The applicant's service having been terminated vide order dated

 27.07.2004 and the validity of that order having been upheld by us in Page 13 of 14 CAT,Lucknow Bench OA No. 332/00248/2012 VirendraKumar Vs. U.O.I. &Ors. paragraph 7 above, no subsistence allowance is admissible to the

 applicant beyond 27.07.2004. Therefore, we see no reason to interfere
