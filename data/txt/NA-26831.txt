Your concern is valid, and it raises an important point about accountability in the banking system. In India, there are mechanisms in place to hold banks accountable for poor service, but they are not as direct or widespread as the penalties customers face for defaults. Here's a detailed analysis:

---

### **1. Current Mechanisms for Accountability**
- **RBI's Guidelines on Customer Compensation:** 
 The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) mandates banks to compensate customers in specific cases, such as:
 - **Delayed NEFT/RTGS Transactions:** If a bank delays crediting funds beyond the stipulated time, it must pay compensation to the customer.
 - **ATM Transaction Failures:** If an ATM transaction debits money without dispensing cash, the bank must resolve the issue within 5 days, or it owes the customer ₹100 per day.
 - **Cheque Clearance Delays:** Compensation may be provided if delays in clearing cheques lead to financial loss for the customer.
 - **Failed Auto-Debit Payments:** Banks are liable if a customer incurs penalties due to failed auto-debits caused by the bank’s error.

- **Banking Ombudsman Scheme:** 
 Customers can file complaints with the Banking Ombudsman, a quasi-judicial authority under the RBI. If a customer proves financial loss due to the bank’s negligence, the Ombudsman can order compensation up to ₹20 lakh and additional ₹1 lakh for harassment.

---

### **2. Gaps in the System**
- **Reactive Instead of Proactive:** 
 Compensation mechanisms typically require customers to file complaints, meaning they must be proactive in seeking redress. This process can be cumbersome and discourages many customers from pursuing their claims.
 
- **Limited Awareness:** 
 Many customers are unaware of their rights and the processes to seek compensation, leading to underutilization of existing mechanisms.

- **No Uniform Penalty for Poor Service:** 
 Unlike interest charges for loan defaults, there's no uniform penalty for general poor service, such as long queues, inaccessible branches, or delayed responses.

- **Corporate vs. Individual Bias:** 
 Banks are often quicker to resolve issues for corporate clients than for individual customers, creating a perception of inequity.

---

### **3. Why RBI Should Mandate Compensation**
1. **Fairness and Accountability:** 
 If customers are penalized for late payments, banks should be similarly held accountable for failing to provide timely services. This would ensure a balanced and fair relationship between banks and customers.

2. **Improved Customer Service:** 
 Mandating compensation for poor service would incentivize banks to improve their operations, leading to better customer experiences.

3. **Restoring Trust:** 
 A transparent system for compensating customers can build trust in the banking system, especially in a country where millions of citizens depend on banks for their savings and transactions.

---

### **4. Suggestions for a Mandate**
The RBI could consider implementing the following measures:
1. **Standard Compensation Rates:** 
 Create clear compensation guidelines for common service failures, such as:
 - Fixed compensation for delays in customer service resolution.
 - Penalties for repeated failures in digital banking or transaction processing.

2. **Automatic Compensation:** 
 Banks should automatically credit compensation to customers for issues like failed transactions, delayed services, or network downtimes.

3. **Transparency and Reporting:** 
 Banks must report service failures and compensation payouts to the RBI, ensuring greater accountability.

4. **Increased Awareness:** 
 RBI and banks should conduct campaigns to educate customers about their rights and compensation mechanisms.

5. **Penalties for Chronic Failures:** 
 For consistent poor service, RBI should impose fines on banks and use those funds to compensate affected customers.

---

### **Conclusion**
While mechanisms for customer compensation do exist in India, they are limited in scope and awareness. A stronger mandate from the RBI, ensuring automatic compensation for service failures, could create a more equitable and customer-friendly banking system. This would hold banks accountable, incentivize better service, and restore trust among customers.
