(19/12/2017)
Rohit Arya, J
 This writ petition under Article 226/227 of the Constitution
of India by one Dev Raj Kataria s/o late K.L.Kataria, one of the
accused in criminal complaint case N0.741 of 2010 registered on
17/05/2010 for the offence punishable under sections 417 , 418 , 420 , 467 , 468 and 120 IPC by the Judicial Magistrate First Class,
Shajapur at the instance of respondent No.4 and thereafter made
over to the Court of Sessions for trial as sessions trial No.55 of
2016 pending in the Court of II Additional Sessions Judge,
Shajapur (Mansinghka Charities Vs. Vishwa Jagriti Mission and
others) is pending consideration, taking exception to the order
passed by the State Government on 17/04/2017 (Annexure P/1)
appointing respondent No.3 as Special Public Prosecutor in 2 W.P.No.2835/2017 exercise of the powers conferred under section 24(8) of Cr.P.C.,
purportedly, on the ground that the aforesaid appointment has
been made on a mere asking of respondent No.4, however, there
is no material on record justifying appointment of Special Public
Prosecutor. It is further submitted that the respondent No.3 was an
advocate of respondent No.4, complainant in the aforesaid case
and had filed an application under section 301 Cr.P.C., in the
aforesaid case. The same was allowed by the trial Court vide
order dated 23/11/2016 within the scope of section 301(2) Cr.P.C., Under such circumstances, it is submitted that the
appointment of respondent No.3 as Special Public Prosecutor is
wholly unwarranted. Learned counsel for the petitioner relied upon the judgment
of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of Mukul Dalal and
others Vs. Union of India and others , (1988) 3 SCC 144 and a
decision of coordinate Bench of this Court in the case of Paras
Kumar Jain and another Vs. State of M.P., and others,
2012(3) MPLJ 223. 2. On notice, the respondents No.1 and 2 & respondents No.4
and 5 have filed two sets of counter-affidavits. 3. Respondents have commonly raised a preliminary objection
as regards maintainability of the writ petition questioning locus
standi of the petitioner to challenge the appointment of
respondent No.3 as Special Public Prosecutor under section
24(8) Cr.P.C., one of the accused to file the instant criminal case;
a multi crore scam case in the teeth of the fact that the High Court
has rejected the prayer for quashment of the complaint on a
petition filed under section 482 Cr.P.C., vide order dated
15/06/2015 passed in Mis. Cr. Case No.3714 of 2010 [Vishwa
Jagriti Mission (Regd.) & others Vs. M.P.Mansinghka Charities &
another ] and also dismissed criminal revision preferred against
framing of charges vide oder dated 06/07./2017 passed in Cr.
Revision No.538 of 2017 ( Dev Raj Kataria and others Vs. State of
M.P., and another ). 4. Respondents No.1 and 2 have submitted that a detailed
application (Annexure R/1) was filed explaining necessity for
appointment of respondent No.3 as Special Public Prosecutor in 3 W.P.No.2835/2017 sessions trial No.55 of 2016 to ensure the effective representation
of the prosecution in this multi crore scam, a sensitive case where
influential persons are accused and allegations of fraud,
manipulation, concoction and fabrication of documents have been
alleged against them in the matter of misappropriation of huge
public funds collected in the form of donations. Respondent No.3
is well aware of intricacies of the case having assisted
complainants as counsel during the complaint proceedings.
Hence, a request was made for appointment of respondent No.3
as Special Public Prosecutor at the expense of complainants. 5. State Government upon due consideration of the facts and
circumstances of the case and as the respondent No.3 has above
10 years experience in active legal practice has been appointed
as Public Prosecutor. Moreso, the complainant has expressed
apprehension of indirect support to the accused persons by the
Public Prosecutor under the influence of such persons and the
possibility of impartial effective presentation of the prosecution
being jeopardized, could not be ruled out. Hence, no illegality is
committed by the respondents No.1 and 2 in exercise of powers
under section 24(8) Cr.P.C., appointing respondent No.3 as
Public Prosecutor. 6. Respondents No.4 and 5 in the detailed counter-affidavit
have submitted that as urged in the private complaint now
committed to the Court of sessions for trial, all the accused in the
complaint upon misrepresentation accepted donations from
respondents No.4 and 5 and have issued the receipts thereof
showing that Vishwa Jagriti Mission holds a certificate of eligibility
for grant of exemption from payment of income tax under section
80G of the Income Tax Act, (For short, 'the Act') and assured tax
rebates to donors; whereas no such certificate was ever issued
by the Income Tax Department. By playing such fraud not only
from the respondents No.4 and 5 but also other persons and
public were cheated under the pretext of exemption from income
tax also resulted into tax evasion. It is also submitted that a
private complaint case has been filed against all the accused
persons in the year 2010 which was committed to the Court of
Sessions and at this stage, over a period of seven years has
passed by, even the trial programme has not been settled as 4 W.P.No.2835/2017 under one pretext or the other, the accused persons have been
evading the trial. Besides, this Court has rejected the petition
filed under section 482 Cr.P.C., for quashment of the complaint
whereby directed them to appear before the Court (supra).
Further, revision petition under sections 397 read with section 401 Cr.P.C., preferred against framing charges has also been
dismissed by this Court (supra). As the respondent No.3 has
been effectively presenting the case for the complainant before
the Court below, therefore, for effective presentation of the
prosecution case, the appointment of respondent No.3 who has
more than 10 years practice to his credit, as Special Public
Prosecutor under section 24(8) Cr.P.C., cannot be faulted with.
That apart, there is serious apprehension as against the public
prosecutor being not fair and independent in conducting the trial
for the prosecution as the same is evident from certain
correspondence between the AGP and the respondent No.3
being disclosed to the accused persons (referred to Annexure
R/4-2). Learned counsel has relied upon the following judgments ( i) State of Maharashtra and others Vs. Prakash
 Prahlad Patil and others , (2009) 12 SCC 159; (ii) A Division Bench order passed at Principal Seat
 in W.A.No.158 of 2015 dated 15/04/2015; (iii) Dr. P.S.Thakur Vs. State of M.P., and others, 2016
 (3) MPLJ 392; (iv) Order dated 08/05/2015 passed in
 W.P.No.5811/2014 (Bhramdutt S/o late Shri Shyamlal
 and another Vs. State of M.P. , through Principal
 Secretary, Law and Legislative Department, Vallabh
 Bhawan, Bhopal and two others)
and submits that the present writ petition is an attempt to misuse
the process of law only with an intention to delay the proceedings
and protract the trial. 7. Heard. 8. Before adverting to the rival contentions, it is considered
apposite to observe that the jurisdiction under Article 226 of the
Constitution of India is subjected to self-restricted limitations.
Extra ordinary Constitutional power is conferred upon the High
Court in the Constitution of India; it is nevertheless equitable
jurisdiction. Normally, this Court does not sit in appeal over a 5 W.P.No.2835/2017 decision of a State or an authority ( Article 12 of the Constitution of
India) to address on merits of the decision. Instead it ensures
that the authority taking a decision was competent to do so in law,
decision making process is based on relevant considerations and
is free from the elements like, arbitrariness, unreasonableness,
bias, malice or perversity. The power of judicial review under writ
jurisdiction is not omnipresent to set right anything under the sky. 9. In view of the above, the decision of respondents No.1 and
2 to appoint respondent No.3 as Special Public Prosecutor in
exercise of statutory power under section 24(8) Cr.P.C., by itself
is not vulnerable unless, the same falls in the ambit and scope of
interference under the circumstances detailed above
circumscribing the power and jurisdiction under Article 226 of the
Constitution of India. That apart, the concept of play in joints
acknowledged in our jurisprudence is always kept in mind while
the writ Court examines the sustainability of an executive action. At this stage, it is expedient to refer to the judgment of the
Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of State of Maharashtra and
others (supra) as regards the scope of judicial review in such
cases: (Rohit Arya)
