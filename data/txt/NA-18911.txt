--- POST TEXT ---

So one of my close relatives (late 20s) has been seized by this opportunity to earn a lot (double/triple/quadruple your investments in about 3 years time), and has solicited cash from me of the order of 1M Rupees towards investment, which I am promised will be returned after 3 years with atleast a doubling of the sum. I hoped it would not be some MLM scheme, as I know this person to be well educated, hard working, honest, intelligent etc. and I was hoping that (s)he has struck on some great creative business idea which (s)he wanted me to believe in and invest. On probing it turns out to be some "network marketing" scheme dealing with selling travel packages. Given our close family ties, there was just a simple request to put in the money and expect the fallback in 3 years.

The seemingly valid and rational reasons behind these and the salient points as explained to me were, in no particular order (some of these are clearly linked to rationalizing a MLM or Affiliate marketing scheme):

* It is a legal scheme and not MLM. I could not get the name other than "Infinity", for reasons of secrecy (I wondered whats so secret if it is legal - probably because my question about why, if it gives so much returns, is not every one enrolled in it (which, on second thoughts, is what these MLM kind of things depend on to prosper - selling the scheme and not any product - which is incidental to the scheme)).

* People who introduced it (colleagues) are earning in crores. You get a bi-monthly cheque, and for some of the guys it seems it runs in crores every time.

* It is a new scheme in India, though internationally it has been running for ~15 years (Infinity downline does not exactly fit in with this description, though it fits the name). So it is best to get into it ASAP without the delay of even a day! (perhaps this is how they are asked to approach new people)

* The 1M deposit will ensure a quadrupling of the sum, a lesser amount say 0.5M will return say 2-3 times, etc.

* (S)he really intends to work hard on this, and I have absolute trust that the intentions behind this are to be able to generate income not just for him/herself but for close relatives (like me).

* (S)he is confident that if things do not work out, (s)he can still use the regular income from job (which (s)he will still work full time) to repay the funds taken from me/others.

* The scheme is not MLM, as somehow the compensation is equal at lower levels (could not get the details), and probably the hierarchy is not too deep, so it seems that the benefits are equally distributed (not sure how exactly), and there is incentive for your higher ups to ensure that the ones below you are successful.

* Though I and even (s)he may not trust the scheme ((s)he knows about things like MLM/Ponzi etc), I can trust him/her - (s)he will 100% compensate me in any case (through personal commitment from job earnings). His/her commitment to doing something which has a promise of earning on our behalf is there without doubt. Discussion on the fact that in such schemes the success % is low, there is no intrinsic value other than just selling an idea, is countered by saying that (s)he is fully committed to make it work by working hard on it.

* (S)he has nothing to lose, as there is already some fallback in terms of owning a house, and having a stable job. If it works, the promise is about getting a windfall which can set you up for life, and benefiting your near and dear ones all the same.

* I will be a "sleeping partner" who does not have to do anything other than give some money and yet earn the same benefits - which (s)he has committed by taking the money from me.

I tried to explain that:

* I am never for such schemes as they do not add value to anything (in terms of creating a new product, solving some problem, building some skills etc), and so it goes against my ethics,

* it may benefit some people dis-proportionately, but a majority may suffer,

* there is a factor of luck and also of talent of certain kinds which everyone may or may not have,

* may end up taking up too much time doing something one may not like (if one does like it - marketing and sales, talking to random strangers trying to sell something you may not completely believe in etc., then it is probably Ok),

* should not strain the relation if I refuse - I know how things can go bad in such cases

etc.

I am not sure if I convinced him/her. I still have the request to lend some money so (s)he does not feel bad about having helped a total stranger instead (by returning their investment with a guaranteed return in 3 years), when it could have been a close family person like me instead. (S)he almost pleaded to give atleast some thing and unfortunately I am still thinking about it (which (s)he has taken as a negative).

I just needed the views from people out here.. what would you do in this case. Would you help out as a token of well wishing and trust, be neutral and non-committal (giving some - probably valid - reasons for not be being to help but wishing well all the same), or actively discourage him/her for going into this - though the person really believes they have their bases covered and want to take this as a good opportunity to make it better in life, though there is nothing bad the way it is now.

Thanks!
