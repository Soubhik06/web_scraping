Let me tell you a story of an IT service company called Revature. 

**TL;DR- Revature is an IT company that preys on recent college grads. Do your homework on them if they contact you, because there's a high chance it's a scam of some sort.**

Last May, I graduated college with a degree in physics. My job hunt began the January before that, because I wanted to try and be set up for employment as soon as I could after graduation. 99% of my job applications went out through Indeed, and the other 1% went directly to big companies that I knew off the top of my head in my area (like NASA, Boeing, etc.)

One day I get a call from someone with a heavy Indian accent. They claim to work for a company called Revature, and they *insist* that I applied to them on Indeed. Except, I didn't, because I even looked through my list of job applications and Revature was never on there. Furthermore, the worker proceeded to ask about my education, employment history, skills, etc... all of which would've been known had I actually applied. No matter, maybe a third-party company pointed them my way since they saw I was applying to computer science related jobs. So I accept their offer to do a "video technical interview" later that week.

The company then sends me maybe 40 or 50 websites that entail all kinds of technical information about IT, programming, Javascript, OOP, and whatnot. Seriously? They want me to study for an interview, and memorize an amount of knowledge that would take several months to master in less than a week, while I'm in my last semester of college? I glanced over the material, but decided it would be fruitless; there was no way I'd be able to learn all this stuff in 4 days!

So Friday comes around and I prepare for the video interview. Thinking it was a regular interview, I dressed appropriately, had my resume at hand, and was ready for the normal questions. But, after a brief introduction, the interviewer jumped right into super-intense questioning about the material from before. It was about 8 minutes of non-stop interrogation about my CS knowledge, and being a physics grad, I obviously didn't know most of it. I totally *bombed* the interview, and left the video call feeling embarrassed and defeated. 

The following week I receive a call from Revature. Despite the fact that I completely butchered the interview and obviously knew *nothing* about the material and did *not* study for it, they offer me a job with them. They want to send me to Virginia for a 12-week long training session, where I would get paid minimum wage and have to share an apartment with 3 other candidates, after which time I would begin actual full-time work and be contracted in for a year. I said no, because I felt uncomfortable working for a company that I (and no one in the field that I spoke to) had heard of, and that still wanted me after destroying an interview. It just seemed like a bad idea all around, and after finding a few other forum posts that said something similar, I think I made the right call. I also asked the person who called me where they got my information- they said they received it from my application through Indeed, so I guess that's still a mystery.

Also, I found some reviews for the company on Glassdoor. It seemed like all the positive reviews were placed at around the same time, and I did find a source somewhere that said employees at the end of the training/contract period would sit you down and watch over your shoulder to make you leave a good review for the company. I'll see if I can dig that up.

Moral of the story: Revature seems like a scammy company that preys on recent, desperate college grads to exploit them for money/labor. I could be wrong, maybe they're a great company, but if they contact you, beware!
