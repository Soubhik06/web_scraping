Smartphone makers, including Apple, Samsung, and Google, have pushed back against at least six government requests to pre-install apps on phones sold in India, the Manufacturers’ Association for Information Technology ( MAIT ) said.

The most recent request came from the Unique Identification Authority of India ( UIDAI ), asking manufacturers to pre-install the Aadhaar biometric identity app , as per industry documents reviewed by Reuters .

What UIDAI asked for : In January 2026, UIDAI asked the IT Ministry to engage Google, Apple, and leading manufacturers on pre-installing the new Aadhaar app, which lets users update personal details, manage family profiles, and lock biometric data.

UIDAI argued that citizens could access Aadhaar “without the need for separate downloads.” The request was not a formal order, and it remains unclear whether it is still active.

Why the industry pushed back : Apple and Samsung raised safety and security concerns . MAIT argued that pre-installation “would not drive greater public good” and would force manufacturers to maintain separate production lines for India vs. export markets.

India’s smartphone exports hit $30 billion in 2025 , driven largely by Apple and Samsung, making this a direct commercial threat, not just a policy dispute. MAIT also pointed out that Russia is the only other country that mandates government app pre-installs on phones.

A pattern of pushback : On March 10, MAIT wrote to IT Ministry official Ravinder Kumar Meena opposing Sachet , a government disaster alert app, and referenced Aadhaar and five other pre-install requests in the same letter, saying the industry “has been consistent in its recommendation against pre-installation” each time. The full list of six apps remains undisclosed. MediaNama has asked MAIT for details.

The Sanchar Saathi precedent : In November 2025, the Department of Telecommunications directed manufacturers to pre-install Sanchar Saathi , a telecom security app, on all new devices, with functionalities that “must not be disabled.”

The Internet Freedom Foundation ( IFF ) called it converting “every smartphone sold in India into a vessel for state-mandated software that the user cannot meaningfully refuse.” The government rolled it back within days after IFF and opposition parties pushed back.

The Aadhaar security context : Aadhaar holds biometric data of 1.34 billion residents and carries a documented breach history. In 2023, a hacker put data of 81.5 crore Indians including Aadhaar and passport details up for sale on the dark web.

IFF founder Apar Gupta told Reuters the proposal was “clearly problematic,” saying it shows “a greater amount of government desire of controlling smartphone usage from the very beginning.”
