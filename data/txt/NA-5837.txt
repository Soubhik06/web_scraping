unsuccessful in both the Courts below whereby his suit for relief of

permanent injunction has been dismissed. 2. The suit was filed by the plaintiff on the ground that the

were owners in possession of agricultural land mentioned in para 1 of the

year 1960-61. It was alleged that 402 kanals 16 marlas of land had been

acquired by the State of Punjab and Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh had sold

112 kanals and a partition was effected between the two brothers and 273 RSA No.2963 of 2011 2 kanals 19 marlas was given to plaintiff No.1 and 273 kanals 17 marlas was

given to Ganga Singh. Plaintiff's father, Ganga Singh transferred his 2/3rd

share to the plaintiffs as per judgment and decree dated 13.03.190 and the

plaintiffs were owner in possession of other agricultural land and details

per the pleadings, plaintiffs, their father, Ganga Singh and his brother Bhim

Singh and his sons filed an application before the S.D.M./Prescribed

Authority for exemption of surplus land in April 1997 which was

dismissed on 31.03.2003. The said order of the prescribed authority was

arbitrary, illegal and against the law and no land had ever been declared

surplus on 21.02.1959 which was alleged in the order dated 31.03.2003.

The plaintiffs had applied for certified copies of the order dated 21.02.1959

and the copying branch made a report that no file of title Ganga Singh Vs.

State was entered in their report, and accordingly, it was pleaded that the

plaintiff's father had filed an application on 02.12.1977 before the

prescribed authority who vide order dated 03.12.1977 had held that there

was no land of the plaintiffs which was declared surplus. Accordingly, the

mutation No.4006 dated 28.10.1980 in the name of the defendant-State was

wrong and there was no formal order for changing the revenue record and

that the State has no concern with the property of the plaintiffs. RSA No.2963 of 2011 3 Accordingly, mutation No.4006 was challenged and the suit was filed on

31.07.2006 with the further prayer that plaintiffs were entitled for

rectification of the records and permanent injunction. 3. In the written statement, the defendants stated apart from taking

various preliminary objections regarding maintainability of suit, lack of

notice, non-joinder of necessary parties, jurisdiction of Court etc. and on

merits, denied the facts and pleaded that the land of Ganga Singh and Bhim

Singh, who were big land-owners, was declared surplus to the extent of 156

S.A. 4 Units under the provisions of Punjab Security of land Tenures Act,

1953 (for brevity, 'the Act') vide order dated 21.02.1959 passed by the

Collector, Agrarian, Rohtak. 402 kanals 16 marlas of land of both brothers

were transferred to Punjab State vide mutation No.2586 dated 15.06.1962

and it was denied that land mentioned in mutation No.293 belongs to the

plaintiffs. The partition which was effected between both the brothers also

included the land which was declared surplus vide order dated 21.02.1959.

Accordingly, the transfers made by the plaintiffs after declaring the land as

surplus are illegal null and void and the owners had no right to resume the

possession of the said property. The civil Courts jurisdiction was objected

to since the property vested in the State having been declared surplus and

proper procedure have been followed by the Collector, Agrarian, Rohtak. 4. The trial Court, on the pleadings of the parties, framed the

following issues: 5. Plaintiff No.1 examined himself as PW1, one Satpal Singh,

PW2 and Prem Singh, Clerk as PW3 whereas the defendants examined

Raghbir Singh, Naib Tehsildar as DW1. 6. The trial Court, after taking into consideration the evidence on

the record, came to the conclusion that the brothers, Bhim Singh and Ganga

Singh who were big land-owners of 1292 kanals 18 marlas and out of this

chunk, 644 kanals 14 marlas had been declared surplus and was acquired

vide mutation No.2586 dated 15.09.1962, 2693 dated 12.10.1962 and 2085

dated 05.09.1962. As a precautionary measure, both the brothers had sold

land measuring 155 kanals 3 marlas vide mutation No.2545 dated

27.05.1960, 2651 dated 24.09.1962, 2666 dated 16.10.1962 and 2667 dated

25.09.1962 and before possession of land could be taken by the State, got

effected a mutual partition whereby 273 kanals 17 marlas came to Ganga

Singh and 273 kanals 19 marlas came to Bhim Singh vide mutation No.3012

dated 21.12.1966. Ganga Singh, thereafter, transferred his 2/3rd share in the

name of his sons vide judgment and decree dated 13.03.1977. RSA No.2963 of 2011 5 7. Vide order dated 21.02.1969, the land measuring 152 S.A. and

3 units was declared surplus and as per the plaintiff the prescribed

authority/SDM, Rohtak, vide order dated 03.12.1977, held that there was no

surplus land. The trial Court noticed the Full Bench judgment of this Court

in State of Haryana Vs. Vinod Kumar & others 1986 PLJ 161 that the

jurisdiction of the civil Court was not barred provided the authorities had

followed the procedure prescribed and noticed the admission of the

plaintiffs Bhawar Singh, PW1 in his cross-examination that on 21.02.1969,

156 S.A. and 3 units were declared surplus. Accordingly, keeping in view

the provisions of Section 58 of the Indian Evidence Act, that there was a

clear-cut admission and no further proof was required regarding the order of

surplus being passed. Litigation arising of the persons who had purchased

land on 13.5.1958 measuring 24 kanals from the plaintiffs and their

predecessors-in-interest/Ganga Singh was also taken into account and the

whereby the appeal filed by Lal Chand was dismissed, in which it was

noticed that Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh were respondents No.2 & 3 in the

order passed by the Collector, who dismissed the appeal on 12.06.2002 and

Lal Chand had filed revision and the same was dismissed on 22.12.2004 and

second revision filed before the Financial Commissioner was dismissed on

28.08.2007. Accordingly, the factum of knowledge of the surplus

proceedings and the subsequent purchasers litigating with the State was

held to be known to the plaintiffs. It was further noticed that in RSA

No.4798 of 2001 decided by this Court on 28.09.2004, subsequent

purchasers from Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh had challenged the orders

dated 05.08.1996 and 23.10.1996 passed by the Collector, Agrarian and

31.10.1996 by the Assistant Collector since the land had been transferred RSA No.2963 of 2011 6 after the appointed day under the Act on 30.07.1958, this Court had

dismissed the second appeals on the ground that the land vested in the State

Government and the purchase was ineffective. Accordingly, it was held that

the plaintiffs could not plead that they had no knowledge to the extent of

their land being held surplus vide order dated 21.02.1959 . 8. The facts that State could not place on record the original order

of 21.02.1959 was taken into consideration and it was observed that the

State Government had suggested taking legal action against the concerned

officials who committed fraud by writing letters Exhibit D12 & D13 to the

tune that there was no surplus land of the plaintiffs. Accordingly, it was

held that Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh had knowledge regarding the

surplus land and had cleverly sold the land and even the vendees could not

protect their title as the sale was effected after 30.07.1958 the appointed day

under the Punjab Security of Land Renures Act, 1953. The plaintiffs had

also initiated proceedings in the year 1997 to the effect that their land had

not been declared surplus and an order had been passed on 31.03.2003

adversely against them which order was never challenged before the

prescribed authority. Instead, a civil suit was filed. The order dated

03.12.1977 wherein it had been held that there was no surplus land was

discarded since there was no reasons in the said order and neither any

details as to how much was the land in the hands of the big land-owners and

what was the relief claimed was noticed, and accordingly, it was held that

civil Court had no jurisdiction and the possession of the plaintiff was of no

value and the said fact that the Government being owners could oust the

plaintiff at any point of time and the mutation dated 28.10.1980 in the name

of the State was right, and accordingly, the relief of permanent injunction RSA No.2963 of 2011 7 was declined. Even the claim was held to be time barred having been filed

35 years after the proceedings had become final and even the application

was dismissed earlier on 31.03.2003, and no appeal or revision was filed

under the Haryana Ceilings of Land Holdings Act, 1972 , and accordingly,

the suit was dismissed on 30.04.2010 by the Addl.Civil Judge, Sr.Division,

Rohtak. 9. The plaintiffs filed an appeal before the lower appellate Court

and the same was dismissed on 07.03.2011 by the District Judge, Rohtak

which has led to the filing of the present regular second appeal. 10. Counsel for the appellant has contended that the order dated

21.02.1959, declaring the land surplus was never on record and in the

absence of the same, the Courts below were in error in deciding the suit

against the plaintiffs. It was, accordingly, pleaded that civil Court had

jurisdiction since the mutation had been done without there being any order

on the record. The submission of the counsel for the appellant is without

any basis. The suit has been filed by the plaintiff and on issues No.1 to 3,

onus is on the plaintiff to prove that the land was never declared surplus as

the plaintiff had come to the Court seeking declaration. It is the plaintiffs

who have to stand on their legs to prove that the order dated 21.02.1959 has

not been passed. Even otherwise, other factors which have to be noticed

that before the lower appellate Court, no such submission was made and

rather the findings on issue No.2 was not touched. This would be clear by

judgment of the Court, para 11 of which reads as under: 11. Thus, from the above, it would be clear that in a regular second

appeal, a question which has not been argued before the Courts below,

cannot be agitated. Before the lower appellate Court, only the question of

jurisdiction of the civil Court regarding the grant of injunction was argued

and it was held that the relief of injunction being a consequential relief and

in the absence of any declaration, injunction could not be granted. To be

fair to the counsel for the appellant, at the very outset, he also pointed out

that in a similar situations, purchasers from Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh

have also raised this issue in RSA No.3993 of 2011 decided on 27.09.2011,

titled as Anil and others vs. State of Haryana . Since the same counsel

argued the appeal, the submission was also regarding the order dated

21.02.1959. The said submission was rejected in regular second appeal by

making the following observations: 12. The said observations pertaining to the land belonging to the

same family of Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh is binding upon this Court

having been passed by a Coordinate Bench. Even otherwise, as noticed

above, the onus was on the plaintiffs to stand on their own legs. Various

vendees who were purchasers from Ganga Singh and Bhim Singh,

challenged the matter before various revenue authorities wherein the order

declaring the land surplus was passed vide order dated 21.02.1959. No

effort was made by the plaintiffs to place this order on record and to

produce the record of the revenue authorities from the said case solely

because for obvious reasons it did not support their case. Thus, the

submission of the counsel that in the absence of the order on the record, the

Courts below were wrong in dismissing the suit cannot be accepted. 13. The suit being patently time barred having been filed on

31.07.2006 would be a ground to reject the plaint itself as the order of

declaring the land surplus was dated 15.6.1962 and the predecessor in

interest of the plaintiff had made no effort to challenge the same and the

first cause of action arose on 31.3.2003 when an application was dismissed

for declaring the land exempt. The Mutation No. 4006 being dated

28.10.1980 also gave cause of action but the plaintiff being in possession

kept sleeping over their rights and accordingly the suit itself was liable to

be dismissed in view of Section 3 of the Limitation Act, 1963. RSA No.2963 of 2011 10 14. Accordingly, in the absence of any question of law much less

any substantial question of law arising, the present regular second appeal is

dismissed and the judgments and decrees of the Courts below are up-held.
