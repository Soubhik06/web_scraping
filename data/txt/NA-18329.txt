This is just a rundown of what my personal experience has been as a tourist. I hope it helps someone. This is all as of today, Thanksgiving, 2016.

* Arrived at IGI Airport (Delhi) earlier this week. Despite what I heard online last week, the foreign exchange counters at the airport were only issuing 70 USD worth of rupees this week, which is Rs4200 at their atrocious 60:1 rate.

* Queued for an ATM at the airport but when I got to the front of the line the ATM wouldn't read my card. That was IndusInd Bank.

* The other bank that had an ATM at the airport was out of cash.

* The next day I tried to change money at a foreign exchange in the city but they were out of cash. They gave me a slip of scrap paper with the name &amp; address of the international branch of a bank and told me to show up there at 10am the next day.

* I did show up at 10am and waited two hours and was able to exchange another 70 USD at 68:1 rate. 

* I walked around looking for a functional ATM. *Most* of them are non-functional.

* It's hard to tell sometimes if the queues are for banks or ATMs. You can ask people but sometimes it is difficult to understand due to language barrier. 

* Sometimes a person will see an ATM and try their luck and a line will form instantly but it's a "fake" line because the ATM is out of cash from the start.

* I got in line at a bank to change money and the official told me they couldn't change money there but to go to their other branch a few blocks away. I started walking and...

* ...two men approached me and told me they knew of an "official place" that had no lines and could change money for me. Considering the scrap of paper on which I had received good information about the first money exchange, and how tucked away the office was on the first floor of that building and how I would never have found it myself, I trusted them. They walked me to a damn travel agency. The agent tried to sell me train tickets, airplane tickets and tours. Then he offered to change my dollars but it would be with "old and new money". I left.

* Today I have visited at least 10 ATMs, which took all day. All but two were non-functional. One was functional but only accepting deposits. One was dispensing cash so I got in line but when I got to the front it wouldn't read my card. That was Punjab National Bank.

* I did eventually find the "other branch" of the one bank, and I got in line to exchange more USD. There was a fist fight between two men. The bank "bouncer" saw me in line and let me into the bank because my transaction was not the usual. In the bank I watched as two of the bank employees had a shouting match between them. I've never seen that in a bank before. The hours are long and tempers are short. I was able to exchange the rest of my USD for INR. However, I did not bring enough USD for my whole trip - not even close. I am not naming this bank because of the things that happened outside and inside.

* As of today I have been here for four days and have not been able to use an ATM to obtain any money. 

* I looked at the US Embassy website but the only statement they have issued is the original information from November 9 with no updates. They still have the wrong amounts for ATM withdrawals posted.

* I checked into wiring money from Western Union but, while they will *take* the money from your bank account in the US, apparently they won't *give* the money to you in India these days. There's been at least one story written on this, and all of their ratings since November 9 have been one star because people aren't being able to pick up their cash anywhere.

I am lucky to have brought with me enough money to get by for a few weeks and am not going hungry. However, I wasn't planning to stay in hotels because they are expensive. I was planning to rent an apartment and they don't take credit cards. I am open to suggestions, of course, but otherwise I am just going to try to hold out and wait for things to settle down a bit. I'm sorry that I can't contribute more to the economy but the amount of money I have is finite and when it's gone I don't know what I will do. 

EDIT: Before I left home I called my own bank and I checked with the foreign exchange offices and none had any rupees to sell. I checked at the foreign exchanges at four airports on the way to India and none of them had rupees either. 

EDIT 2: Just now the rules have changed again and "foreign nationals" (aka tourists) are only allowed to change their currency for **Rs5000 per week**. That's about $73. It will be noted in your passport.
