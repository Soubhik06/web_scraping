1. Read the situation correctly — and only from officia channels

- Today the J‑&amp;‑K administration has already started moving people out of the most exposed villages after heavy cross‑border shelling. Keep checking the DC‑Jammu / DC‑Samba / DC‑Kathua Telegram and X (Twitter) handles, AIR Jammu (100.1 MHz) and Doordarshan for fresh instructions. 
- The Union Home Ministry has asked all border‑states to rehearse full civil‑defence drills (air‑raid sirens, black‑outs, mass‑evacuation). Treat every siren or SMS alert as real until the authorities declare “all clear”. 
- International and national media confirm that Pakistan has authorised “corresponding action”, so further strikes are possible without warning. 

2. Pack a 72‑hour “go‑bag” now

|**What**|**Minimum quantity**|**Notes**|
:--|:--|:--|
|Water|3 litres / person / day|small bottles easier to carry|
|Ready‑to‑eat food|2 000 kcal / person / day|high‑energy bars, dry fruit|
|Medicines &amp;amp; first‑aid|3 days of regular meds + ORS, antiseptic, dressings|pack prescriptions|
|ID &amp;amp; documents|Aadhaar, voter card, land papers, bank passbook – sealed in zip‑pouch + phone photos|keep originals on you|
|Phone power|Power‑bank (charged) + cable + spare SIM if possible|enable emergency alerts|
|Light &amp;amp; tools|Torch, spare cells, multi‑tool, whistle, lighter|no candles in shelling zones|
|Cash|Small notes ₹100/₹50|ATMs may shut|
|Clothes|Warm layer, rain layer, sturdy shoes, scarf/handkerchief (dust mask)|avoid bright colours at night|

Add infant supplies, sanitary pads, pet food, spectacles, hearing‑aid batteries as needed.

3. Know where you will take cover

|**If told to shelter‑in‑place**|**If told to evacuate**|
:--|:--|
|• Move everyone to the strongest interior room (ground‑floor, no windows).• Shut doors, turn off lights; tape windows or cover with thick blankets to stop glass shards.• Lie flat by an inner wall during blasts; protect head with arms or a hard object.|• Leave immediately with your go‑bag; lock gas and electricity mains first if time permits.• Follow the route and transport arranged by district officials – they will move you to pre‑identified schools/colleges or community bunkers outside the 5‑km belt.• Keep convoy spacing; headlights off at night if instructed.|

Tip: Community bunkers in many Jammu border villages (e.g., Chandu Chak, Pargwal, Arnia) exist but some are unfinished. Check the nearest usable one with your panchayat or BSF post durin daylight; note two alternate shelters. 

4. Hard‑en your house (if you still have to stay)

Stack sandbags or soil‑filled sacks outside the room facing Pakistan; even two layers cut lethal shrapnel.
Remove LPG cylinders, fuel cans, farm fertiliser and loose metal roofing sheets.
Store extra water in covered vessels (supply cuts are common after strikes).
Black‑out curtains at night; turn off outside lights – makes you less of a target.

5. Create a family communication plan

Write phone numbers of all relatives, local police (100/112), ambulance (108), fire (101), district control room, Red Cross helpline on a paper card for every adult and older child.
Agree on a single WhatsApp group OR SMS list for quick “I’m safe” check‑ins.
Decide two rendez‑vous points: one inside the village (primary school), one outside (nearest tehsil HQ).

6. During shelling / air‑raid

Drop – Cover – Stay: get flat on stomach, feet toward blast, mouth open to equalise pressure.
Count 10 minutes after the last explosion before moving; secondary rounds are common.
Do not film or livestream troop movements – it endangers forces and attracts legal action under the Official Secrets Act.

7. After a strike

Treat bleeding immediately; apply firm pressure, improvise tourniquet above limb wounds.
Check gas leaks; shut valve, open windows only when firing stops.
Mark unexploded shells or debris with cloth and inform Army/BSF; never touch.
Listen for evacuation orders before re‑entering damaged houses.

8. Looking after vulnerable groups

Elderly &amp; disabled: Assign one able‑bodied escort per person; keep mobility aids handy.
Children: Explain events calmly; give them a simple task (holding the torch, counting supplies) to cut panic.
Livestock &amp; pets: Keep fodder for 3 days in a rear shed; spray‑paint your mobile number on cattle in case of escape.

9. Financial &amp; digital back‑up

Withdraw a week’s cash (ATMs near the border are often shut first).
Take screenshots of key documents, upload to a cloud drive with 2‑factor authentication.
Switch to low‑data “lite” apps; networks may throttle bandwidth for security.

10. Mental health &amp; morale

Prolonged tension is draining. Use short routines (stretching, prayer, humour, helping neighbours) to keep a sense of control. If anxiety or sleeplessness becomes overwhelming, J‑K mental‑health helpline 1800‑180‑7159 operates 24×7.

11. Stay legal, stay responsible

Curfews, night‑time vehicle bans, weapon/ explosive restrictions and social‑media advisories are legally enforceable under CrPC 144 &amp; Disaster‑Management Act. Follow them exactly.
Any relief material you distribute must go through the local Tehsildar or BDO so that supplies reach everyone fairly.

13. If you have time today – three quick wins

Walk to your closest bunker or evacuation pick‑up point and back; note the time it takes.

Bundle your documents in a waterproof envelope and place it in your go‑bag.

Charge every power‑bank and phone; keep one switched off to save battery.

Doing these three things puts you ahead of 90 % of the population in a sudden escalation.

Stay alert, stay calm, and always let the official instructions overrule anything you read on social media. My thoughts are with you and your family.
