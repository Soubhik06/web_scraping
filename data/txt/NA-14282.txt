As per the report, Kirti Sharma, using the Instagram handle “kirtisocial,” encouraged the assassination of Banerjee and disclosed the identity of a 31-year-old postgraduate trainee doctor who was sexually assaulted and murdered at Kolkata’s RG Kar Medical College and Hospital on August 9.

This is not the first time the city police has doubled down on social media posts related to the hospital incident. Between August 14 and 16, two X (Twitter) users received a take-down notice from the police for posting offensive and malicious content under Section 168 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023.

Another X user with the handle ‘epicnephrin_e’ took to the platform to talk about how Kolkata police searched her house at 7:30PM due to a social media post she had put up. The user was then instructed by the police to delete the post and refrain from posting further content without verification. While the tweet has since been removed, the user talked at length how the police actions had scared her and her entire family.

Why it matters: The actions of the Kolkata police are concerning in terms of free speech. The heinous nature of the hospital incident incited widespread anger in India with many enraged users circulating incorrect information regarding the autopsy report. However, the extreme behaviour of the police – visiting users houses and asking them to take down the post as alleged by epicnephrin_e – is creating an atmosphere of fear and muzzling speech. A sympathiser of epicnephrin_e pointed out that asking people to take down dissenting posts only intimidates individuals and discourages open discussions which can lead to a surveillance state in the long-run.
