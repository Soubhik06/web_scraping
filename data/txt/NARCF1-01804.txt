I wanted to share my recent experience with a scam call to spread awareness and hopefully prevent others from falling victim to such scams. It’s scary how convincing these scammers can sound, so here’s what happened and the lessons I’ve learned from previous incidents.

1. Telecom Authority Scam

This morning I got a call that wasn’t flagged by Truecaller, so I assumed it was legit. The name and picture showed as "Pradeep Gowda," which made it seem genuine. When I answered, a machine voice said something like my phone number would be deactivated in 30 minutes and that I should press 1 to find out why.

Out of curiosity and slight caution, I pressed 1, and a guy speaking in Hindi came on. He claimed to be from the "Telecommunication Authority of India" or something similar. I asked him to speak in English, but he kept rambling in Hindi and eventually started asking why I pressed the number. At this point, I realized this was definitely a scam, cut the call, and blocked the number.

2. The Electricity Board Scam

This happened few years back when my mother was home alone, and I was in a rush. I was not aware of these phone scams and very inexperienced at the time. A caller claimed that our electricity connection would be cut unless I acted immediately. They asked me to download an app to resolve the issue. It was a remote access app ( AnyDesk I think ), which lets someone control your phone or computer.

I followed their instructions at first without thinking because I was worried, but the app didn’t connect due to poor network issues. That gave me time to think and I realized my stupidity and error as there were too many logical gaps. I told the caller I would visit the electricity board in person, and he immediately hung up.

Thankfully, I escaped that one by sheer luck. After that, I changed my phone to be safe and got rid of the old one.

3. The Police Station Scam

Another time, someone called pretending to be from a police station. The caller tried to sound intimidating, but the entire conversation was in Hindi. As someone from the South, I don’t understand Hindi well, which made the whole thing seem fishy. I cut the call, blocked the number, and moved on.

Precautions to Protect Yourself:

From these experiences, here’s what I’ve learned and what you can do to stay safe:

1. Avoid Engaging: 

Don’t press numbers or engage in conversations. Scammers are trained to manipulate you.

2. Verify Information:

If someone claims to be from an official organization, hang up immediately and contact the company or authority or customer care directly using the official contact details from their website.

3. Never Download Apps: 

Scammers often ask you to download apps like AnyDesk, TeamViewer, etc., to gain control of your phone or computer. Legit organizations will never ask you to do this.

4. Report the Number: 

You can report suspicious calls to your telecom provider or cybercrime helpline. In India, dial 1930 for cyber fraud complaints.

5. Stay Calm: 

Scammers rely on creating a sense of urgency to make you act without thinking. Always take a moment to analyze the situation.

6. Use Call Blockers: 

If you receive a suspicious call, block the number immediately.

Final Thoughts:

Phone scams are getting more sophisticated, and it’s easy to fall for them, especially when they catch you off guard. Please share this with others so they can avoid becoming victims. Stay vigilant and remember: if something feels off, it probably is.
