Warren Buffet has a Contrarian Philosophy. The idea of contrarian philosophy is inherently risky but seems very rewarding. 
A study by Stern Business School (NYU) says an extra return of 30% over the market and about 40% relative to an investor who bought a set of 35 loser stocks(*stocks that went down the most in a year*) against a winner portfolio of 35 winner stocks when tracked over a period of 60 months. 
Link - http://people.stern.nyu.edu/adamodar/pdfiles/invfables/ch8new.pdf 
 
Now few questions I have related to this - 
1. Most people don't have this much surplus capital that they can create a portfolio of 10 (if not 35) loser stocks. Should they even adopt this philosophy considering the risk and their limited capital? If they still go about it - do they invest in handful of such stocks or keep it to one or two? 
2. When taking a contrarian call can we adjust for any scams/misdealings/being caught in political crossfire sort of events? 
Or rather a stock caught in political crossfire makes for an attractive contrarian bet? 
3. Can anyone share his success/fail stories of making a contrarian bet in the Indian stock market.
