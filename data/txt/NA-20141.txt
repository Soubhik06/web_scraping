# World
This section takes a look at the top security news around the world which impact most of us.

This week saw a triple whammy of major vulnerabilities from the top tier Software/Hardware/OEM developers. All having major implications for the end users leaving them completely open to all sorts of attacks without them even knowing.

&amp;nbsp;

*[Remote exploit in most recent Windows products](https://bugs.chromium.org/p/project-zero/issues/detail?id=1252&amp;desc=5)*

Most windows products (if you are not on an ancient device, you are impacted) have a Microsoft "Malware Protection Service". Ironically, this service allows attackers to remotely execute almost anything on your system through a missing input sanity check. This bug can be exploited by just downloading a file/email onto your system, the user doesn't even need to open/read it. Unfortunately, the attack payload can't be detected by any malware scanner. 

_What you should do:_ 

 - Microsoft has released a [patch](https://technet.microsoft.com/en-us/library/security/4022344) to fix the issue. Update asap. Turn off windows malware protection (or exclude all your drives from automatic scanning) in the meanwhile.

&amp;nbsp;

*[Remote Exploit in all Intel processors since 2010](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2017/05/intels-management-engine-security-hazard-and-users-need-way-disable-it)*

All intel processors produced since 2010 have this vulnerability and you are impacted if a feature called AMT is enabled on your PC. It allows the attacker to control your device completely, sometimes even if the device is switched off. Apple devices are not impacted since they ship with this disabled but for others, you need to check.

_What you should do:_ 

 - Check your device vendor's website to see if they've released a BIOS update with a fix
 - If not, then you should turn off the AMT feature through BIOS if possible. (For some devices though, it may cause other issues and you are unfortunately stuck. So push your device vendor to release a fix)

&amp;nbsp;

 *[KeyLogger in HP laptops](https://www.modzero.ch/modlog/archives/2017/05/11/en_keylogger_in_hewlett-packard_audio_driver/index.html)*
 
 Many HP laptops are shipping with an audio driver which logs all your keystrokes (including usernames/passwords etc, basically anything you type). These are saved on your machine itself but accessible to any trivial program running as an unprivileged user so the bar of attack is very low and can easily lead to losing your personal information. If you are not using HP's provided audio driver on your system, you are safe.
 
 _What you should do:_
 
 - Delete or rename C:\Windows\System32\MicTray64.exe or C:\Windows\System32\MicTray.exe if they exist on your system
 - Delete C:\Users\Public\MicTray.log file if it exists on your system
 - Also delete the above log file from any backups that may have been taken through your backup systems.

**References**

 Attack payload: Data/file that has the malicious code or properties to trigger a bug and take advantage of the same.

 Remote Exploit: A bug that can be exploited without gaining physical access to your machine
 
 Script Kiddies / Skiddies: Technology savvy persons who are just good enough to run attack payloads/scripts developed by others to exploit known bugs rather than find/exploit a real bug themselves. They like to deface websites or do other mischief to earn internet brownie points.
