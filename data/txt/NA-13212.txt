We missed this earlier: WhatsApp banned 9.08 million (9,089,000) accounts between December 1 and 31, 2024, Meta said in its transparency report published on February 1. Out of these, it banned 1.3 million (1,300,000) accounts before any reports from users. This is a slight decrease from the 9.6 million accounts banned in November 2024 . The platform publishes this transparency report about the various kinds of grievances received under Rule 4(1)(d) and Rule 3A(7) of the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021.

GAC orders: The platform complied with all three Grievance Appellate Committee (GAC) orders it received in December. For context, GAC is the final entity users can approach for grievance redressal under the amended IT Rules, 2021. The orders of the GAC are binding on platforms that wish to retain their safe harbour protections provided to them under Section 79 of the IT Act. However, the nature of the GAC orders remains undisclosed.

User grievances: The platform received 6,750 grievances in December 2024, out of which 2,637 were ban appeals. WhatsApp took action against 48 of those reported accounts. WhatsApp clarifies that taking action denotes either banning an account or restoring a previously banned account as a result of the complaint.

WhatsApp has emerged as one of the top platforms to carry out scams and cybercrimes, as per a recent report by India’s Home Ministry , The Hindu reported. In the first three months of 2024, there were 43,797 complaints regarding cyber fraud via WhatsApp, the report said.

Thus, tracking the numbers and such enforcement actions becomes crucial for assessing platform accountability and the effectiveness of regulatory measures.
