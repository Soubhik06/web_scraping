After considering the arguments advanced by learned counsel for the applicants and learned A.G.A.-1 for the State and after perusal of the record, this Court is of the view that the case originated from an application under Section 156(3) Cr.P.C. submitted by the opposite party, leading to the registration of FIR No. 63/2023 against Kamla Singh and others. The FIR alleged fraudulent withdrawals of the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PMKSN) scheme funds using forged documents. The primary allegations include fraudulent claims and withdrawals under the PMKSN scheme by the applicants and on Indu Bala Singh using forged identities and documents. However, the evidence collected and attached, such as bank statements, khatauni, and family register (Pariwar Register), does not substantiate these claims. The documentation indicates legitimate ownership and entitlement under the scheme for Kamla Singh. Notably, the bank account details provided do not link Sushila Devi to the alleged fraudulent withdrawals. Additionally, the accounts associated with the fraudulent claims belong to other individuals unrelated to the applicants. The FIR alleges that the applicants fraudulently received funds under the PMKSN scheme using forged documents and multiple identities. Specifically, it accuses Kamla Singh of misusing his identity and land records to claim benefits and implicates Sushila Devi in conspiracy without clear evidence of her involvement. The investigation included scrutiny of khatauni records, family registers, and account statements, which showed that the applicants' names were correctly listed, and no fraudulent activity was connected to their accounts. The accounts opened much before the scheme's initiation further discredit the allegations of scheme-specific fraud. An RTI response from the Deputy Director of Agriculture confirmed that Sushila Devi wife of Kamla Singh, was not registered under the PMKSN scheme, nor had she received any payments, thereby negating the allegations of fraudulent claims. The applicants have provided documents, including bank statements and RTI replies, which suggest that Sushila Devi has not received any funds from the PMKSN scheme. Furthermore, there is no concrete evidence linking her to the alleged fraudulent accounts. The investigation also failed to conclusively establish any direct link between the applicants and the other accused individuals or fraudulent activities. Applicant No.1 (Kamla Singh): Kamla Singh asserts that he is a bona fide beneficiary of the PMKSN scheme as a co-tenure holder of agricultural land. There is no substantial evidence that he committed fraud or forgery to receive the benefits. Applicant No.2 (Sushila Devi): Sushila Devi claims no involvement in the scheme or any fraudulent activities. The FIR and charge sheet fail to provide material evidence linking her to the crime. Her identity appears to have been confused with another individual bearing a similar name. The evidence presented includes Khatauni records, bank statements, and responses from authorities under the Right to Information Act . The applicants argue that the records do not show any fraudulent activity on their part. The accounts mentioned in the FIR as belonging to Sushila Devi and Kamla Devi do not match the applicants' details, indicating a possible mix-up or false implication. Based on the material evidence and the arguments presented, it is apparent that the allegations against Kamla Singh and Sushila Devi lack substantive proof. The documents and investigation reports do not establish a prima facie case against the applicants. Moreover, the RTI responses and account details further support the applicants' claim of false implication, thus the allegations made against the applicants under Section 419 , 420 , 406 , and 120B of the Indian Penal Code ( IPC ) are not substantiated by the evidence on record. 25. 	Hon'ble the Supreme Court of India in the case of Lalankumar Singh and Others vs. State of Maharashtra reported in 2022 SCC Online SC 1383 has specifically held in paragraph No.38 that the order of issuance of process is not an empty formality. The Magistrate is required to apply his mind as to whether sufficient ground for proceeding exists in the case or not. Paragraph No.38 of Lalankumar Singh and Others (supra) is being quoted hereunder:- 26. 	Further, Hon'ble the Supreme Court of India has provided guidelines in case of State of Haryana Vs. Bhajan Lal reported in 1992 Supp (1) SCC 335 for the exercise of power under Section 482 Cr.P.C. which is extraordinary power and used separately in following conditions:- (2) where the allegations in the First Information Report and other materials, if any, accompanying the F.I.R. do not disclose a cognizable offence, justifying an investigation by police officers under Section 156(1) of the Code except under an order of a Magistrate within the purview of Section 155(2) of the Code; (3) where the uncontroverted allegations made in the FIR or 'complaint and the evidence collected in support of the same do not disclose the commission of any offence and make out a case against the accused; (4) where the allegations in the FIR do not constitute a cognizable offence but constitute only a non-cognizable offence, no investigation is permitted by a police officer without an order of a Magistrate as contemplated under Section 155(2) of the Code; (5) where the allegations made in the FIR or complaint are so absurd and inherently improbable on the basis of which no prudent person can ever reach a just conclusion that there is sufficient ground for proceeding against the accused; (6) where there is an express legal bar engrafted in any of the provisions of the Code or the concerned Act (under which a criminal proceeding is instituted) to the institution and continuance of the proceedings and/or where there is a specific provision in the Code or the concerned Act, providing efficacious redress for the grievance of the aggrieved party; (7) where a criminal proceeding is manifestly attended with mala fide and/or where the proceeding is maliciously instituted with an ulterior motive for wreaking vengeance on the accused and with a view to spite him due to private and personal grudge." 27. 	Further the Apex Court has also laid down the guidelines where the criminal proceedings could be interfered and quashed in exercise of its power by the High Court in the following cases:- (i) R.P. Kapoor Vs. State of Punjab, AIR 1960 S.C. 866, (ii) State of Bihar Vs. P.P. Sharma, 1992 SCC (Crl. )192, ( iii) Zandu Pharmaceutical Works Ltd. Vs. Mohd. Saraful Haq and another , (Para-10) 2005 SCC (Cri.) 283 and ( iv) Neeharika Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. Vs. State of Maharashtra , AIR 2021 SC 1918. 28. In S.W. Palankattkar & others Vs. State of Bihar , 2002 (44) ACC 168, it has been held by the Hon'ble Apex Court that quashing of the criminal proceedings is an exception than a rule. The inherent powers of the High Court itself envisages three circumstances under which the inherent jurisdiction may be exercised:-(i) to give effect an order under the Code, (ii) to prevent abuse of the process of the court ; (iii) to otherwise secure the ends of justice. The power of High Court is very wide but should be exercised very cautiously to do real and substantial justice for which the court alone exists. 29. In M/s Pepsi Food Ltd. and another Vs. Special Judicial Magistrate and others : 1998 (5) SCC 749, Hon'ble Apex Court has observed: 30.	 In the instant case, there is nothing in the summoning order to show that the Magistrate concerned perused the material available on record before passing summoning order. Hence the summoning order is bad in the eyes of law and resultantly it is not sustainable. 31. 	Thus, in view of the law laid down by the Hon'ble Apex Court and the facts and circumstances, as narrated above and also with the assistance of the aforesaid guidelines and keeping in view the nature and gravity and the severity of the offence which more particularly is a private dispute and differences, it deems proper and meet to the ends of justice that the proceeding of the aforementioned case be quashed 32. Accordingly, the present 482 Cr.P.C . application stands allowed. Keeping in view the law laid down by the Hon'ble Apex Court in the above referred judgment and the observation made above, the entire proceedings of Criminal Case No.41381/2023 "State Vs.
