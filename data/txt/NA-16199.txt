--- POST TEXT ---

So whenever we buy something on Amazon or Flipkart via a card, there is a requirement of an OTP to authenticate such a transaction. But the same does not happen for Netflix, and no this is not me just coming to realize this. But on searching on the internet for a while, I have came to know that apparently Indian merchants require an OTP to authenticate transactions, whereas foreign merchants DO NOT.

First of all, why? Why even have this system where money can be deducted without consent. What prevents them from charging as much as they like (I know Netflix has far more to loose by doing something illegal and they are very profitable now anyway, but that does not justify them having the power or the potential to do so)? Is the only security if they do empty my bank account, fraud detection from my bank?

Second, it really boils my blood to see companies adopting this as a business model with an illusion of a "free first month". Although it is distasteful from the companies end, it is even sadder that this can be so easily avoided just via an authorization.

Third, solutions - 

("Problems that arise from a de-regulated free market is probably solvable by the free market" - Some Anarcho-Capitalist dude, probably)

Is there any company that provide solutions like maybe create an account which has credentials like "card number" that may make websites like Netflix's believe that it is a real debit card. Except here the account mandates all transactions to be authenticated, has no minimum limit on how much money you can store, etc.?

&amp;#x200B;

PS. You might think I am being very over the top and yes, Netflix most probably will not steal our money, but I am really pissed at the idea that companies can even have such power, especially when it comes to individual's finances.
