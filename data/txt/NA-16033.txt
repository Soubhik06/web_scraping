I've seen a number of people asking about joining gyms or starting workouts here in the last few days. And with the new year, I'm sure many of you are resolving to get fit too. So here are some tips from someone who has been training since 2015. Note that this advice mainly considers people in the 15-40 age range, who have no major medical issues. If you are older or have major medical issues, consult your physician. 

**General Advice**

1. The first and most important point is to start. There is nothing to afraid of when you are just starting. No one cares if you are lifting baby weights or running at low speeds, everybody is busy with their own workouts. To quote Fuegoleon, being weak is nothing to be ashamed of. Staying weak is. 

2. It really doesn't matter what your baseline is. If you haven't ever hit the gym, you can only go up. I have seen people go from not being able to walk faster than 5km/h without getting winded, to doing taxing movements like the front lever and clean and jerk as a part of their routine. 

3. Don't worry about "aesthetics"/"ideal physique" if you are just starting. First build up your stamina and get into a routine. Learn to walk before attempting to scale Mt. Everest. 

4. It's okay if you just start with cardio, but don't be afraid of the weights. Start with baby weights, but keep applying progressive overload. You will develop good musculature which will make even non-weighted exercises easier in the long term. Increased basal metabolic rate will also help keep the fat off. You will not become ridiculously buff unless you specifically target that. Even lean machines like Usain Bolt incorporate weights into their program.

5. You don't need to purchase any equipment in order to start. Even if you are working out at home. The only thing I would suggest would be comfy stretchable shorts or sweatpants, loose t-shirts, and a good pair of shoes. But chances are, you already have them lying in your home. 

6. If you are stuck or need help, don't be afraid to ask for help. Most of us started weak and became strong, and know all to well what it is like to start. Just a word of etiquette - don't interrupt someone during a set. 

7. Have the right mindset. Working out isn't an act of contrition for your lifestyle sins. It is a celebration of what the human body can achieve. You might not feel like that in the beginning, so focus keep track of your progress. Celebrate the small things. You went from 5 pushups the last session to 7. You managed to do 10 dumbbell curls non-stop. You added 5kg to your deadlift. 

8. Workout within your limit, and allow for recovery. I see far too often that people give it their all in the beginning, don't allow for recovery, and then end up burning out. Don't do more than 3 days of weight training in a week, and 5 days of working out overall. 

**Programming**

The most important thing with exercise is to be consistent. Once you have a routine set, you should start following a program. Don't just hop from machine to machine. There is a lot of programming advice on the internet. Some of it good, a lot of it bad. But the few key points I would emphasize would be 

1. Progressive overload - The program increases in difficulty as you progress. 

2. Appropriate difficulty level - If you are a beginner, don't overdo it. Find your level and increase from there. Don't follow a celebrity routine just because you want their physique. And since everybody's physique is different, the exercises the find easy or difficult will also be different. A tall guy will have a much harder time doing deadlifts because of the increased range of motion, but a much easier time sprinting than a short guy. So don't blindly copycat another person's level. And remember, if you are just starting, it is better to go light and progress slower, than to go too heavy and injure yourself. 

3. Adequate rest - I cannot stress this enough. Exercise literally breaks your muscles down. So you need to allow yourself time to recover. So any good program will have off days. 

4. Splits - After a point, you won't be able to do full body workouts every workout day and still progress. You should split which muscle group/exercise you focus on each day. The most common splits are brosplit (one body part each day), upper/lower split (complementary body parts each day), and push pull legs. This is going to be somewhat controversial, but if you are just beginning weight training, do a brosplit. Learn how activating each muscle feels. And get the form and technique down right. An example split would be 

* Monday: Arms 

* Tuesday: Legs

* Wednesday: Chest

* Thursday: Rest

* Friday: Back

* Saturday: Shoulder

* Sunday: Rest

Incorporate some cardio and core exercise everyday. 

You may have observed that this violates the no more than 3 days of weight training in a week suggestion, but you will be lifting light weights and allowing each body part a week of recovery so it will be fine. The 3 day rule applies more to full body workouts like the snatch and clean and jerk. You need to give each muscle 2-3 days to recover after taxing it. 

Don't stick with this once you have achieved a basic level of strength in each muscle (1-2 months in most cases). You can then start to do a upper/lower split or PPL. 

So something like

* Monday: Arms, Chest, and Shoulder (Chest and biceps emphasis, lighter shoulder and triceps)

* Tuesday: Legs and back (Legs emphasis, lighter back exercises)

* Wednesday: off

* Thursday: Arms, Chest, and Shoulder (shoulder and triceps emphasis, lighter chest and biceps)

* Friday: Cardio

* Saturday: Legs and back (Back emphasis, lighter leg exercises)

* Sunday: Rest

5. Incorporate a variety of exercises in your routine. Most training programs include the big four - Bench, Deadlift, Squat, and Military Press. But do accessory work too. For example, along with the Flat barbell bench press, incorporate some flies or pullovers for your chest. Incorporate lateral barbell rowing and single hand dumbell rowing for back. Do some front and lateral raises for the shoulders. Do sumo, front and goblet squats for legs along with the conventional back squat. 

The routines/splits I have posted here are examples and mostly apply to weight training. But the same principle applies for calisthenics or cardio focused programs. 

6. Rep ranges - I would advise against following a static set and rep count like 3x5 or 5x5. Target a range like 5-7 reps for each set, doing more in lighter or earlier sets and fewer in heaver or later sets. 

7. All things said and done, there is no single "ideal routine". You have to learn your body's strengths and limits, and figure out what is the best for you. Which can be pretty hard if you have never trained before. But remember, that is what gym bros are for. 

Once you advance past the beginner/novice level, you will be able to program better and really start targeting the kind of physique you want. But if you are just starting out the most important thing is going to the gym and moving/lifting weight. 

Once you outgrow the most basic routines, you might go looking online for more advanced workouts. At this point, you should start making sure that the workout you select targets your goals. So if you want hypertrophy, stronglifts and madcow will give you less ideal results than a hypertrophy focused programme. And don't be afraid to incorporate elements of different programmes into yours. 

**Personal Trainers**

This is more hit or miss. There are many great personal trainers and many not so great ones. The best way to find one would be through your contacts. If you know someone who trains with a trainer, ask them for their trainers contact. Gyms will also have trainers who can help you. A good trainer will show you how to workout and set a program. He will act as your spotter for heavier exercises. And again, trainers are not one size fits all, so if you feel that your workouts are not effective with your current trainer, don't be afraid to get a new one. 

Just beware of ones who suggest you hop on protein powder or supplements. As a beginner you do not need anything. Just eat healthy whole foods with enough protein and you are golden. 

And trainers are absolutely not necessary. Just start exercising and eat well. 

**Diet**

A good diet is absolutely essential to fitness. This bit is especially pertinent in India, where we tend to eat a lot of carbs by default. Target a protein intake of 1.2-1.3x of your ideal lean body mass everyday. Cut down on the rotis and rice. If you want to lose weight, eat a small calorific deficit. If you want to gain weight, eat a small surplus. Don't crash diet, you will lose muscle, and emerge less fit on the other side. Don't fall for memes like dirty bulk or GOMAD (gallon of milk a day). Make slow and consistent losses or gains. Milk, eggs, chicken breast, chenna/paneer are your best friends. If you are vegetarian, focus on milk and beans like chole and rajma, and of course dal. If you aren't, fish and lean meats. If you are vegan, add you can add some soy based products to your diet. 

You don't have to diet everyday, it is okay to have days where you eat junk. But remember, the only person you cheat on a cheat day is yourself. And honestly, try to make your cheat days coincide with parties and festivals, so that you can enjoy them fully without screwing your diet plan over. 

And remember to stay hydrated. 

And for heavens sake, don't reward yourself with food after a workout. I have seen so many fat uncles who huff and puff in the local park, and then undo all their efforts by going to the mithaiwala and having chai and samosas. 

**A word on fitness influences and culture**

Fitness, for better or worse, is rife with fraud. Along with obvious roiders at the top level of every competition there are a lot of fake "natural lifters". And even actual natties will look a lot more impressive in instagram photos, due to proper posing, lighting, angles, and having their muscles inflamed after a workout. And these natties achieved their physiques after years of hard work, so don't be shocked if you don't get the same results in a few months. You will become strong long before you look good. 

However, this is not an excuse to take the black pill, and assume that you can never have a six pack or lift 200kg+. People had impressive physiques well before anabolic steroids - the OGs like Eugene Sandow, George Jowett, Charles Atlas etc. You can too, but it will take years of hard work, dedication and self control. 

Have high but realistic expectations, and push yourself. Work and you will see results.
