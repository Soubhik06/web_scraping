In a recent legal victory for consumers’ rights, the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission-I, U.T. Chandigarh, ruled in favor of a complainant who was overcharged at a local dhaba. The case, filed by Prashant Sethi against GAZB 26 @ Royal Dhaba, serves as a stark reminder of the importance of fair business practices and consumer protection.

&amp;#x200B;

The consumer complaint was filed on 25th January 2022, following an incident on 19th January 2022, when Mr. Sethi and his friends visited the Royal Dhaba in Sector 26, Chandigarh, for a meal. Upon ordering food items totaling ₹465, Mr. Sethi handed over his debit card for payment. To his shock and dismay, the dhaba charged an exorbitant ₹4,650 instead of the agreed-upon amount.

&amp;#x200B;

Despite Mr. Sethi’s repeated requests for a refund of the excess ₹4,185, the dhaba refused to cooperate, prompting Mr. Sethi to pursue legal action, alleging both deficiency in service and unfair trade practices by the dhaba.

&amp;#x200B;

The case proceeded after the dhaba, represented by the name GAZB 26 @ Royal Dhaba, failed to appear before the commission, despite proper service. Consequently, the commission heard Mr. Sethi’s evidence and issued its ruling on 1st September 2023.

&amp;#x200B;

In the ruling, Commission President Shri Pawanjit Singh Pawanjit Singh and Surjeet Kaur, Member stated,“At the very outset, it may be observed that when it is an admitted case of the complainant that on 19.1.2022 he along with his friends had gone to the OP dhaba and had ordered selected food items and thereafter the OP had raised a bill for the sum of ₹465/- which fact is also evident from Annexure C-1 and as the OP had deducted an amount of ₹4,650/- instead of ₹465/- from the account of the complainant through the debit card of the complainant and despite repeated requests of the complainant as the OP has not refunded the excess amount of ₹4,185/-, till date, the case is reduced to a narrow compass as it is to be determined if the said act amounts to deficiency in service and unfair trade practice on the part of the OP and the consumer complaint deserves to succeed, as is the case of the complainant.”

&amp;#x200B;

The commission found that the dhaba had indeed issued an invoice for ₹465, but erroneously debited ₹4,650 from Mr. Sethi’s account. Given the dhaba’s failure to refund the excess amount, the commission determined that this constituted both a deficiency in service and an unfair trade practice.

&amp;#x200B;

As a result, the commission ruled in favor of Mr. Sethi, ordering the dhaba to:

&amp;#x200B;

* Refund the amount of ₹4,185 to the complainant along with interest at 9% per annum from the date of payment (19.1.2022) onwards. 

* Pay ₹3,000 to the complainant as compensation for causing mental agony and harassment. 

* Pay ₹3,000 to the complainant as costs of litigation.

&amp;#x200B;

The dhaba has been given thirty days to comply with the order; failure to do so will result in additional interest at a rate of 12% per annum from the date of the order, along with compliance with the cost of litigation.
