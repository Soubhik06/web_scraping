--- POST TEXT ---

It feels like being on a sinking ship, where a third of the crew is desperately trying to save it, another third is actively cutting holes in it, and the rest are either playing cards or cheering for the ones destroying it. That is exactly what is happening to Indian democracy.

AAP did not do as well as it could have, but let us not pretend the fight was fair. The BJP used every institution under its control to suffocate its opposition. Its biggest move? Jailing AAP’s top leaders for months without bail, without concrete evidence, just to break them, distract them, and paint them as corrupt while BJP’s own scams—electoral bonds (chanda do, dhanda lo), the Adani shell company scandal, and the infamous “40% ki sarkar” corruption—went untouched.

The timing of the budget was another masterstroke of manipulation. Despite opposition demands to delay it, Modi went ahead just before the elections, dangling income tax rebates like a bribe to Delhi’s voters. The BJP then plastered advertisements everywhere, calling these rebates a “gift” to the people, while ignoring the reality: real incomes have stagnated for over a decade, and inflation, GST, and indirect taxes eat away at whatever little relief is given.

Then there is the Election Commission of India (ECI), which no longer even pretends to be neutral. Ever since the Chief Justice was removed from its appointment panel, it has functioned more like a BJP wing than an independent body. Voter lists are mysteriously altered in every election—real voters deleted, ghost voters added, all at levels far beyond natural population growth. The BJP has mastered this electoral manipulation, ensuring it always has an edge before a single vote is cast.

Meanwhile, the Lieutenant Governor of Delhi, an unelected BJP appointee, blocked AAP at every turn, preventing it from functioning effectively. The media, backed by BJP’s corruption money, floods the country with Modi’s face, pushing the idea that “Modi nahin to kaun?”—as if India, a country of 1.4 billion people, has only one semi-educated bigot leader fit to rule.

The truth is, BJP wins not just by campaigning but by controlling the game itself—weaponizing the Enforcement Directorate (ED), the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), the Income Tax department, and even the ECI. When democracy is played like this, how can the fight ever be fair? Yes, AAP made mistakes. It could have done more. But when its leaders were jailed without trial, when its governance was constantly sabotaged, when its ability to function was crippled by an entire system rigged against it, what chance did it have?

BJP has created a system where democracy is an illusion, where elections are a performance, and where the rules are rewritten to ensure one outcome: their victory, no matter what.
