The Karnataka High Court (HC) asked PhonePe to share merchant information related to an online betting transaction, while rejecting the fintech company’s petition that challenged orders from the Karnataka Police during the investigation. The court rejected PhonePe’s claim that, as a UPI system provider, it is not obligated to disclose such data under the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891, and the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. The judgment clarifies the balance between financial confidentiality and law enforcement access under Section 91 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

The case centred around a user’s ₹6,150 payment via PhonePe to a betting website, which led to police seeking merchant data after the user’s account was blocked. PhonePe initially refused to provide this information, citing its limited role as a transaction facilitator. However, the court held that companies in the digital payment ecosystem can be compelled to provide relevant data when formally requested during criminal investigations, reinforcing law enforcement agencies’ authority to investigate financial fraud and illegal activities.

This ruling has significant implications for digital payment intermediaries in India, requiring them to comply with lawful data requests while maintaining privacy safeguards. It also establishes that technological neutrality does not exempt fintech companies from legal scrutiny when involved in investigations. Furthermore, this lawsuit serves as a key precedent in aligning older financial laws with modern digital payment systems.
