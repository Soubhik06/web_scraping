1.1. A preliminary analysis into the buying, selling or dealing in the shares issued through Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) of various companies during the period 2003-2005, by Securities and Exchange Board of India (hereinafter referred to as SEBI) prima facie showed that, certain entities opened many demat accounts in fictitious/ benami names and the said entities had cornered/ acquired the shares of such companies allotted in the IPOs, by making applications in fictitious/ benami names with each of the applications being of small value so as to make it eligible for allotment under the retail category. The said demat accounts opened in fictitious/ benami names are hereinafter referred to as the afferent accounts. It was further revealed that, subsequent to the allotment of IPO shares, the fictitious/benami allottees had transferred the said shares to their principals who were prima facie identified by SEBI as key operators / master account holders. The modus operandi as detailed above led to the prima facie view that the thousands of entities in whose names demat accounts and bank accounts had been opened and IPO applications made, were either benami (name lenders) or non existent. 1.2. In the above facts and circumstances, SEBI vide an ad interim ex parte order dated April 27, 2006 inter alia directed various key operators including Shri Deepakkumar Shantilal Jain and Opee Stock-Link Ltd. not to buy, sell or deal in the securities market including in IPOs, directly or indirectly till further directions as it was inter alia found that they had prima facie violated the provisions of Regulation 3 of Securities and Exchange Board of India (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices Relating to Securities Market) Regulations, 2003 (for short the FUTP Regulations) and the provisions of Securities and Exchange Board of India (Disclosure and Investor Protection) Guidelines 2000 (for short DIP Guidelines). It was further directed to treat the said interim order as show cause notice against the entities named therein and an opportunity was also provided to the said entities to file their objections, if aggrieved. 2.1. Opee Stock Link Ltd. (for short Opee) vide letter dated nil (received by SEBI on May 10, 2006) inter alia stated that it had not violated the provisions of the FUTP Regulations and that the interim order of SEBI dated April 27, 2006 was passed without giving an opportunity of hearing. Opee contended that it had not made even a single application in the public issue of Jet Airways Ltd. (for short, Jet) and that the allegation that it had cornered the shares in the said IPO was not correct. Opee added that it had not financed anybody to make application in the public issue of Jet. It claimed that all 553 demat account holders from which it had received shares of Jet were not afferent account holders. According to Opee, the said demat account holders wanted to sell the shares allotted to them and since none of them had trading account and was familiar with market procedure to sell and buy shares, they had requested Opee to purchase the said shares. Subsequently, the said entities transferred the same to the demat account of Opee. In short, the contention seems to veer round that the said demat account holders were persons on whose behalf Opee sold the shares and thereafter made payment to them for the shares which were sold. Opee further clarified that it had sold the said shares through the stock broker viz. Mahipat Raichand Share Brokers Pvt. Ltd. Opee claimed that it was engaged in buying and selling in the capital market and that it had bought shares from different individuals who were desirous of selling their shares. It added that the same practice was followed in the case of Jet. According to Opee, it had not made any arrangement or agreement with any individual with respect to the public issue of Jet. It also requested for an opportunity of hearing. 2.2. Shri Deepakkumar Shantilal Jain (for short Shri Jain) vide letter dated nil (received by SEBI on May 10, 2006) inter alia stated that he had received 2,36,859 shares of IDFC Ltd. from 686 demat accounts. According to him, the holders of the said demat accounts approached him and requested him to sell such shares as they were not having trading accounts and accordingly, he purchased the said shares. He also stated that the said shares were transferred in his name and thereafter the payment was made to the different clients. He claimed that he did not finance the investors. He further submitted that the shares which were credited to his demat account were from individuals who were existing, identifiable legal entities. He claimed that he had not played any fraud or scheme to defraud the public at large. He also requested for an opportunity of hearing. 2.3. SEBI granted an opportunity of hearing to Shri Jain on November 6, 2006 and Opee on November 7, 2006. Shri Rajeev Desai, Chartered Accountant as authorized by the aforesaid person/entity appeared before me on the appointed dates on behalf of both the entities and made submissions on behalf of the entities. The submissions were mostly on the lines of written representations. It was inter alia submitted that, Shri Jain was a student and also a relative of one of the directors of Opee and used to attend the office of Opee on regular basis to learn the business. Pursuant to the hearing, the entities were advised to provide certain information / documents including the details of shareholding and directors of Opee, demat account/ bank account opening details of Shri Jain, ledger accounts, statement of the parties (including the address) who sold shares to Shri Jain etc.

Prima facie it would appear that Opee and Shri Jain were synergizing in a common modus operandi with access to the same demat accounts in cornering shares. 3.6 . According to Opee and Shri Jain, since the allottees were inexperienced in the field of securities market trading and that they were not having trading accounts, they sold the shares to Opee and Shri Jain. Prima facie, the said contention is difficult to accept. It is not possible to accept the contention that all the demat account holders (553 and 686) were either inexperienced or without trading account. Further, the application money in respect of the IPOs of Jet and IDFC was in the range of about Rs.50,000/-. Further, I note that there are contradictions in the submissions made by the aforesaid entities (Opee and Shri Jain). Shri Jain in his letter to SEBI (received by SEBI on May 10, 2006) stated "Since I am knowledgeable about the shares market and more particularly the procedures involved in buying and selling shares in the share market various individuals approached me for help in selling their shares. I have informed by these entities that they did not have any trading account and they wanted to sell the shares which were allotted to them." However, he had deviated from the said stand in his subsequent letter dated December 25, 2006 in which he had stated " I was new to the business. I have started to learn the capital market process. 
 I was not aware any rules As I was new some procedural aspects may not have been observed. 3.7 . Opee also reiterated the aforesaid position taken by Shri Jain in his letter dated December 25, 2006. In its letter dated February 15, 2007, Opee submitted ".... Mr Deepak Shantilal Jain is a student and relative of the director, he was attending office on regular basis to learn the business the person were known to us as a family friends & relatives therefore, some of the persons may be common." The contention of Shri Jain that the allottees approached him to help them in selling their shares falls to the ground, when, as admitted, he is new to dealing in capital market. Further, his versions bristle with inconsistencies, detracting a great deal from their merits. 3.8 . Yet another contention of Shri Jain was that, since the allottees did not have trading account in their name they had approached him to help them in selling their shares. In this regard, I note that Shri Jain vide letter dated December 25, 2006 enclosed the copies of statement of persons from whom the shares were purchased by him. The factum of the allottees not having trading accounts is conspicuously absent in all the declarations submitted, raising doubts about the veracity of such an assertion.
