--- POST TEXT ---

I was at my Nana's house and her phone rang. The caller ID said out loud, "Rachel." Nana answered it and I could somewhat hear the voice on the other line. It was a woman who sounded like she had an Indian accent. She told Nana a bit about her day. Nana then told her I was visiting her, and I hear Rachel say, "Oh yes, I remember you told me yesterday they were coming over today." Soon after that they end the conversation. 

\~\~\~

Me: Who's Rachel?

Nana: Well, I'll tell you, but you have to promise not to tell anyone else.

Me: Ok...

Nana (looking excited, like this is a fun adventurous part of her life) : Rachel is a very dear friend. We talk on the phone every day. She is an officer in the RCMP so I can't tell anyone about our friendship because it would put her job in jeopardy. We first got in touch when I had those credit card problems a couple years ago and she helped me through that, and she's been calling me every day since then. 

Me (all the alarm bells going off) : ...Has she ever asked you for money?

Nana: No. We've been talking every day for two years and she's never asked for money. We're friends.

Me: How did you get in touch with her? 

Nana: I didn't. She got in touch with me.

Me: Nana, I'm happy for you, and I believe that you're good friends, but if she ever asks you for money, do not give it to her. It sounds a lot like things that happen to other people where they then ask for money.

Nana: Ok.

\~\~\~

This sounds like a friendship version of a romance scam? I follow this sub but have never seen a long-term friendship scam like this exactly. Has anyone heard of this? But it's weird to me that "Rachel" hasn't asked for money yet. Some possibilities:

1) Nana is lying and Rachel has asked for money.

2) Rachel knows everything about Nana at this point and will use that info to steal her identity?

3) Rachel is still building up the friendship and will ask for money in the future. 

I'm leaning toward 3. I told my SIL and she says her stepmom's ex-husband lost \~$30,000 to a friendship scam just like this. They talked on the phone for years before the "friend" asked for money for a sick relative he'd always been talking about. 

SIL then visited Nana and found the number saved as Rachel in Nana's phone. It does call every day. She wrote down the number and looked it up at home. It's a landline in North Carolina.

\~\~\~

Some context: 

\-The RCMP is the Canada version of the FBI.

\-The "credit card problems" were when she got scammed twice in a short period of time. Once by someone calling claiming to be a computer repair guy and telling her she had computer problems he needed to fix and then charging her CC, and once by someone calling claiming to be the bank and needing to verify some info.

...What should I do??
