          This is a complaint filed under Section 17(1)(a)(ii) of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 by the Complainant with a prayer to direct the PDA bank to restore the status of all the DBDs which were foreclosed, with interest, valued at Rs.23,60,571/- as on date of filing complaint; to furnish details of excess payment; quash the closure order of the Banking Ombudsman dated 28.03.2014 upholding the act of the PDA bank; to allow costs of the complaint and award any other relief or reliefs. 2.       The case of the Complainant, in brief, is that he is an ex-serviceman served in the army was last leg posted to 1-EME Centre, Secunderabad as he was to retire from service on 31.01.2009.  Ordinarily pension documents will be sent (18) months in advance.  With only 14 months in hand, pension documentation including Release Medical Board proceedings (to decide disability pension in addition to normal pension) was taken-up expeditiously.  That a retiring Officer is required to opt to draw pension either from the nearest DPDO under Principal Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension) (PCDA) Allahabad or to draw pension from any PSU/Private bank approved as Pension Disbursing Authority (PDA) bank.  If a retiring officer opts to draw from a PDA bank, he is required to designate an already existing savings bank account or can open a new savings bank account in any PDA bank of his choice eight months in advance.  It will be then called the Pension Savings Bank Account (Pension Acoount).  The retiring Officer will then receive his "salary" from the PCDA, Pune till retirement and Pension against Pension Payment Order (PPO) issued by the PCDA post retirement for life. 3.       The Complainant opted for Bank of India (PDA), the Opposite party where a single savings bank account No.860410110002547 (pension account) was opened w.e.f. 05.05.2008.  He retired from Army on 31.01.2009.  He was issued new identity card and original Pensioner copy of PPO No.M/003011/ 2009 dated 09.02.2009 for non-formal pension, which was confirmed by the OP bank.  The OP bank, based on the PDO-1 made a payment of Rs.11,85,783/- as 'DCRG/Gratuity' and another Rs.14,587/- as 'monthly pension for the month of February 2009' on 13.03.2009 and thereafter continued to pay monthly pension and pension arrears.  On 02.04.2009, it credited a sum of Rs.8,77,957/- as 'Pension March 2009' but recovered a sum of Rs.8,63,370/- as 'pension excess paid'. 4.       Meanwhile, a sum of Rs.90,000/- was hacked from complainant's pension account on 01/02 Jan 2010 to 5 beneficiary account with branches of Bank of India at Mumbai & Surat.  It was detected by Ops head office at Mumbai which blocked pension account immediately but did not block the beneficiary account.  With IP address of the hackers and account numbers of the beneficiaries already established, OP assured the complainant of recovery of Rs.90,000/- and advised to open a second savings bank account bearing number 860410110003973 in the same PDO (second account) with internet facility for his routine, which was opened on 15.02.2010. 5.       On receipt of fresh PPOs, the OP bank made payment of Rs.13,68,265/- on 08.03.2010 as 'DIFF CPV/GRATUITY' against PDO-2 and a sum of Rs.2,83,139/- on 10.03.2010 as 'CPV DISABILITY' against PDO-3 in the second account instead of pension account.  However, OP did not return the hacked amount of Rs.90,000/-.  Vide letter dated 01.08.2013, OP claimed a sum of Rs.13,68,265/- paid on 08.03.2010 in the second account against PDO-2 was a double payment as a sum of Rs.11,85,783/- was already paid in his pension account on 13.03.2009 against PDO-1.  In fact, they were two different entitlements and hence were not a double payment.  Similarly, a sum of Rs.2,61,697/- paid in his second account on 11.05.2010 as 'BP & Disability arrears' was mixed up as a double payment as an amount of Rs.2,83,139/- as 'CPV disability' paid against PDO-3.  Hence, there were no twice payment/ excess payment as claimed by the OP bank.  Despite the same, the OP bank claimed twice payment of Rs.15,23,010/- due to 'phishing attack'.  As there was no sufficient balance in pension account as well as second account, in the said letter, OP asked the complainant to pay back the amount with interest.  By 06.08.2013, it even arranged an approval from its Controlling Officer to invoke banker's lien to appropriate the deposits held in his name. 6.       The Zonal Manager of OP blamed the OP for making excess payment without verifying with Nodal branch on two occasions.  This shows the negligence on their bank staff in handling the pension section, however, it allowed the OP to swing into action to delay his pension, block his debit cards, deferred payment against PDO-4 and debarred him to foreclose any of his own DBDs to meet the routine expenses causing irreparable damage.  Having no other option, complainant informed the PCDA on 19.08.2013 to redress his grievance as the OP was not complying with his request.  However, OP paid a sum of Rs.1,17,890/- against PPO-4 as pension arrear on 31.08.2013 but warned that his DBDs valued at Rs.18,60,955/- marked liens would be closed prematurely to recover and remit to concerned department of Government of India, to which, the PCDA vide letter dt.24.09.2013 asked the OP to furnish all details on twice payment made to complainant.  Meanwhile, it recovered a sum of Rs.19,368/- and Rs.9,870/- on 04.10.2013 and 08.10.2013 as excess amount paid, on 31.08.2013.  Instead of furnishing the details, the OP fore-closed all the eight DBDs on 11.10.2013 and manipulated each and every deposit reducing it to Rs.2,97,003/- and inflating the interest from Rs.1,82,602/- to Rs.3,84,744/-.  It then recovered a sum of Rs.15,23,010/- from his pension account to its CPPC branch account at Nagpur while retaining the interest of Rs.3,84,744/- in its undisclosed office account. 7.       The request of complainant to restore the status of DBDs was denied, to which, he lodged a complaint on15.10.2013 which was rejected on 06.01.2014.  The OP had foreclosed the DBDs non-complying the provisions of para 103.2 of DPPI 2005 by PCDA.  When the matter was brought to the notice of Armed Forces Tribunal, Chennai, it was heard and dismissed with finding not a 'service matter'.  The OP had paid Rs.8,77,957/- on 02.04.2009 and recovered Rs.8,63,370/-.  It paid Rs.1,17,890/- on 31.08.2013 but recovered Rs.19,638/- and 9870/- in October 2013 and in both cases, sufficient credit was available when detected.  The OP bank invoked unilaterally the provision of para 1 (i) of the RBI Circular to recover excess payments.  Hence, the complaint. 8.       Opposite party bank resisted the claim contending that the amount of Rs.13,68,265/- was made based on the Corrigendum PPO No.M/CORR/ 014747/2009 comprising of difference in gratuity and capitalized value of pension.  This amount was twice paid, once on 29.09.2009 by credit to account No.860410110002547 and another on 08.03.2010 to by the OP to account no. 860410110003973.  There was wrong/excess payment to a tune of Rs.2,61,697/- on account of disability pension which was also certified by a Chartered Accountant who reconciled the payments, at the request of the complainant. 9.       The claim of the complainant is nothing but unjust enrichment to usurp the public funds.  Vide letter dated 16.07.2014, the PCDA has upheld the action of the bank in exercising bankers general lien against the deposits for recovering the excess paid amount.  The amount enjoyed by the complainant is public funds and thereby bank can recover the amount.  The Banking Ombudsman upheld the action of the bank and dismissed the petition of the complainant.  And an appeal lies to Appellate Authority/Deputy Governor, RBI but he cannot raise/challenge the same before this Commission.  The provisions of para 103.2 of DPPI 2005 will not apply to the subject case since the complainant had already executed an undertaking in favour of the bank to recover the excess/wrong payment from any of his account/deposit.  The same has also been upheld by the Defence pension authority in its letter dated 28.05.2014. 10.     The complaint given by the complainant to Police, Neredmet as regards the forging of his signature appears to have been dropped and till date there is no communication from the police. The complainant has to work-out the appeal remedy available under the scheme but not before this Commission.  It is false that Banking Ombudsman debarred the complainant to prefer an appeal.  Hence, prayed to dismiss the complaint with costs. 11.     On his behalf, the Complainant filed his affidavit and the documents, Exs.A1 to A38.  On behalf of the Opposite party, Sita Mahalakshmi, its Chief Manager had filed her affidavit and the documents Ex.B1 and B11 in support of its case. 12.     The points for consideration are : Explanation.-- For the purposes of this clause, "commercial purpose" does not include use by a person of goods bought and used by him and services availed by him exclusively for the purposes of earning his livelihood by means of self-employment; 2(g)       "deficiency" means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inade­quacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service; 2(o)      "service" means service of any description which is made avail­able to potential users and includes, but not limited to, the provision of facilities in connection with banking, financing insurance, transport, processing, supply of electrical or other energy, board or lodging or both, housing construction, entertainment, amusement or the purveying of news or other information, but does not include the rendering of any service free of charge or under a contract of personal service; The aforesaid statutory provisions make it crystal clear that the Act is made to deal with the rights of consumers wherein marketing of goods, or "services" as defined under the Act have been provided.  Therefore, the question does arise as to whether the Forum under the Act can deal with the service matters of government servants.
