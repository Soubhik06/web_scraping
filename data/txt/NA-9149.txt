 grant of anticipatory bail to the applicant/co-accused in FIR No.0055/2022,
 registered at Police Station Chanakya Puri, under Sections
 420 / 468 / 471 / 120B IPC. 4. Mr. Yudhishtar Kahol, learned counsel for the applicant, submits that
 the applicant has certain tie-ups with certain Universities in the U.S. and in BAIL APPLN. 1549/2022 Page 1 of 3 Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:MANJEET KAUR Signing Date:19.05.2022 18:46:19 connection with admissions to those Universities, he had extended some
 help to the co-accused Nigam Vamshi Krishna. However, the said Nigam
 Vamshi Krishna preferred not to join the college in respect of which the
 applicant had franchise, but seems to have applied to some other University
 through some other agent. Therefore, it is submitted that the alleged forged
 documents cannot be connected with the applicant. It is further submitted
 that the other agent has not even been named in the FIR. The learned
 counsel submits that the applicant is willing to join investigations but
 without the threat of arrest. 5. Ms.Manjeet Arya, learned APP for the State, on the other hand,
 submits that the co-accused Nigam Vamshi Krishna has pointed only to the
 applicant as the source of all the documents which included a certificate of
 employment, bank loan, offer letter, pay slip, experience certificate etc. all
 of which were forged. It is further submitted by the learned APP that the
 letter dated 7th April, 2022 received by the Investigating Officer (I.O.) from
 the Punjab National Bank establishes that no bank loan had been granted to
 the co-accused Nigam Vamshi Krishna from the said branch. 6. In these circumstances, custodial interrogation was essential to
 investigate in detail the extent of the forgeries that have been committed and
 the transfer of a sum of Rs.28 Lakhs into the account of the co-accused
 Nigam Vamshi Krishna which was almost immediately withdrawn through
 RTGS into four different accounts, in respect of which also investigations
 were still pending. It is further submitted that though notices had been issued
 to the applicant, he has chosen not to join investigation. 7. It is clear that the very serious allegations have been levelled against
 the applicant. The co-accused Nigam Vamshi Krishna had even appeared
 before the Embassy of the U.S. with these forged papers to get a visa. In
 other words, the applicant is allegedly a facilitator and for which quite
 reasonably, he would have received consideration. All these aspects require
 in-depth investigation. Understandably, the request of the I.O. for custodial
 interrogation cannot be brushed away. 8. In these circumstances, as the applicant has been reluctant to join
 investigations despite notices having been issued by the I.O., this Court does
 not find it fit to grant anticipatory bail to the applicant. 9. The anticipatory bail application is accordingly dismissed. 10. The order be uploaded on the website forthwith. ASHA MENON, J
