 (Order on delay condonation application)
 (Dated 18/12/2025)
Per: Mr. Mukesh V. Sharma, Hon'ble Presiding Member 1. The Applicant has filed the present application seeking condonation
of delay in filing the Appeal arising out of Consumer Complaint No.
CC/198/2008. It is stated that the Appeal is directed against the order 2 dated 06.02.2010 passed by the Ld. District Forum, Mumbai Suburban,
Bandra, which, as per the Applicant, was modified subsequently. 2. The Applicant's primary contention is that he did not receive the
first copy of the said order by post. According to him, the address shown
in the order and the address mentioned in the consumer complaint were
different and, therefore, he could not receive the first copy, resulting in
inability to take steps within limitation. 3. The Applicant has further stated that he got knowledge of the
impugned order only through Execution Application No. 62 of 2010,
wherein summons were served through the Senior Inspector, Dahisar
Police Station, at his new address i.e. Flat No. 502, Bhavani Apartment,
Yeshwant Tawade Road, Opposite Railway Station, Bhavini Building,
Dahisar (East), Mumbai-400068. He has pleaded that thereafter,
somewhere on or around 26.02.2014 onwards, he started appearing in
the said execution proceedings. 4. The Applicant has expressly admitted that the Appeal is hit by
delay of 3589 days. For the purpose of computation, he has taken the
date of issue of the second copy of the order as 21.02.2014. He has
further pleaded that, on such computation, the Appeal was scheduled to
be filed on 22.03.2014; however, as 22.03.2014 was a fourth Saturday
and 23.03.2014 was Sunday, the Appeal was scheduled to be filed on
24.03.2014. He has then stated that the Appeal is actually filed on
27.11.2023 and, therefore, there is "inadvertent delay" of 3589 days,
necessitating the present separate delay condonation application. 5. The Applicant has asserted that he was always under an
impression that there was no case for him to defend on merits and that
he was blaming his deceased father Late Mr. Kedar Pande for the alleged
agreement with Respondent No.1. He has then introduced the plea of
fraud by stating that the alleged fraud played by Respondent 3 No.1/original complainant was detected by his Advocate Rohan
Pawaskar on 13.11.2023. On that basis, he has contended that if such
fraud is considered and appreciated, then the Appeal would be within
time and deserves compassionate hearing. 6. In support of the plea of fraud, the Applicant has relied upon
events in the complaint proceedings after the death of his father on
23.01.2009. He has stated that his name was substituted by order dated
24.11.2009, with directions to serve him notice dated 25.11.2009 by
humdust, which, according to him, must be personal service on the
addressee. He alleged that instead, the Advocate for Respondent No.1
served the notice by courier on a person named "Mishra", filed an
affidavit of service portraying due service, and relied upon an
acknowledgment not signed by the Applicant but by said "Mishra". 7. Applicant also pointed out that while his address in the proceedings
is shown as "27, Jain Society, Chandavarkar Road, Borivali (W),
Mumbai-400092", the affidavit/acknowledgment relied upon shows
service at "Satellite Heights, Anand Nagar, Oshiwara, Jogeshwari (W),
Mumbai-400102", and therefore he contended that the complainant
circumvented the humdust direction by allegedly fabricated courier
service, thereby playing fraud upon the Forum to obtain a favourable
order. 8. The Respondent No.1 has filed an additional reply opposing the delay
condonation application. It is contended that, from the perusal of the
application, the Applicant has not stated any cogent reason explaining
such inordinate delay and has merely made false and baseless
allegations. According to Respondent No.1, the Appeal is filed with the
intention to harass Respondent No.1 and to delay the ongoing execution
proceedings, and hence the delay condonation application deserves
dismissal with compensatory cost. 9. The Respondent No.1 specifically disputed the Applicant's assertion
that he got knowledge of the impugned order only when summons were
served in Execution Application No.62 of 2010. It is pleaded that, even
prior to issuance of summons, the Advocate for Respondent No.1 had
served upon the Applicant, by hand delivery, a copy of the entire
execution application along with a copy of the impugned order on
08.03.2011 and that the Applicant acknowledged receipt thereof. On this
basis, Respondent No.1 asserted that the Applicant had knowledge of the
impugned order at least on 08.03.2011 and the contrary statement on
oath is made only to reduce the period of delay and to mislead this
Commission. Respondent No.1 relies upon the letter dated 08.03.2011. 10. The Respondent No.1 further submitted that, apart from the above
service by Respondent No.1, the Applicant had also received notice from
the District Commission, which is alleged to have been suppressed by
the Applicant. It is pointed out that even in the present delay application
the Applicant admitted computation of delay from the date of receiving
the second copy of the impugned order. Respondent No.1, therefore,
asserts that the delay is even more and quantifies it as 4617 days in
filing the present Appeal. 11. Dealing with the Applicant's plea regarding service on "Mishra", the
Respondent No.1 submitted that the Applicant does not dispute the
address on which the notice was served and, therefore, it is an admitted
position that the notice was served at the correct office address of the
Applicant. 12. It is further pleaded that the person who received the notice, namely
Mr. Rajkumar Mishra, was the employee/partner of the Applicant.
Respondent No.1 stated that the underlying project is an SRA project,
that the Applicant had undertaken the project jointly with the society
situated on the said plot, and that Mr. Rajkumar Mishra was the 5 Chairman/Secretary of the said society and was working with the
Applicant. Respondent No.1 further asserted that the said Mr. Rajkumar
Mishra, as Secretary/Chairman along with the Applicant, had issued
allotment letters of flats to society members, showing their association,
and that the same person is also shown as surety in the execution
proceedings, thereby supporting the case that acceptance of notice by
Mr. Rajkumar Mishra on behalf of the Applicant is valid. 13. The Respondent No.1 accordingly contended that service through
registered courier was accepted by the District Commission; that the
notice of the complaint was served through proper mode at the correct
office address and accepted by the Applicant's employee/partner who
had also put his mobile number on the acknowledgment; and hence the
allegations regarding defective service and fraud are devoid of merit. 14. It is also alleged by the Respondent No. 1 that the Applicant
deliberately avoided appearance before the District Commission and has
approached this Commission with unclean hands. Respondent No.1
relies upon documents collectively referred as Annexure "B" (including
acknowledgment slip of service, allied documents, allotment letter and
surety bond), and prays for dismissal of the delay condonation
application with compensatory cost. 15. Respondent No.2 has filed Affidavit in Reply through its partner Mr.
Nitesh Patani stating that he is the authorized partner of Respondent
No.2 firm i.e. M/s. Nileshwar Developers and is duly authorized by the
other partner to depose. He stated that Respondent No.2 was served with
summons in the present proceedings. 16. Respondent No.2 has stated that the original consumer complaint
appears to be for alleged deficiency of service as between the Appellant
(and/or his late father) and Respondent No.1; that Respondent No.2 was
not a party to the alleged agreement dated 1999 between them; and that 6 matters such as the agreement, alleged deficiency, filing of the complaint
and service are exclusively within the knowledge of those parties, hence
Respondent No.2 is not in a position to comment thereon. 17. Respondent No.2 further stated that it was not aware of any order
of 2010 being passed against it and that, in any case, a perusal of the
impugned order indicates that Respondent No.2 was exonerated as the
transaction/deal was exclusively between the Appellant and Respondent
No.1; therefore, Respondent No.2 claims no role in the inter se dispute
and prays for appropriate orders as this Commission may deem fit. 18. We have heard the learned advocate for the Applicant/Appellant
and the learned advocate for Respondent No.1 at length on the Misc.
Application seeking condonation of delay. The learned advocate for
Respondent No.2 submitted that Respondent No.2 does not wish to
advance separate oral arguments and requested that its Affidavit in
Reply be considered in lieu of oral submissions. We have also perused
the delay condonation application, the replies filed by Respondent Nos.1
and 2 and the written synopsis/notes of arguments tendered by the
Applicant/Appellant. 19. The Applicant/Appellant has urged that the impugned order is
vitiated by fraud and, therefore, the delay deserves to be condoned. The
emphasis of the submissions is that fraud vitiates all judicial acts; that
the proceedings allegedly concluded in an order without proper service
upon the Applicant after the demise of his father; and that he was
informed by his Advocate Mr. Rohan Pawaskar that fraud has been
committed on the Appellant, upon which he immediately approached this
Commission after a decade. The Applicant has relied upon various
judgments to contend that limitation cannot be allowed to protect fraud. 20. The Applicant/Appellant, in support of the plea of fraud, has
referred and relied upon the following judgments as reflected in the
written synopsis: Raghunathpur Nafar Academy (Para 15 justice-oriented
 & Ors. clauses approach. referred) 21. Respondent No.1 has opposed the application by filing a detailed
reply. In substance, Respondent No.1 contended that the delay is
inordinate and the application does not disclose any satisfactory
explanation for such prolonged inaction. Respondent No.1 asserted that
the Applicant had knowledge of the impugned order much earlier,
including during execution proceedings, and that the plea of fraud is an
afterthought raised to overcome the bar of limitation and to stall 8 execution. Respondent No.1 has also disputed the allegations regarding
service and contending that due service was effected, and the Applicant
is suppressing material facts. 22. Respondent No.2, through its partner, has filed an Affidavit in Reply
stating that it was not a party to the alleged agreement between the
Applicant's late father and Respondent No.1; that the controversy is
essentially inter se between those parties; and that Respondent No.2 was
exonerated in the original complaint. Respondent No.2 has, therefore,
submitted that it has no role in the present delay dispute and has
requested appropriate orders, while relying upon its reply as filed. 23. We have considered the entire material on record. At the outset, it is
an admitted position that the delay is huge. The right of appeal is a
statutory right, and it has to be exercised within the prescribed period of
limitation. Condonation is an exception and can be granted only when
sufficient cause is shown, covering the entire period of delay by a
plausible and convincing explanation. 24. The governing principle is well settled: vigilantibus non dormientibus
jura subveniunt, i.e. the law assists those who are vigilant and not those
who sleep over their rights. When an applicant approaches the appellate
forum after an extraordinary lapse of time, the explanation must be
proportionate to the delay and must disclose due diligence, bona fides
and continuity of steps. 25. In the present case, we find that the application and the synopsis
concentrate overwhelmingly on the theme of fraud, but do not furnish a
satisfactory account of the delay itself. The only reason pressed with
emphasis is that Advocate Mr. Rohan Pawaskar informed the Applicant
that fraud has been committed on the Appellant and thereafter he filed
the Appeal. Even if this assertion is accepted at face value, it does not
explain the long intervening inaction, especially when Respondent No.1 9 has pointed out earlier knowledge and participation in execution, which
objections have not been effectively met by a time-wise, factual
explanation. 26. We also find that, on the material placed before us at this stage, the
Applicant has not demonstrated fraud in the manner known to law.
There can be no dispute that fraud vitiates proceedings; however, fraud
is a serious allegation. It must be pleaded with material particulars and
supported by credible material. Mere repetition of the word "fraud", or
reliance upon propositions of law divorced from proof of foundational
facts, cannot lead to a finding of fraud, nor can it, by itself, justify
condonation of such an inordinate delay, hence the citations referred by
the Applicant is of no use in present matter. 27. The objections raised by Respondent No.1, particularly on
knowledge, service and conduct, create a serious dent in the Applicant's
plea that he was prevented from approaching the appellate forum within
time. Respondent No.2 has also clarified its limited role and exoneration
in the original complaint. In the overall conspectus, we are unable to
hold that the Applicant has shown sufficient cause for condonation of
delay. 28. We, therefore, pass the following order: [ Mukesh V. Sharma]
