          The complaint is filed under section 17 (1)(a)(i) of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 by the Complainant complaining deficiency in service against the Opposite party and claiming refund of Rs.51,05,100/- along with interest @ 12% per annum from 30.11.2013 till the date of realization together with compensation of Rs.5,00,000/- and pay costs of Rs.50,000/-. 2.                 The brief facts of the case as seen from the complaint are that the complainant no.1 is a multi-State Scheduled Bank rendering Banking Services to its customers and the complainant is one of the Branches of the complainants situated at A.S.Rao Nagar, Hyderabad.  During the course of Banking operations, to safe guard the interests of the Bank the complainant no.1 obtained various types of insurance policies from the opposite parties and renewed the policies from time to time.  One of such policies is Bankers Indemnity Insurance Policy bearing No.551703/46/13/77/00000014 for a sum of Rs.400 crores. 3.                 While so on 29.11.2013 on opening the branch of the complainant no.2 at 9 a.m. the Branch Manager found that the main door shutter locks were opened and found that certain golden jewellery bags of the customers were missing from the store well in the strong room.  Immediately a complaint was lodged with the PS Kushaiguda vide FIR 714 of 2013 dated 29.11.2013 u/s 457 and 380 IPC.  The complainant no.1 immediately intimated about the burglary that has taken place on 29.11.2013 in the branch of opposite party no.2.  The Bankers indemnity insurance claim form dated 02.12.2013 was submitted by the complainant no.1 to the opposite partyno.2.  The police authorities during the course of investigation recovered all the missing golden bags except 4 bags.  Thereafter another claim form was also submitted on 08.01.2014 by the complainant no.1 to the opposite party no.1 claiming about Rs.50,00,000/-.  The police authorities have arrested the Bank gold appraiser Machiraju Brahmachary and recovered all the stolen bags (356 Nos.) except 4 bags.  During the investigation of the police, the appraiser admitted that he colluded with some of the customers and deposited spurious gold jewellery into the Bank.  After receiving the recovered golden bags from the police authorities, the complainant Bank once again got appraisal of the golden jewellary by some other gold appraiser and found that 49 gold bags deposited with the Bank were spurious. 4.                 All the required documents were furnished to the opposite parties and also furnished the necessary information to the Surveyor Sri Krishna, appointed by the insurer and inspite of the same, the opposite parties have not settled the claim on one pretext or another.  Though the opposite party no.1 received the total premium without any murmur, erroneously, without the knowledge and consent of the complainant  no.1 covered such risk only for a sum of Rs.1,00,000/- instead of Rs.5,00,000/-.  On 27.03.2015 the complainant no.1 submitted a representation to the Chief Manager of opposite party no.2 narrating all the facts and requesting to settle the claim for sum of Rs.5,00,000/-.  On 09.04.2015 the complainant no.1 submitted a comprehensive letter to the opposite party no.1 requesting him to settle the matter immediately.    On 27.03.2015 the opposite party no.1 informed the complainant that the claim was repudiated on the ground that from the records it was evident that the offenders used false/duplicate keys in opening the main door shutters and strong room to commit the offence and that there was no forcible/violent entry/exit from the premises and that use of duplicate keys in opening the main door and strong room, the loss falls outside the scope of the policy.  The investigation conducted by the police authorities clearly reveals that the culprit used battery operated saw/cutter for cutting iron rods of the grill gate and gained entry into the strong room.  Subsequently, the pan and lever rods of the store-well in which gold ornaments were kept, were cut-off and the gold ornament bags were stolen.  The investigation further reveals that the culprit attempted to open forcibly other store wells in the said strong room but could not succeed in his attempts.  He also used voucher stitching iron rod in the said operation.  All the material instruments were seized by the police.  The said facts establish that the culprit made entry into the strong room by applying force.  On 15.05.2015 the complainant no.1 lodged a complaint before the Insurance Ombudsmen against the opposite parties.  On 19.05.2015, the Insurance Ombudsman informed that it has no jurisdiction to entertain the complaint. 5.                           The unrecovered 4 gold bags are 30 gms of Sri Parameswar Kumar (MGL A/c 2955), 32.7 Gms of Satyanarayana (MGL A/c.2976), 72.4 Gms of Chandramouli (MGL A/c 3017) and 61.11 Gms of Jagadeesh Chowdary (MGL A/c.3050) totaling 196.21 gms.  The market value of 196.21 gms of gold when pledged with the Bankw as about 4,43,828/- @ Rs.3,080/- per gram.  In 29 MGL Accounts, Bank sanctioned different amounts of loan to a tune of Rs.17,00,800/- on pledging of 1,031,180 gms (gross weight) whose net weight was 817.00 gms.  The new Gold Appraiser appointed by the Head office/complainant no.1 on test found that the entire deposited gold is found to be 100% spurious.  Similarly the Bank sanctioned an amount of rs.13,61,500/- to 20 MSSGL Loan Accounts on pledging of 900.930 gms (gross weight) whose net weight was 696.40 gms of gold by the customers.    On test, the new Gold Appraiser found that the entire pledged gold is found to be 100% spurious.  Thus the market value of 1,513.40 gms net weight of gold at the time of sanctioning of the loans @ Rs.3,080/- per gram works out to Rs.46,61,272/-.  Because of the loss of 4 bags golden jewellary and the value of 49 spurious golden jewellary bags, the bank sustained the loss of Rs.51,05,100/-.  Though in the letter dated 23.04.2015 the opposite party no.1 repudiated the claim after elaborative correspondence on 23.02.2015 the opposite party no.1 directly deposited Rs.99,999/- in to the account of the complainant no.1 account.  The opposite parties have not yet furnished a copy of Survey Report and Investigation Report to the complainants which is mandatory under IRDA Regulations 2002.  The action of the opposite parties in not paying the entire amount of loss sustained by the Bank and paying a paltry amount of Rs.99,999/- and thereafter repudiating the claim not only amounts to unfair trade practice but also amounts to deficiency in service.  Hence, the complaint praying the reliefs as stated in paragraph no.1 supra. 6.                 The opposite party filed written version resisting the case and contended that the complaint is not maintainable either in law or on facts.  The complainant is not a consumer as the complainant is running the business for profits being a Bank. 7.                 It is true that the opposite party issued  Bank's Indemnity Insurance Policy No.551703/46/13/77/00000014 valid from 01.04.2013 to 31.03.2014  for apeirod of one year to the complainant.  While so on the  intervening night of 28/29.11.2013 due to the burglary the gold was stolen from the complainant's Bank and made the claim with the opposite party.  After receiving the claim from   the complainant, the opposite party appointed P.Sree Krishna, Chartered Accountant, Registered Surveyors and Loss Assessors to investigate the case and assess the loss.  The Surveyor after his investigation found that due to fraud of the appraiser in the alleged burglary loss was caused and submitted his report on 09.05.2014 assessing the net loss at Rs.2,28,380/- to the opposite parties and they repudiated the claim on the ground that there is no forcible entry of the thieves in the Bank premises and as per the terms and conditions of the policy the complainants are not entitled to the amounts as claimed in the complaint.    It is false to say that the complainants requested the risk for frauds/irregularities of the daily deposit agents and appraisers for Rs.5,00,000/-.   Since the opposite parties handed over the policies in the second week of April 2013 the complainant never complained till the date of loss occurred to them. 8.                 The opposite parties basing on the scrutiny of various documents like FIR, Remand Case Dairy, Claim Form, the Survey Reports and as per the terms and conditions of the Bankers Indemnity Policy the complainants are not enetitled to the amounts claimed in the complaint.  Since there are disputed facts and the complainants are running the Bank Business for profits and conducting the commercial activities this complaint was filed before the Commission to avoid Court fee before the Civil Court.  As per the IRDA the surveyor was appointed within time and the surveyor assessed the loss without any delay.  Hence, the opposite parties prayed for dismissal of the complaint. 9.                          On behalf of the complainant, the Deputy General Manager  has filed his evidence affidavit and got Exs.A1 to A19 marked.   On behalf of  the opposite parties,  the Assistant Manager has filed his affidavit as RW1  and the Chartered Accountant filed his evidence affidavit as RW2 and got  Exs.B1 to B6 marked. 10.                        Counsel for complainant present and was heard.   No representation for opposite parties.  The counsel for both parties have filed their respective written arguments. 11.               The points that arise for consideration are : "12. I see no reason to discard the report of the Surveyor. He appears to be a guideless witness. No motive was ever attributed to him. There must be some reasonable ground or doubt to reject his report. The report of the Surveyor carries infinite significance as was held in Roshan Lal Oil Mills Ltd. & Ors., 2014 (SLT Soft) 1 = 2014 (CPJ Soft) 1 = (2000) 10 Supreme Court Cases 19 and in D.N. Badoni v. Oriental Insurance Co. Ltd., I (2012) C.P.J. 272 (NC)." 28. In New India Assurance Co. Ltd., vs. Pave Infrastructures Pvt. Ltd. , 2015 (3) CPR 577 (NC), Hon'ble National Commission has observed that "Loss of assessment by approved Surveyor can be discarded only on cogent reasons". 29.                In Garg Acrylics Ltd., Through Sh. Anish Bansal G.M. (G.M.) Authorised Representative vs. United India Insurance Co. Ltd., 2015 (1) CPR 273 (NC), Hon'ble National Commission has observed thus :- 30. In The Oriental Insurance Co. Ltd., Through its Regional Manager vs. Ishwar Singh , 2015 (1) CPR 157 (NC), Hon'ble National Commission has observed thus :- 34.                  The report of the Surveyor is reliable document, therefore, the report of the Surveyor  can be made a basis for adjudication of the case of the complainant. The Report of the Surveyor can be given due weightage and it cannot be discarded lightly. Correct assessment of loss was made by the Surveyor. There is no discrepancy in the said report so as to make a ground for the same to be unreliable.   The entire loss, as assessed by the Surveyor had not been paid to the complainant   and, as such, it cannot be said that there was any deficiency in service on its part. 35.               In the light of the above, we note,  there is no dispute that there was  a theft occurred on the intervening night of 28/29.11.2013   and Complainant had a valid policy.  The theft of the gold ornaments by the then appraiser is not in dispute.  So since the most of the gold ornaments were recovered except loss of four bags, we rely on the report of the Surveyor  to grant total net loss estimated by him.     Hence, the Points No.1 and 2 are answered in favour of the complainant and against the opposite party. 36.                         In the result, the complaint is allowed directing the opposite party to pay Rs.2,28,380/- with interest @ 9% from 09.05.2014 i.e., the date of the survey report   till the date of payment together with costs of Rs.5,000/-.  Time for compliance four weeks.
