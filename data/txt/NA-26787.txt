--- POST TEXT ---

Throw in some flashy graphics, confident delivery, and promises of “**10x returns**,” and suddenly you’ve got thousands of DIY investors jumping headfirst into products they barely understand. 

These influencers aren’t financial advisors, they’re marketers. They know exactly which buttons to press - FOMO, greed, insecurity and they exploit them ruthlessly. 

I feel sorry for these investors, but I also feel frustrated because this was entirely avoidable. 

Investing isn’t supposed to be glamorous or exciting. Yet, thanks to social media, we’ve turned it into entertainment. People scroll through reels about “*how to retire by 30*” or “*the next multi-bagger stock*,” and they forget one fundamental truth: **risk cannot be eliminated, only managed.** 

Why do people fall for this stuff? 

Fear-based content doesn’t get shares; optimism does. No one wants to hear, “*Hey, slow down, understand the product, read the fine print.*” They want to hear, “*You too can become a crorepati!*” 

So they skip the research, ignore the warnings, and dive in headfirst. This behavior stems from two powerful biases: overconfidence (“I’m smarter than the average investor”) and recency bias (“The market has been going up, so it’ll keep going up”). Both are dangerous.

If an influencer gives bad advice and thousands lose money, nothing happens to them. They’ll pivot to another trend, another buzzword, another scam. Meanwhile, the victims are left picking up the pieces.
