Fintech company BharatPe has filed a trademark infringement suit against Internet of Things (IoT) prototyping platform BharatPi at the Delhi High Court. As part of this lawsuit, BharatPe is seeking a permanent injunction against BharatPi, restraining it from infringing the company’s trademark and copyrighted material.

In a hearing on April 17, BharatPe’s lawyers argued that while the IoT platform is claiming that it “independently coined, conceived, and adopted” BharatPi as its name, the two companies’ names are phonetically identical.

The Delhi High Court has referred the matter for mediation before the Delhi High Court Mediation and Conciliation Centre on April 22, after which it will take up the matter again on May 16, 2025. During the course of the April 17 hearing, Justice Amit Bansal told BharatPe’s lawyers during the April 17 hearing that he would not grant the company an ex parte stay in this case and that mediation would be BharatPe’s best bet. For context, an ex parte stay is a temporary legal order that a court issues without notifying or hearing from the other party involved.

As per the discussion in court, BharatPi says that the visual, phonetic, and structural composition of its name is different from BharatPe. It further mentions that its operations, which are focused on IoT, are fundamentally different from fintech operations. This, it says, negates the possibility of consumer confusion.

Besides pointing out phonetic similarities, BharatPe’s lawyer argued that the company had abandoned the registration for Bharat Pi. Further, he explained that Bharat Pi’s parent company WaterBot Online Solutions Private Limited, does not own the company’s name. Instead, another company with the same director, which was constituted in March 2025, has ownership over Bharat Pi. BharatPe’s lawyer explained that this new company is essentially the same as WaterBot, with WaterBot handing its website and business to this company.

While moving the matter for mediation, the court also mandated that BharatPi must submit a reply to the trademark infringement suit within two weeks. The court has also instructed BharatPi to not use the domain bharatpi.com till the next date of hearing. This came after BharatPe’s lawyer specified that BharatPi was already in the process of switching its domain to bharatpi.net. For context, BharatPe’s domain name is bharatpe.com, and the company has held this domain since November 2017.

This is not the first time that BharatPe has engaged in a lawsuit against a company with a similar name. In August 2018, PhonePe filed a lawsuit against BharatPe at the Delhi High Court, seeking to prevent the fintech company from using the suffix “Pe.” However, in 2021, the court dismissed PhonePe’s demand for an interim injunction in the matter, stating that the suffix “Pe” was common and the trademarks of the two companies were not “deceptively similar.” The two companies finally resolved the issue in May 2024 .

Similarly, BharatPe is also fighting a trademark lawsuit against fintech service provider Bharat Pay. In March this year, the Delhi High Court suspended the bharatpay.net website as part of the case. The court held that Bharat Pay’s domain name and app could mislead and defraud innocent members of the public and could cause irreparable damage to BharatPe. Besides blocking the site, it also asked telecom companies to block the mobile number associated with Bharat Pay and disclose all available details associated with the number to BharatPe.
