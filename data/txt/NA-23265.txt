Hi, a client approached me today regarding a peculiar matter today.

**FACTS:**

My client who is a female, initiated DV Complaint before a MM in the year 2015 against her mother-in-law (MIL) and sisters-in-law (SIL1 &amp; SIL2) and subsequent to the aforementioned complaint case, a separate FIR was registered u/s 498A, 406 &amp; 34 IPC against the same persons. My client prior to the year 2022 was residing in a rented accommodation whereas MIL was residing on the ground floor of her house, SIL1 was residing on the first floor of the same house and SIL2 was residing on the second floor of the same house. Third floor of the aforementioned house was leased to a tenant. The aforementioned property was transferred by the Husband of MIL to her by way of gift deed prior to his death.

**2019:**

Fast forward to the year 2019, parties were referred for mediation in the DV case and the connected FIR was specifically mentioned in mediation. Both the parties agreed to a settlement before the Mediation Centre that:

* Complainant will withdraw the DV Complainant before the MM.
* Complainant will assist in quashing of FIR in High Court
* MIL will transfer the third floor of her house to the husband of Complainant, ie. son of the MIL by way of gift deed, within three months of the date when this settlement agreement is made.
* MIL will transfer the second floor to SIL 2 and first floor to SIL 1 in the same manner as mentioned above, within three months of the date when this settlement agreement is made.
* It is specifically recorded that MIL has free will to live with whichever child she wants, and the children will have to take care of her.
* All the jewellery seized by the police in the FIR from the house of SIL1 will be handed over back to the SIL1 and Complainant will have no claim over the same.

Within three months of settlement, Complainant withdrew the DV case.

(As of now I do not know what the complainant and respondents have stated in their statement before MM while withdrawing the case; final order isn’t uploaded on eCourts. I'll be applying for a certified copy.)

**Fast forward to the year 2022:**

The MIL did not transfer the third floor to her son but allowed him and the complainant to reside on the third floor. This gave the Complainant hope that she will transfer the third floor, however late it may be.

MIL, SIL1&amp;2 moved a petition for quashing the FIR and specifically mentioned that an MOU containing the terms of mediation settlement has been made with the consent of all the parties and annexed the same. The FIR was quashed subsequently.

Also, in the year 2022, Water and Electricity connection of the third floor was transferred in favour of the husband of my client with the consent of MIL.

**Fast forward to the year 2023:**

My client is alleging that she is, once again, suffering from constant nagging and harassment from the aforementioned persons and the MIL has refused to transfer the third floor to her son.

**What my client wants:**

She and her husband want the MIL to abide by the mediation agreement stating that she will transfer the third floor of her house. She does not want to initiate any criminal proceedings against the MIL and SILs.

**My Observations:**

* Usually in cases like DV, the terms of settlement are mentioned in systematic order, for eg. - Withdrawal of DV/125/Divorce&gt;&gt;Transfer of Consideration&gt;&gt;Quashing of FIR&gt;&gt;Possession, etc., in order to safeguard the interests of both the parties. The Complainant only after receiving the final consideration goes for complete withdrawal. However, in this case, owing to the mistake/or disingenuousness of the previous counsel of the complainant, ie. my client, the MIL and SILs have had the benefit of quashing and withdrawal without having to transfer the floor beforehand.
* The MIL, SILs and Complainant must have mentioned the terms of Mediation Agreement before the MM at the time of withdrawal of DV case. If they have specifically stated the same and as the DV case is now disposed off, should I move a contempt petition in HC?
* The MIL and SILs have filed an MOU before the HC at the time of quashing mentioning the same terms. Can/Should I exhibit the same in HC as evidence in the aforementioned Contempt Petition?
* The point is, I've read case laws and observations of the HC, for instance *Dayawati v Yogesh K. Gosain, 2017 SCC OnLine Del 11032* pertaining to mediation agreements arising out of criminal proceedings which state that these agreements are not subject to the same standard of a decree arising out of Sec. 89 CPC. The mediation agreements cannot be executed in Civil Courts like Arbitral Awards and can only act as evidence in trial for the offences.
* In criminal cases where mediation agreement is reached, like 138, the Courts wait for the agreements to be honoured by both the parties and then proceed to dispose the case. In case the terms of mediation are not abided by any of the parties, the court proceeds with trial. In the present case, the DV has been withdrawn and FIR has been quashed.
* At the cost of sounding silly but to clear a wild doubt, can a person move for specific performance against the mediation agreement for transfer?
* The Limitation of Contempt is 1 year. As the Mediation Agreement of 2019 states that the transfer must be within three months of the date of agreement, I doubt that HC will entertain me. I think alleging the assurances being given for transfer by MIL in verbal forms and possession being given to the complainant and her husband along with Water and Electricity Bill might be considered by the HC as a ground for condonation. But I'm not sure.

**TLDR**, Should I move a Contempt Petition in HC by exhibiting the mediation agreement, statements at the time of withdrawal of DV and Quashing Petition along with MOU, and if so, will the HC entertain my case as it is not within 1 year?

If not, what option is left for my client to obtain the title of the property? 

In case the complainant and MIL, SILs have not stated the terms of Mediation while withdrawing the DV Case, can the Mediation Agreement somehow be enforced?

Although after completing the matters tomorrow in court, I'll be consulting with other lawyers but I felt like sharing here as well because I've observed that there are few study materials regarding enforcement of mediation agreement.
