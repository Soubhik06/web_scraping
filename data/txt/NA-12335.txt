1. This revision petition under Section 397 CrPC preferred by the CBI
lays challenge to orders dated 23.10.2021 and 06.12.2021 passed by the Ld.
ACMM, whereby the applications of the respondent under Section 457 CrPC seeking defreezing of bank accounts and fixed deposits in the name
of her deceased husband Anuj Jain were allowed. 2. The factual matrix, sans unnecessary details, is as follows: FIR No.
RC2212020E002 was registered by the CBI on 11.03.2020 under Section
420 IPC and Sections 43 read with 66, 66C & 66D of the Information and CR No. 26/2021 CBI v. Pooja Jain Page No. 1 of 7 Technology Act, 2000 against six companies including M/s. Saburi TLC
Worldwide Services Private Limited and M/s. Saburi Global Services
Private Limited and other unknown persons on the basis of a written
complaint received from M/s. Microsoft Corporation (India) Private
Limited alleging that the accused companies through their directors
committed Tech Support Scams involving millions of dollars by
impersonating themselves as part of Microsoft's technical support team,
misrepresenting its customers by way of false alert messages/pop ups that
their computers were infected with malware and inducing them to
download/purchase programs, which were 'Potentially Unwanted Programs'
(PUPs), for resolving the said problem. Investigation revealed that accused
Anuj Jain and his wife Pooja Jain (respondent herein) were directors of
M/s. Saburi TLC Worldwide Services Private Limited and M/s. Saburi
Global Services Private Limited (renamed as M/s. VantagePoint Consulting
Services Private Limited w.e.f. 20.05.2020) during the period of occurrence
of crime. Searches were conducted at the office premises of the said two
companies and voluminous incriminating materials were seized. Four
savings accounts bearing Nos. 04852470004871, 04851140033131,
914010000304756 & 151522051974 maintained by accused Anuj Jain in
his individual name with different banks were also freezed under Section
102 CrPC on the premise that the crime proceeds had been received in the
said accounts from the said two companies. During the course of
investigation, accused Anuj Jain expired on 01.05.2021. On 03.06.2021, the
respondent Pooja Jain and her son Kartikeya Jain filed an application under Section 457 CrPC in the capacity of legal heirs for defreezing of the said
bank accounts of deceased Anuj Jain. CBI opposed the application. Vide CR No. 26/2021 CBI v. Pooja Jain Page No. 2 of 7 order dated 23.10.2021, the Ld. ACMM allowed the application subject to
furnishing of separate indemnity bonds by the applicants for the total
amount deposited in the said accounts. Referring to the decision rendered in U. Subhadramma & Ors. v. State of Andhra Pradesh , (2016) 7 SCC
797, the Ld. ACMM observed that after the death of accused Anuj Jain, the
criminal proceedings can not be continued against him as he is not
available to defend himself and thus no purpose would be served by
keeping his bank accounts freezed. Thereafter, the respondent moved
another application dated 26.11.2021 seeking defreezing of fixed deposits
which were linked to the said bank accounts of deceased Anuj Jain. This
application was also opposed by the CBI. Vide order dated 06.12.2021, the
Ld. ACMM allowed the above application and directed that the bank
accounts of deceased Anuj Jain and the fixed deposits linked thereto be de­
freezed upon furnishing of indemnity bonds by his legal heirs for the total
amount deposited therein. On 13.12.2021, the Ld. ACMM accepted the
indemnity bonds of Rs. 19.50 crores submitted by the respondent and her
son. Aggrieved from the orders dated 23.10.2021 and 06.12.2021, the CBI
has instituted the present revision petition. 3. Sr. PP for the CBI has submitted that a total sum of Rs. 19.11 crores
approximately is available in the bank accounts and fixed deposits of
accused Anuj Jain and out of the said amount, around Rs. 6.81 crores and
Rs. 5.30 crores were received by him from M/s. Saburi TLC Worldwide
Services Private Limited and M/s. VantagePoint Consulting Services
Private Limited respectively, which are the proceeds of crime as the same CR No. 26/2021 CBI v. Pooja Jain Page No. 3 of 7 had been generated by the said companies from selling fake software/PUPs.
He has assailed the impugned orders by contending that the Ld. ACMM
failed to appreciate that if the bank accounts and fixed deposits of accused
Anuj Jain are defreezed, his legal heirs would fritter away the proceeds of
crime and nothing would be left to compensate the victims. 4. Per contra, the counsel for the respondent has argued that in terms of
the law laid down in U. Subhadramma (supra), the Ld. ACMM rightly
ordered for defreezing of the bank accounts and fixed deposits of accused
Anuj Jain and no interference is called for. 5. It is a matter of record that the instant FIR was registered on
11.03.2020 against six companies including M/s. Saburi TLC Worldwide
Services Private Limited and M/s. Saburi Global Services Private Limited
(now renamed as M/s. VantagePoint Consulting Services Private Limited)
and the investigation is still underway. Admittedly, the accused Anuj Jain
and his wife Pooja Jain (respondent herein) were the directors of both the
above companies at the relevant time. It is also not in dispute that all the
four bank accounts in question stand in the individual name of accused
Anuj Jain and the fixed deposits linked thereto were created out of the
money deposited in the said accounts. Consequent upon his death on
01.05.2021, the proceedings qua accused Anuj Jain have abated. 6. While ordering for defreezing of the bank accounts and fixed
deposits of the deceased Anuj Jain, the Ld. ACMM placed reliance on CR No. 26/2021 CBI v. Pooja Jain Page No. 4 of 7 U. Subhadramma (supra). In the said case, accused No. 1
Ramachandraiah and accused No. 2 Subbarayudu were prosecuted for the
offences under Sections 409 , 468 & 471 IPC. Accused No. 1
Ramachandraiah expired during trial. After the trial, the trial court
acquitted the accused No. 2 Subbarayudu and held that accused No. 1
Ramachandraiah alone had committed the offence. After the judgment, the
State moved an application under the Criminal Law Amendment
Ordinance, 1944 for attachment of property in the name of
Ramachandraiah before the Ld. District Judge, who passed an order of
attachment on the basis that Ramachandraiah had procured the property
from the proceeds of the scheduled offences. In the above background, the
Hon'ble Supreme Court held as under: 7. Following the decision rendered in U. Subhadramma (supra), the
Hon'ble Telangana High Court vide judgment dated 21.01.2020 passed in
Civil Miscellaneous Appeal No. 1030/2019 titled as State of Aandhra
Pradesh v. Manga Radha Krishna & Ors. observed that orders of
attachment in respect of the property of an accused should be withdrawn
upon his acquittal or abatement of proceedings on account of his death. 8. Similarly, in Nirmala K. S. v. State of Karnataka,
MANU/KA/4190/2021, the Hon'ble Karnataka High Court held that the
abatement of a criminal trial results in total termination of proceedings and
it can not be kept pending for collateral enquiry in respect of confiscation
of the seized property. 9. Further, in Birajini Panda v. State of Odisha , 2014 (I) ILR CUT
927, the Hon'ble Orissa High Court held that in the case of death of an
accused and abatement of criminal trial against him, the confiscation
proceedings in respect of properties held by him in his own name or in the
names of other persons can not proceed as there is no possibility of him
being convicted for the offence under the Prevention of Corruption Act . 10. The legal position which emerges from the above judgments is that
the abatement of a criminal trial on account of death of an accused results CR No. 26/2021 CBI v. Pooja Jain Page No. 6 of 7 in total termination of proceedings against him and in such an eventuality,
the orders of attachment in respect of his properties should be withdrawn.
Considering the same, the Ld. ACMM rightly ordered for defreezing of
bank accounts and fixed deposits of deceased Anuj Jain by observing that
the allegation of investigating agency that the amount deposited therein is
interlaced with proceeds of crime can not be adjudicated as he would not
be available to defend himself. No infirmity is found in the impugned
orders. The revision petition is, accordingly, dismissed.
