As per my history I am a bank worker. I help fraud and scam victims. Usually cases are routine: preliminary investigation, consultation, then decision on a refund - the victim already knows that they have been scammed.

However, some scam victims are specifically engineered to lie. Here's how the process works.

1. Imposter calls victim out of the blue. No caller ID or spoofed number.

2. Imposter impresses by using a professional tone. Throws out senior names (easily googled) and customer's card details (stolen)

3. Insists that their account is at risk and money needs to be sent. No strategy here - just a professional tone and aggression.

4. Patiently educates them how to send all their money to a bizarre entity in Pakistan, India, Columbia etc.

5. Victim is now completely manipulated. Contacts their bank, sends their money, money gone forever.

6. Victim loses, scammer wins.

A conversation with an engineered scam victim is like talking to an addict in denial. They make up dramatic abuse stories, wax lyrical on their game, strategically design artificial alibis, tell blatant lies, use all their skills and intelligence to fabricate a story. It is really fucking hard to make a scam victim tell the truth.

We must protect customers, but we also have to protect ourselves as employees. Successful scam incidents do not look good on paper. There is a threshold between open discussion and keeping your job. 

So.. off the clock. What is your opinion on trying to get a scam victim to tell the truth?
