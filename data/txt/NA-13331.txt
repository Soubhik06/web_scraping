The United Payments Interface (UPI) system has become the primary channel for processing deposits on illegal gambling platforms, revealed a report from the think tank CUTS International published in May 2025.

These operations centre around malicious mobile applications like XHelper, which present themselves as legitimate money transfer businesses but actually function as management systems for orchestrating large-scale money laundering. Other than illegal gambling, such apps also facilitate other online scams like fake loans and e-commerce fraud.

Regulatory authorities have taken cognisance of this problem. In December last year, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) authorities launched MuleHunterAI, a tool developed to detect and flag mule bank accounts involved in financial fraud in India.

The illicit flow of funds follows a structured path that begins when gambling deposits are made into the mule’s personal or merchant bank account. Mules receive immediate instructions to forward these funds to corporate accounts controlled by the app operators, who then convert the money to cryptocurrency, typically USDT, through exchange platforms. After deducting their commission from the converted funds, the operators pay a portion back to the mule as an incentive.

Apps like XHelper include training systems that teach mules how to receive and transfer funds, handle frozen accounts, create fake business documents, and respond to bank officials when questioned. Mules prefer opening merchant or corporate bank accounts due to higher transaction limits and less scrutiny. The training covers opening such accounts using fake GST registrations, trade licences, and business certificates.

The recruitment works like a pyramid scheme, with agents using Telegram groups to find participants who want easy money for moving funds through their accounts. Once recruited, mules follow a simple process: gambling deposits arrive in their accounts, and they immediately transfer the money to corporate accounts controlled by the app operators, who convert it to cryptocurrency before paying the mules their cut.

When accounts are frozen, the apps provide scripts for mules to follow to recover their accounts. This includes contacting UPI support with fake business explanations, visiting banks with forged documents, and directly approaching fraud victims to negotiate settlements or get “No Objection Certificates” to unfreeze accounts.

The system teaches mules to avoid detection by using bank-specific UPI apps instead of popular ones like PhonePe, registering UPI with different mobile numbers to intercept verification codes, using merchant payment addresses to make transactions look legitimate, and claiming transfers are to friends or vendors when questioned.

Despite government warnings about these illegal payment gateways in October 2024, traffic analysis shows these operations continue processing huge volumes of transactions, suggesting current enforcement efforts remain ineffective against this criminal infrastructure.

Illegal gambling operators extensively exploited digital wallets by using them as intermediaries. Users deposited funds into wallets, which operators then transferred to betting platforms, obscuring the transactions’ final destinations.

In 2020, the Enforcement Directorate had uncovered a major transnational betting racket operated by Chinese nationals through Indian companies, which ran overseas betting websites targeting Indian users with high-return gambling games. They created a sophisticated network using online wallets like Paytm and Cashfree to collect huge deposits and pay agent commissions. Chinese operators, aided by local chartered accountants, set up Indian firms and recruited associates to open HSBC bank and wallet accounts while retaining control from China.

The report alleged that cryptocurrency had become a significant payment mechanism for illegal gambling. Some international platforms like Stake encouraged users to make deposits in cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, USDT, or Ethereum that provided anonymity and bypassed the Indian financial system entirely. Earlier in May, MediaNama reported how Stake’s influencer marketing is luring teenagers, mostly Reddit and Instagram users, towards gambling in India.
