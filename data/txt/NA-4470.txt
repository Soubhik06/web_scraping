This revision petition is filed to set aside the order dated 12.01.2022

 made in Crl.M.P.No.13 of 2022 on the file of the learned Judicial

 Magistrate-I, Alandur. 2.The contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the

 petitioner who is using the card bearing No.4155 7594 3434 9102 of State

 Bank of India for more than a decade received a call on 04.10.2021 from

 63831 66385 claiming that the caller is an employee of State Bank of India

 and enquired about the credit card details for enhancing the credit limits.

 Believing his words, the petitioner provided the details. On 11.10.2021, the

 petitioner received a phone call from State Bank of India Credit Card Team

 requesting the petitioner to convert the debited amount of Rs.92,000/- into

 equated monthly installments, at that time only, the petitioner came to know

 that he was cheated and a sum of Rs.92,000/- was fraudulently withdrawn

 from his credit card. Thereafter, on 12.10.2021 the petitioner preferred a

 complaint to the Police and he was instructed that case involves cyber fraud 2/6 https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Crl.R.C.No.110 of 2022 and hence, directed him to approach the Inspector of Police (Crime),

 St.Thomas Mount, who assigned complaint as C.S.R.No.416 of 2021. The

 petitioner produced call details and particulars and even after lapse of

 several months, no action taken. The petitioner sent a representation to the

 Commissioner of Police on 07.12.2021 which was forwarded to the Deputy

 Commissioner of Police, St.Thomas Mount but no action taken. Hence, the

 petitioner filed a petition under Section 156(3) Cr.P.C. seeking direction to

 register a FIR, conduct investigation and file a report. The learned Judicial

 Magistrate No.I, Alandur dismissed the petitioner's petition for the reason

 that procedure under Section 154 Cr.P.C. not satisfied. Thus, on a wrong

 presumption the Trial Court failed to look into the fact that the petitioner

 had sent a representation to the Commissioner of Police on 07.12.2021

 which was forwarded to the Deputy Commissioner of Police which complies

 with Section 154 Cr.P.C. 3.The learned Additional Public Prosecutor submitted that on the

 petitioner's complaint, enquiry was conducted and in the meanwhile, the

 petitioner had given a representation to the Commissioner of Police which 3/6 https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Crl.R.C.No.110 of 2022 was forwarded to the Deputy Commissioner of Police in

 C.No.1006/CCB/DCP/Mount/2021 which was again forwarded to the Sub-

 Inspector of Police [Cybercrime], St.Thomas Mount, Chennai attached to the

 office of Deputy Commissioner of Police. Thereafter, the Sub-Inspector of

 Police, Cybercrime called the petitioner for enquiry to submit certain

 documents. But the petitioner failed to respond to the notice. In the

 meanwhile, the Cybercrime team located that the fraudsters is from

 Uttarkand and further found that the amount from the petitioner's account,

 Rs.50,000/- and Rs.39,500/- has been transferred to Account

 No.678602010002534 of Union Bank of India on 04.10.2021, thereafter

 fraudsters account freezed. He would further submit that if the petitioner

 appears before the Cybercrime police and responding to the notice sent

 earlier by providing the details, the amount of Rs.89,500/- can be handed

 over to the petitioner after petitioner filing appropriate petition. 4.In view of the above, the petitioner is directed to approach the

 Cybercrime Team, St.Thomas Mount, Alandur, provide all the details which

 are required by the Cybercrime Team and retrieve the money which the 4/6 https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Crl.R.C.No.110 of 2022 petitioner lost due to cybercrime fraud. 5.Accordingly, the criminal revision petition stands disposed of. 07.08.2024
