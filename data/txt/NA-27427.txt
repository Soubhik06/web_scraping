--- POST TEXT ---

It looked legit. Had the Amazon with the smile at the top. Said my account had been logged into from New Delhi India. Gave a link to remedy the problem. I (stupidly, yes) clicked the link. It wanted my password or a key code. I checked the address bar and it DID NOT SAY Amazon dot com. So I clicked out and went to the Amazon site and chatted with a very helpful customer service rep. My account is fine, no problems, but she is passing the whole thing to a supervisor for further investigation. 

Be very careful. There was a blaring clue as why I shouldn’t trust the text. So I went right to the actual Amazon site, they sent me a passcode to verify me. And asked for a screen shot of the text. The way it should be done. 

Just a heads up. I get a few phishing texts a year. This one was pretty good, but even this old lady could see the glaring mistakes they made if they really wanted my password.
