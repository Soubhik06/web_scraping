BRIEF STATEMENT OF REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE:- accused Prahlad was arrested vide memos Ex. PW4/A and Ex. PW4/B
and disclosure statement Ex. PW4/C was recorded. Thereafter, he was
was cross examined and discharged on 11.12.2017. 6. PW5 and PW6 have proved their verification reports Ex.
PW5/A, Ex. PW5/B and Ex. PW6/A (OSR). Thereafter, they were cross
examined and discharged on 18.09.2017. 7. PW7 is Retd. SI Zile Singh who is IO of the present case
and he deposed that on 19.03.2002, while on patrolling duty he formed
a raiding party on secret information and apprehended accused with
stolen motorcycle and forged documents. He further deposed that he
prepared rukka Ex. PW7/A and sent PW2 to PS for registration of FIR.
He then deposed that on receipt of copy of FIR, he prepared site plan
Ex. PW7/B and arrested accused and made further recovery at his
instance. He further deposed that on 20.03.2002 he went to Mordabad,
UP with accused in police custody and at his instance, arrested co-
accused Prahlad. He further deposed that on 20.03.2002, he wrote
letter to concerned authority for verification of the recovered
motorcycles Ex. PW7/C and also obtained specimen signatures of co- accused Prahlad and sent the same for comparison to FSL. He further
deposed that on 07.05.2002, he received letter from concerned
authority that the documents of the recovered motorcycles were fake
and that motorcycle bearing No. UP-21G-2743 was stolen from PS
Krishna Nagar in FIR No.106/02. He proved that letter Ex. PW7/B. He
further deposed that on 26.03.2002, co-accused Intezar was arrested
vide Ex. PW7/E and he recorded his disclosure statement Ex. PW7/F
and then prepared charge sheet and filed the same in the court.
Thereafter, he was cross-examined and discharged on 06.04.2018. 8. No other PW was examined by prosecution and hence,
PE was closed. 9. Statement of accused was recorded U/ Sec.313 Cr.P.C. on
07.07.2018 wherein he denied the case of the prosecution and pleaded
innocence. However, he declined to lead any evidence in defence. 10. Final arguments were advanced at length by Ld. APP for
the State and by LAC for accused. 11. I have considered the submissions and perused the
record carefully. 12. This being a criminal case, the burden of proving guilt of
accused lies upon the prosecution for which the standard required is
beyond reasonable doubt. On the basis of evidence adduced in the
present case, the following reasons come to light: 13. The case property i.e. the recovered motorcycles have
been correctly identified by PW ASI Abdul Gaffar. It is the case of the
prosecution that initially accused was apprehended with one motorcycle
but later on, he got recovered two more motorcycles from GTB Hospital FIR No. 66/02 State vs. Rashid Page No. 4 parking. Although the disclosure statement made by accused Rashid is
inadmissible in evidence under section 25 of Indian Evidence Act, but if
some recovery is made upon disclosure of accused, then the portion
pertaining to actual recovery is admissible in evidence u/s. 27 of Indian
Evidence Act. Thus, the portion pertaining to recovery of motorcycles
bearing fake No. UP-21E-4235 and UP-21G-2743 from GTB Hospital
parking is admissible u/s. 27 of Indian Evidence Act, which has been
proved against accused in Ex. PW1/F and Ex. PW1/G. 14. All the material recovery witnesses have been examined
by prosecution and later on, cross-examined under Section 311 Cr.P.C.
by accused except for PW1 ASI Abdul Gaffar. However, his cross-
examination under Section 311 Cr.P.C. was allowed vide order dated
18.09.2017, subject to the condition of his availability. Thereafter, PW
ASI Abdul Gaffar has remained unserved, mainly on account of the fact
that since he has retired from the department. Thus, on account of non-
availability of PW ASI Abdul Gaffar, he could not be cross-examined
and therefore, as per order dated 18.09.2017, his examination-in-chief
continues to be admissible in evidence. Moreover, PW2 HC Prakash
Chand, PW3 HC Mohd. Ismail and PW4 Ct. Birbal Singh have
corroborated the testimony of PW1 in all material particulars and they
have also been cross-examined under Section 311 Cr.P.C. by accused.
All PW2, PW3 and PW4 have withstood cross-examination and have
deposed that in a fashion which is consistent with their respective
examinations-in-chief. The major thrust of cross-examination by accused of PW2, PW3
and PW4 is non-joining of any public witness in investigation at the time FIR No. 66/02 State vs. Rashid Page No. 5 of arrest or recovery of case property. It has been held in Ajmer Singh
vs. State of Haryana , (2010) 3 SCC 746, Hon'ble Supreme Court that
lack of independent witness does not necessarily vitiate investigation
carried out as it is common knowledge that public persons, for several
reasons, do not wish to associate themselves with police cases. It has
been observed as under: 15. All PW2, PW3 and PW4 have corroborated each other in
regard to preparation of documents at the spot, their coming and going
from the spot and lack of their knowledge regarding investigation of
actual registration numbers of the recovered motorcycles or the cases
in which they were reported stolen. Since PW2 and PW3 had been
examined in the year 2012, PW4 had been examined in the year 2015
and they have all been later on cross-examined on 02.06.2018,
07.05.2018 & 11.12.2017 respectively i.e. after a gap of five to six
years, it is understandable that some answers could not be given in
cross-examination due to memory lapse. Therefore, minor
inconsistencies in the cross-examination of said witnesses is not
sufficient to discredit their testimonies altogether. 16. PW7 SI Zile Singh who is IO of the present case has also
given consistent and cogent testimony. He has further deposed that
during investigation, at the instance of accused Rashid, co-accused
Prahlad Sharma (proceedings since abated) was arrested on
20.03.2002. Later on, during investigation specimen handwriting were
obtained of accused Prahlad and were sent for comparison to FSL with
the handwriting Q1 and Q2, which are the alleged fake documents
bearing No. UP-21G-2558. The said FSL report was admitted as Ex. A1
by accused Rashid vide his statement dated 18.09.2017. by way of the
said FSL report, it has been proved as per the opinion in Ex. A1 that the FIR No. 66/02 State vs. Rashid Page No. 7 specimen handwriting taken from accused Prahlad is matching with the
handwriting Q1 and Q2 on allegedly fake documents, recovered from
possession of accused Rashid. Arrest of accused Prahlad at the
instance of accused Rashid also goes against plea of innocence of
accused Rashid as it leads to the conclusion that accused Rashid must
have been actively involved atleast in preparation of fake documents of
vehicles and therefore, he knew about involvement of accused Prahlad.
Furthermore, no cross-examination has been done by accused of PW7
regarding taking accused Rashid in police custody to Moradabad and
later on, about arrest of co-accused Prahlad at instance of accused
Rashid, except for non-joining of public witness as witness of arrest of
accused Prahlad. Therefore, it is deemed to be admitted by accused
Rashid that he was taken into police custody to Moradabad and that he
got accused Prahlad arrested for preparation of fake documents, which
is also corroborated by FSL report Ex. A1. 17. PW5 and PW6 have proved their verification reports Ex.
PW5/A, Ex. PW5/B and Ex. PW6/A OSR which establishes that the
documents recovered from accused Rashid were fake documents.
Nothing contradictory has come in their cross-examination and
therefore the verification reports have been proved by the prosecution
beyond reasonable doubt. 18. Accused Rashid has not led any defence evidence.
Merely making bald statements u/s. 313 Cr.P.C cannot take the place of
defence evidence and on account of lack of any defence evidence, the
allegations made by prosecution have not been rebutted by leading of
any positive evidence of innocence of accused. Furthermore, there FIR No. 66/02 State vs. Rashid Page No. 8 appears to be no ulterior motive in the present case for false implication
of accused Rashid. Anyhow, false implication with recovery of a motor-
cycle is difficult as motorcycles cannot be said to be easily available for
the purpose of planting upon accused. 19. Thus, as a cumulative effect of all the points discussed
above, I am of the opinion that the prosecution has proved its case
beyond reasonable doubt against accused Rashid and accordingly he
is convicted for offence punishable under Section 482 / 471 IPC and
under Section 103 DP Act. 20. No evidence at all has been produced by the prosecution
for alleged offence under Section 201 IPC as none of the prosecution
witnesses have deposed that accused Rashid indulged in destruction of
evidence for commission of any offence. Accordingly, accused Rashid
is acquitted of offence punishable under Section 201 IPC. (Typed upon dictation directly
