Rapido’s zero-commission pitch to restaurants sounds compelling on paper. However, the critical question is not whether restaurants want this model (they clearly do) but whether it’s economically sustainable.

Rapido’s Head of New Initiatives, Vivek Vashishta, openly told restaurant partners at a National Restaurants Association of India (NRAI) town hall that “In the beginning, we’re not going to be earning money.” This raises the obvious next question: what changes later?

The company faces three paths: flip to commission-based pricing and become what it criticised, exit like Ola and Uber did, or find an alternative revenue model that preserves the zero-commission promise.

A subscription model, like the one Rapido initially proposed in June 2025 before abandoning it, might prove more sustainable and honest than the current approach. Restaurants would know their costs upfront rather than watching platform fees creep up quarter by quarter.

Moreover, flat fees align incentives: as the platform earns from participation, not from taking a percentage of every sale. Whether Rapido returns to subscription-based pricing or eventually mirrors the incumbents’ commission structure remains the central question as it expands beyond Bengaluru.

The unit economics tell the real story. With no restaurant fees, no subscription charges, and only Rs. 30 per order from customers, Rapido currently has no clear revenue stream beyond delivery fees. The company has not disclosed plans for advertising revenue or other monetisation models as of now.

Consequently, Rapido may have to either find a different revenue stream or accept that Ownly operates as a loss leader indefinitely. The food delivery graveyard is full of well-funded platforms that couldn’t crack this problem.

Rapido formally launched its food delivery app Ownly citywide across Bengaluru on March 3, 2026, moving beyond a pilot phase that began in August 2025 in select Bengaluru neighbourhoods including Koramangala, HSR Layout, and BTM Layout.

The platform operates on a zero-commission model. Restaurants pay nothing. Customers pay a flat Rs. 30 delivery fee per order. According to Storyboard18 , the company has onboarded approximately 20,000 restaurant partners and uses Rapido’s existing bike taxi network, called Captains, for deliveries.

“There are absolutely no charges to restaurants. There are no commissions, there are no marketing fees, [and] there are no subscription fees,” Vashishta said at the NRAI town hall.

Rapido has significantly simplified its pricing structure since first announcing Ownly in June 2025 , revealing a strategic shift toward pure zero-commission positioning.

Rapido’s current model presents a fundamental revenue challenge. Restaurants pay zero. Customers pay Rs. 30 for delivery. The platform charges no subscription fees, collects no advertising revenue, and takes no commission on orders.

MediaNama’s June 2025 investigation into restaurant experiences documented widespread frustration with opaque fee structures and forced spending.

Rapido’s ability to differentiate itself will depend on whether it can address the trust deficit that existing platforms have created.
