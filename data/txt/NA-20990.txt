I've been seeing lots of posts lately criticizing life in India - from civic sense to infrastructure. While India definitely has its issues, having lived in the US, Canada, and Germany (and visited several other countries), I want to share a reality check about life abroad vs the often romanticized version people post about.

**Safety &amp; Crime:**

* York University (Toronto) regularly sends out security alerts about sexual harassment and armed robbery on campus. I have been a student there and for the first time in my life was worried about my safety. Don't believe me? Check r/yorku. Imagine if this happened at Delhi University - there would be nationwide protests!
* NYC isn't the safe haven people think it is. I once had a group of kind strangers insist on walking me home because the area was too dangerous to walk alone at night. The crime rates in many US cities are shocking compared to what people assume.

**Taxes &amp; Public Services:**

* The high taxes in these countries often don't translate to the amazing services people imagine:
* Healthcare can be a nightmare. Getting a doctor's appointment for non-emergencies takes 6-8 weeks in many places. A friend in the UK had to fly back to India for a root canal because NHS wait times were 6 months and they wouldn't even prescribe proper pain medication.
* Public schools in the US are highly variable - unless you live in an expensive area, quality isn't guaranteed. And let's not forget the terrifying reality of school shootings. Meanwhile, in India, while getting into KVs is tough and government schools need work, private schools are actually quite affordable compared to their Western counterparts.

**Infrastructure &amp; Bureaucracy:**

* Germany, despite its reputation, is surprisingly bureaucratic. Want to open a bank account? Prepare for a 2-week paper chase with multiple in-person visits and endless letters for different passwords and codes.
* Everything requires appointments - doctors, banks, government offices. And you'll still wait for hours.
* Address changes must be reported in person. Think Aadhaar is invasive? Try German registration laws.
* German trains are notorious for not being on time.
* Their banking system is shockingly behind India's. Bank transfers take 1-3 days and instantaneous transfers cost money, while we have free, instant UPI and IMPS. 
* A lot of their businesses only accept cash. No digital revolution like UPI exists in the west. 

**Daily Life Reality Checks:**

* Work-life balance for high achievers is a myth everywhere. If you're ambitious, you'll work long hours whether you're in Bangalore or Berlin.
* Racism exists everywhere. You'll occasionally be reminded you're a "brown immigrant" no matter your achievements.
* People complain about Indians' civic sense, but you should see the mess after a football match in Germany or a typical weekend in any major Western city - beer cans and trash everywhere.
* They're better at hiding poverty, but it exists. They actively design urban spaces to be hostile to homeless people (like putting spikes on benches) rather than addressing the issue.
* Being vegetarian abroad is tough unless you cook. Outside food is expensive, bland, and often relies on meat substitutes that taste nothing like proper vegetarian cuisine.

The point isn't that India is perfect or that these countries are terrible - every place has its pros and cons. But the "everything is amazing abroad" narrative needs a reality check.
