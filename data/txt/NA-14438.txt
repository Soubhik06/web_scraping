The act penalises

For offences involving adults, the act has a fine in place and a prison sentence of not more than two years. For offences related to minors, the prison sentence can extend up to three years. The Take It Down Act exempts disclosures of intimate imagery to law enforcement as part of a legal filing to seek support regarding the receipt of unsolicited intimate imagery and disclosures intended to assist the person depicted. It also exempts disclosures made as part of medical education or treatment or for a legitimate medical, scientific, or educational purpose.

Besides publication, threatening someone with disclosure of intimate imagery (be it real or deepfakes) for the purpose of intimidation, coercion, extortion, or to create mental distress is also punishable under the Act. For threatening an adult, a person can be penalised with a fine and a prison sentence of up to 18 months. In case of minors, the prison sentence can extend up to 30 months.

Within one year of the act coming into effect, platforms covered under it have to create a system by which an affected person (or an authorised person acting on their behalf) can notify the platform and get the content removed.

Such a notification must include a signature of the affected person, contact information for the affected person, an identification of, and information reasonably sufficient for the platform to locate the intimate imagery.Â  It should also include a brief statement that reflects that the affected person did not consent to the publication of the intimate imagery and relevant information that the platform can use to determine that the publication was non-consensual.Â

The act specifies that a platform won’t be held legally responsible if it removes or blocks access to content that appears to be non-consensual intimate images, as long as the platform acted in good faith. This protection applies even if it turns out later that the content wasn’t actually illegal.

In India, the government previously sent advisories to platforms to remove non-consensual deepfakes under the Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act) and its rules.
