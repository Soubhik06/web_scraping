I keep coming across posts, here and elsewhere, about people complaining w.r.t high tax rates and not getting the worth of what they have paid in form of infrastructure from the government. Since, I have worked in taxation, I'm here to give my 1.614 rupees (equals to 2 cents I guess)

Fist of all, let's get this straight! Your **CTC ≠ Taxable Income**. Your **CTC will always be &gt; In hand Salary &gt; Taxable Income.**

If your CTC is 10LPA (for the sake of simplicity let's assume it is just Basic Salary+ PF and nothing else), then your taxable income will be ₹8.4 lakhs. And total taxes on that will be ₹84k. That is **8.4%** of your **CTC** effectively, even though the taxable income falls in the 20% slab.

Now say, your **taxable income** (i.e. after considering your eligible deductions &amp; exemptions) is 15 lakhs and hence you under the 30% slab rate. The total tax you will end up paying is **₹2,73,000**. Which is effectively **18.20%** of your **Taxable Income**. And even **a lesser percentage** when compared to your **CTC.**

And if you opt for the alternate regime under section 115BAC (i.e. scam regime) introduced by Nimmu Aunty; for the same taxable income of 15L, you will pay total taxes of ₹1,95,000. Which is effectively **13.00%** if your **taxable income**.

I have prepared Permissive Tax Planning for clients whose CTC range from 20 to 60 LPA. Trust me not every one of them pays 30% of, neither their CTC nor Taxable Income. It ranges from 13-22% of CTC max and 25-30% if CTC is above 52 LPA; with all the benefit of Home Loans, HRA, Travel Allowances etc. And no, this isn't some Abdul Kalam's Rocket Science, it is Nimmu Aunty's Taxation. Fairly simple.

To get taxed at an effective rate of 30%, one has to have **at least ₹51.85 lakhs** **of** **taxable income**. Not CTC. Remember **CTC is always &gt; taxable income**? So CTC would be much higher than ₹51.85 LPA. Yes, you read it right!

I'm not taking sides. I just wanted to point out a lot of you are misinformed and exaggerate the rates and taxes.

In a country where earning even ₹50,000 p. m will probably put you in top 10%, expecting everything to work seamlessly is far fetched. Just because, few people and colleagues around you are earning 15, 20 LPA doesn't mean, it is the average income of the country.

I have had clients who tell taxing at 30% is too much for income above 10 lakhs, everybody earns 10 lakhs nowadays, income tax should be abolished, etc. etc. Trust me, being a student of Taxation I wish to God they abolish it, solely based on how fucking vast and complicated the law is. My exams would be way easier.

To those who still say &amp; believe so, here's my rebuttal. Let's look at some data.

A simple Google search and reports from reliable sources tell you, **Direct Tax to GDP ratio is 6.1%** (2021-22). In 200-21, **Personal Income Tax contributed to 28.3%** **of the total revenue**. That is HUGE!

Infrastructure is definitely not up to the mark I do know that for a fact. And sadly, building roads and streetlights, won't get the **majority** out of the poverty **directly**; by that I mean into the tax brackets. It is a totally different game and much more complicated factors are involved.

I know, people don't find it worth to pay so much taxes, and again, I'm not here to take sides. I'm neither telling rich brats don't want to pay taxes, they just want everything for free. Nor I'm saying why pay taxes at all?

All I'm saying is that, have a bit of patience. Not a bit. A lot of patience actually. It is a sad fact that a handful of you are rich in a poor country. The infrastructure, the services, the quality of such, the efficiency and effectiveness won't change because this year you ended up paying higher taxes when compared to last year while ignoring the salary hike you got. It will get better and better as we approach closer &amp; closer to 100% fruitful education (Not literacy. My grandfather could read but couldn't earn for shit) and towards a very insignificant percentage of poverty.
