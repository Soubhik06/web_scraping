Meesho is set to become the first horizontal e-commerce platform to go public in India, ahead of rivals Flipkart and Amazon. The company has filed its updated red herring prospectus (RHP) with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), seeking to raise Rs 5,421 crore through an initial public offering (IPO).

According to the updated prospectus, the IPO will comprise a fresh issue of equity shares aggregating up to Rs 4,250 crore, and an offer for sale (OFS) component of up to 10.55 crore shares, estimated to be valued at Rs 1,171 crore at the upper price limit. Notably, Meesho has reduced the OFS size from 17.57 crore shares earlier.

The company has fixed its price band at Rs 105–111 per share, valuing it at roughly Rs 50,096 crore ($5.6 billion) at the upper price band.

Elevation Capital, Peak XV Partners, and Y Combinator, collectively holding over 26% stake in Meesho, are set to earn stellar returns from the listing.

Elevation Capital owns 57.94 crore shares, equivalent to a 13.6% stake. These shares were acquired at an average cost of Rs 3.04 per share. At the upper end of the price band, its holding is valued at Rs 6,431 crore, compared with Rs 176 crore before the price announcement, translating into a gain of more than 3,550%.

Similarly, Peak XV Partners owns 48.12 crore shares, amounting to an 11.3% stake in the company, acquired at a weighted average cost of Rs 4.29 per share. This stake is now valued at Rs 5,341 crore, up from Rs 206 crore previously, marking a jump of more than 2,490%.

Y Combinator, which holds 5.2 crore shares, is also set for major gains. Its holding—acquired at an average cost of Rs 1.02 per share, is now valued at Rs 577 crore, translating into returns of over 11,440%.

The OFS will also see promoters Vidit Aatrey and Sanjeev Kumar offload some of their shares. The co-founders owned a combined 18.5% stake in the company. At the upper price limit, their combined stake is now valued at Rs 8,748 crore. They had acquired these shares for a little under Rs 3.5 crore.

Meesho reported a narrowing of net loss in the first six months ended September 30, 2025 (H1 FY26) to Rs 700.7 crore, down 72% from Rs 2,512.9 crore posted in the year-ago period.

The improvement in the bottom line came on the back of strong growth in its top line. Operating revenue surged 29% to Rs 5,577.5 crore during the first half of the ongoing fiscal, compared to Rs 4,311.3 crore in H1 FY25.

Net merchandise value jumped over 44% to Rs 19,194 crore during the period under review, from Rs 13,318 crore a year earlier.

In its RHP, the company acknowledged that it has been loss-making since its inception in 2015. During H1 FY26, it also reported negative cash flows.

If we are unable to generate adequate revenue and manage our cash flows and expenses, we may continue to incur losses…Our future growth and profitability depend on increasing our consumer base, order frequency and consumers continuing to have access to products at low cost, said Meesho.

For the six months ended September 2025, annual transacting users stood at 234.2 million, compared to 175.1 million a year earlier, an increase of 34%. During the same period, users placed over 1,261 million orders on the platform, up from 825 million orders in H1 FY25.

Whether Meesho’s affordability pitch holds completely true remains debatable, but average order value on the platform fell to Rs 265.5 in H1 FY26, from Rs 274.18 in the year-ago period. This indicates that users are not buying high-value electronics, premium clothing, or branded products in large quantities. Instead, they are largely purchasing low-cost items that fit tight budgets.

Of the total IPO proceeds, Meesho plans to deploy Rs 1,390 crore towards investments in its subsidiary, Meesho Technologies Private Limited, for cloud infrastructure. The fund allocation will occur in phases, with Rs 610 crore expected to be deployed in FY27 and FY28 each, and another Rs 170 crore in FY29.

Additionally, the company has earmarked Rs 480 crore for payment of salaries for existing and new recruits in machine learning, artificial intelligence, and technology teams.

Further, Meesho intends to utilise Rs 1,020 crore towards marketing and brand initiatives. While the company said it plans to allocate some capital to drive inorganic growth through acquisitions and other strategic initiatives, it did not mention the exact amount.

Meesho’s unique proposition lies in its zero-commission marketplace model, which allows sellers to offer low-cost products while the company monetises through advertising, logistics, and data tools. However, any failure to retain or expand its seller base could adversely affect its business, financial condition, and cash flows.

The seller base may decline for several reasons, including the company’s inability to provide a low-cost platform for sellers, delisting of sellers, or removal of products due to quality issues.

For instance, Meesho delisted over 2 lakh products from its platform in the September–November quarter of 2023 due to poor quality.

For the six months ended September 2025, 72% of orders on Meesho were paid for using cash on delivery (CoD). This figure stood at 77% in FY25 and 85% in FY24. CoD remains a preferred mode of payment because a large portion of Meesho’s customer base is “value-conscious,” with many users from Tier-II+ cities who are less familiar with digital payments.

Heavy dependence on CoD exposes Meesho to operational and financial risks.

“Additionally, CoD requires physical cash handling by our last mile delivery partners, including those operating through Valmo. As Valmo partners are typically smaller and more fragmented players, the risk of delayed or incomplete cash collection increases,” The company said.

CoD orders also increase the risk of unsuccessful deliveries when consumers refuse to accept products, leading to higher logistics costs and operational inefficiencies.

Meesho faces fierce competition from other horizontal marketplaces with deep pockets, such as Flipkart and Amazon , as well as fast-growing quick commerce players like Blinkit, Instamart, and Zepto, which leverage dark stores to offer 10-minute deliveries.

Failure to differentiate itself in a cost-effective and scalable manner could hinder Meesho’s growth and operational performance.

Meesho’s financial services arm, Meesho Payments Private Limited, and its grocery business, Meesho Grocery Private Limited, reported net losses of Rs 13.5 crore and Rs 29.4 crore, respectively, in H1 FY26. These units are yet to turn profitable.

If they continue to incur losses in the future, Meesho may need to lend financial aid to these entities, which could take a toll on its cash flows, operations and financial condition.

Meesho relies on third-party payment gateway providers to process prepaid transactions. Any disruption, especially during peak periods such as festivals or sales, could cause transaction failures, reduced order conversions, and a poor consumer experience.

For example, in May 2025, Meesho experienced disruptions in payment processing due to third-party infrastructure issues, leading to payment page load failures and affecting prepaid order processing. Prolonged or frequent disruptions may erode user trust.

Orders placed on Meesho are fulfilled either through Valmo, its proprietary logistics platform, or third-party logistics partners. Any disruption in this multi-stage network could lead to delays, cancellations, or increased return requests.

While logistics partners are liable for damages during transit, there is no guarantee of full compensation, which could hurt Meesho’s reputation.

According to the RHP, Meesho is entangled in several ongoing legal proceedings involving tax, contractual, and criminal cases. These include an income tax demand of Rs 572.1 crore and multiple vendor and arbitration claims. Any adverse judgment could affect the company’s financial position and reputation.

Meesho launched its content commerce business in FY24 and introduced a dedicated content commerce platform, Creator Club , in February 2025. The platform enables creators to collaborate directly with sellers and monetise their content.

In the 12 months ended September 2025, active creators stood at 50,319. The company relies heavily on creators to post videos on third-party social media platforms to bring in new users and revenue.

However, the content commerce space in India is still in its infancy, and Meesho’s limited operational history increases uncertainty. Additionally, termination of creator contracts or intellectual property violations by creators could materially impact operations.

Earlier in November, an RTI filed by MediaNama revealed that 25 platforms, including Flipkart, Amazon, ixigo, Tata 1mg, Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto, Cleartrip, and BigBasket, submitted their mandatory self-declarations to the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA). Notably, the list did not include Meesho.

Following our reportage, however, the Ministry of Consumer Affairs released a list of 26 e-commerce platforms, including Meesho, that confirmed their compliance with the Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023.
