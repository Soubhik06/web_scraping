BRIEF STATEMENT OF THE REASONS FOR THE
 DECISION 1. The complainant has instituted the present complaint under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
 alleging that the accused had issued the cheque in question
 towards discharge of his legal liability, which on
 presentation was dishonoured, and despite service of legal
 demand notice, the accused failed to make the payment. 2. The complainant's case is that the accused had earlier
 entered into a settlement with him in relation to two prior
 cases for an amount of Rs. 4,00,000/-. A series of
 Ikrarnama (Ex.CW1/7, Ex.CW1/8 and Ex.CW1/9) were
 also executed in this regard by accused. In pursuance of this
 settlement, the accused issued the present cheque bearing
 number 029081 dated 11.11.2019 for a sum of Rs.
 4,00,000/- drawn on Union Bank of India, Mal Road, New
 Delhi which on presentation was dishonoured vide return
 memo dated 16.11.2019. Upon failure of the accused to
 make the payment despite service of legal notice dated
 10.12.2019, the present complaint was filed. 3. The complainant led pre-summoning evidence by way of
 affidavit (Ex.CW1/A) and relied upon documents i.e.: Copy of Notice for cheating and fraud served to accused
 dated 10.10.2019 alongwith post office record of service
 (Ex.CW1/10); which were duly considered and the accused
 was summoned vide order dated 23.11.2021 for offence u/s.
 138 NI Act. 4. After the accused entered appearance, he was admitted to
 bail and notice was framed against him on 31.03.2022.
 Accused stated in his notice framed u/s 251 Cr.PC that " The
 cheque in question does not bear my signature and same has
 not entirely filled by me. I have taken loan amount of Rs.
 03 Lakhs from the complainant and I have paid him the
 amount of Rs. 2.5 Lakhs in cash in front of Sriram Mahela.
 Rs. 50,000/- was due and for the balance amount, blank
 cheque was taken without my signature, and I was made to
 sign upon blank page and documents and my thumb
 impression were also taken on blank cheque. I did not
 receive any legal notice." 5. Vide order dated 10.10.2022, the accused was permitted to
 cross-examine the complainant. 6. During the evidence, the complainant examined himself as
 CW-1 and he was duly cross examined by the Ld. counsel
 for accused. 7. The statement of accused was thereafter recorded under Sec
 313 Cr.PC on 30.07.2024 wherein the entire incriminating
 evidence was put to the accused and accused reiterated the
 defence taken already. Accused stated that " Cheque in
 question bears my signatures. I did not fill the particulars in
 cheque in question. I only owe an amount of Rs. 50,000/-
 towards complainant as I had already repaid Rs. 2.50 Lakhs.
 I did not receive legal notice. However, the address
 mentioned in legal notice is mine. I do not owe any liability
 towards the complainant. Complainant misused my
 cheque." Accused has chosen to lead defence evidence. 8. Accused has examined his father namely Dharamraj as
 DW-1 and he has examined himself as DW-2 and they were
 duly cross examined by Ld. counsel for complainant.
 Thereafter, matter was fixed for final arguments. 9. Final arguments advanced by Ld counsel for the
 complainant and Ld. counsel for the accused have been
 carefully considered along with the entire evidence on
 record. 10. To prove an offence under Section 138 NI Act, it is required
 to be proved that: (i) The accused issued a cheque on an account maintained
 by him/her with a bank for payment of money to another
 from out of that account; (ii) That cheque has been issued for the discharge (either in
 whole or in part) of any debt or other liability; (iii)That cheque has been presented to the bank within a
 period of six months from the date on which it is drawn or
 within the period of its validity whichever is earlier; (iv) That cheque has been returned by the bank unpaid,
 either because of the amount of money standing to the credit
 of the account is insufficient to honour the cheque or it
 exceeds the amount arranged to be paid from that account
 by an agreement made with the bank; (v) The payee or the holder in due course of the cheque
 makes a demand for the payment of the said amount of
 money by giving a notice in writing, to the drawer of the
 cheque, within 30 days of the receipt of information by
 him/her from the bank regarding the return of the cheque as
 unpaid; and (vi) The drawer of such cheque failed to make payment of
 the said amount of money to the payee or the holder in due
 course of the cheque within 15 days of the receipt of the
 said notice. 11. In the case at hand, the accused has not disputed that the
 cheque in question have been issued on an account
 maintained by accused with a bank and hence the ingredient (i) is deemed to proved as not disputed. 12. In respect of ingredient (iii) and (iv), the complainant has
 testified that the cheque in question ie Ex.CW1/1 dated
 11.11.2019 was returned dishonoured on 16.11.2019 During
 the cross-examination of complainant, no questions were
 put to the complainant nor any suggestions were given to
 the complainant as to the cheque not having been presented
 to the bank within the period of its validity. Hence the
 ingredient (iii) ie the factum of the cheque in question
 having been presented during the period of its validity is
 deemed to be proved as not disputed. 13. Further, with the factum of dishonour of the cheque in
 question being not disputed by the accused and rather as
 having been admitted by accused in statement under Sec
 313 Cr.PC , the ingredient (iv) is also deemed to be admitted
 as not disputed. 14. In respect of the legal notice, the complainant has testified
 that upon dishonour of cheque in question, complainant sent
 notice dated 10.12.2019 (Ex.CW1/3) to the accused for
 return of the cheque amount. Accused has denied the
 receiving of legal notice. The Ld. counsel for the accused
 has sought to challenge the service of legal notice on the
 ground that the PIN code on the notice was either not
 mentioned or was different. However, during his statement
 under Section 313 CrPC, the accused has clearly admitted
 that the address mentioned on the legal notice was his
 correct address. Once such admission has been made, a
 mere discrepancy in the PIN code cannot render the service Case No. 1297/2020 Arun Bansal Vs. Sanjay Page No. 6 of 13 DIVYA Digitally signed by DIVYA

 invalid. It is settled law that when a notice is sent by
 registered post at the correct address of the drawer of the
 cheque, a presumption of due service arises in terms of Section 27 of the General Clauses Act and Section 114 of
 the Indian Evidence Act. In the present case, the accused
 has not been able to rebut this presumption. Accordingly,
 the objection raised by the defence with respect to the PIN
 code is devoid of merit. Hence, this ingredient stands
 proved through admission of fact. 15. In respect of ingredient (vi), it is pertinent to note that
 admittedly the accused has not made any payment to the
 complainant in respect of the cheque in question till date.
 Hence even the ingredient (vi) stands proved. DEBT/LIABILITY 16. It is a well settled position of law that once execution of the
 negotiable instrument is admitted, the presumption under Section 118(a) NI Act would arise that it is supported by a
 consideration. However, such presumption is rebuttable and
 the accused can prove the non-existence of consideration by
 raising a probable defence. If the accused is proved to have
 discharged the initial onus of proof showing that the
 existence of consideration was improbable or doubtful or
 the same was illegal, the onus would shift to the
 complainant who will be obliged to prove it as a matter of
 fact and upon its failure to prove would dis-entitle him to
 the grant of relief on the basis of the negotiable instrument.

 presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that the holder of a
 cheque received the cheque, of the nature referred to in Section 138 for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any
 debt or other liability. Thus, Section 139 of the Act is an
 example of a reverse onus clause that has been included in

 credibility of negotiable instruments. It is a settled position
 that when an accused has to rebut the presumption under Section 139 , the standard of proof for doing so is that of

 able to raise a probable defence which creates doubts about

 1020 of 2010 decided by the Hon'ble Supreme Court)". 18. The accused, during the course of trial, admitted to having
 signed the cheque in question. However, with respect to the
 filling of particulars on the cheque, the accused has taken
 contradictory stands. In his statement under Section 251 CrPC, the accused admitted to having filled in the
 particulars except the name of the payee. However, in his
 statement under Section 313 CrPC, he denied having filled
 in any particulars and claimed the cheque was misused. 19. The accused has taken the defence that he had taken only a
 sum of Rs. 3,00,000/- as loan from the complainant in the
 year 2017. He claims to have repaid Rs. 2,50,000/- and that
 only Rs. 50,000/- remained due. It is his case that he had
 issued a blank cheque at the time of taking the loan and that
 the said cheque has been misused by the complainant. The
 accused has also alleged that the complainant has falsely
 stated that the settlement amount of earlier cases was Rs.
 4,00,000/-. He has further claimed that the cheque in
 question does not bear his signatures. In support of his
 defence, the accused examined his father as DW1, who
 reiterated the version put forth by the accused. 20. The complainant examined himself and reiterated his
 version. He has stood firm in his testimony regarding the
 issuance of the cheque pursuant to the settlement and its
 dishonour. In his cross-examination, the complainant did
 state that the Ikrarnama dated 08.10.2018 was executed on a
 blank paper. However, the mere fact that the Ikrarnama was
 executed on a blank paper does not by itself negate the
 liability arising from the cheque in question. The accused
 has admitted that earlier two cases filed by the complainant
 against him were settled and disposed of by the learned
 predecessor court. This admission strengthens the
 complainant's version that there was indeed a settlement
 between the parties. 21. The issuance of the cheque by the accused is admitted. The
 presumption under Section 139 of the NI Act, therefore,
 operates in favour of the complainant, that the cheque was
 issued in discharge of a legally enforceable debt or liability.
 It was incumbent upon the accused to rebut this
 presumption by leading cogent and reliable evidence. The
 accused has failed to do so. His defence is not only
 inconsistent but also unsubstantiated by any documentary or
 oral evidence. The contradictions in the different stages of
 trial dilute the credibility of the accused's case. Even the
 alleged intermediary, Purushottam, who is stated to have
 expired, has not been shown to have any documented link to
 the transaction. No complaint has been lodged against the
 complainant alleging any coercion or fraud. 22. The complainant has placed on record three documents,
 namely Ikrarnama dated 10.08.2017 exhibited as
 Ex.CW1/7, Ikrarnama dated 06.09.2017 exhibited as
 Ex.CW1/8, and Ikrarnama dated 08.10.2018 exhibited as
 Ex.CW1/9. During cross-examination, the complainant
 admitted that the Ikrarnama dated 08.10.2018 was executed
 on a blank paper. However, such an admission by itself is
 not sufficient to rebut the statutory presumption under Sections 118 and 139 of the NI Act. The presumption of
 liability arising from the issuance of the cheque continues to
 operate unless the accused is able to show a credible
 defence. 23. It is pertinent to note that the accused has not offered any
 explanation in regard to the other two Ikrarnamas, i.e.
 Ex.CW1/7 and Ex.CW1/8. There is no plea that these
 documents do not bear his signatures or that they were
 executed under any coercion or undue influence. The
 silence of the accused on these documents further weakens
 his defence. Mere assertion that one of the Ikrarnamas was
 on a blank paper does not dislodge the presumption,
 especially when no cogent explanation has been given with
 respect to the other two Ikrarnamas. 24. As regards the defence of the accused, it is noticeable that
 except for a bare assertion, no evidence has been brought on
 record to substantiate the plea that he had already repaid Case No. 1297/2020 Arun Bansal Vs. Sanjay Page No. 11 of 13 DIVYA Digitally signed by DIVYA
