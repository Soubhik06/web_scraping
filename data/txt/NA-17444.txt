Jokes aside, it's important to stay vigilant. These scammers can be incredibly convincing, often armed with personal information that makes them seem legitimate. They might even send fake photos of arrest warrants with your name on them. If you ever receive such calls from supposed police officers, don't hesitate to hang up. Remember, if the police were really going to arrest you, they wouldn't give you a warning call. Stay safe!

For context, let’s call myself Felicia and my mom Betty.

The scammers tried every trick in the book—courier scams, fake electricity bills, pretending to be the police—none of which worked on Felicia.

Desperate, they started calling Betty. Unfortunately for them, Felicia picked up the call. They began by asking if they were speaking to Betty, and Felicia, ever the actress, confirmed, ‘Yes, this is she.’

Then they asked if Felicia was Betty’s daughter. After confirming the relationship, the scammers introduced themselves as high-ranking officials from the Mumbai police.

Betty (aka Felicia) corrected them, ‘Actually, Felicia is my sister, not my daughter.’ The scammers fell for it, hook, line, and sinker.

They inquired if Felicia was away working or studying. Betty nonchalantly replied, ‘Oh, she ran away with someone and got married.’

The scammers then dramatically declared, ‘Do you know Felicia has committed a serious offense?’

Betty calmly replied, ‘Yes, I’m aware. She sold her husband’s property and then divorced him.’

At this point, the scammers, frustrated by the lack of gullibility, cussed and hung up.
