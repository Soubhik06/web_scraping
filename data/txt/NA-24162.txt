--- POST TEXT ---

Just two days ago, I was driving on my bike back to home on ORR, when a stranger uncle beside me flagged me down. He pointed out that my rear tyre seemed low on air pressure. A little concerned, I decided to stop by the nearby tyre repair shop, which was just ahead as mentioned by this stout uncle who looked to be in his 50's.

Once there, I met the shopkeeper, a burly guy who after checking the tyre mentioned that there might be a puncture and asked me to pull my bike closer for a better inspection. Following his instructions, I shifted my bike into neutral as he requested, making it easier for him to work. While I was doing this, the uncle had me nicely engaged and wrapped up in a conversation with him - he had fully captivated my attention and I got focused on thinking and answering his questions - he was very curious to know about me (where I am from, what I am doing here, how old I am and got other personal details from me during the chat nicely)

As the shopkeeper inspected the tyre, he began spraying what I assumed was water to check for leaks. To my shock, he declared that he had found a staggering eleven punctures! He quoted a price of ₹1,650, stating that each puncture repair would cost ₹150. This was really strange to me, especially since I hadn’t felt any issues while riding. I voiced my concerns, but he insisted that these were micro-punctures that could lead to a blowout at any moment. I hesitated but then thought about the consequences of ignoring it, and agreed to doing the repairs. This fat uncle who flagged me down earlier grinned looking at me and told me I am a smart kid and made the right choice and took his bike and left saying he has work to attend to. Post which the shopkeeper suggested adding a sealant to both tyres for extra protection. I agreed to this as I was already spending so much and was pretty exhausted at that point. After finishing the repairs, I ended up paying ₹2,350 in total to him.

After a few kilometres down the road, I was coming back to my rational thoughts and decided to stop at another shop to get a second opinion. I asked the uncle there to check for punctures, and he quickly confirmed that my tyres were completely fine—no punctures whatsoever. When I recounted the earlier incident and told him what happened, the uncle stopped and looked at me with a grin, and said something in Kannada - I just blinked at him, confused. He laughed again, “You’ve been made a nice fool!”

“What do you mean?” I asked, feeling a bit defensive. He explained saying the guy who flagged me down gets a cut for bringing customers here And that shopkeeper? He probably made those ‘punctures’ while I was nicely distracted into shifting gears and talking to the uncle. There are no punctures at all!”

It was a well-coordinated scam, and the uncle even accurately guessed the location where it happened. I fell for this scam and got played really well for a fool - my face just heated up in embarrassment. I left that shop feeling both foolish and frustrated...
