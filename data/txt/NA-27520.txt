--- POST TEXT ---

For at least 7 or 8 years, I seem to get an extremely high number of friend requests from random strangers on Facebook. Most are really new, have few friends, and post pictures where they're standing next to luxury cars, or they've got kids with them. TinEye or Google Image Search works for identifying these photos about 30-50% of the time. 

&amp;#x200B;

The stupid burns, because many times, they'll pretend to be military but the FB name doesn't match the patch on the uniform, the rank doesn't match what the person says (or it's from the wrong country), and the don't understand American names (Paolilli Bill or Roberts William, for instance). I'm a grammar and linguistics nerd, and I'm game for poking holes in stories, so I engage in messaging. I like tripping them up in their stupidity to the point that they tell me that God will punish me. Hmmm, and God won't punish you for trying to scam people out of their money? 

&amp;#x200B;

It's even better when I've poked so many holes in their stories that they block ME, like tonight, when the guy insisted he was on NATO's US Peacekeeping force in Syria and I kept asking him how he's in Syria with NATO when they don't list any missions in Syria. (I don't share where they DO list missions!) 

Most of the scammers claim they're Americans, either in the military, they are on an oil rig, or they are working in the middle east as a peacekeeper. This is a typical exchange when I open with this question. 

\-------------------------------------------

**Me**: Hi. Your name isn't familiar and I don't recognize your picture. How do we know each other? 

Scammer responds with some variation of this (C&amp;P from a recent one): 

**Scammer**: OK, I add you as my friend I want you to be my friend. 
Am from US based at California

We're see you from

We're are you from

&amp;#x200B;

**Me**: You didn't answer my question. How do we know each other? Why did you send me a friend request? 

**Scammer**: What question friend

&amp;#x200B;

**Me**: How do we know each other? Why did you send me a friend request?

FYI, no American says 'Based AT', so lie number 1. Full disclosure, I work in linguistics and patterns of speech are part of my job 

\[Stretch of the truth. As part of my job, I edit content, which sometimes requires 'fixing' the writing of non-native speakers\].

\----------------------------------------------

Scammer continues on with some BS about how they were looking at profiles and were struck by my beauty and had to contact me. Yeah right, 50 year old woman, overweight, average looks. Their conversation uses wrong syntax, the noun verbs tenses do not agree, they overuse 'dear' in the conversation, and they engage in excess flattery. Lather, rinse, repeat. 

&amp;#x200B;

Obviously, I am the target age and gender for romance scammers. As a result, I feel really bad for women who are so lonely that this obvious bullshit suckers them in and before long, they're sending money to these people. My FB account is open to friend requests because I volunteer on a committee that puts on an annual event. It is best for me to leave my account open to any friend requests for our prospective event participants.

&amp;#x200B;

I tend to engage with most of them and find the glaring speech errors and/or holes in their stories. Each one is reported as a fake account. Fortunately, Facebook is getting better about these. In the past, the response to reports stated they didn't see anything wrong with the account. I'd give them my unhappy feedback (the frowning emoji), THEN the second look would uncover what I'd seen, a profile with pictures from somewhere in Africa from 6 or 7 years ago, and new pictures in the past week of some European guy of a different race and age. 

As the conversation progresses, if I say they don't sound like an American, the excuse is that they lived with their mother outside the US. Last week, the idiot listed he'd earned a Master's degree from a school that only teaches K-6 grades. I asked him about the master's and he went on and on about it and the school, then boom, I shared a screencap from the school's website, saying they'd proudly taught K-6 grades for the past sixty years. A recent one listed the university I attend. He didn't realize that I attended this school because he just listed the initials, while I had the name spelled out. Rookie mistake. I didn't point this out, I just asked if he commuted or lived on his school's campus. I wouldn't allow him to evade the question, then dropped on him that I currently attend the school. He blocked me. 

&amp;#x200B;

Of my friends, most get a sporadic friend request of this nature. Meanwhile, I can go months without any, then get 5+ requests a week for months on end. Everyone is convinced that I'm on some list in Nigeria or India and they're all trying to get me to crack and accept one of them. Not happening. 

Yesterday, my husband and I were waiting for a concert to begin and it looked like I'd gotten another friend request (wasn't wearing my glasses). We decided that after the show, we'd send a video message of the two of us and see how the scammer reacted. There wasn't a request, just a message from a non friend from a group I'm in that came up strange. Still think we're going to shoot a short video of the two of us together, because it may dissuade them from constantly contacting me. 

&amp;#x200B;

Anyone else that seems to be a constant target for these? If you've been successful in getting the barrage of requests to stop without resorting to locking down friend requests to friends of friends, what did you do?
