DALAL Date: 19. The Ld. Trial Court erred in its application of legal
 principles under Order VII Rule 11(d) of the CPC by failing to
 comprehensively examine the pleadings in the plaint. Section 34 of
 the SARFAESI Act bars civil courts from entertaining suits relating
 to matters that fall within the jurisdiction of the DRT or DRAT.
 However, the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Mardia Chemicals (supra)
 carved out a limited exception, allowing civil courts to entertain suits
 where the actions of the secured creditor are alleged to be fraudulent
 or so absurd and untenable as to require no further inquiry. 20. In the present case, the Plaintiff/Appellant alleged that
 the officials of Respondent No. 1/Defendant No. 1 Bank acted in
 collusion with the principal borrower, relying on fabricated
 documents to fraudulently mortgage the suit property. These
 allegations of fraud were pleaded with sufficient material particulars
 in the plaint, including references to multiple FIRs and criminal
 investigations against the respondents/defendants. The Ld. Trial
 Court, however, rejected the plaint solely on the bar imposed by Section 34 of the SARFAESI Act, without assessing whether the
 exception under Mardia Chemicals (supra) was applicable. The trial
 court, in rejecting the plaint, has not given due weight to the nature
