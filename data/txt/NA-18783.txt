1. The Enron Scandal: In 2001, the energy company Enron was found to have committed accounting fraud, leading to a loss of billions of dollars for investors. This caused a significant drop in stock prices for Enron and other companies in the energy sector and many foreign investors pulled their money out of the company and the sector.
2. The 2008 Financial Crisis: The global financial crisis was caused in part by the widespread practice of issuing risky mortgages and then packaging them into securities and selling them to investors. When the housing market collapsed, many of these securities became worthless, causing a significant drop in the stock market .
3. The Madoff Investment Scandal: In 2008, it was revealed that Bernard L. Madoff had been running a Ponzi scheme, defrauding investors out of billions of dollars. This caused a significant drop in the stock market and loss of confidence in the financial system, many foreign investors pulled their money out of other hedge funds and investment firms in a rush to avoid similar scams.
4. The bribery scandal of Odebrecht: The Brazilian construction company Odebrecht was found to have paid bribes to officials in several countries in Latin America and Africa, which caused a loss of confidence in the company and the countries where it operates. This caused a significant drop in the stock prices for Odebrecht and other companies in the sector and many foreign investors pulled their money out of the company and the sector.

These are just a few examples and there have been many other instances where foreign investors have taken their money out of a country due to corruption or financial scandals. Such events can have a negative impact on a country's economy as they can lead to a loss of confidence in the country's ability to protect investments and can also cause a decline in the overall market.
