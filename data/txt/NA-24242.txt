Hi everyone,

I recently came across a post on the Mumbai subreddit about a scam happening on the streets. It got me thinking about a similar incident I experienced in Bangalore, and I felt it was important to share this story to warn others.

This happened pre-COVID, around six months before the nationwide lockdown. I had been living in Bangalore for two years and was out near Kengeri. On my way back, I stopped somewhere near the Mysore Sandal Factory and was walking toward my destination (I can’t recall exactly where).

A man, probably aged between 34 and 40, approached me with a heavily stuffed backpack. He said he was a laborer who urgently needed to go to his village and had a flight to catch in the evening. To raise money, he claimed he was selling his iPhone.

I wasn’t interested at first, but he persisted and offered to sell the iPhone for ₹25,000. He even showed me the phone, which looked like a brand-new iPhone. Being a tech-savvy person, I inspected it thoroughly to ensure it wasn’t stolen or a counterfeit. Convinced that it was genuine, I arranged the cash, paid him, and walked away with the phone. (I’ll explain later what happened with that phone.)

**Fast forward to two weeks ago**:

My cousin was waiting for a bus from Bangalore to Dharmasthala when he called me, panicked. He said, *“Bro, I just got scammed out of ₹50,000.”* I asked him what had happened, and his story was eerily similar to mine.

A man approached him with the same "urgent flight" story and an iPhone to sell. Since my cousin owns an iPhone 15 Pro, the scammer probably assumed he’d be willing to pay. After some negotiation, my cousin agreed to buy the phone for ₹50,000.

Here’s where the trick happens:

The scammer puts the phone into a drawstring pouch, carefully handling it in front of you multiple times—showing you the phone, putting it in, taking it out, and then finally handing it over. Everything seems normal.

However, when my cousin opened the pouch after the scammer had left, there was no phone inside—just a marble slab cut to the size and weight of an iPhone. The same thing happened to me as well—it wasn’t a phone but a marble slab cut to the size and weight of an iPhone.

**Why am I sharing this?**

I know my cousin and I both fell victim due to greed, but I don’t want anyone else to go through this experience. If someone approaches you with a similar story, please stay vigilant and walk away. These scams are well-executed, and the scammers rely on people’s trust and desperation for a "good deal."

**Stay safe, Bangalore!**
