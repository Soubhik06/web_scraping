Hey! So I haven't seen any mention of this scam that my partner fell for recently. It is a clever variation of a Pin scam.

* **WHO?** My partner
* **WHAT?** Answering a call for a free hamper from her telephone operator as a reward for loyalty.
* **WHEN?** See post below, but roughly: phone call offering a free gift (earpods or apple watch), sending an identification code, receiving "free" phone by "mistake" (with all green flags that this is a legit offer from the phone operator), sending back the phone hoping to collect the "appropriate" free gift.
* **WHERE?** On the phone, talking to multiple persons (2 women + 1 man) through different calls asking to confirm reception of the package.
* **WHY?** Reporting a scam
* **HOW?** I was suspicious from the beginning - but the fact that no personal details (other than the code) was provided to the scammers made this look legit to my partner. But I got even more suspicious when an expensive iPhone arrived instead of the selected reward -- asked partner to check her customer space on the operator's website, where we realised that her plan was upgraded to an expensive plan. We then figured out the trick. The phone was returned to the legitimate phone operator, and the subscription was cancelled - no money loss here.

\_\_\_

 
\- The scammer calls you, saying they work for your telephone provider (e.g., Vodafone, EE, etc.).

\- They will tell you that, as a loyal customer, you get rewarded with free airpods, or even an apple watch. However they need you to verify your identity using a \*legit\* pin sent by the telephone company.

\- The scammer will use your phone number and the pin you sent them to access your personal account on the phone company, they will then suscribe to a premium account that will go with a free Iphone delivered directly to your place.

\- The day you receive your Iphone (wow!), the scammer will call you to confirm that you received the free gift from Vodafone/EE/etc.; being an honest person, you will tell them they got it wrong, those are not ear pods or an apple watch, but a very expensive iPhone. They will ask you to send back the iPhone and say that the 'correct' item will be sent immediately.

\- Obviously, you will not receive any item: you just sent an iPhone you paid for by suscribing to a premium service at Vodafone/EE/etc. to the scammers.

My partner fell for it, but realised it was a scam just before sending the iPhone back. None of our friends suspected a scam because: PIN verification is legit, and the delivery of the iPhone is legit with a letter from your phone provider that states all the information and that you do not need to pay for anything. Note that my partner had several different persons (all with an indian accent) on the phone -- looks like a scam with a solid organisation behind.

I think something I find interesting about it is that... somehow your phone provider might not want to actively fight this scam (of course they will help you, etc., because they need to build trust with customers), in the end, they also win something from this scam...

Anyway, stay safe.
