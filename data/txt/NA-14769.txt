Electric mobility company Ola Electric filed its Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) on July 26, offering fresh shares of Rs. 5,500 crore and selling more than 84.94 million shares. Founder and CEO Bhavish Aggarwal will be offloading more than 37.92 million shares, while investor Indus Trust will sell more than 4.18 million shares. The IPO is scheduled to open on Friday, 2nd August.

The price range offered for the Initial Public Offering (IPO) is Rs. 72 to Rs. 76 per share, with a lot size of 195.

The company plans on using the net proceeds from the IPO in the following ways:

Distribution network : Ola Electric operates a direct-to-customer omnichannel distribution network across India. As of October 31, 2023, this network comprised 935 experience centres and 414 service centres. The company also utilizes its website as part of its distribution strategy.

Assets and liabilities : The company has more than Rs. 7,735 crore worth of assets as of March 2024, around Rs. 4,046 crore of which are made up of current assets, while over Rs. 368 crore comprise of non-current assets. Ola Electric touts more than Rs. 2,019 crore as assets but is also faced with approximately Rs. 5,716 crore worth of liabilities.

Income and Expenses : The company had total expenses of over Rs. 6,277 crore with an income of only around Rs. 5,243 crore, leading to a total loss of over Rs. 1,587 crore in the financial year ending in March 2024.

Operational Metrics : Ola Electric sold almost 330,000 units in the latest financial year, more than double the previous year’s tally of 156,000 units. Revenue from operations doubled as well, standing at just over Rs.5000 crore. However, losses increased from around Rs. 1,472 crore to over Rs.1,584 crore. The company’s EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortisation) also went down from Rs. 1,197 crore to Rs. 1,040 crore. Regardless, the company was able to capture 35% of the electric two-wheeler (E2W) market share, an increase of 14 percentage points over the previous year.

The electric battery alone makes up over 30% of the Bill of Materials for an Ola S1 Scooter, which makes it a crucial risk factor for the company. The company states that any disruptions in the supply chain or an increase in prices of raw materials could result in an increase in the price of their products, affecting their manufacturing and delivery timelines.

While Ola Electric does plan on manufacturing cells at its ‘GigaFactory,’ it will still import raw materials from Chinese suppliers. Thus, any geopolitical tensions or increase in tariffs could easily lead to supply chain disruptions or price increases.

Ola Electric does manufacture some EV components in-house while sourcing others from both domestic and international suppliers. The company imports battery cells from two foreign manufacturers and obtains plastic, electronic, and metal parts from various suppliers in India and abroad. In fiscal years 2024, 2023, and 2022, imported supplies made up 37.03%, 31.11%, and 29.76% of Ola Electric’s total material costs, respectively. Domestic supplies accounted for the remaining 62.97%, 68.89%, and 70.24% during those same periods. This data shows a gradual increase in the company’s reliance on imported components over the three-year period. Furthermore, the cost of materials accounted for almost 70% of the company’s total expenses in the latest financial year.

The cost of the materials, alongside the international supply chains, means that any disruption (for example, pandemics, lockdowns, geopolitical tensions) could impede the manufacturing process or raise the price of the final product.

The company also acknowledges its reliance on government incentives like FAME Phase II (Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of [Hybrid &] Electric Vehicles), which offers a series of incentives to buyers and producers of electric vehicles, subsidies from the government of Tamil Nadu and goods and services tax reimbursements. It states that any change in these policies could easily impact the demand for their products.

Ola Electric recognizes that the demand for its EVs is partly dependent on the availability of public charging infrastructure. According to a Redseer Report commissioned by Ola, charger gun locations in India are significantly less common than traditional fuel pumps. This scarcity presents challenges for EV owners, particularly during long-distance travel, as they may struggle to find convenient charging locations on the road.

Ola Electric’s technology comprises two sections – the hardware and software of the EVs themselves and their MoveOS software architecture. The company claims to design most of its hardware and software components internally, including the operating system, cells, and electric powertrain. Ola Electric’s EVs are designed to receive remote updates, enhancing functionality over time.

However, the company acknowledges potential technology challenges. Its systems are vulnerable to various disruptions, including natural disasters and technical failures. While Ola Electric has implemented security measures, it recognizes the risk of unauthorized access to its networks and vehicles. The company’s data centres are also susceptible to physical and natural threats, which could lead to service interruptions.

Another possible risk is customer attempts to modify their EVs, potentially compromising safety and security systems. The company warns that unauthorized changes could lead to injuries and negative publicity.

The company acknowledges that the lithium-ion cells it manufactures may catch on fire or explode. It provides an example of an incident in Pune, Maharashtra, where one of Ola Electric’s S1 Pro scooters caught fire. The government ended up imposing a penalty of Rs. 15 Lakhs on the company.

Ola Electric anticipates significant challenges in information security and privacy, particularly concerning the collection, storage, and transmission of personal and sensitive information of its employees and customers. This includes names, account details, customer IDs, passwords, EV information, and payment-related data. While the company has not identified any confirmed losses or compromises of sensitive information, it acknowledges the possibility of undetected incidents. A data breach could disrupt Ola Electric’s systems and could lead to data leaks, liability issues, and financial repercussions. Moreover, such breaches could compromise the safety of employees and customers.

Ola Electric utilizes open-source software in developing and deploying its EVs, which exposes the company to potential legal challenges regarding ownership and compliance with open-source licensing terms. Undetected errors in open-source components could impair Ola Electric’s systems and damage its reputation. Additionally, the public availability of such software might make Ola Electric’s platform more vulnerable to cyber-attacks.

As an example, Ola Electric was recently sued by MapMyIndia for allegedly copying the latter’s proprietary product, a claim that the company denied.
