The Competition Commission of India (CCI) initiated an investigation into Google, over allegations of anti-competitive practices favoring certain companies, on November 28, 2024. WinZO, a real money gaming platform that offers pay-to-win games such as ludo, rummy, and fantasy sports, lodged the complaint.

Google manages the ‘Google Play’ platform, which allows third-party developers to make their apps available to users. WinZO alleged that hosting apps on Google Play required developers to adhere to Google’s unilateral Developer Distribution Agreement (DDA) and Developer Program Policies (DPP), which it described as unreasonable and unjust.

WinZO further asserted that Google Play’s policies restricted the hosting of gaming applications offering Real Money Games (RMGs) in India. As a result, users could only download apps featuring real money skill-based games through their respective websites via a process called sideloading. Consequently, WinZO’s app was available for download exclusively through its website. When users attempted to download the app on Android devices via the website, a disclaimer or warning appeared, which WinZO argued was specific to Google’s Android operating system. WinZO contended that this warning lacked merit, was misleading, and was entirely unlawful. It claimed the warning tarnished WinZO’s reputation in the market, amounted to active misrepresentation, and caused significant business losses.

Google argued that the Indian Constitution allows both central and state legislatures to regulate “betting and gambling,” resulting in a fragmented regulatory framework across the country.

While some states follow the central Public Gambling Act, 1867 (Gambling Act), others have their own legislation. The Gambling Act does not apply to “Games of Skill,” which the Supreme Court of India has defined as games where “success depends principally upon the superior knowledge, training, attention, experience, and adroitness of the player,” as opposed to “Games of Chance,” where “chance predominates over skill.” Therefore, the Gambling Act does not prohibit games that require more skill than chance.

Google further stated that there is no clear, objective definition of “Games of Skill” in India, and determining whether a game qualifies as such depends on its specific format, features, and rules, requiring a case-by-case assessment. Due to legal uncertainties, Google stated that it decided not to include India in the list of countries where it allows RMGs and instead launched a short-term pilot program for one year starting September 2022.

WinZO has further stated that sideloading requires users to follow several extra steps, creating an entry barrier for app developers. Users without technical knowledge are hesitant to take the risk of sideloading due to warnings about potential malware infections. WinZO claimed that this conduct by Google is illegal and misleading, damaging its market reputation and leading to a loss of business.

Google referred to a March 2023 ruling by the NCLAT (National Company Law Appellate Tribunal) in Google LLC & Anr. v. Competition Commission of India & Ors . The NCLAT determined that displaying a warning when a user sideloads an app does not constitute an anti-competitive practice, as long as sideloading is permitted. The court also ruled that such warnings are neither restrictive nor disproportionate, and are not illegal, as they comply with the Intermediary Rules.

It was also alleged that when users attempted to make payments to WinZO for skill-based games using Google Pay, Google displayed an “unwarranted” warning stating: “This person might be flagged as risky. Check again or cancel this payment.”

Additionally, users were reportedly shown warnings for payments exceeding certain thresholds arbitrarily set by Google, with messages such as: “This is an unusually high amount. Check again before sending money.” These warnings were described by WinZO as baseless and lacking justification. It was further asserted that the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) had neither mandated nor directed payment service providers to issue such warnings for transactions involving real money skill-based game merchants.

Google has stated that the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and NPCI, through their Master Directions and Circulars, have encouraged payment system participants to implement fraud prevention measures, including transaction monitoring and velocity checks. In line with this, Google Pay (GPay) issues warnings to alert users before engaging in transactions that may pose a risk. These warnings, according to Google, are not specific to WinZO or any particular merchant/consumer.

WinZO further stated that on September 7, 2022, Google revised its policies to introduce a Pilot Program for hosting only two categories of Real Money Games (RMGs) on its Play Store in India—Daily Fantasy Sports (DFS) and Rummy. This program was set to run for a year, from September 28, 2022.

This practice, WinZO argued, could create a false impression of legitimacy for DFS and Rummy over other skill-based games. It alleged that Google’s decision to limit the Pilot Program to only DFS and Rummy apps while excluding all other RMGs was arbitrary, discriminatory, and lacked any reasonable justification, amounting to an abuse of its dominant market position. Furthermore, it stated that these games, categorized as Real Money Games (RMGs), have been declared legal by the Supreme Court and various High Courts in India. It argued that hosting such games on app stores is standard industry practice, which Google unjustifiably refuses to follow.

WinZO also said that Dream11, WinZO’s closest competitor with a 90% share of the DFS market, gained an additional 1.7 Crore users within two months of the Pilot Program’s launch. It further added that Google stated that the Pilot Program was not launched based on a classification of apps as either games of skill or games of chance and did not make any such distinction.

Google stated that while expanding to new app categories is complex, Google considered ending the pilot but avoided disrupting developers and users by extending the grace period. The company emphasized that it has not discriminated between DFS and Rummy apps, and WinZO’s app is available under the pilot. Google stated that WinZO cannot pressure it to accommodate commercial preferences, as it is applying uniform, objective criteria for the RMG Pilot.

WinZO claimed that Google had begun implementing the Pilot Program through its online advertising platform, Google Ads. This platform, owned and operated by Google, allows third parties and app developers to advertise across Google’s ecosystem, including YouTube and Android OS applications.

According to WinZO, starting November 21, 2022, Google revised its advertising policy to permit only Daily Fantasy Sports (DFS) and Rummy app advertisers to host ads via Google Ads. WinZO argued that this modified advertising policy, which prevents its own advertisements from being included in Google’s Ad program, represents an abuse of Google’s dominant position. WinZO highlighted that access to users through Google Ads presents a significant business opportunity, and the restrictive policy unfairly limits this potential.

Google maintained that Google’s Ads Policy is clear and consistently applied. It stated that it allows ads for all online games as long as they (1) don’t involve winning items with real-world value and (2) comply with Google’s policies and applicable laws. For RMGs, Google permits gambling ads in line with local laws and industry standards, but restricts certain types of gambling-related advertising. In India, since 2019, Google has allowed ads for DFS and Rummy RMGs based on legal classifications of these games as skill-based. Google stated that its decision is aimed at minimizing legal risks and complying with the law, not driven by commercial interests.

Google further added that due to ongoing legal uncertainty about whether games are of skill or chance, as highlighted by Winzo’s own legal suit, Google does not decide the classification of these games. Moreover, the company said that Google’s Ads Policy has not changed since November 2022. They said that it has remained the same since February 2022, when it was extended to allow DFS and Rummy ads in Karnataka following changes in local laws.

WinZO said in its submission that it has filed a suit before the Delhi High Court seeking a declaration in its favour that the games which are hosted by the app are predominantly games of skill. Consequently, the declaration would grant WinZO the right to request that its platform and the games hosted on it be made available on the Google Play Store.

The CCI noted that over 68% of WinZO’s app installs from January to September 2022 came through Google Ads, highlighting the platform’s dominance. CCI noted that Google’s selective inclusion of DFS and Rummy apps in its pilot program disrupted competition, which is disadvantageous to other RMG apps. This preferential treatment risked entrenching the market positions of these apps and creating barriers for competitors. The Commission identified Google’s dominant position in relevant markets, including licensable OS, app stores, and online search advertising. Given the evolving regulatory framework, the Commission directed an investigation under Section 26(1) of the Act, with a report due in 60 days.
