Pine Labs is working on agentic payments as part of its AI roadmap, chief executive officer (CEO) Amrish Rau said during the company’s Q3FY26 earnings call, describing it as one of several innovations the fintech is developing but has not yet formally demonstrated. Rau said Pine Labs has integrated bill payments with a ChatGPT-equivalent system, enabling users to make payments through what he described as “agentic e-commerce” rather than traditional checkout flows. This comes after Razorpay , the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), and OpenAI piloted India’s first agentic payments system on ChatGPT in October 2025.

Later in the call, an analyst pressed the company on the implications of this approach, asking: “You launched agentic bill payment on LLMs. So how are you going to monetise this? And what is the privacy like? How much data reaches LLMs, and how much stays within your safe setup?” In response to the privacy aspect, Rau said, “I don’t have that answer for you just now.” In terms of the monetisation, the CEO described the transaction as happening completely within ChatGPT via prompts to the chatbot.

The exchange came amid broader disclosures about Pine Labs’ use of AI across its platform and internal operations. Rau said the company operates a UPI switch and an Account Aggregator, which he said helps Pine Labs “penetrate deep into the ecosystem” for identity-linked and other use cases. He added that the company continues to invest in AI-driven innovation because it sees “a lot of potential” in the space.

Beyond agentic payments, Rau said Pine Labs has already deployed AI in merchant and transaction fraud prevention. He also detailed automation initiatives on the operations side, saying the company has opened up data related to standard operating procedures, product information and support tickets and “entirely automated” those workflows using AI. Earlier in the call, Rau said Pine Labs uses AI to write “about 21% of all code”, and added that these deployments reduce the need to scale engineering headcount going forward.

Rau told analysts that the company does not replicate India’s payments infrastructure overseas. “What we are not just doing is taking Indian payment platforms and going global,” he remarked, adding that Pine Labs instead builds for each market’s local payment systems, such as Aani in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and PayNow in Singapore.

To illustrate this strategy, Rau pointed to Malaysia as the company’s first international market around 2019-2020, saying that “we work with 11 bank issuers in Malaysia”. According to Rau, the business in Malaysia has continued to grow at about 40%. He further explained that Pine Labs later entered Singapore, and is now also present across Middle Eastern markets.

During the call, Rau connected international expansion to cross-border payment use cases. “We actually enabled for Apple Pay,” he said, explaining that this allows overseas consumers to transact with Indian merchants.

From the consumer’s perspective, Rau said that payments can be completed “within seconds, and no card, no OTP [is required]”, while merchants gain access to international customers. Rau said these capabilities sit alongside a continuing stream of international agreements, including a recent deal with a card-issuing platform in Sri Lanka.

Pine Labs also showcased the Bharat Yatra Card as a consumer-facing application of its prepaid platform during the earnings call. “Bharat Yatra is a national mobility card entirely powered by Pine Labs platform for prepaid services”, Rau said. He added that consumers can buy the card through platforms such as Blinkit, Zepto, and Flipkart, as well as at Metro stations, and load money digitally.

Further, Rau explained that users can activate the Bharat Yatra card through the Bharat Yatra app, load funds using UPI, and complete transactions using the Near Field Communication (NFC) technology.

“You can just simply use the card, tap, and complete the transaction”, Rau said, adding that balances update instantaneously on Android phones. “We made sure you don’t have to stand in line, not to buy the card, not to recharge it, not while you travel”, he remarked.

Pine Labs reported higher platform activity in Q3FY26, with growth led by transaction volumes and merchant expansion. During the quarter, the company processed 1.93 billion transactions, up 23% year on year (YoY), while platform gross transaction value (GTV) reached Rs 4.5 lakh crore, reflecting a 29% annual increase. At the same time, the number of digital checkout points rose to 1.93 million, up 11% YoY, indicating continued expansion across physical and online merchant touchpoints.

Meanwhile, Pine Labs expanded its merchant base to 1.05 million merchants, representing a 14% increase from the previous year. The company also reported growth in its issuing and acquiring platform, with fintech infrastructure transactions rising to 310 million, up from 220 million during Q3FY25. In addition, prepaid cards issued during the quarter stood at 200 million, compared with 180 million a year earlier. And international operations contributed a larger share of activity, with overseas revenue accounting for 15% of total revenue, up from 11% in FY24.

Against this operational backdrop, Pine Labs reported revenue from operations of Rs 744 crore, a 24% YoY increase and their highest-ever quarterly revenue. Adjusted Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation (EBITDA) rose 59% to Rs 171 crore, while profit after tax (PAT) stood at Rs 42 crore, compared with a loss of Rs 57 crore in Q3FY25.
