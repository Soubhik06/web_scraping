 an offence of cheating against an accused the necessary ingredients are,
 firstly, dishonest inducement to deceive a person, secondly, delivery/
 alteration/making/destruction of a property or valuable security or
 something that may be converted into a valuable security by such dishonest
 inducement. Hence, dishonestly inducing for the purposes as stated above is
 a necessary component to constitute the offence of cheating under Section
 420 of the IPC. 13. The Hon'ble Supreme Court, in Vijay Kumar Ghai & Ors vs. State of
 West Bengal & Ors. , 2022 SCC OnLine SC 344, has discussed the

 is not in itself a criminal offence and gives rise to the

 distinction between mere breach of contract and

 Court as well as contents of the provision, it is evident that there is a pre-
 requisite of intention or prior knowledge for dishonestly inducing a person
 for constituting an offence under Section 420 of the IPC. This intention or
 prior knowledge is relevant from the very beginning of the alleged offence.
 The offence of cheating cannot not be made out against a person if the
 intention to deceive is not existing at the time of initiation of the alleged
 offence. The intention to deceive, dishonestly induce or cheat, hence,
 becomes irrelevant if not existing from the stage of inception and
 subsequently infringing acts are found to be done by the accused. 15. In the instant matter, there is an absence of intention of the petitioners
 herein to criminally or dishonestly induce the respondent no. 2 at any stage
 as has been seen in the conduct of the petitioners during their transaction
 with the respondent no.2. At the very first instance the petitioners had
 presented the cheque for clearing however the same was not honored or
 returned until the 11th of August, 2008, that is two days after it was Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:PRAVEEN CRL.M.C. 1008/2014 Page 8 of 10 KUMAR BABBAR Signing Date:18.05.2022 18:19:45 presented, by the respondent no.2. There was clearly no intention of malice
 by the petitioners at this stage. Moreover, when the cheque was neither
 cleared nor dishonoured and a representative of the respondent no. 2
 approached petitioner no.1, it declined to accept an over-the-counter return
 of the cheque, and subsequently, it was accepted by petitioner no.3, with an
 endorsement bearing the remarks "subject to acceptance from the party,
 account balance is not available". It is admitted that by the time a counter
 return was served to the petitioner the fund was withdrawn by the concerned
 customer on the same day. Even at this stage, no allegations could be
 established against the petitioners that they acted in furtherance of common
 intention to dishonestly induce the respondent no. 2 since it had not
 misguided, deceived or cheated respondent no. 2. 16. As per the mandate of the provision under Section 420 of the IPC as
 well as the observations of the Hon'ble Supreme Court, it is evident and
 definite that the ingredients of cheating and dishonestly inducing are
 necessary to establish, even prima facie, a case against the accused.
 however, the same is not the case in the dispute between the parties before
 this Court. The criminal complaint made has failed to make out criminal
 allegations and specific roles of the petitioners in the alleged offence under Section 420 / 34 of the IPC and only blanket allegations have been made to
 accuse and involve a bank in the transaction of private nature. 17. Keeping in view the aforesaid facts and circumstance, the contents of
 the complaint, the requisites of the provision under Section 420 of the IPC,
