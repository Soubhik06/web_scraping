DETAILS:
I’ve been talking with this supposed landlord on FurnishedFinder (FF) about renting a unit in Chicago later this year. The unit is a 1BR/1BA for $1300/mo (utilities included) in West Chicago which is kind of low. It is a ground unit though, with no in-unit washer and dryer. I do not live in Chicago, so I have been doing everything online. While I have AirBnB’d site-unseen with a verified, superhost owner before, I am new to dealing with leases from private landlords. He has two listings, each with a five star review under them. Both reviews were posted within the last few months and use wording that makes everything seem great. Landlords can remove negative reviews on FF though. His listed phone number matches with the expected county area code. I could not find anything by image-searching the pictures.

After asking him some questions through FF and me wanting to proceed (IMAGE 4), he required that I do an online background/credit check through TransUnion’s MySmartMove program. He first emailed me through a FirstinitialMiddleinitialLastname@hotmail.com email address (which matched his name on FF) and set up a background and credit check through SmartMove. SmartMove sent me an account signup and payment link through the email address: mysmartmove@mail.mysmartmove.com (IMAGE 2). Everything looked official. SmartMove is apparently used a lot for background/credit checks for rentals and has been around for a while.

From what I could see, the site was legit, so I entered my SSN, DOB, and credit card info to pay for the background check. He did not offer a refund for the ~$40 background check which is a good sign from what I’ve read on this sub. He even mentioned that I could try finding a discount code online. This served as the application fee mentioned in the listing.

On the SmartMove website, there was then some kind of error where I couldn’t continue the application and had to call 1-866-775-0961 (IMAGE 3). I mentioned it to the landlord, and he said he had never seen the error in the 10 years he had used SmartMove. He said to contact them if I had not been charged yet which I hadn’t. Everything I’ve looked up points to the number being a real number for a TransUnion center in Colorado, so I called them. The guy I talked to had an Indian accent which was kind of weird coming from Colorado, but not impossible. I told him my last four digits of SSN and DOB, and had to then email mydocs@transunion.com my drivers’ license and bank statement, with my account number visible on it. A day later, they replied to me from that same email address saying my identity was confirmed and “Your reports are now available to the landlord or are available to share with the landlord”. Again, everything looked official. The application was now complete.

Does all of this all seem legit? If not, this has been the most elaborate scam I have come across. I couldn’t find much by looking up the landlord’s name, but propertyiq.com has the property address’ Deed of Trust listed under his name since 2016. However, his other FF listing is listed on an AirBNB account with his name but with conflicting availability dates. Maybe he forgot to update the FF listing? His FF profile says he has lived in Chicago for 25 years and rented for nearly a decade. His verified, superhost AirBnB profile says he is a longtime resident and lives near the area which adds up; his listed address from both the SmartMove application and lease is near the unit I’m looking at. His AirBnB PFP indicates he is an older, plain-looking white man, but his grammar doesn’t match that of someone who has lived in the states for decades. He could be bad at typing or an immigrant, idk. He hasn’t said clear scam words like “kindly” or “dear”, hasn’t asked for any other money, and has not rushed me at all. I initially asked him if I could move in a couple days earlier, and he said he would talk to the nurse currently renting the unit to get her expected move out date. Days later, he confirmed that I should be able to move in early. This would be a lot of effort for a scam, but not impossible.
