International money transfer company, MoneyGram, confirmed on October 7 , that they suffered a massive data breach last month that resulted in hackers obtaining customers’ personal data. In an announcement, MoneyGram stated that on September 27, 2024, it determined that an “unauthorised third party accessed and acquired personal information of certain consumers between September 20 and 22, 2024.”

This information included certain consumer names, contact information (such as phone numbers, email and postal addresses), dates of birth, a limited number of Social Security numbers, copies of government-issued identification documents (such as driver’s licenses), other identification documents (such as utility bills), bank account numbers, MoneyGram Plus Rewards numbers, transaction information (such as dates and amounts of transactions) and, for a limited number of consumers, criminal investigation information (such as fraud). The types of impacted information varied by the affected individual.

Moneygram took certain systems offline and launched an investigation with external cybersecurity experts and has been coordinating with law enforcement. On September 24 , the company shared that it had halted operations and would complete pending transactions once systems were back online. Moneygram’s systems are operational now after a week of outage. Moneygram has recommended that users remain vigilant for incidents of fraud and identity theft by reviewing account statements and monitoring their free credit reports. The company serves over 50 million people in more than 200 countries and territories each year, including India.

The U.K.’s data protection regulator, the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO), has confirmed that they are investigating MoneyGram. Companies are required to report data breaches within 72 hours of discovering the incident, which MoneyGram did.

However, the company’s services are also no longer available at UK Post Offices after its contract renewal was cancelled following the data breach. The UK Post Office and MoneyGram had been in contract renewal negotiations before the cybersecurity incident. Post Office said it offered to extend the current contract for a shorter period so that it could understand the impact of the situation as MoneyGram renewed its services, but the company declined. India has had a similar International Money Transfer Service agreement with MoneyGram since 2011.

Earlier in September, the United States District Court For The Northern District Of Illinois dismissed a case against MoneyGram for having a weak anti-fraud and anti-money laundering (AML) program. In 2018, the US Department of Justice and the Federal Trade Commission extended its settlement with the company for $125 million. The money transfer firm also agreed to enhance its anti-fraud and AML compliance programs.

In 2012, MoneyGram’s corrupt agents and others perpetrated consumer fraud schemes. This subsequently led to the regulators and the remittance provider signing a deferred prosecution agreement (DPA). However, it was alleged that MoneyGram breached the DPA between April 2015 and October 2016 by processing at least $125 million in additional consumer fraud. However, in 2024, Judge Martha M. Pacold ruled that plaintiffs’ arguments were unpersuasive in proving that MoneyGram had intentionally caused consumer fraud.
