[1] Heard the learned advocates for the respective parties
by video conferencing. [2] By way of the present application filed under Section
438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, the applicant-
accused has prayed for bail in connection with the FIR being
C.R.No.11993003201647 of 2020 registered with Anjar
Police Station, District Kachchh (East), Gandhidham, for
the offences under Section 306 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860
( for short " the IPC ") and Sections 33(3) , 42(a) and 42(d) of
the Gujarat Money Lenders Act. [3] The case of the prosecution in brief is that the deceased
Pankajbhai i.e. the brother of the complainant had committed
suicide on 03.09.2020, as various accused, who are named in
the FIR had time an again harassed him for recovering the
money, which were borrowed from them. [4] Learned advocate for the applicant has submitted that
there is a delay of one (01) month and eight (08) days in
registering the FIR. It is further submitted that, in fact, the Page 1 of 7 Downloaded on : Sat Jan 15 08:34:08 IST 2022 R/CR.MA/18072/2020 ORDER DATED: 25/06/2021

applicant has not harassed the deceased and has not asked for
any type of higher interest or money and, therefore, the
allegations leveled in FIR, are far from the truth. It is
submitted that the applicant was having friendship with the
deceased and no incident of any quarrel has been taken place
between them and as the applicant and deceased were
friends, they were used to help each other. It is further
submitted that the applicant is neither doing business of
money laundering nor had given money to the deceased, on
interest base. It is submitted that even in the FIR, the
complainant has stated that the deceased has obtained loan
from Muthoot Finance Bank and IIFL finance bank against the
gold ornaments, which itself suggests that the applicant has
no connection with the loan and interest. [4.1] Learned advocate has submitted that the ingredients of Section 107 of the IPC, which define "abetment" are not
satisfied in the present case, as there is no instigation from
the applicant compelling the deceased to commit suicide. It is
submitted that the applicant is demanding his rightful money,
which was lent, however, since the complainant has taken
various loans from other people and from the bank also and
was unable to repay the same, he has committed suicide.
Thus, it is submitted that the present application may be
allowed. [4.2] Learned advocate for the applicant, on instructions, has
submitted that the applicant is ready and willing to abide by

investigating agency, the right of the applicant accused to
oppose such application on merits may be kept open. Learned
advocate, therefore, has submitted that considering the above
facts, the applicant may be granted anticipatory bail. [5] Learned APP Mr. H.K.Patel has submitted that the

Rs.2,00,000/- to the deceased, with monthly interest at the

submitted that the applicant was harassing the deceased and

monthly interest at the rate of 1.75% and the promissory note

[8] Prima facie, it appears that the ingredients of Section
306 of the IPC are not established as there is no instigation or
abetment by the present applicant as defined under Section
107 of the IPC to the complainant and it cannot be said that
the complainant had no other option but to end his life
because of such instigation. [9] Having heard the learned advocates for the parties and
perusing the material placed on record and taking into
consideration the facts of the case, nature of allegations,
gravity of offences, role attributed to the accused, without
discussing the evidence in detail, at this stage, I am inclined
to grant anticipatory bail to the applicant. [10] This Court has considered the following aspects; (a) The role attributed to the present applicant; (b) There is delay of 1 month and 8 days in registering the
FIR and no explanation is given for the same; (c) Prima facie it appears that the ingredients of Section
306 of the IPC are not established in the present case, as it
appears that there is no instigation or abetment of the
applicant; (d) The nature of allegations are such for which custodial
interrogation of the applicant at this stage is not necessary. [11] This Court has also taken into consideration the law laid
down by the Apex Court in the cases of Sushila Aggarwal vs.
State (Nct of Delhi ), AIR 2020 SC 831 and Siddharam
Satlingappa Mhetre vs State of Maharashtra , AIR 2011 Page 4 of 7 Downloaded on : Sat Jan 15 08:34:08 IST 2022 R/CR.MA/18072/2020 ORDER DATED: 25/06/2021
