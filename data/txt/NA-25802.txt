I had been hit by this scam twice, so I'm posting here to raise awareness.

 
Both times I've been approached by normally dressed guys who made some excuse or the other to make me give them some money, 50 the first time and 200 the second time. The first guy told me he needed to get some fruits for his grandma in the hospital and that he'll talk to his parents and have the money (100 rupees) sent back to me. I never get my money back. The second time around, the guy made a ruse saying he needed to get tickets for a train and that his UPI was not working. I was skeptical but eventually gave him cash after getting his number and checking that it was correct. As expected, the dude doesn't take my calls.

In both situations, I was conned because of the circumstances. Both the scammers were normally dressed and behaved casually. And in both cases, I was a bit apprehensive of heckling someone with a genuine problem.

Here's how you can avoid this scam:

* In today's age of UPI, no one would approach a stranger for money. They'll always call family/friends.
* If their UPI is not working and they ask you for cash, tell them to make a friend or family transfer money to you. Give them cash only after you receive the money. People with a genuine problem will understand.
* If their phone has no net connection, offer your hotspot. Change your password afterwards.
* If someone appears overtly friendly when asking for money, that's a yellow flag. When it comes to asking for money, people are more likely to be awkward and nervous.
