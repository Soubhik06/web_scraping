Apologies this is a long writeup. 
Not sure if this is the right sub to share, but I just needed to get this off my chest. 
 
This happened today in a village in Kannur district. I work from home in a rural area, and often- about two to three times a week, young people, mostly in their 20-25s , come to our house with backpacks. Not sure this is common across the rural area of Kerala. I think yes. 
 
A typical conversation starts with, 
"Chetta/Amma , this is part of training, After 6 months i can become assistant manager and no need walk like this anymore. Please buy something" 
 
Years ago, my parents and I used to believe their "training program" stories and, like most people, and buy something to help. But, for the past year, we have stopped entertaining this, as we now know it's a scam. The companies that send these people profit by exploiting people's sympathy seeing them carry heavy bags in the heat. In reality, these guys receive little to no benefits. 
 
Nowadays, when these people come to my house, I usually have a friendly chat with them and say: 
 
*&gt;&gt; Bag onnum open cheyynada , Traininginta bagam alle, 6 months kazhinje promotion kittum alle* 

*&gt;&gt; Ellarum ithu tanna any parayunne , innala 2 peru vannu ellarum manager ayal enthakum?*

If they seem friendly, I ask: 
*&gt;&gt; Vellam veno, enthalum kazhikan veno , veedu evide anu ?, Enthinu anu ingne nadakkunne, Why can't try another Job, what did u study etc.* 
 
I also share stories and comments I’ve read on Facebook to explain why I don’t buy anything from them. Some understand and say they plan to continue this for a few months before moving on to another job. Others, strongly believe in this and think they will eventually become Assistant Managers.

 
Today, a girl came with a backpack and rang the doorbell. I stepped out and told her, "Please don’t open the backpack. A girl came the day before yesterday, and we already know what’s inside." I then asked if she needed water or anything. 
 
She wasn’t listening and kept insisting that I call my mother because she wanted to show her what was inside and talk to her. I explained, **"She’s inside with my baby and can’t come out right now."** 
 
I told "*ithu ningalyum njaglayum pattikunna parupadi anu*" ,there are many articles in Facebook about this please read them. We stopped buying or helping after realizing the truth. So please leave."

At that point, she raised her voice and said she receives a salary every month and will be promoted to manager next month if she meets her target. I simply replied, **"Okay, good then. Good luck!"** with a smile and stepped back inside, returning to my laptop.

 
But she didn’t leave. She stood in the courtyard, yelling, **"**Call your mom"—and not in a polite way.

Since she showed no signs of leaving and kept repeating the same demand, I stepped out again and firmly said, "Please leave. Mom is busy."

She then raised her voice and said, "The only thing I’m asking is for you to call your mom! Why are you telling me to leave instead of just calling her?"

 I just lost my temper and said with angrily,

"Ithu ente veedu anu! Njan thalikkan ariyum, vilikkunnilla. Onne poye"

She then started shouting, "Enthinu anu deshyapedunnathu? Njangalkum jeevikande".. bla... bla

Frustrated, I closed the main door and said in English, "Just get out from here and get lost. I don’t want to talk to you anymore."

she refused to leave. She kept standing outside, yelling for my mother and questioning why I wasn’t speaking politely.

Finally, I have to call my mom. When she came out, she said calmly, "Mole, please go. We don’t want anything."

But the girl just retorted, "Vendangil venda ennu paranja pore, bla bla." 

My mom, still trying to be patient, said, "Njangal ellam oro karyathil busy anu. Njangal ithu vangarilla, athanu avan pookan paranjathu. Please go." 

Then, she raised her voice at my mother and said, "You go inside!"

My mother lost her temper too and said, "Ente veetil njaan engotte pookan anu? Nee irangi poodi! Ninte ee nakke vachit engane sadanam vilkunnathu?**"**

She responded , "Ente nake ningal onnum kanditilla. Enikke ithinte 10 times parayan ariyam." 

Then, my mom suddenly remembered hearing a heated argument at our neighbor’s house just minutes earlier. She asked, **"**Was that you shouting at the other house?"

The girl immediately yelled back, "Athe, njan anu! Ee nattukaar ellarum kanakka! Njangale jeevikan anuvadikkilla! Sadanam vaangilla!" 

At this point, my mother gave up and said, "Nee poye ivide ninnum"

Finally, after cursing us and the entire nattukar, she went off.

 
Later, my wife, who had been busy in a meeting, came back and said, "Ninake ammane vilichu kodutha porayirunno? Ella veetil ninnum rejection kitti kitti aa pennu maduthu kaanum. Athu thanne pattennu chodu aayathu." 
 
Hearing that, I felt bad. She was right—maybe that was the reason the girl lost her patience and her polite tone. I think It was my ego and arrogance that ruined someone’s day. 
Like I read in another sub, *we never truly know the emotions and burdens these people carry on their backs.* 

But for sure I am not going to buy anything and support the real frauds who exploit these young people.

 
I really don’t understand why people are still falling into this "training program" trap, going door-to-door believing they’ll eventually get a higher position. Why aren’t the police, the youth commission, or any relevant authorities raising awareness about this on Instagram or Facebook? 
 
I haven’t seen any media reports covering this as news. If there are any, please share the links—I’d love to have something to show the next person who comes to my door, so they can see the reality for themselves.
