Following the Supreme Court's judgement in the [Justice K.S.Puttaswamy(Retd) vs Union Of India case](https://lawlex.org/lex-bulletin/case-summary-k-s-puttaswamy-retd-v-s-union-of-india-2017/18929), according to which the right to privacy is a fundamental right and it is necessary to protect personal data as an essential facet of informational privacy, there has been somewhat of a growing awareness about privacy among the aware citizens of India. Government of India also intends to make laws regarding this. While the previously introduced [Personal Data Protection Bill, 2019](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-editorials/personal-data-protection-bill-2019) has been withdrawn this august because of some [reasons](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/withdrawal-of-personal-data-protection-bill), the Government of India is claiming to introduce a newer bill. I don't know if it would bring in any improvements or what but for now, all we can do is cross our fingers until it is introduced.

The real reason why I am writing this post today is to inform you that whatever the upcoming privacy law may be, it should not be considered an absolute solution to the growing issue of privacy on the internet. Why do I sound negative? Actually I am not, I am happy about it but as someone who has dabbled quite a bit with the internet, I know, for one, that state regulations aren't enough to save you. You have to become aware, you have to take steps to protect your privacy on the internet on your own first. You have to choose what is right and safe for your privacy depending on your own threat model. You have to decide to whom are you willing to entrust your personal data. And this is exactly what this post of mine is about, how you can safeguard your privacy on the internet.

Let's first understand what are privacy and internet privacy. As Wikipedia puts it, privacy is the ability of an individual or group to seclude themselves or information about themselves, and thereby express themselves selectively. Internet privacy involves the right or mandate of personal privacy concerning the storing, repurposing, provision to third parties, and displaying of information pertaining to oneself via Internet.

Umm ok so uhh why you should care about your privacy, particularly on the internet? Your data can be stored indefinitely and used in both beneficial and unwelcome ways. Information related to anything, even things that are seemingly innocuous, can be used to make inferences about your socioeconomic status, preferences and more. Ad and content personalization is one of the biggest cancers born out of the breach of one's privacy. There's even a popular quote that says "if something's free, you are the product", I don't know about its origin but it is correct in the context of internet privacy. The biggest argument, that is "I have nothing to hide", has been already debunked by several people like Edward Snowden and it feels too boring to even talk about it, so let's not even get into that.

Now, how can you safeguard your privacy on the internet?

* Read privacy policies even if they seem complex, and if they are too complex to the point where an educated person can't understand them or if they are too vague, you should question the service providers.
* Become aware, follow the news related to privacy. Subreddits like r/privatelife and many others can be very useful in this regard. Not that I promote them or something, I do dislike many of these subreddits because of the constant drama and stuff, but you needn't get involved in that, just go there for the news and guides.
* Create your own threat model and choose only those services that suit it. This can also help in tackling the paranoia that people face when they get too involved in all this internet privacy mess. [This](https://www.privacyguides.org/basics/threat-modeling/) is a decent enough read to get you started with thread modeling.
* Encryption is your best friend. Using encrypted messaging services, https site(sites with valid ssl certificates), encrypting your PC's and such is what I mean here.
* Use free and open-source software. It is a myth that such software is always good for your privacy but more often than not, such software is still far better than the proprietary counterparts. Some good examples are Firefox, Chromium and its open-source forks(not Google Chrome, Edge and such), Linux, Matrix and Signal.
* Keep the data you put on the internet to a minimum, especially such data that can be used to uniquely identify you and anything about you.
* Try to move away from big tech like Google, Meta, Apple, and such as much as you can. Use services provided by non-profits and support them if you like them.
* Don't get too involved with centralized services(like those provided by Google, Apple, Microsoft and Reliance Jio) for everything, use different service providers for most if not all the services you need. Decentralization will help by not entrusting all of your data, which will definitely also include un-encrypted and un-obfuscated data, to a single entity.
* Use content blockers(like uBlock Origin on a browser and Rethinkdns app on android) and private DNS(like AdGuard, Quad9 and Cloudflare) to block trackers and for other quality of life improvements to your experience on the internet.

You can definitely find many posts and such and about many advance things you can do to safeguard on the internet, which I could have mentioned as well(like hardening your browser for privacy and security etc) but this post was only meant to spread awareness and I decided to avoid bringing up any complex suggestions.
