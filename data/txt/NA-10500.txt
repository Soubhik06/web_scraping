 legal liability, and the burden of proof lies upon the accused to rebut the said
 presumption. 23.However, in the present case, the cheque in question admittedly does not
 relate to the account of the accused and the accused also denied his
 signatures upon the same. As the fundamental facts themselves are in
 question in the present matter, the onus of proof does not get shifted to the
 accused rather the burden of proof still lies upon the complainant to prove
 the contents of his complaint. 24.The complainant in the present matter heavily relies upon the loan
 agreement Ex. CW1/1, however, on denial of the execution of the same by
 the accused, the complainant failed to prove the execution of the said
 agreement by the accused. Complainant admitted that the loan agreement
 Ex. CW1/1 has not been executed in his presence and infact the witnesses
 mentioned on the said agreement are not real persons. The complainant also
 failed to prove the fact that he actually advanced a loan of Rs. 50,000/- to the
 accused. 25.It is well settled that the initial burden of proof lies upon the complainant to
 prove the case and the complainant has to stand on his own legs to prove the
 contents of his complaint. In the present case, as discussed above, the
 complainant failed to prove the factum of advancing loan to the accused, the
 execution of loan agreement Ex. CW1/1 and that the cheque in question
 belongs to the account maintained by the accused. There is no other
 evidence produced by the complainant to substantiate its version. Therefore,
