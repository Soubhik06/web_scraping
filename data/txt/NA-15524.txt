The Indian Railways has deployed an anti-bot system with the help of a content delivery network (CDN) service provider in an attempt to curb unauthorised automated ticket bookings.Â The Indian Railways Catering and Tourism Corporation (IRCTC) mentioned that with this new system, it has been able to mitigate all the bot traffic that the ticketing system gets within the first five minutes of Tatkal (which allows you to book tickets for immediate travel). Bot traffic accounts for 50% of all login attempts that IRCTC experiences during the first five minutes of Tatkal.

This development comes after multiple reports came out late last year and in January this year about IRCTC outages. A Local Circles survey reveals that seven out of 10 people who have tried booking tickets via Tatkal in the past 12 months found that tickets become unavailable within one minute of booking opening, and they were waitlisted. Besides the system, the IRCTC will now also require Aadhaar authentication for users who want to book Tatkal tickets immediately after creating an account. In case a user does not undergo Aadhaar authentication, they will have to wait three days after registration to book Tatkal tickets.

Content delivery networks, which include service providers like Cloudflare and Akamai, optimise web content delivery by distributing it across a network of servers. Bot traffic includes all the non-human traffic that comes to a site, be it through a distributed denial of service (DDoS) Attack or search engines. A lot of bot traffic can overwhelm a website and cause it to crash. CDNs can help mitigate site crashes by distributing the traffic across their many servers.Â
