Apprehending arrest in Crime No.1098/2022 of North Parur

 police station, registered for offences under Sections 406 and 420 of the Indian Penal Code, accused 1 to 5 have filed this petition. 2. The prosecution allegation is that the petitioners, who are

 Managing Directors of the Company, namely NFBI, have collected

 an amount of Rs.10,22,770/- from the defacto complainant with

 the intention of making an unlawful gain to the accused and

 unlawful loss to the complainant. The allegation is that accused

 persons 1 to 5 owners of the firm NFBI headquartered in

 Kozhikode promised to pay Area Dealership at Paravur

 Constituency. After receiving Rs.10,43,700/- in total till date, the

 area dealership along with the products, has not been delivered

 and also did not refund the amount paid by the complainant and

 thereby committing the aforementioned offences. B.A.No. 8570 of 2022 4 3. The learned counsel appearing for the petitioners and the

 learned Public Prosecutor were heard. 4. Learned counsel for the petitioners argues that they are

 innocent of the allegations levelled against them. They have been

 running the NFBI Business Service LLP for the past several years

 without any complaints from any quarter. Accused Nos.1 to 3 are

 the Designated partners of the LLP, and accused No. 1 is handling

 the business operations mainly. The other petitioners are

 employees of the enterprise. Understanding the electric segment

 market in the economy, the petitioners herein launched NFBI

 shopping cart promoting electrical bicycles or e-bikes. An e-bike

 is a notarized bicycle that comprises an integrated electric motor

 to assist propulsion. The fact that they emit less pollution per

 kilometre than cars and bikes is why they are popular. Riding an

 electric cycle is similar to riding a normal cycle but with the

 added benefit of an electric motor. The petitioners were giving

 franchises all over Kerala promoting NFBI e-bikes, and one

 Sandhya Sreekanth approached the petitioners herein to start a

 franchise. 5. The defacto complainant and his wife applied for a

 Mudra loan. The project was for starting an MSME. The company B.A.No. 8570 of 2022 5 named J Trade was the financial consultant. The first petitioner

 herein is the Managing Director of the enterprise. The petitioner's

 enterprise also provides loan assistance for starting the NFBI E

 SHOPPING CART, which has the required business place. The

 same is mentioned in Annexure A2, dealership application. The

 defacto complainant's wife applied for a business loan from the

 Bank of India, North Paravoor Branch, submitting the SME

 project for starting a firm styled M/s.SAAS NFBI E SHOPPING

 CART. The bank after verifying the credentials of the petitioner's

 company, sanctioned the loan and credited the same to the NFBI

 Bank account. The loan was sanctioned on 12.8.2022, and the

 amount credited was Rs.10,22,770/-. Thereafter, Sandhya

 Sreekanth executed a memorandum of understanding with the

 NFBI Business Services LLP on 15.8.2022. 6. Learned counsel for the defacto complainant argues that

 Annexure A4 and Annexure A5 filed by the

 petitioners/respondents herein are forged documents. The

 signatures seen in the copies marked as Annexures A4 and A5

 are not signed by the third respondent and are not within the

 knowledge of these respondents. The Mudra loan of Rs.10 lakh is

 sanctioned to the third respondent from BOI, Paravur Branch and

 not from Indian Bank, Paravur Branch, as stated in Annexure A5. B.A.No. 8570 of 2022 6 The amount of Rs.1,00,000/- has been transferred to the ICICI

 North Paravur Branch account maintained by the additional

 second respondent as MSME subsidy promised by the petitioners

 at the time of dealership discussions, not as stated in Annexure

 A5. Annexure R1, wherein the additional third respondent (the

 defacto complainant) has not signed till this date due to the

 disagreement in the terms and conditions of the agreement. The

 petitioners demanded the additional second and third

 respondents to collect Rs.2500/- each from 100 people, totalling

 Rs.2,50,000/- by issuing a slip, an illegal lottery-like business

 against the terms and conditions of the dealership agreement. 7. Learned Public Prosecutor opposing the bail

 application submitted that with an intention to cause unjust profit

 to the accused and unjust loss to the complainant, accused Nos. 1

 to 3 of NFBI firm headquartered at Kozhikode started collecting

 money from the complainant by promising to provide area

 dealership in Paravur constituency. Believing the words of the

 accused, the complainant started transferring money from

 09.06.2022 to 12.08.2022 from his ICICI Bank, North Paravur

 Branch from account No. 105301500421 to the accused bank

 account No.626505500459. On 9.6.2022, the complainant

 transferred Rs.3000/-. On 14.6.2022 and 4.7.2022, he transferred B.A.No. 8570 of 2022 7 Rs.10000/- and Rs.7930/- to the accused bank account

 No.922020008036099 through this Google Pay account on

 12.08.2022. The complainant transferred Rs.10,22,770/- from his

 account No.856420110000260 to the accused account

 No626505500459. Altogether, Rs.10,43,700/- was transferred by

 the complainant to the accused till date without giving the area

 dealership or products and without returning the amount; all

 accused cheated the complainant by committing fraud. 8. Having considered the rival contentions, it is to be noted that

 the defacto complainant had availed a Mudra loan, and the said

 amount was directly transferred to the account of the petitioners.

 Notwithstanding the payment, it is the specific contention of the

 defacto complainant that the dealership or the vehicles are not

 given, disabling them from functioning. It is also their argument

 that the agreements and documents produced on behalf of the

 petitioners are forged, and their signatures are forged, the

 agreement was not signed, and the document is produced as if

 there is a signed agreement between the parties when none of

 these things has happened. On the face of the allegation of

 cheating and also having forged documents, there has to be a

 proper investigation as regards the role of each accused, whether

 there are other similar antecedents against the petitioners, B.A.No. 8570 of 2022 8 whether the Mudra loan advanced by the complainant could have

 reached the account of the accused. These are all matters where

 a proper investigation, including a custodial interrogation, should

 be carried out, and the grant of anticipatory bail in such a case

 will certainly hamper a proper investigation. Under such

 circumstances, I am not inclined to grant anticipatory bail to the

 petitioners, and accordingly, the bail application is dismissed and

 the following direction issued. In the event the petitioners surrender before the Investigating

 Officer in two weeks, they shall be interrogated and

 thereafter, shall be produced before the Magistrate having

 jurisdiction on the date of surrender itself. If the petitioners

 move for bail, the court below shall, untrammelled by any of

 the observations in this order, consider the bail applications

 on merit as expeditiously as possible. If the petitioners do not

 surrender before the Investigating Officer, as directed above,

 the Investigating Officer will be free to arrest the petitioners

 as if no order has been passed in this case. Subject to the above, the bail application is dismissed. Sd/- MOHAMMED NIAS C.P.
