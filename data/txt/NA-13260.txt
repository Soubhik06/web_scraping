The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has submitted a status report to the Supreme Court (SC) outlining a proposed Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to tackle so-called âdigital arrestâ scams, when the Court was hearing a suo motu case on the issue.

The report details steps to strengthen inter-agency coordination, freeze and restore defrauded funds, and streamline grievance redressal mechanisms. It also sets out the roles of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the Department of Telecommunications (DoT), the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and investigative agencies in curbing such frauds, according to LiveLaw .Â

The case originated after the SC took cognisance in October 2025 of an incident in which scammers defrauded a senior citizen couple from Ambala of Rs 1.05 crore through a âdigital arrestâ scam. A bench comprising Chief Justice of India (CJI) Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice NV Anjaria is hearing the matter.Â

Furthermore, the apex court had earlier authorised the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) to investigate digital arrest cases, as well as the role of banking and financial institutions. According to the MHA status report, the CBI has identified digital arrest cases involving losses of Rs 10 crore or more and will investigate them. Meanwhile, the other updates from the status report are as follows:

The DoT has notified the Draft Telecommunications (User Identification) Rules, 2025, to address negligent SIM issuance, multiple SIMs and mule SIMs through biometric-based identification under the Telecommunications Act, 2023.

It expects to notify the Draft Rules within the next two months. Additionally, it has notified draft Telecommunications (Radio Equipment Possession Authorisation) Rules to address SIMBOX-related issues. For context, SIMBOX is a device used to facilitate fraud by housing multiple SIM cards and making them appear to be local numbers.Â

Furthermore, on November 28, 2025, the DoT issued directions mandating SIM binding and fixing session duration for Telecommunication Identifier User Entities (TIUEs) providing app-based communication services in India, including WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and Arattai, to curb Voice Over Internet Protocol (VoIP) -based digital arrest scams.Â

It has also taken enforcement action, including imposing penalties in non-compliant Customer Acquisition Form (CAF) cases, blocking spoofed international calls through CIOR implementation, re-verifying mule SIMs, and operationalising Sanchar Saathi and the Digital Intelligence Platform. However, realisation is pending in the CAF cases.

Moreover, the DoT plans to finalise and notify the Telecommunications (User Identification) Rules, examine the feasibility of a victim compensation framework with stakeholders, review gaps in bulk and corporate SIM issuance, and identify SIM supply chain vulnerabilities that fuel cyber crime.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has informed the Inter-Departmental Committee that 26 banks have adopted the AI-based tool MuleHunter.AI , while other institutions continue to deploy proprietary AI and machine learning systems to detect mule accounts.Â

Additionally, the RBI is evaluating whether Section 12AA of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) can be used to temporarily suspend suspicious transactions. It has said this requires further legal scrutiny through consultation between the Department of Regulation, RBI, I4C and the MHA. To explain, Section 12AA deals with enhanced due diligence by banks, financial institutions, and other entities regarding high risk financial transactions.

Meanwhile, the RBI has also formulated a draft SOP to place temporary debit holds to curb mule accounts and cyber fraud, in compliance with directions issued by the Kerala High Court (HC). At the same time, it is developing a liability framework to address compensation in cases of authorised transactions induced by fraud.

The central bank is also examining the feasibility of delayed transaction mechanisms to mitigate fraud risks, and working on integrating the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (NCRP) with the Account Aggregator and Central KYC (CKYC) frameworks to enable quicker reporting. Moreover, it will establish strict accountability for personnel involved in KYC lapses or complicity, including invoking the Prevention of Corruption Act (1988) where applicable.

The MHA outlined protocols for freezing and unfreezing bank accounts linked to cyber fraud incidents through the NCRP mechanism. In addition, it has specified mechanisms for placing and rescinding liens on fraudulent funds within the banking system.Â To explain, lien is anÂ officialÂ orderÂ thatÂ allows someoneÂ toÂ keepÂ theÂ propertyÂ of anotherÂ personÂ until money owed is repaid.

Further, the MHA has detailed procedures for refunding and restoring funds secured through the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFCFRMS), using the Online Money Restoration Module as the central mechanism to return defrauded amounts to rightful owners. It has also proposed a transparent, time-bound and accountable online grievance redressal system for people aggrieved by unwarranted freezing, lien marking or related enforcement actions.

Moreover, the SOP incorporates internal monitoring mechanisms to oversee implementation and identify operational challenges during the SOP’s rollout. The MHA says that it has grounded the SOP in statutory provisions of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS, 2023) the PMLA, and relevant judicial orders, thereby ensuring judicial oversight and legal remedies at each procedural stage.

MeitY convened a high-level meeting onÂ  January 6, 2026, with major intermediaries like Google, Meta and Microsoft, to review measures adopted to prevent platform abuse in digital arrest cases.

And during the compliance review, it was observed that Microsoft acted expeditiously on intelligence shared by the Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C), whereas WhatsApp did not take adequate remedial action despite prolonged engagement, even though most digital arrest cases are allegedly perpetrated through the Meta-owned app.

MeitY is also actively pursuing compliance by concerned platforms with the SIM binding directives issued by the DoT. In parallel, MeitY is collaborating with the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (CDAC) to develop a dedicated portal for reporting complaints under Section 43 of the IT Act and for streamlining compensation-related applications before the Adjudicating Authority.Â
