# Legal &amp; Social Challenges Faced by Men in India

# 1. Difficulty in Proving Adultery in Court

* Even with video evidence, courts may refuse to consider it.
* Proving a wife’s adultery does not always impact alimony decisions.
* Judges may watch the video but still rule in favor of the woman based on financial dependency.

# 2. Paying Child Support for a Child Not Biologically Theirs

* Even after a DNA test proves non-paternity, courts may still order men to pay child support if they played a fatherly role.
* The child's “best interest” is prioritized over biological truth.

# 3. Forced to Pay Alimony to an Unfaithful Wife

* Even if a woman cheats, she can still claim alimony under Section 125 CrPC.
* Adultery is rarely considered a strong enough reason to deny maintenance.
* Courts often assume women are financially dependent, even if they are capable of working.

# 4. False Rape &amp; Domestic Violence Allegations (K.K. Verma v. Union of India (2016), State of Punjab v. Ramdev (2012),Bharatbhai Patel v. State of Gujarat (2015))

* A woman’s verbal statement is often enough to register an FIR, leading to arrest without proper evidence.
* Men falsely accused of rape often face job loss, social stigma, and legal battles lasting years.
* Even if proven innocent, there are no strict laws to punish false accusers.

# 5. Misuse of Section 498A (Dowry Law) (Sushil Kumar v. State (2005),Rajesh Sharma v. State of U.P. (2017)) 

* A man and his entire family can be arrested without investigation.
* Even elderly parents and distant relatives are often falsely accused.
* There are many cases of women using this law for financial extortion.

# 6. Unequal Child Custody Laws, (Shalini v. State of Haryana (2009))

* Mothers are almost always given custody, even if the father is a better guardian.
* Fathers struggle to get visitation rights, let alone full custody.

# 7. Unfair Maintenance Payments

* Men pay alimony even if their ex-wives are well-educated and financially stable.
* Some women deliberately quit their jobs to claim higher maintenance.

# 8. No Legal Protection for Male Victims of Domestic Violence

* The **Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act (PWDVA)** only protects women.
* Male victims of spousal abuse have no clear legal recourse.

# 9. Biased Divorce Settlements

* Men often lose a significant portion of their assets, regardless of who was at fault.
* Women can demand financial support even if they initiated the divorce.

# 10. Arrest Without Evidence in Family Disputes, (Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014))

* Police can arrest a husband and his family solely based on a woman's allegations.
* There is no requirement for preliminary evidence before making an arrest.

# 11. No Workplace Harassment Protection for Men,(Vishakha v. State of Rajasthan (1997))

* **The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act (2013)** does not provide protection for male victims.
* Men’s complaints are often ignored, and false harassment claims can ruin careers.

# 12. No Legal Recourse for Male Victims of Harassment

* Laws like PWDVA and sexual harassment acts exclude men as victims.
* Even if a man faces harassment, there are no specific laws protecting him.

# 13. No Legal Support for False Allegations

* Women falsely accusing men of rape or domestic violence face little to no punishment.
* Courts rarely prosecute women for filing false complaints.

# 14. Paternity Fraud (No DNA Rights for Fathers)

* A man cannot demand a DNA test of his child without court approval.
* Even if a DNA test proves he isn’t the father, he may still be legally required to support the child.

# 15. Biased Matrimonial Laws (Section 125 CrPC)

* Even if a man is unemployed, he may still be required to pay maintenance.
* Courts rarely question why a woman cannot support herself.

# 16. Forced to Marry After a Relationship Ends (Promise of Marriage Cases) (J. G. Samraj v. Union of India (2006),Sandeep Bansal v. State of Delhi (2019))

* Women can file cases for "rape on the pretext of marriage," forcing men into marriage or legal battles.
* Courts often assume the man is guilty if a woman claims he broke a promise to marry.

# 17. Fake Molestation Cases in Public Places (Sarvjeet Singh v. State (2015) Delhi)

* Men are often accused of molestation in crowded places, leading to immediate arrest or mob violence.
* Even if proven false later, reputations are permanently damaged.

# 18. Forced Financial Responsibilities After Divorce

* Even after divorce, men may be forced to pay for their ex-wife’s lifestyle.
* There are no legal provisions to ensure men get financial relief.

# 19. No Protection for Male Victims of Stalking or Acid Attacks

* Laws against stalking and acid attacks focus only on women, even though men are victims too.
* Male victims receive little media attention or legal support.

# 20. Criminalizing Men for Relationship Disputes

* Women can file cases under IPC 376 (rape) or 354 (outraging modesty) for personal disputes.
* Courts and police often assume the woman's version of events is true.

# 21. Misuse of the SC/ST Act Against General Category Men

* Some women misuse **The Scheduled Castes and Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act** to settle personal scores, leading to false arrests.

# 22. No Protection from Digital Harassment &amp; Revenge Porn for Men

* Laws against revenge porn and online harassment mainly protect women.
* Men face social ridicule if they are victims of revenge porn or cyberstalking.

# 23. Anti-Men Bias in Media &amp; Society

* False accusations make headlines, but acquittals get little coverage.
* Society often assumes "men are guilty until proven innocent."

# 24. No Government Support for Male Mental Health Issues

* There are government initiatives for women’s mental health but none specifically for men.
* Men suffering from legal harassment or depression get no legal or psychological support.

# 25. High Male Suicide Rates Due to Legal Harassment

* Studies show that many male suicides are linked to false accusations and financial burdens from biased divorce settlements.
* Suicide prevention campaigns often ignore issues affecting men.

I have added references to legal cases that set precedents for other cases, and one thing is painfully common among them: the outcome was always a loss for men. They never won. The system, instead of delivering justice, often subjected them to years of emotional, financial, and reputational damage, regardless of the truth.

I’m just a normal guy living in Delhi. I was once falsely accused of rape, but fortunately, the matter never escalated because I had proof—on the day of the alleged incident, I was at the airport, flying to Indore for two weeks. That evidence saved me, even though she and her family tried to change their statements to ruin my life. She was my neighbor, and I had a dispute with her father because he attempted to build a house on top of my house’s terrace. They are SC/ST, but ironically, they’re wealthier than my family. Since then, no one in my neighborhood speaks to me or respects me.

I stayed here because I was born here, and my mother was here. She fought and survived cancer twice, but sadly, she passed away four months ago. Now, this place feels empty. I can’t see her anymore, nor could I take care of her like I wanted to.

My girlfriend, whom I convinced my traditional family to accept, never came when I needed her most. I told her how much I needed her, but she didn’t show up. I forgave her, but my bitterness and anger grew. Finally, in January, she met with me and explained why she didn’t come. She said she wanted me to have space to handle things, as she thought I was emotionally unstable. That honestly broke me. Even my manager consoled me better than she did. I told her clearly that I needed her, but all she could say was that she couldn’t come because of her family and because she wanted to give me space.

Five years of a relationship, and this was the result. Nothing. I went from 77kg to 104kg in just four months due to stress eating. When she finally came to see me, all I could see in her eyes was disgust. I cried and opened up to her, only for her to break up with me two weeks later.

At this point, I don’t think I want to marry or love anyone anymore. Honestly, I’d rather end my life than trust someone again, because I know that if I were married and she did the same thing, I’d rather face death than go through another long, draining legal battle and be repeatedly betrayed.

I’ve decided to start working on men’s issues and rights because, too often, we’re not heard. We’re blamed, expected to carry all the toxicity, and we can’t raise our voices without being called misogynists or insecure. Our feelings are dismissed, and we are pushed aside like we don’t matter.
