Hello everyone,

I need legal advice regarding a company that scammed investors and participants in a blockchain-based music platform, promising an ad-free streaming service. Here is the detailed context of the situation:

1. **Company Background**:
 * The company positioned itself as a revolutionary music streaming platform leveraging blockchain technology to create a fair and transparent ecosystem for artists and fans.
 * **Promises to Artists**: The platform promised artists a space where they could upload and manage their content, directly connect with fans, and sell merchandise and tickets without intermediaries. They would receive fair compensation for their work through transparent payout mechanisms.
 * **Promises to Fans**: Fans were promised opportunities to earn money by curating and verifying music on the platform. This unique feature was supposed to create a more engaging and rewarding experience for users.
 * **Initial Hype**: The CEO secured an immediate licensing deal with a leading Indian music company and signed up numerous independent artists who were ready to go live on the platform upon its launch. This created a significant buzz and added credibility to the project.
2. **Funding and ICO**:
 * **Ad-for-Equity Deal**: In 2016, the company raised **$7 million** from a major Indian printing media conglomerate house through an ad-for-equity deal. This deal involved the media house company taking an equity stake in the company in exchange for advertising, boosting the company's visibility and perceived legitimacy.
 * **Promotion and Media Presence**: The CEO of the company and his team are still very active in professional networks, social media, and other channels. The partnership to promote the ICO was associated with a **very big Indian news printing house**. Multiple full-page ads were published, and more than five articles promoting the ICO appeared in the media. The articles not only covered India but also many other countries, including mentions in Forbes and other major leading news blogs.
 * **ICO and Tokens**: The company launched an Initial Coin Offering (ICO), selling tokens called "Coins." These tokens were to be used for monthly music subscriptions and voting on favorite musicians and songs, driving community engagement.
 * **Fixed Supply and Scarcity**: The ICO was marketed with a fixed supply of 400,000,000 Coins, valued at $0.10 USD per token. The limited availability of tokens was highlighted as a selling point, likened to the scarcity of gold, promising potential value appreciation.
 * **Revenue Model**: The company claimed its new revenue model would significantly enhance the growth of the music ecosystem. Investors were led to believe that the platform’s unique model would result in larger and more transparent payouts to both musicians and contributing listeners.
3. **My Involvement**:
 * **Early Investment**: I was an early investor who believed in the company's vision and invested in the ICO, making a payment based on the promising prospects.
 * **Project Failure**: However, as time passed, it became clear that the project failed to deliver on its promises. Reports indicated the company engaged in fraudulent activities, particularly related to the ICO.
 * **Accusations of Fraud**: The CEO and the company were accused of not fulfilling their commitments. The project, which promised a blockchain-based music ecosystem with cryptocurrency rewards, turned out to be a scam.
 * **Shutdown**: Around 2020, mentions of the project shutting down surfaced, leaving investors and participants in a lurch.

**My Questions**:

1. What legal actions can I take against the company and its CEO for the investment scam?
2. Are there any specific Indian laws or regulations that could help me in recovering my investment or holding the company accountable?
3. Can I join any class-action lawsuits or collaborate with other affected investors?
