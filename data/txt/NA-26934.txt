--- POST TEXT ---

Yesterday I almost/partially fell for a phone call scam offering a Rogers Internet deal. My contract is almost up so I was interested in knowing what prices were like without intending to sign up, but the caller (with an Indian accent) offered to “buy out” my current contract. It was my first brush with a somewhat sophisticated/coordinated attempt: being transferred to another agent, being called from a (spoofed) Rogers number (which the scammer told me to google to see that it was Rogers when I raised some doubt). What solidified it as a scam to me was seeing the domain name of the email they sent with the offer details. Had they spoofed the sender’s address, I might have completely fallen for it (why didn’t they do that?).

Prior to that realization, in my distracted multitasking-in-the-kitchen, sleep-deprived-parent-of-young-children state, I did give away my name, address, DOB, email and credit card number (without expiry/CVV). Out of precaution, I had locked the card and requested a different card number right after getting off the scam call. I live in Ontario where credit freezing isn’t allowed, it seems. I registered for Potential Fraud Alert with Transunion.

From what I’ve read, the other information I gave away is not particularly sensitive. What I’m wondering is what kind of upcoming targeted scams I/my family should be on the lookout for. Anything else I should do?

My knee-jerk reaction is wanting to drop the email address and change my phone number, but doing these would be a real hassle and probably doesn’t add any value. I am moving on with my life, but this bothers me quite a bit to realize what some people are willing to do and deliberately bring harm to others for gains, what kind of a world my children will have to face and what I will need to teach them, somehow.
