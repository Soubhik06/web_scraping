asserted that the plaintiff caused damage to the property

and that deductions were warranted for repairs. 17. The Court examined the evidence. The plaintiff's

claim is largely based on narrative and personal hardships.

There is no documentary proof, expert report, or other

evidence to quantify or establish actual damages suffered.

As per Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872,

damages for breach must be a direct consequence of the

breach and reasonably certain. Speculative or unproven

claims of mental anguish or personal inconvenience do not

meet the standard of recoverable damages. Therefore, this

Court holds that the plaintiff has failed to prove the claimed

damages of Rs.2,00,000/-. Accordingly, this Court answers

the Issue No.2 in the Negative. 18. Issue No.3: The defendant No.3 contended that

the suit is barred under Section 34 of the SARFAESI Act,

2002, which precludes Civil Court jurisdiction in matters

concerning recovery of secured debts. The Bank also

submitted that it has no contractual or statutory

relationship with the plaintiff. Furthermore, any surplus

proceeds from the sale of the mortgaged property belong to

the borrowers, i.e., Defendant Nos.1 and 2, in accordance

with Section 13(7) of the SARFAESI Act. 19. The evidence shows that the Bank lawfully took

possession and sold the property. The plaintiff never had a

registered lease, prior knowledge, or any charge over the

property. As per Section 34 of the SARFAESI Act bars Civil

Court interference in the Bank's statutory functions,

including possession, sale, and distribution of proceeds.

The plaintiff has no legal right to restrain the Bank from

releasing funds to the borrowers. Therefore, this Court

holds that the relief sought against defendant No.3 is legally

impermissible and cannot be granted. Accordingly, this

Court answers Issue No.3 in the Negative. 20. Issue No.4: Considering the findings on Issue Nos.1,
