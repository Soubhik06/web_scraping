On August 20, 2025, the Indian government introduced the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Bill, 2025, in the Lok Sabha. The bill aims to establish a comprehensive legal framework for the online gaming sector, encompassing e-sports, educational games, and social gaming, while enforcing a strict ban on online real-money games (RMGs). The bill made a speedy move from the Lok Sabha to the Rajya Sabha and received the president’s assent on August 22, making it law. MediaNama organised a discussion on September 19, 2025, to examine the constitutional, legal, and practical implications of the law.

Participants raised concerns about the constitutional validity of the law, particularly regarding the classification of online money games. They noted that gambling is a state subject, which could present challenges to the law’s enforcement. Several speakers highlighted the potential conflict between state and central jurisdiction over online games, especially as the law classifies certain games as “money games” based on whether they involve a stake for monetary rewards. Issues surrounding constitutional rights, such as the right to personal liberty and the right to trade, were also flagged as possible grounds for legal challenges. Participants emphasised that these provisions could affect individuals who depend on online gaming for their livelihood, such as professional players.

The discussion also focused on how the law’s provisions might impact legitimate monetisation practices in online gaming and e-sports. Speakers pointed out that the broad language in the law, especially regarding “stakes”, could inadvertently affect games with legitimate monetisation models, such as in-app purchases or subscription-based services. Concerns were raised about the potential challenges businesses might face in classifying their games under the law, particularly when monetisation involves mechanisms like loot boxes, which could be misinterpreted as a form of gambling. The difficulty in distinguishing between legitimate in-game purchases and those that involve monetary stakes was a key issue, with some participants suggesting that further regulatory clarification would be needed.

A major concern discussed was the potential shift of users to illegal offshore platforms in response to the law’s ban on RMGs. Some participants argued that prohibiting legal RMG platforms could inadvertently fuel a grey market, exacerbating issues like fraud, money laundering, and financial losses for users. The potential for offshore platforms to evade regulation and offer unregulated services was flagged as a significant risk to both the industry and consumers.

While the law could potentially reduce some forms of fraud, it was noted that simply banning online money gaming might not resolve the broader issue of online fraud. Some participants drew comparisons with other sectors, such as stock trading, where issues like addiction and fraud are addressed through regulation rather than prohibition. The participants also expressed concerns that the lack of regulation in offshore platforms could result in greater risks for consumers, including exposure to fraud and manipulation.

The social costs of online money gaming were also discussed, with participants highlighting concerns about addiction, financial losses, and the impact on mental health. Some suggested that the law’s ban on RMGs would not fully address these issues and that regulatory measures should focus on promoting responsible gaming rather than outright prohibition.

While the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Law, 2025, is seen as a significant step towards regulating India’s online gaming sector, there are numerous concerns about its broad application, potential legal challenges, and impact on businesses and consumers. Stakeholders called for greater clarity in the law’s provisions, particularly regarding the definition of online money games and the governance of the sector. Many emphasised the need for a balanced approach that promotes the growth of the gaming industry while mitigating risks such as fraud, addiction, and financial losses.
