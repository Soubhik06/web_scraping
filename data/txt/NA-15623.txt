Platforms should carry out abusability testing to assess how malicious users can exploit the features of their services, the UK communication regulator Ofcom states in its draft guidance for digital platforms to protect women and girls from online harm. This guidance is part of the UK’s Online Safety Act, 2023 , which sets requirements for platforms to ensure user safety.

While discussing abusability testing, Ofcom references its 2024 research on red teaming in tech companies. This method helps prevent users from generating and sharing deepfake images using generative AI. Based on red teaming results, platforms can improve content filters and update blocklists for specific public figures. They can also remove nudity content from their training datasets.

Ofcom’s guidance applies to companies providing user-to-user and search services. This includes a range of digital platforms such as social media, gaming, discussion forums, pornography services (with user-to-user interactions), dating services, and online marketplaces. The deadline for submitting comments on the draft guidance is May 23, 2025, and Ofcom intends to publish the finalized guidance by the end of the year.

Ofcom notes that platforms should consider relying on external assessors to monitor emerging threats. They should engage with survivors and victims of online harms to ensure their voices are taken into account when assessing risks. Platforms can also conduct user surveys to better understand people’s preferences and experiences of risk. Furthermore, they can conduct impact assessments on women’s and girls’ freedom of self-expression and discrimination to understand how different user groups, including women and girls, may be affected by their services.

This includes sharing information about the prevalence of online harms, details about which posts the platform’s automated content moderator flags, and the users targeted by harmful content. Ofcom says this can help shed light on women’s experiences on the internet.
