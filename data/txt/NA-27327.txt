This happened in February 2021, but I’m still seething with fury whenever I think about it.

Little bit of background: Both of my parents have died in recent months. Both were diagnosed with varying degrees of Covid right before Thanksgiving. My dad (stepdad, but he raised me) had a severe case, and was hospitalized for 3 weeks, my mom had a mild case, and was left to recover at home. Both fully recovered from Covid in time for Christmas. But my dad died of a heart attack the day after Christmas. My mom died in her sleep of a heart attack 3 days before Valentine's Day. For my dad, it was understandable, he'd had a history of heart problems for decades. But for my mom, it was a complete and total shock. She did have type 2 diabetes, but it had been under control for years, and other than that, she was healthy as an ox. There was also no history of heart issues with her, or any other member of my mom's family. That, and I spoke to her 2 days before she died, and she was her normal self, making plans, getting active, doing things. So, her death knocked my brother and I on our collective a\*\*es. We were ripped apart inside. Which is where this story starts. Oh, I should probably also mention my brother is in his 40's, and divorced, but had been living with my parents to act as care giver, as they were getting older, and our dad couldn't do all of the normal maintainence that needed to be done around the house. I'm in my 50's, and live about 3 hours away.

My youngest son (we'll call him Evan) and I got up to my parents’ house the day after my mom died, to help deal with the craziness surrounding the death of a family member: funeral arrangements, getting death certificates, locating life insurance policies/ wills to make sure there weren't any special requests for funeral arrangements, that sort of thing. Mom died right before the weekend, so the earliest appointment at the funeral home was the following Monday. With all of the phone calls we had to make, and all of the things we had to do, including writing an obituary, then going and actually making the arrangements for her funeral, by Monday afternoon, when my brother (we'll call him John) and I got back to the house, we were tired (from lack of sleep), grieving (obviously), and emotionally in turmoil. The phone had been ringing fairly steadily since I got there on Friday (understandably). Numbers from friends and family we answered, numbers we didn't recognize went to the answering machine (yes, my parents still had one). Problem is, I, living 3 hours away, didn't recognize any of the numbers. So, when the phone started ringing 10 minutes after we got home, and John was in one of the back rooms, I answered the phone without thinking.

Scammer = scammer, me= me

*Me, picking up the phone*: Hello?

*Scammer (heavy Indian sounding accent)*: Good Afternoon, Madam. I'm (some name I can't remember) with (Company I've never heard of), and I need to speak to you about vehicle (vehicle, I think) maintainence.

Now John is a mechanic and has his own business. So, in my braindead state, I thought he might be looking for a mechanic, and was trying to reach John.

*me:* are you looking for a mechanic?

*Scammer*: no ma'am, I'm with (Company I never heard of), and deal with vehicle service contracts. We work with several Dealerships in the area, including Honda. Now, the reason I'm calling, ma'am, is your previous service contract is about to expire -

Ahh... my foggy brain finally begins to register the scam trying to happen, although this was a varient I wasn't aware of. And he mentioned Honda. My mom's car. So, yes, it was slightly rude, but I'm just barely functioning, mentally and emotionally, so it's allowed.

*me:* okay, wait, sorry to interrupt, but I need to stop you there, for just a moment. Who is it you are trying to reach?

*Scammer:* I'm talking to you, ma'am, because the service contract on your Honda is about to expire, ma'am. Now is the best time to renew your contract, before there is a lapse in coverage -

*me:* please, just stop a minute. Can you please give me the name of the person you are trying to reach?

*Scammer:* I'm trying to talk to Anna Scao(name has been changed). This is Anna Scao, yes?

Now I know this is a scammer. My parents lived in a small town, out in the country. Everyone knew everyone else. Including the dealership that sold my mom her car, and whom she takes it to for maintainence. This guy was using her full name, Anna. Not her shortened nickname, Ann. And he pronounced her last name Scow, instead of Scay-o, which is how it's pronounced. So, by now, I've had enough.

*me:* you are trying to reach Ann Scay-o?

*Scammer:* yes, now, Miss Scow, as I was saying...(he was still pronouncing her name wrong. It was time to get her out of their database.)

*me:* I need to stop you right there. I'm not Ann Scay-o, I'm her daughter. I'm sorry, but I need to inform you, Ann Scay-o died, 3 days ago. So, she will not be renewing any contracts. Can you please remove her from your data base? (I said this, just in case it wasn't a scam, but a legitimate company)

*Scammer:* You're lying!

Oooooh no. This mother f\*\*\*\*r did **NOT** just **DARE** say this to me! Now, John had walked back into the room a few minutes before, while I was trying to determine who the guy was trying to reach, and he and Evan had been trying to get me to hang up on the guy. So, they were both in the room to hear me ghasp. And then they heard my voice get deadly quiet.

*me:* I want to speak to your manager or supervisor, right now. You had **better** have his ass, on the phone, in less than 5 seconds.

After everything I had gone through, especially in the last few days. I'd had my heart ripped apart twice in the past couple of months, and I was still reeling from the pain. In that moment, all my grief, and all that pain, became a white, hot, seething, barely controlled rage. Once this manager, or supervisor got on the phone, I was going to rip him a new a\*\*. If I could, I would have reached through the phone and ripped this f'er and his supervisor's throats out.

Unfortunately, for me (*very* fortunately... for them) John and Evan **knew** what that deadly quiet voice meant.

So, as I'm hearing the f'er calling me a lying b\*\*\*h and telling me his manager isn't going to fall for my crap, the phone is suddenly pulled out of my hands, by John, who hangs up, and puts the phone back on the charger.

*John:* This is why Mom and Caleb (our stepdad - not his real name) never answered the phone, if they don't know the number.

&amp;#x200B;

I tried three separate times to call the scammers back, to verbally rip them to shreds, but the number didn’t work. It’s been almost a year since my mother passed, and my blood still boils whenever I think of that scammer calling me a liar when I tried to inform him my mother died. Phone scammers are the lowest scums of the Earth, the cockroaches of humanity. 

I take that back, calling them cockroaches is the worst insult to cockroaches everywhere.
