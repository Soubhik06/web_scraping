Web hosting provider and domain registrar MotherHost sent notices to users who owned .in domains, informing them that they would have to undergo a Know Your Customer (KYC) process to register or renew their domains, starting August 1.

“This verification will be carried out through DigiLocker, the secure digital documentation platform developed by the Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology (MeitY) under the Digital India initiative,” said the email, which was reviewed by MediaNama.

The e-KYC mandate is the result of a revised Registrar Accreditation Agreement (RAA), brought in place by the National Internet Exchange of India (NIXI) five months ago,” said a source at NIXI.

The KYC requirement would allow the authorities to identify domain holders in case of any cyber incidents, the source added.

According to the existing RAA on NIXI’s website, domain owners can submit their passport, voter ID, driving license, Aadhaar card, PAN card, or any other official identification card.

In its initial email, MotherHost claimed that MeitY would no longer allow non-Indians to register or renew .in domains. However, they retracted the claim in a follow-up email, stating that both Indian and foreign citizens could register .in domains.

Foreign nationals would have to submit their passport, driving license, a letter issued by the Foreign Embassy or Mission of India, and income tax returns for the last two years.

Additionally, NIXI requires foreign entities to demonstrate a “legitimate business connection or purpose connected to India” by submitting a signed declaration outlining their business connection or purpose in India.

NIXI’s KYC requirements were first brought up in 2023; however, they were only voluntary at the time. Experts told MediaNama that while KYC requirements could help curb cybercrime, they may go against the anonymous and decentralised nature of the internet. Instead, the government could have imposed additional compliance only on large platforms, similar to the obligations for significant social media intermediaries under the Information Technology Rules, 2021.
