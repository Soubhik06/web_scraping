Zoomcar, the self-drive car rental firm, has disclosed in a regulatory filing that it suffered a cyberattack in which hackers gained access to the personal information of approximately 84 lakh users. The leaked data includes names, phone numbers, car registration numbers, personal addresses, and email addresses.

The company stated that the incident occurred on June 9, 2025, and it became aware of the breach after bad actors emailed some of its employees, alleging unauthorized access to its data.

However, the company has not revealed any further details about the hackers or whether affected users have been informed. MediaNama has reached out to Zoomcar for more information.

We will update the copy once the company responds.

To enhance security, the Bengaluru-headquartered company has started implementing additional safeguards across its cloud infrastructure and internal network. It is also increasing system monitoring and reviewing access controls. “To date, the incident has not resulted in any material disruption to the Company’s operations,” the company noted in its regulatory filing.

“At this time, there is no evidence that financial information, plaintext passwords, or other sensitive identifiers were compromised,” Zoomcar informed the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

In recent times, bad actors have started targeting consumer-facing brands that provide services or sell goods. Hackers are now able to breach databases and access sensitive customer information with the help of emerging and sophisticated technologies. These bad actors then use this data to manipulate and scam Indian citizens online and via phone calls.

In April 2025, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) launched India’s first Digital Threat Report, 2024 , which highlighted how AI has enabled cybercriminals to exploit vulnerabilities in the cybersecurity systems of financial firms.

The report noted that the average cost of a data breach globally reached an all-time high of $4.88 million in 2024, a 10% increase from 2023. In India, the cost was estimated at $2.18 million. The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) has warned that AI-driven cyberattacks will become one of the most scalable and adaptable threats by 2025, challenging traditional defenses and requiring innovative countermeasures.

Protecting customer data is paramount, as a breach of data amounts to a breach of privacy. Companies must work with the government and cybersecurity firms to deploy robust safeguarding measures and build impenetrable firewalls to curb future data breaches.

Adidas recently experienced a cyberattack in which bad actors accessed “certain” customer data via a third-party customer service provider. The company clarified that it did not compromise passwords, credit card information, or other payment-related details.

In May 2025, British retailer Marks & Spencer (M&S) suffered a ransomware attack that led to millions of pounds in revenue loss and temporarily halted its operations.

Similarly, in November 2020, hackers gained access to the personal information of over 20 million BigBasket customers in an online attack . This included names, email IDs, password hashes, PINs, and contact information. The bad actors subsequently sold the data on the dark web for around Rs 29.5 lakh. The grocery delivery platform, however, claimed it did not store financial data and was confident that the compromised data did not include such details.

In August 2020, RailYatri, a ticketing and travel website, suffered a data breach that compromised the personal details of around 700,000 users. The breach occurred due to the platform’s production server being exposed publicly for a few days.
