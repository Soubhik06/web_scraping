	It
may not be out of place to state at this stage that there is no
dispute as regards the list which has been referred to above and the
same has been accepted by each of the respondents. In
such circumstances referred to above, the petitioners have
prayed for an appropriate writ, order or direction on the Central
Vigilance Commission to initiate necessary inquiry into the
complaints alleging corporate corruption by M/s.OOMS, controlled by
the ONGC Management under the provisions of Section 8(1)(d) of the
Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003. I.
Stance of the Respondent No.5 OOMS : Most
of the entities/companies who have contributed towards sponsoring the
charitable exhibition cricket match are business associates of the
ONGC. They were aware that such an event was taking place for a noble
social cause. The sponsorship document was distributed to such
associates by the OOMS members in Mumbai. However, no sponsorship
letters were issued by the OOMS or the ONGC for seeking support.
Donations were sought only from those who were showing inclination
towards the noble cause initiated by the OOMS or volunteered for
sponsorship. The
sponsorships were received at Mumbai/Delhi by the ONGC offices as
well as the OOMS units. Some sponsorship amounts in the form of
cheques were received directly at the venue of walkathon on 28th
February 2010 and cricket match on 2nd March 2010 and few
were transferred to the OOMS Bank Account through RTGS. A total of 45
numbers of cheques of different values were received as sponsorship
for this event and 3 sponsorships were received through electronic
transfer (RTGS). All payments were received by cheques/RTGS and no
cash payment was received and receipts were issued against all
payments. The
ambitious plan of organising such a mega event for the noble cause
was a high profile event which received encouragement from the Union
Ministers, CEOs of ONGC and Air India besides other high profile
dignitaries. Before the mega event of cricket match between the ONGC
and the Air India XI on 2nd March 2010, the following
events were organised : The

on the response to the above queries and documents, the Central
Vigilance Commission conducted necessary inquiry and communicated
vide Office Memorandum No.010/POL/061 dated 28th November
2011 the following : Mr.Sinha
submitted that para 4 of the Code of Conduct for Board Level
Officers/Senior Management Officers reads Avoid any dealing
with a contractor or supplier that compromises the ability to
transact on a professional, impartial and competitive basis or
influence decision to be made by the company; use their prudent
judgment to avoid all situations, decisions or relationships which
give or could give rise to conflict of interest or appear to conflict
with their responsibilities within the company . Para 6 reads
as The Directors and Senior Management shall neither receive
nor offer or make, directly or indirectly any gifts, donations, or
comparable benefits which are intended to or perceived to obtain
undue favours in the transactions detrimental to the interest of the
company, except for nominal value which are customarily given and are
of commemorative nature for special events and should never be of a
kind that could create an appearance of impropriety .
Therefore, the OOMS and the officers in the Board of Directors of the
ONGC by directly involving themselves and allowing their spouses in
their efforts to obtain the contributions/donations from the
contractors of the ONGC in the name of CSR activity etc. have
violated all norms and provisions of the Code of Conduct and,
therefore, are liable to face the consequences for such violations. The
integrity pact signed between the ONGC and the contractors, which is
compulsory in all contracts, contain the following provisions : No employee of the Principal, personally or through family
			members, will in connection with the tender for, or the execution
			of a contract, demand, take a promise for or accept, for
			himself/herself or third person, any material or immaterial
			benefit which he/she is not legally entitled to. Section 2 The
	Bidder/Contractor commits itself to take all measures necessary to
	prevent corruption. He commits himself to observe the following
	principles during his participation in the tender process and during
	the contract execution: The Bidder/Contractor will not, directly or through any person or
	firm offer, promise or give to any of the Principal s employees
	involved in the tender process or the execution of the contract or
	to any third person any material or immaterial benefit which he/she
	is not legally entitled to, in order to obtain in exchange any
	advantage of any kind whatsoever during the tender process or during
	the execution of the contract. It
is submitted that the amount collected from the contractors is
absolutely illegal and in complete violation of the provisions under
the integrity pact and, therefore, the officers of the
ONGC as well as that of the OOMS are liable to face the consequences
including refund of the contribution and the expenditure incurred in
connection with the cricket match also should be met with from their
personal accounts, apart from the punishments as provided under the
Act. Mr.Sinha
submitted that this is a fit case for issue of appropriate writ,
order or direction on the Central Vigilance Commission to undertake
necessary inquiry into the allegations as provided under the
provisions of Section 8(1)(d) of the Central Vigilance Commission
Act, 2003. Mr.Ajay
R.Mehta, the learned advocate appearing for the ONGC, submitted that
the allegations levelled in this petition are vague and baseless. He
submitted that the allegations are only against the respondent no.5
OOMS and no relief whatsoever has been prayed for against his client.
He submitted that public corporations have legal responsibility to
maximise shareholder s profits but Corporate Social Responsibility
(CSR) is a concept whereby organizations serve the interests of
society by taking responsibility for the impact of their activities
on customers, employees, shareholders, communities and the
environment in all aspects of their operations. Project activities
identified under CSR are to be implemented by specialized agencies
and generally not by staff of CPSE concerned. According
to Mr.Mehta, such specialized agencies would include Mahila
Mandals/Samitis like the OOMS. Mr.Mehta laid stress on the fact that
no further direction at the end of this Court is warranted in light
of the fact that the CVC had already conducted the necessary inquiry
and had advised no action against Shri R.S.Sharma or any other
official. Mr.Mehta also submitted that the Commission did observe
that unethical practices appear to have been adopted as funds of
around Rs.3.5 crore had been collected from various contractors of
the ONGC but, at the same time closed the chapter by advising that
the ONGC should avoid such unethical practices in future. According
to Mr.Mehta, nothing more remains to be looked into or done at the
end of this Court and, therefore, the petition should be dismissed. Mr.Gaurang
Mathur, the learned advocate appearing for the respondent no.5
adopted the submissions canvassed by Mr.Mehta and laid stress on the
fact that the Central Vigilance Commission has already given a
warning which shall be abided by his client strictly. A
N A L Y S I S : Functions and Powers of Central Vigilance Commission.-
(1) The functions and powers of the Commission shall be to - or cause an inquiry or investigation to be made into any complaint
	against any official belonging to such category of officials
	specified in sub-section (2) wherein it is alleged that he has
	committed an offence under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (49 of 1988) and an offence with which a public servant specified in
	sub-section (2) may, under the Code of Criminal Procedure , 1973 (2
	of 1974), be charged at the same trial; In
the result, this petition is allowed. We direct the Central Vigilance
Commission to initiate a proper and a detailed inquiry or
investigation to ascertain, whether any public servant by corrupt or
illegal means has obtained for himself or for any other person any
valuable thing or pecuniary advantage or by abusing his position as a
public servant has obtained for himself or for any other person any
valuable thing or pecuniary advantage by extending any undue favour
to the contractors named in Annexure-1 to this petition. The
Central Vigilance Commission shall initiate the inquiry within a
period of two weeks from the date of receipt of this order and
conclude the same within a period of three months thereafter. In
view of the order passed in the main matter, the connected Civil
Application would not survive and is accordingly disposed of. (BHASKAR BHATTACHARYA,
CJ.)
