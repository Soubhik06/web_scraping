--- POST TEXT ---

TLDR (thanks to u/gkkiller): OP booked an Uber auto but the ride was unexpectedly canceled during the journey. The driver continued driving without acknowledging the cancellation, repeatedly claiming he hadn't received payment. Despite OP's attempts to show him the payment confirmation and resolve the issue with Uber's help, the driver refused to cooperate and caused a scene. OP contacted Uber support but couldn't communicate due to the driver's interference. Eventually, with the help of a friend who spoke Kannada, the driver agreed to accept another payment. OP later received a refund from Uber.

&amp;#x200B;

For context, I am one of the people who, until this day (and even after the fact), has held an absolute opinion that

1. Uber is one of the safest ways to commute within the city (the safest being a personal vehicle).
2. Not all auto annas are bad. Just like not all people are bad. There are all kinds of people, and we deal our best with what we come across. I have met really good Uber drivers as well.
3. We don't need to worry about discrimination based on language, and everyone is very welcoming and helpful. Even if not fluent, anyone can do well, even with little Kannada knowledge.

Although one amendment to this is don't expect any help from Uber. They will "help" but like a sloth. So slow that I handled the situation, got my stuff done, came back, took a shower, and was writing this post when I received a callback from them. That is more than 2 HOURS late. The support was through the "Safety" feature. Interaction details in the story.

And since language aggression part can be a bit sensitive for some people, I'm making this clear that I don't have any bias towards it since I'm aware of similar situations:

1. I lived in a similar place with a very similar situation before I came to Bangalore. Locals in that capital city also had language issues, and \[most\] auto drivers and other radical groups would discriminate against and harass people. So I've learned to handle situations like that. It was easier because I eventually learned the language since the friends I made there were very welcoming and used to keep translating if they ever spoke anything in the local language. Eventually, in about 2 years, I became fluent. And I also know these are, a lot of times, driven and fueled by political agendas since I used to be part of youth committees (back in the city I was brought up in, not Bangalore and not the other capital city I am talking about) where we were given food, alcohol, money, and petrol for participating in rallies during the election with their party flag. Advertisement, essentially. So, I have a fair knowledge of how communal issues operate.
2. I also had to de-escalate situations once or twice in the mentioned capital city where the same local auto goons were against ride-hailing services^(\[I had booked one\]), even when I didn't know the local language because Hindi worked. That was back when Ola and Uber were fairly new to the Indian market. So I'm talking way back (almost 9-10 years back).
3. The local shop near my apartment, which is run by a very wholesome couple, has never criticized me for not knowing Kannada well because they probably understand that with the Covid lockdown for 2 years, WFH after that, and usually busy life being a male bachelor living in a family apartment where it is difficult to find house help, etc only increases responsibility and impacts social life. But I have also seen (I won't give examples, but I can if asked) discrimination and scams, so I know there are all kinds of people. Hence, I refrain from blanket statements, especially ones that put forward criticisms.

**The Story**

I booked an Uber auto to the salon about 2 km from my place. The first 800ms or so is a very secluded road where you won't find any other vehicle most of the time.

I got on the auto, shared the OTP, and the ride started.

After about 500ms, the auto driver tells me the ride has been canceled. I checked my phone, and indeed it was. I told him I didn't cancel and had received a payment notification (Google Pay).

\[Weirdly\] He didn't stop the auto; instead continued driving. He was talking to someone on the phone and started telling them. I didn't ask him to stop because it's better to continue at least the next 300ms of the curved road until we reach the main road, which is fairly busy. Better than stopping on a secluded road.

But he doesn't say anything to me. Keeps driving and telling the person on call that the trip is canceled etc. It probably repeated 4-5 times for the rest of the journey.

When we reached, I got down, showed him the google pay payment, said thank you, and moved on. He stopped me and said he hadn't received anything because the trip was canceled. I showed him the payment again and said I have paid, and since I didn't cancel, he should talk to Uber and sort this out. *\[****Context****: I'm speaking in English, and he's speaking in Kannada. I can follow, but I don't have the speaking skills.\]*

We repeat the same sentences a few more times. I offer to stay while he speaks to Uber. But he doesn't want to talk to Uber and keeps telling me that trip is canceled and he isn't getting paid. I again explained to him and showed him the Google payment (read out the time and date as well this time) and that he should try talking to Uber since I have paid. He doesn't budge and starts making a scene. *\[****Context****: He had stopped on the other side of the road where a stall was swarmed with other autos and nearby workers who came for lunch. About 15 or more people and 6 other autos. The rest of the road is fairly empty because not a lot of buildings around.\]*

So I offer to help him by contacting Uber myself *\[not to let him escalate the situation\]*. I selected Safety in the help section because I couldn't find any quick way to call Uber except the safety option in Help. The auto driver kept shouting and trying to involve the nearby people, so I couldn't focus properly, so maybe there was another way, IDK.

Get connected (maybe in 20-30 seconds), and when I start speaking, he tells me to hand him the phone and let him speak. I don't. There's no way I'm handing over an expensive phone to someone sitting in a running auto while I stand here. *\[Expensive as in 6 figures expensive\]* He insists and keeps shouting, and I decline. The Uber support is on call, but I haven't been able to talk because of him.

I got on the auto, turned on the loudspeaker, and he started shouting on the phone (He kept his hand around my hand) and didn't let me speak. I tried speaking, but he didn't let me. Shouted in Kannada to the Uber representative.

The Uber representative asked if he could speak English, Hindi, Tamil or Telugu. This enraged the auto driver even more, and then I started speaking. The representative said, "Am I speaking to Mr. OP?". I guess he understood that the driver was speaking when the call connected. I quickly explained the situation *(all the while driver shouting in the background, telling the people in the stall and trying to get them involved saying how I didn't know Kannada and the Uber guy didn't know Kannada and asked if he knew English, Tamil, Telugu, etc).* The Uber representative told me he would arrange a call back with someone who knows Kannada. I agree and hang up.

I wait in the auto for the next 5 mins or so. No callback from Uber. The driver continuously kept shouting and talking to the nearby people. This time, language stuff is included. One person heard him and moved on. I have also told him by this time *\[he asked me why I don't know Kannada\]* that I understand well but can't speak Kannada, so I don't know much. He scorned me and continued shouting and trying to involve the people in the stall. Nobody gave much attention to him anymore, though.

At this point, I told him that I had to go and to respect both our time, I'll pay him the trip amount again on Google Pay, take his phone number and a photo of the screen as proof that I'd paid the amount twice so I can get one of it refunded from Uber.*\[I just wanted to get out of there because it didn't seem like he wanted a resolution and would only keep trying to escalate this. It would be safer to dispute the payment while I'm physically safe\]*

The auto driver keeps shouting the same thing again and again.

After several more attempts, I decided to call my friend (who knows Kannada) so he could translate if that were the issue. I didn't think language was an issue because he could follow it just fine. But it was worth a shot because it is better to act like I believe he is not doing it deliberately. Sort of De-escalation tactics 101: Assume malice if you want to, but act like you don't. It took a couple of tries, but even while I was calling my friend, he was signaling me to hang up and kept repeating himself. I told him I was calling my friend so he could translate what I was saying.

My friend picks up the call, and I explain the situation. He translates for me on the loudspeaker *\[Not handing the driver my phone, obviously\].* And not very surprisingly, his tone instantly changes when talking to my friend. Keeps calling him Anna. And in just a few sentences, he agreed to what I had asked. And what did my friend say? Nothing more than what I asked him to. And the auto driver also tried to cut him off in between, saying the trip was canceled and he wasn't getting money. *\[After my friend said I would send money again and handle the refund myself\]*. My friend continued; telling him to let me take the proof to get a refund.

I made the payment again to his google pay and continued my day. I spoke with my friend, and we laughed it off. I got a haircut, showered, and received a refund from Uber. And now, mid-way through the post, I received a call from Uber following up on the Safety call. I told them I had handled it and politely told them it would have been helpful that time but it's sorted now.

&amp;#x200B;

**Takeaways**

1. DON'T RELY ON UBER. YOU'RE BETTER OFF CALLING 100 if you ever have a safety concern.
2. I don't know if his unwillingness to contact Uber for his money had any reason or the point in escalating rather than trying to, at the minimum, get this sorted. There were other weird cues I haven't mentioned, but I won't speculate and will defer to anyone reading this post about what you think this whole thing was based on the event objectively.
3. Now I have to prioritize learning Kannada even though I don't get enough time to myself on a daily basis. Not because it is needed but because it makes de-escalation easier (or prevents escalation, depending on the person you're dealing with). I strongly support learning languages (for social, mental, and socio-economic reasons), but I wanted it to be more because of a good reason like before than a sour one now.
