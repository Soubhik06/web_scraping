 TALWAR Date: 9. I have heard the arguments advanced by all
concerned and have perused the records including documents
relied upon by the prosecution carefully. Observation of the Court 10. Accused persons have been charged for offence
under Section 498A IPC and 304B IPC read with Section 34 IPC. 11. Section 498A IPC provides punishment for cruelty
to a woman by her husband or relative of her husband. The term
cruelty within the meaning of Section 498A IPC has been
explained in the explanation to the said section. It consists of two
clauses (a) and (b). To attract Section 498A IPC, it must be
established that the cruelty or harassment to wife was to coerce
her to cause grave bodily injury to herself or to commit suicide;
or the harassment was to compel her to fulfill illegal demands of
dowry. Thus, every type of harassment or cruelty would not
attract Section 498A IPC. 12. An offence u/s 498A thus, has following essential
ingredients: 13. Section 304B IPC deals with dowry death and
provides that where the death of a woman is caused, otherwise
then under normal circumstances, within 7 years of her marriage
and it is shown that soon before her death, she was subjected to
cruelty or harassment by her husband or any relative of her
husband for, or in connection with, demand for dowry, such
death shall be called dowry death and husband or relative shall be
deemed to have caused her death. 14. In order to bring home the charge of dowry death u/s
304B IPC, it is essential for the prosecution to prove the
following ingredients: 15. The harassment, torture and violence on a married
woman occurs within the four walls of the matrimonial home, the
prosecution case usually depends on circumstantial evidence and
because it is difficult to define the crime by circumstantial
evidence the rule of presumption is enacted under Section 113B of Indian Evidence Act. 16. Section 304 B(i) mandates the drawing of
presumption that the husband or relatives of the husband of the
victim girl caused her death and this presumption of dowry death
corresponds to the presumption to dowry death envisaged as per Section 113B of Indian Evidence Act. 17. Both under Section 113B of Evidence Act and Section 304B of IPC have used expression "Shall be presumed".
However, the presumption under Section 113B of Evidence Act
reserves the rule that burden of proof is on the prosecution.
Reliance is placed on Bhoora Singh Vs. State of U.P. , 1992 Crl.
LJ 2294. 18. First part of Section 304B IPC defines dowry death
and the second legal implications of occurrence on dowry death.
Hence when other ingredients of Section 304B are fulfilled, the
husband or the other relatives shall be deemed to have caused
death of the victim. Presumption against the husband or relatives
shall be drawn by the court in cases where prosecution succeeds
in proving the basic ingredients of Section 304B IPC.
Nevertheless, the presumption is rebuttable and the husband or
his relatives can disprove the same by leading cogent evidence. 19. Having said that harassment or cruelty caused to the
