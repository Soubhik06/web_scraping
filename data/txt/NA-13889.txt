The Bombay High Court will be hearing a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) that has sought the court to direct state and Central Governments to prevent cyberattacks on official websites of the Maharashtra Government. According to a LiveLaw report, the petition was filed by a Information Technology Consultant and Visiting Professor of Law, who alleged negligence on part of the government in securing their websites.

The petitioner stated that he had discovered compromised government websites, upon which he had called the Maharashtra Cyber Helpline number to make a report. However, the Helpline allegedly refused him, stating that they only accepted complaints related to personal injury and financial fraud. He also claimed that even the National Cyber Crime Helpline refused to take up the complaint and that he received no response from the Computer Emergency Response Team of India (CERT-In).

The petition argues that government websites have come under attack due to inadequate security measures and unmonitored cyber infrastructure, which has caused some websites to redirect visitors to gambling websites that attempt to collect their personal data. It also contends that government websites lack sufficient security measures like web application firewalls, anti-virus software, intrusion detection/prevention and anomaly detection software. The websites also run on an allegedly outdated Content Management System, allowing cybercriminals to inject malicious code.

The petition expresses the fear that compromised government websites could allow cybercriminals to prey on ordinary citizens through phishing attacks, digital arrest, fake e-challan payments and fake government notices. It urges the court to direct the government to take steps to block the infected websites and ensure that such attacks do not occur again in the future. It also asks the state government to establish a cyber task force and for the police to investigate the matter and register FIRs against those responsible.

There have been several instances of government websites coming under attack in the past, especially from gambling operations.
