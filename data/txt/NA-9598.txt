G.Girish, J. The order of preventive detention passed by the 2nd respondent under Section 3(1) of the Conservation of Foreign Exchange and Prevention Of

Smuggling Activities Act, 1974 (for short COFEPOSA Act ), is under challenge in

this writ petition filed by the wife of the detenu. 2. Mr.Suresh Babu.V.S, the husband of the petitioner (referred

hereafter as detenu), was involved in the business of exchange of foreign

currencies, under the name and style M/s.Suresh Forex Services Pvt. Ltd and

M/s.Suresh Trading Company. While so, he was accused in hawala dealings in

the Nurses Recruitment Scam in Kochi during the year 2017, and the Enforcement

Directorate had registered a case against him in that regard. The licence issued

by the Reserve Bank of India to the detenu, for conducting the business of foreign

exchange operations, was revoked on 23.06.2017. Against the above revocation

of licence, a writ petition filed by the detenu as W.P.(C) No.23459/2017 is pending

consideration before this Court. The detenu, thereafter, took franchisee licence

from a company by name Daspan Forex Pvt.Ltd Mumbai for foreign money

exchange in the name of his wife, the petitioner herein, as the proprietrix of the

establishment by name Travel Time Tours and Travels, and continued the foreign

Directorate conducted a search at the office and residence of the detenu at

Kottayam on 19.06.2023, and seized unaccounted foreign currencies and Indian

currencies amounting to a value of Rs.1,71,82,506.62. The statements of the

petitioner herein, the detenu and that of one Preetha Pradeep, the office

manager of the detenu, were recorded on various dates from 19.06.2023 to

18.07.2023, by the authorised officers of the Enforcement Directorate. On the

basis of the aforesaid statements, as well as the materials and documents seized

from the office and residence of the detenu, the 2nd respondent arrived at a

finding that the detenu was involved in illegal foreign exchange operations, and

hence his detention is necessary to prevent him from acting in any manner

prejudicial to the augmentation of foreign exchange in future. Accordingly, the

2nd respondent passed Ext.P1 order supported by Ext.P2 grounds of detention on

31.08.2023. The detenu was thus arrested on 02.09.2023 and confined to

Central Prison, Thiruvananthapuram. Reference under Section 8(b) of the

COFEPOSA Act was made to the Advisory Board on 03.10.2023. The Advisory

Board heard the detenu online on 13.11.2023. After considering the opinion of

the Advisory Board, as well as the other relevant records, the Central Government

confirmed the detention of the detenu vide Ext.P4 order and directed his
 2024:KER:62900 5 W.P(Crl.)No.1342/2023 detention for a period of one year from 02.09.2023. The aforesaid detention

orders passed vide Exts.P1 and P4 by the respondents 1 and 2 are under

challenge in this writ petition. 3. The petitioner has enumerated around thirty grounds for assailing

Exts.P1 and P4 orders passed against the detenu. Many of the said grounds are

perfunctory in nature. Many other grounds are apparently irrelevant in the facts

and circumstances of the case. The respondents 1 to 3 filed a common counter

affidavit, and the 4th respondent filed a separate counter affidavit opposing the

aforesaid grounds as well as the reliefs sought for by the petitioner in this writ

petition. For the sake of brevity, we propose to deal with the only substantive

and relevant challenge raised by the petitioner against the detention order, and

its sustainability in the light of the objections raised by the respondents. 4. Heard Adv.Mr.V.Vinay, the learned counsel for the petitioner,

Adv.Sri.Suvin R.Menon, the learned Central Government Counsel for respondents

1 to 3 and Adv.Mr.Jayasankar V. Nair, Retainer Counsel, Directorate of

Enforcement, for the 4th respondent. 5. The learned counsel for the petitioner brought to our notice that the

statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep, the Office Manager of the
 2024:KER:62900 6 W.P(Crl.)No.1342/2023 detenu, is the most crucial document relied on by the 2nd respondent for arriving

at the conclusion that the detenu has been involved in the illegal business of

purchase and sale of foreign currencies, cross border hawala transactions and

unaccounted stock of foreign currencies. However, it is pointed out that the

aforesaid statement obtained from Mrs.Preetha Pradeep was not given to the

detenu, and thus he was deprived of an opportunity to explain the insinuations

contained in that document, by way of a representation to the Advisory Board

and Central Government. The learned counsel for the petitioner invited our

attention to the list of relied-upon documents that form part of Ext.P1 order, and

submitted that the statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep has been

deliberately excluded from the aforesaid list notwithstanding the fact that the

said document had been extensively relied on by the 2nd respondent for passing

Ext.P1 order against the detenu. 6. The learned counsel for the respondents countered the aforesaid

argument by submitting that the statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha

Pradeep was not a relied-upon document, and hence there was no obligation on

the part of the respondents to serve a copy of the said document to the detenu.

Furthermore, it is submitted that if the detenu felt that the aforesaid statement

dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep ought to be perused for answering the
 2024:KER:62900 7 W.P(Crl.)No.1342/2023 allegations levelled against him, he could have very well requested the

respondents to supply a copy of the said document to him. Thus, it is contended

that the detenu cannot blame the respondents for the non-supply of the

statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep to him, without making a

request for providing a copy of that document. The learned counsel for the

respondents further argued that it was not incumbent upon the respondents to

serve copies of those documents which only come within the purview of

'documents which are referred upon', to the detenu while initiating proceedings

against him. 7. Now, the matter which deserves serious consideration is whether

the statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep, mentioned

hereinabove, could be termed as a 'relied upon document', or it is only a

document which is 'referred upon'. To answer the aforesaid question, it is

profitable to look into Ext.P2 grounds of detention, on the basis of which the 2nd

respondent passed Ext.P1 order. Paragraph No.i of Ext.P2 is about the search

conducted at the residential premises and business premises of the detenu

leading to the seizure of foreign and Indian currencies amounting to

Rs.1,71,82,506.62. Paragraph No.ii relates to the revocation of the Full Fledged

Money Changing licence issued by the Reserve Bank of India to the establishment
 2024:KER:62900 8 W.P(Crl.)No.1342/2023 of the detenu, and the disqualification of the detenu to have the above licence

renewed, due to the registration of a criminal case against the detenu at the

instance of the Enforcement Directorate. It is also stated thereunder that the

detenu had availed a franchisee licence from Daspan Forex Pvt. Ltd Mumbai in

the name of Travel Time Tours and Travels, proprietory concern of Smt.Nirmala

Devi, the wife of the detenu. Paragraph iv of the said document refers to the

statement recorded on 19.06.2023 from Smt.Nirmala Devi, in which she is said

to have confided that she does not know anything regarding the business or any

other related activities, and that everything in her name was being handled by

her husband, the detenu herein. Paragraph Nos.viii, ix, x, xi, xii and xiii are about

the statements recorded from the detenu and the disclosures allegedly made by

him about the unauthorised transactions of foreign currency trading carried on

by the detenu. In paragraph No.xiv, there is extensive and elaborate description

of the statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep in which she had

disclosed the modus operandi of the detenu who indulged in large-scale foreign

currency purchases and sales without the necessary licence issued by the

Reserve Bank of India. It is in the aforesaid statement given by Mrs.Preetha

Pradeep on 04.07.2023 that the fulcrum of accusations of violation of law of

foreign currency exchange, at the instance of the detenu, rests upon. Pursuant
 2024:KER:62900 9 W.P(Crl.)No.1342/2023 to the aforesaid statement, the authorities concerned are seen to have recorded

the statements of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep on 05.07.2023, 06.07.2023 and

17.07.2023. The statement dated 05.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep, which is

appended to Ext.P1 order, starts with the narration that it is in continuation of

her statement dated 04.07.2023. A reading of the aforesaid statement of

Mrs.Preetha Pradeep, made on 05.07.2023, would also make it clear that the said

statement is incomplete without her prior statement given to the authorities

concerned on 04.07.2023. Thus, it is apparent from the aforesaid records that it

was not possible for the authorities concerned to rely upon the statement dated

05.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep in isolation of the statement made by her on

the previous day i.e. 04.07.2023. But, strangely, the statement dated 04.07.2023

of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep has not been included in the list of relied-upon

documents appended to Ext.P1 order. Admittedly, respondents 2 to 4 did not

serve a copy of the aforesaid statement dated 04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep

to the detenu. Needless to say, the above omission made by the authorities

concerned cannot be justified by saying that the statement dated 04.07.2023 of

Mrs.Preetha Pradeep was not a relied-upon document. When Ext.P2 grounds of

detention itself speaks volumes about the act of the Detaining Authority placing

extensive reliance upon the elaborate statement given by Mrs.Preetha Pradeep

of foreign exchange regulations, it is not possible for the respondents to find

excuse for their default in serving copy of the said statement to the detenu, on

the basis of a contention that the aforesaid statement dated 04.07.2023 of

Mrs.Preetha Pradeep, was not a document which was relied upon. After delving

in detail and placing heavy reliance on a document and arriving at a conclusion

about the detrimental acts attributed to the detenu, the Detaining Authority

cannot be heard to say that it was on the basis of some other records that they

arrived at the finding about the illegalities perpetrated by the detenu, and the

need to invoke the provisions of preventive detention against him. Therefore, we

are of the view that the 2nd respondent committed serious violation of the

mandatory requirements of law by not serving the copy of the statement dated

04.07.2023 of Mrs.Preetha Pradeep to the detenu, and arriving at conclusions of

insinuations against the detenu by relying on the aforesaid statement. 8. While dealing with the obligation of the authorities concerned as per

the mandatory requirements of Article 22(5) of the Constitution of India and Section 3(3) of the COFEPOSA Act to communicate the relied upon documents

to the detenu, the Apex Court held in Kamla Kanyalal Khushalani v. State

of Maharashtra & Anr. [AIR 1981 SC 814] that if there are any documents,

also be communicated to the detenu, because being incorporated in the grounds

of detention, they form part of the grounds and the grounds furnished to the

detenu cannot be said to be complete without them. It is further observed

thereunder that if this requirement of law is not satisfied, the continued detention

of the detenu would be illegal and void. 9. In Khudiram Das v. The State of W.B & Ors. [AIR 1975 SC

550], the Apex Court had observed that the plain requirement of the first

way of the detenu in making an early, comprehensive and effective

representation against the order of detention which deprived his freedom. 2024:KER:62900 12 W.P(Crl.)No.1342/2023 10. In Smt.Icchu Devi Choraria v. Union of India & Ors. [AIR

Court had observed that by providing that the grounds of detention should be

communicated to the detenu, what is meant is that the grounds of detention in

the entirety must be furnished to the detenu, and that if there are any

documents, statements or other materials relied upon in the grounds of

detention, they must also be communicated to the detenu, because being

incorporated in the grounds of detention, they form part of the grounds, and the

grounds furnished to the detenu, cannot be said to be complete without them.

It is further observed thereunder that it would not therefore be sufficient to

communicate to the detenu a bare recital of the grounds of detention, but copies

Apex Court also made it clear in the aforesaid decision that if this requirement of
