(per the Hon'ble Sri Justice P.SAM KOSHY)

 Heard Mr. Laxmaiah Kanchani, learned counsel for the petitioner and

Mr. Swaroop Oorilla, learned Special Government Pleader representing learned

Additional Advocate General, for the respondents. 2. The instant is a writ petition which has been filed challenging the order

dated 25.11.2024 (Annexure P-1) passed by respondent No.2 putting the detenu

under preventive detention under Sub-Section (2) of Section 3 of the "The

Telangana Prevention of Dangerous Activities of Bootleggers, Dacoits, Drug-

Offenders, Goondas, Immoral Traffic Offenders, Land-Grabbers, Spurious Seed

Offenders, Insecticide Offenders, Fertiliser Offenders, Food Adulteration

Offenders, Fake Document Offenders, Scheduled Commodities Offenders, Forest

Offenders, Gaming Offenders, Sexual Offenders, Explosive Substances Offenders,

Arms Offenders, Cyber Crime Offenders and White Collar or Financial Offenders

Act, 1986 (for short, 'the Act of 1986'). 3. The writ petition is one which has been filed seeking for issuance of a Writ

in the nature of Habeas Corpus with prayer for production of the detenu before the

Court and quashment of the aforementioned orders. 4. The brief facts of the case are that the detenu has been detained on

24.11.2024 in Cr.No.863 of 2024 for the offence as "BOOTLEGGER" under

Section 2 (b) of Telangana Prevention of Dangerous Activities Act, 1986, in

Narsampet Police Station limits. The detenu has been systematically involved in

the illegal possession, purchase, transport, manufacture and distribution of I.D.

(Illicitly Distilled) Liquor, in direct violation of section 7(A) read with 8(e) of the Telangana Prohibition Act, 1995 and section 34(e) of the Telangana Excise Act,

1968. The detenu's illegal activities have created a significant disturbance in public

order and posed serious health risks to the local community in and around

Nekkonda mandal. 5. The impugned preventive detention order is based on the four separate

criminal cases registered against him at the Prohibition & Excise Station,

Narsampet. These cases, bearing numbers Cr.No.123/2024 (Dated:10.03.2024),

Cr.No.314/2024 (Dated: 02.05.2024), Cr.No.534/2024 (Dated: 27.06.2024) and

Cr.No.863/2024 (Dated: 24.11.2024) demonstrate a pattern of repeated offenses

over time. The detenu's persistence illegal activities indicate a deliberate and

continuous violation of the law, severely impacting the societal fabric and public

health in Nekkonda mandal and its surrounding areas. 6. After careful consideration of the evidence and the nature of the offence the

authorities determined that the ordinary law was insufficient to curb the detenu's

unlawful activities and determined that preventive detention is necessary as a last Page 3 of 11 resort. The order dated 25.11.2024, directed his detention in Central Prison,

Cherlapalli, to prevent further drug-related activities that could harm public health

and order. 7. Thus, the present Writ Petition challenges the impugned order on the

grounds that these alleged crimes fall under the purview of ordinary law and do not

constitute a disturbance of public order, questioning the necessity and legality of

the detention order under the Telangana Prevention of Dangerous Activities Act. 8. Learned counsel for the petitioner contended that the detention order was

passed based on the four cases registered against the detenu with in single year, all

bearing suspicious similarities in their date of occurrence which appears to be

deliberately fabricated. The learned counsel for the petitioner further contended

that the detenu who earns his livelihood through manual labor has maintained a

clean record with no criminal history prior to these disputed cases. He contended

that it is unnecessary and improper invocation of preventive detention order when

the regular criminal justice system provides adequate mechanisms to address the

alleged offenses. 9. Further, the learned counsel for the petitioner contended that the detaining

authority has passed the orders based on mere apprehensions rather establishing

any concrete evidence or any compelling reasons to prove that the detenu is a

habitual offender. However, the authority has not provided any plausible

explanation for believing that the detenue would engage in activities prejudicial to Page 4 of 11 public order if not detained. This absence of rational justification makes the

detention order arbitrary and unsustainable in law. 10. Lastly, the detaining authorities failed to specify detention period as

mandated by Section 31 of the Act which represent a serious procedural lapse.

Moreover, crucial evidentiary materials including witness statements under Section

161 Cr.P.C have not been furnished with the detention order. This omission

severely handicaps the detenue's ability to prepare and present an effective defense

against the allegations. Thus, the learned counsel for the petitioner prayed that the

impugned order of preventive detention be set-aside and the detenu be set free. 11. Per contra, the learned Special Government Pleader argued that the detenu

was actively engaged in manufacturing illicitly distilled liquor through dangerous

and unscientific methods and the production process was particularly concerning

as it involved crude materials including black jaggery, ammonium chloride,

potassium, and aluminum sulphate in disproportionate quantities. The

manufacturing conditions were highly unhygienic, utilizing unclean mud pots,

bamboos and rubber tubes. Laboratory analysis of the seized contraband revealed

an alarming presence of harmful substances including fusel oils, acids, organic

acids, higher alcohols, aldehydes, and furfural. These toxic components were

found to cause severe health issues and also damage the liver and nervous system. 12. Further, the learned Special Government Pleader argued that despite

multiple cases being registered under the Telangana Prohibition Act, 1995 , and Page 5 of 11 Telangana Excise Act, 1968 , the detenu showed no inclination towards reform.

Instead, the detenu deliberately exploited recent amendments to the Criminal

Procedure Code , particularly Section 41, which allowed release on notice. This

legal loophole enabled the detenu to continue his illegal activities with impunity,

creating significant challenges for law enforcement agencies. Moreover, the

learned Special Government Pleader stated how the easy profits and minimal

investment required in this illegal trade were encouraging the proliferation of

similar offenders. The impact extended beyond immediate health concerns, as the

illegal liquor trade was actively undermining government welfare schemes aimed

at helping the poor and downtrodden. The detenu's activities were causing both

health and financial strain on vulnerable sections of society, leading to a wider

degradation of social fabric and public order. The conventional enforcement

methods had proved insufficient to curb these dangerous activities, necessitating

the extraordinary measure of detention under the Preventive Detention Act to

effectively control and prevent further damage to society. Thus, the learned Special

Government Pleader prayed for dismissal of the writ petition. 13. Having heard the contentions put forth on either side and on perusal of

records, it would be relevant at this juncture to refer to a recent decision of Hon'ble

Supreme Court in the case of Arjun S/o. of Ratan Gaikwad vs. The State of

Maharashtra 1 wherein the Hon'ble Supreme Court dealing with a similar 1 SLP (Crl.) No.12516 of 2024 Page 6 of 11 situation made a distinction between a public order and law & order and the

Hon'ble Supreme Court also in the case of Ram Manohar Lohia vs. State of

Bihar 2 and Ameena Begum vs. The State of Telangana 3 held in paragraph

Nos.12, 13, 14 and 15 as under, viz.,

 "12. The distinction between a public order and law andorder has
 been succinctly discussed by Hidayatullah, J. (as His Lordship then
 was) in the case of Ram Manohar Lohia (supra): 13. It could thus be seen that a Constitution Bench of this Court in
unequivocal terms held that every breach of peace does not lead to
public disorder. It has been held that when a person can be dealt with
in exercise of powers to maintain the law and order, unless the acts of
the proposed detainee are the ones which have the tendency of
disturbing the public order a resort to preventive detention which is a
harsh measure would not be permissible. 14. Recently, a Bench of this Court has referred to various judgments
of this Court while following the law laid down by this Court in the case
of Ram Manohar Lohia (supra), it will be appropriate to reproduce the
following paragraph from the judgment of this Court in the case of Ameena Begum v. State of Telangana and Ors . 39. In Arun Ghosh case [ Arun Ghosh v. State of W.B. ,
(1970) 1 SCC 98 : 1970 SCC (Cri) 67] , the petitioning
detenu was detained by an order of a District Magistrate
since he had been indulging in teasing, harassing and
molesting young girls and assaults on individuals of a
locality. While holding that the conduct of the petitioning
detenu could be reprehensible, it was further held that it
(read : the offending act) "does not add up to the
situation where it may be said that the community at
large was being disturbed or in other words there was a
breach of public order or likelihood of a breach of
public order. ( ArunGhosh case [ Arun Ghosh v. State of
W.B. , (1970) 1 SCC 98 : 1970 SCC (Cri) 67] , SCC p. 101, para 5)" 40. In the process of quashing the impugned order,the
Hidayatullah, C.J. while referring to the decisionin Ram
Manohar Lohia [Ram Manohar Lohia v. Stateof Bihar,
1965 SCC OnLine SC 9 : (1966) 1 SCR709] also ruled : There is no formula by which one case can be
 distinguished from another." 41. In Kuso Sah v. State of Bihar [KusoSah v. State of Bihar, (1974) 1
SCC 185 : 1974 SCC (Cri) 84] , Hon'ble Y.V. Chandrachud, J. (as the
Chief Justice then was) speaking for the Bench held that : (SCC pp.
186-87, paras 4 & 6)

 "4. ... The two concepts have well defined contours, it
 being well-established that stray and unorganised crimes
 of theft and assault are not matters of public order since
 they do not tend to affect the even flow of public life. Infractions of law are bound in some measure to lead to
 disorder but every infraction of law does not necessarily
 result in public disorder. ... 6. ... The power to detain a person without the safeguard
 of a court trial is too drastic to permit a lenient
 construction and therefore Courts must be astute to
 ensure that the detaining authority does not transgress
 the limitations subject to which alone the power can be
 exercised." (emphasis supplied) 15. As to whether a case would amount to threat to the public order or
as to whether it would be such which can be dealt with by the ordinary
machinery in exercise of its powers of maintaining law and order would
depend upon the facts and circumstances of each case. For example, if
somebody commits a brutal murder within the four corners of a house,it
will not be amounting to a threat to the public order. As against this, if
a person in a public space where a number of people are present
creates a ruckus by his behaviour and continues with such activities, in
a manner to create a terror in the minds of the public at large, it would
amount to a threat to public order. Though, in a given case there may
not be even a physical attack." 14. Under the given legal dictum in a series of decisions of the Hon'bleSupreme

Court referred to in the preceding paragraphs, we have no hesitation inreaching to

the conclusion that the preventive detention order lacks substantialjustification and

is arbitrary in nature. This Bench finds that the detaining authority has failed to

make the crucial distinction between "public order" and "law and order" while

passing the detention order. Further, this Bench notes that the distinction between

public order and law & order is crucial in preventive detention cases. While law

and order pertains to individual cases of law violations that affect specific persons,

public order refers to even temporary disturbances of the current life of the

community in a particular area. Public order is considered to be more serious and

has a larger impact on the community at large, whereas law & order situations are

more localized and affect specific individuals. The Supreme Court previously has

consistently held that preventive detention is an extraordinary measure that can

only be justified when ordinary law is insufficient to deal with the situation.In the

present case, the alleged activities of manufacturing and selling illicit liquor, while

certainly illegal, fall within the domain of "law and order" rather than affecting

"public order" at large. 15. Further, this Bench observes that for an act to affect public order, it must go

beyond the effect on specific individuals and must disturb the even tempo of

society at large. The distinction between public order and law and order lies in the

degree and extent of the reach of the act in question upon society. The alleged Page 11 of 11 activities of the detenue, even if proven, are localized incidents that can be

effectively dealt with under the regular criminal justice system. The detaining

authority has failed to demonstrate how these activities have created such a

disturbance in the society that would warrant the extreme step of preventive

detention. 16. Therefore, this Court concludes that the impugned detention order is

unsustainable in law. As a consequence the preventive detention order dated

25.11.2024 and also orders passed by the Advisory Board are liable to be set aside

/ quashed and it is ordered accordingly. The detenu shall be released from

detention forthwith if he is not required in any other case. 17. The writ petition accordingly stands allowed. No costs. 18. As a sequel, miscellaneous petitions pending if any, shall stand closed. _________________
