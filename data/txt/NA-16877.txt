I will talk from a competitive point of view and from middle class point of view, not lower societies which are struggling with basics of education. I acknowledge that but I don't know much about that. 

I will start with the atmosphere we have created in our education system. The fierce competition where marks are the standard benchmark of your perceived intelligence (not to say they are not useful). It has resulted in a system where people study to get the marks only, instead of focusing on learning. Now, I know the reasons and fight for survival in India but why can't we strive to improve?

Every Tom, Dick and Harry is a science student with no real passion or interest in science, why? Parents chose their careers. For what? Good jobs. How many actually get the lucrative flagship jobs that parents force their kids for? "Sharma ji ka ladka engineering kar raha hai, tu mera naam roshan karna beta".

Studying is considered only for nerds even in top institutions, bunking classes is "kewl" and students would rather watch TV shows or something like that, while I don't blame them but I would expect (hope?) the students in elite institutions to be intrigued by knowledge (not necessarily about their course). Of course it doesn't mean they don't have to do anything else in life apart from being stuck in books but the percentage of students in top institutions just "kaating" (passing) time to get jobs is too damn high. **Not judging the students**, they were never fascinated by knowledge in their initial years because of our education system. 

Commerce and Arts/Humanities is for "stupid" kids, well, it's not. Stupid kids sure do study it more than science, and that's a fact.

**Cheating**

Cheating is rampant in Indian education system, it's not deemed unethical and most students or even teachers don't think or talk about it. It's just a way to score more in extremely competitive exams. High class shools are *trying* to address it, how? By being strict, more invigilators, CCTV cameras to prevent students from cheating. But what it results in is more and more students getting caught and not really a significant deterrent.

I for once was never told by teachers to actually learn stuff they teach, even if for tests because in my life if I have scored good marks, it is my knowledge and capability that will be tested and I need to prepare for that. Thankfully I had parents to tell me that. 

[India's renowned education system has a flip side: Ingrained Cheating] (http://www.latimes.com/world/asia/la-fg-c1-india-cheating-20140416-m-story.html)

&gt; India's schools are renowned for producing hordes of talented math and science graduates, tens of thousands of whom go on to excel at universities in the United States and worldwide. But an emphasis on rote learning and the overriding importance of the exams have also spawned an inveterate tradition of cheating.

**Background and what could be done**

Indian education system is quite obsolete, I'd say that is generally agreed upon.

I'll sum this up with an excerpt from [Sugata Mitra's Ted talk] (http://www.ted.com/talks/sugata_mitra_build_a_school_in_the_cloud?language=en).

&gt; Schools came from about 300 years ago, and it came from the last and the biggest of the empires on this planet. ["The British Empire"] Imagine trying to run the show, trying to run the entire planet, without computers, without telephones, with data handwritten on pieces of paper, and traveling by ships. But the Victorians actually did it. They created a global computer made up of people. It's still with us today. It's called the bureaucratic administrative machine. In order to have that machine running, you need lots and lots of people. They made another machine to produce those people: the school. The schools would produce the people who would then become parts of the bureaucratic administrative machine. They must be identical to each other. They must know three things: They must have good handwriting, because the data is handwritten; they must be able to read; and they must be able to do multiplication, division, addition and subtraction in their head. They must be so identical that you could pick one up from New Zealand and ship them to Canada and he would be instantly functional. The Victorians were great engineers. **They engineered a system that was so robust that it's still with us today, continuously producing identical people for a machine that no longer exists.** The empire is gone, so what are we doing with that design that produces these identical people, and what are we going to do next if we ever are going to do anything else with it?

We are still made to learn A LOT of facts, some of which are irrelevant to future study in a lot of subjects, all this in the presence of central database online with things just a Google search away.
