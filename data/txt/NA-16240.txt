--- POST TEXT ---

I've lived in the west (the US) for years where the culture of recognizing servicemen and women is extremely visible. You can see folks in malls walk by men in uniform and say, "thank you for your service." 

Managers at Fast-food joints will waive the check for servicemen, especially during 4th July, Veterans day etc. On airlines, stewardesses will offer them a drink and with pilots' consent will move them to first/business class. It is not uncommon for them to publicly announce a "Thanks for servicemen and women on this flight"

Fast forward to Bengaluru where I now live. The other day, I was behind an Air-Force Corporal standing in a queue at the local RTO office. He was in uniform and was there to renew or transfer his RC papers. The Corporal had a printout of the digital application, fees etc. Except for one gotcha the RTO clerk caught on to - He had a photocopy of the local address (probably quarters) where he was stationed. He tried to explain this to the clerk reviewing the papers and showed his service ID. No go. 

He explained that he would have to take another half day off from work to drive down. The clerk smilingly replied: Sorry sir; next please. He would have to come back with a copy “attested” by his officer. 

I am not questioning the need to verify documents to prevent fraud. However, an address proof for a service quarters is not something worth fudging. This must be a routine affair for servicemen who have to renew their papers every two or three years when they are transferred to a new city/state. 

I was left scratching my head: this is the way Indians tell our servicemen "thank you for your service?"
