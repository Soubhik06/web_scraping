- Background of the Issue:
A year ago, during the annual increment period, my previous company introduced policy changes to address employee attrition. Many employees were leaving due to a poor work environment and rigid rules. 

- Key policy changes included: 
1. Employees must serve a 3-month notice period before resigning. If the company terminates an employee, it must pay 3 months’ salary as part of the final settlement. 
2. For 6 months post-resignation, employees were restricted from joining five specific companies, one of which was "YYY Publishers and Distributors."

- The Incident Leading to Termination:
My office hours were 9:30 AM to 6:00 PM. One day, around 5:45 PM, a senior asked me to wait and take a photo of a guest (despite my primary role being video editing). I waited for the guest, but by 6:30 PM, I felt unwell due to a fever. I approached the director for permission to leave, explaining my condition. He denied my request. When I asked if I would receive overtime for staying late (having worked until midnight on two prior occasions without overtime pay), he became rude and said: 
"Don’t show me this attitude. If you don’t want to work, then leave." 

I apologized and left after delegating the photography task to the IT person, who was experienced with the camera. That evening, I visited a doctor and got medication. The next morning, I emailed my office, informing them of my illness. Although I do not have a copy of the email (as the company deleted my work email account), I have a doctor’s prescription, blood test report, and WhatsApp chat with my team leader confirming that I informed him about my illness. 

Despite this, my leave was rejected, and HR called to inform me of my termination. They sent the termination email but mistyped my email address (both personal and official), so I only received it a day later after following up. 

I went to the office to return my belongings, hand over my work, and complete the formalities. HR assured me that my full and final payment would be credited within 45 days.

-Post-Termination Issues:
After 45 days, I contacted HR for an update. Initially, they said the payment was in process. Later, HR claimed I had violated the non-compete clause by joining "YYY Publishers and Distributors."

Here’s the reality: 
The clause explicitly mentioned "YYY Publishers and Distributors" as a restricted company. However, the company I joined is legally registered as "YYY Books Overseas Pvt Ltd" and operates as "YYY Publications." These are distinct entities. "YYY Publishers and Distributors" is based in Delhi and specializes in foreign language books, whereas the company I joined operates differently. 

I explained this to HR, stating: "I haven’t violated any clause. Please review the agreement carefully." Instead of addressing this, HR became defensive and hung up, saying: "Oh, is that so? Fine, we’ll look into it." 

Since then, HR stopped responding to my calls and emails. When they eventually picked up, they misbehaved, and I recorded the call. They immediately hung up again.

Recently, I received a legal notice from the company with several false allegations.

- Addressing the False Allegations in the Legal Notice:
1. Training Expenses in Delhi Office: 
 - Claim: The company stated they spent significant resources on my training in their Delhi office. 
 - Fact: This is false. I did not receive any training, and the company’s office is in NOIDA, not Delhi (it is only registered in Delhi). 

2. Data Theft Allegation: 
 - Claim: The notice alleges I divulged company data to my current employer, causing losses of ₹20,00,000. 
 - Fact: I never had access to sensitive data. My role in the digital marketing team involved creating GK-related videos using publicly available YouTube content. 

3. Handover Process &amp; Misconduct Allegations:
 - Claim: I did not complete the handover process and misbehaved with HR.
 - Fact: I completed the handover and returned all belongings. I only called HR to request updates on my pending payment, but HR ignored my calls and emails and misbehaved with me instead.

4. Demand to Leave My Current Employer:

The notice demands that I leave "YYY Publishers and Distributors" and provide proof.
- Fact: The notice itself names the wrong company, further proving their confusion between "YYY Publishers and Distributors" and "YYY Publications."

5. Unsolicited Emails and Calls to HR:

- Claim: I sent unsolicited emails and misbehaved with HR.
- Fact: My emails and calls were only to request updates on my settlement, which was overdue.

6. Legal Fee of ₹33,000:

The notice demands ₹33,000 as reimbursement for legal expenses. However, the advocate didn’t even sign the notice, making its validity questionable.

Evidence I Possess:

1. Screenshots of my WhatsApp messages to my team leader regarding my illness.
2. Termination email forwarded after HR’s initial typo.
3. Employment agreement, highlighting the actual non-compete clause.
4. Medical prescription and blood report dated appropriately.
5. Call logs with HR and a recording of HR’s rude behavior.
6. The legal notice with incorrect claims and unsigned by the advocate.

Now I really don't know what to do as I have not faced any such situations before, I don't have all this fancy money to fight against an organisation as I come from a basic lower middle class family of 2 (Me and my mother) and I really need my money as my mother have borrowed money from our relatives to meet our household expenses as I was the one who used to cover 80% of our household expenses (Rent, Food and other stuff)
