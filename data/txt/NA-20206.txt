It's great seeing everybody fighting for net-neutrality. Let's also stand up against censorship and mass-surveillance while we're at it.

Yes, I realize that net-neutrality is currently more pressing as we can still put pressure on the TRAI to act, and that should be taken care of first. But we're already gaining momentum and I'd love to see it directed towards other issues threatening our privacy and internet freedom.

Unlike the net-neutrality issue, laws giving the government the power to tap all communications without ANY regulatory oversight were passed with minimal resistance or debate. Contrast that with PRISM, where the entire operation was kept secret and the NSA faced huge public backlash once information about it was leaked by Snowden. Here we have lawmakers khullam-khulah trampling upon our privacy.

From http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance#India:
"The Indian parliament passed the Information Technology Act of 2008 with no debate, giving the government fiat power to tap all communications without a court order or a warrant. Section 69 of the act states "Section 69 empowers the Central Government/State Government/ its authorized agency to intercept, monitor or decrypt any information generated, transmitted, received or stored in any computer resource if it is necessary or expedient so to do in the interest of the sovereignty or integrity of India, defence of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States or public order or for preventing incitement to the commission of any cognizable offence or for investigation of any offence."

India is setting up a national intelligence grid called NATGRID,[28] which would be fully set up by May 2011 where each individual's data ranging from land records, internet logs, air and rail PNR, phone records, gun records, driving license, property records, insurance, and income tax records would be available in real time and with no oversight.[29] With a UID from the Unique Identification Authority of India being given to every Indian from February 2011, the government would be able track people in real time. A national population registry of all citizens will be established by the 2011 census, during which fingerprints and iris scans would be taken along with GPS records of each household.[30][31]

As per the initial plan, access to the combined data will be given to 11 agencies, including the Research and Analysis Wing, the Intelligence Bureau, the Enforcement Directorate, the National Investigation Agency, the Central Bureau of Investigation, the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence and the Narcotics Control Bureau."
