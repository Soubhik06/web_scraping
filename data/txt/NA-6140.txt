ISSUE NO. 1:­
 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree for a sum of
Rs. 2,28,898/­ alongwith pendentelite and future interest @ 18% per
annum, compounded quarterly against the defendants no.1 and 2,
jointly and severally, as prayed for? OPP. 24. The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff. The
important provisions of law on the basis of which the present case has
to be decided is Section 131 of Negotiable Instrument Act which
provides that :­
 "131. Non­liability of banker receiving payment of
cheque. ­ A banker who has in good faith and without negligence
received payment for a customer of a cheque crossed generally or CS­95228/16 19/26 specially to himself shall not, in case the title to the cheque proves
defective, incur any liability to the true owner of the cheque by reason
only of having received such payment. [[Explanation I.] ­ A banker receives payment of a crossed
cheque for a customer within the meaning of this section
notwithstanding that he credits his customer's account with the
amount of the cheque before receiving payment thereof.]
 [[Explanation II.] ­ It shall be the duty of the banker who
receives payment based on an electronic image of a truncated cheque
held with him, to verify the prima facie genuineness of the cheque to
be truncated and any fraud, forgery or tampering apparent on the
face of the instrument that can be verified with due diligence and
ordinary care.] " 26. It is a standard procedure in banking that payee's bank send
the scanned copy of the cheque to the service branch of the drawer's
bank. On the basis of scanned copy the cheque is cleared by the CS­95228/16 22/26 drawer's bank. In the present matter, by looking at the copy of the
cheque it is apparent that name of the payee of the cheque is written/
put by way of stamp alongwith his account number which is in itself a
very dubious/ suspicious thing that too in respect of a personal saving
bank account. As per Explanation II of Section 131 of Negotiable
Instrument Act it is the duty of the banker who receives payment
based on an electronic image of a truncated cheque held with him, to
verify the prima facie genuineness of the cheque to be truncated and
any fraud, forgery or tampering apparent on the face of the instrument
that can be verified with due diligence and ordinary care. In the
present matter, the forgery and tampering in the cheque in question
was apparent on the face of it and could have been verified with
ordinary care and due diligence on the part of receiver bank which is
having original copy of he cheque. Per contra, it was difficult for the
drawer's bank to ascertain from the scanned copy the factum of
alteration in the name of payee, putting stamp and placing cello tape
on the stamp, while it was quite easy for the payee's bank to ascertain
these suspicious circumstances. 27. D­2/R­3 Sh. Mahender Singh Dahiya himself stated that
the additions/ alterations or interpolation, if any in the cheque is
visible in the scanned image as well as in the original cheque. Thus,
even from the testimony of the defence witness, it is clear that the
additions/ alterations or interpolation is clear in the original cheque. 28. The above­stated facts qua alterations in the cheque
coupled with the fact that the saving bank account of the defendant
no.1 was opened without any introduction just few days prior to
presentation of cheque in question i.e. on 05.03.2011, while the
cheque was cleared on 11.03.2011, infact, the entire amount in that
account was withdrawn by 14.03.2011 are showing negligence on the
part of the employees of the defendant no.2. In view of the above­
stated circumstances, it can be safely held that as per Explanation II
of Section 131 of Negotiable Instrument Act payee's bank i.e. the
defendant no.2 was liable to ensure the genuineness of the cheque in
question which was not done by them due to which the drawer's bank
which had cleared cheque and made the payment and even after that
compensated the drawer. Further, the alterations in the cheque are not
minute and microscopic, rather the same was quite apparent on the
face of it. Thus, the plaintiff/ drawer's bank is entitled to recover the
said amount. Since no interest amount was agreed between the
parties, hence reasonable interest @ 12% per annum is granted to the
plaintiff. This issue is decided, accordingly. ISSUES NO. 2 AND 3:­
 Whether the plaintiff has no locus standi to file the
present suit against the defendant no.2? OPD2. Whether the plaintiff is not having any cause of action
against the defendant no.2? OPD2. 29. The onus to prove this issue was upon the defendants no.2,
however the defendant no.2 has not led any evidence on this aspect.
The testimonies of PW­1 on the aspect that they cleared the cheque
from the account of the defendant no.3 and thereafter, paid the amount
to the defendant no.3 remains uncontroverted and unrebutted. Since
the plaintiff made payment to the defendant no.3, hence ultimately it
was the plaintiff bank who has suffered loss due to the negligence on
the part of the defendant no.2 and fraud being played by the defendant
no.1 qua which a FIR was lodged wherein he has been convicted,
further the defendant no.1 has never appeared before the Court, hence
proceeded ex­parte. Therefore, it can be held that the plaintiff has
locus standi in its favour to file the present suit and a valid cause of
action against the defendant no.2. Accordingly, these issues are
decided in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendants no.1 and 2.
