What's the one thing you need to run a complex manufacturing operation in another country?

A system of law and order.

A foreign entity wanting to 'make in India' should have clear and immediate legal recourse in case there is a breach of contract. 

Absence of this basic requirement means that the only parties willing to 'make in India' have to have huge financial and legal muscle, aka the Walmarts and Fords of the world. Any medium-sized business will think 10 times before setting up any sort of manufacturing in the country if it can't enforce its contracts.

Unfortunately, India lags behind severely in this aspect.

[We rank 172 on the Ease of Enforcing Contracts ranking as per World Bank](http://www.doingbusiness.org/data/exploretopics/enforcing-contracts)

On the same scale, China ranks #5. Taiwan at #14. US at #20. Singapore at #2 and South Korea at #1

Conflicts are a part and parcel of any business, especially complex business such as manufacturing or retail.

If we want to encourage businesses to set up shop here, we have to give them the assurance that their conflicts will be resolved quickly

Our courts are far too backlogged and bogged down by bureaucracy and corruption to make that possible.

Heck, [even McDonald's, with all its financial muscle, is struggling in North India thanks to a rogue partner](https://www.wsj.com/articles/mcdonalds-has-a-mcspicy-problem-its-indian-partner-has-gone-rogue-1507563081)

The wonderful thing about manufacturing in China is that even you, armed with a little under Rs. 5,00,000 and some change, can hop on Alibaba, find a manufacturer, and place an order for, I don't know, 5,000 Arnob shaped dildos. 

In India, you'll have to jump through about two hundred legal issues to even get the dildos designed.

Overhauling the judiciary, unfortunately, is a much bigger task than printing some giant logos and making some slogans.

If the Modi government is serious about making India a manufacturing hub, it must get serious about improving the judiciary.

Else, it's all a meme and a PR campaign
