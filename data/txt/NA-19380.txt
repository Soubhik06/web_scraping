--- POST TEXT ---

Yesterday, I made a quick visit to the post office at GolMarket to deposit some money into my account. It was a typical day, and I decided to go alone. As I approached the counter, there was a man in his 40s who caught my attention. He glanced at my passbook and commented, "You should visit more often and deposit money regularly." I responded with a casual "Yeah, sure," explaining that I don't have much free time. Surprisingly, he then asked, "Why? Are you around 16 or 17 years old?" (It's worth mentioning that he had been making me uncomfortable with his constant staring, even though he assumed I was a minor.) I revealed that I was actually over 21, to which he replied, "But you don't look like it," while giving me an unsettling, top-to-bottom gaze. I didn't say much in return; I simply wanted to complete my deposit and leave.

To my surprise, when I got home, I found a friend request from him on Facebook! Apparently, he had managed to obtain all my personal information in order to find me on social media.

Now, the question arises. What's the value of women's safety and privacy in this country? This could've and, for sure is, happening to countless girls around the world. In today's digital age, one only need a name to get enough data on any person online. The incident I experienced at the post office serves as a stark reminder of the challenges we continue to face in ensuring women's safety and privacy.

The invasion of personal boundaries, such as in my case, where a stranger obtained my personal details to connect with me on social media, is a concerning breach of trust. It highlights the urgent need for public authorities to address the loopholes in our digital infrastructure and implement stricter regulations to safeguard individuals, especially women, from such harassment.
