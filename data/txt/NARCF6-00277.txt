Let me tell you about the time I walked into a car service center and walked out as their unpaid influencer.

So, my car was making this *chik-chik-chik* noise that had become the official theme song of my morning drives. After weeks of ignoring it, I finally dragged myself to the service center, prepared for the usual headache of upsells and “urgent repairs” I didn’t need.

As soon as I walked in, I was greeted by **Rahul**, a service advisor who must moonlight as a motivational speaker. “Sir! You’ve come to the RIGHT place! We’ll fix everything—your brakes, your AC, the noise—just ₹6,000!”

“₹6,000 for everything?” I asked, suspicious.

“Yes, sir! Special deal for you. No hidden charges, full transparency.”

Ah yes, “full transparency.” Spoiler: It was about as transparent as Delhi’s air during peak smog season.

I handed over my keys and tried not to cry as Rahul gave my car a dramatic “pre-service walkthrough” video, which mostly involved him poking random parts of my car while saying, “Sir, this is very critical.” I didn’t even know my car *had* that many “critical” parts.

Fast forward three hours. My phone buzzed.

“Sir, your car is ready!”

I walked in to find Rahul grinning like he’d just won the lottery. “Your bill, sir! Just ₹5,800. We even saved you ₹200!”

Now, let’s pause for a moment. Why did that ₹200 discount feel like they were doing me a favor? I still don’t know, but I smiled like an idiot and said, “Thanks, that’s great!”

But just as I was about to leave, another guy popped out of nowhere with a phone and a microphone. “Sir, can we take a quick feedback video?”

Now, I should’ve said no. I should’ve smelled the scam from a mile away. But no—**like a fool, I said yes.**

The guy shoved his phone in my face, and suddenly I was starring in their Oscar-worthy production of *Happy Customer Chronicles.*

“Sir, how was your experience?”

“Uh… good? The staff was helpful… they washed the car… and, uh, the noise is gone.”

“PERFECT!”

And just like that, I was immortalized as their biggest fan.

The next morning, I woke up feeling pretty smug about my under-budget car servicing. But that smugness didn’t last long.

As I started the car, the *chik-chik-chik* noise returned—except now it was harmonizing with a new sound: *clunk-clunk-clunk.* I turned on the AC, and instead of cool air, it blasted me with the sweet scent of dead socks.

Panicking, I hit the brakes at the first speed bump. The car stopped. **Eventually.**

Fuming, I called Rahul.

“Rahul! What did you guys even fix?!”

“Sir, we did everything within the package you selected.”

“What package?!”

“Sir, the ‘Basic Wash and Pray’ package. Very affordable.”

“YOU SAID ₹6,000 FOR EVERYTHING!”

“Yes, sir. And we replaced the wiper fluid for free. Did you notice how smoothly it sprays?”

At that moment, I knew I had been scammed. But the cherry on top came when I opened Instagram later that day and saw… **my face.**

“Another happy customer! Look at this glowing feedback!” the caption read.

There I was, smiling awkwardly, telling the world about how “amazing” their service was. They even added a cheesy filter and inspirational background music, like I’d just discovered the cure for car noises.

So now, every time I hear that *chik-chik-chik-clunk-clunk* symphony, I’m reminded that I’m not just a car owner. I’m a walking, talking **five-star review** for the biggest scam in town.

Moral of the story? If a service advisor offers you a discount and chai, RUN.

 

**TL;DR**: Took my car to the service center, paid ₹5,800 for them to do nothing but wash it and refill the wiper fluid, got tricked into filming a five-star review, and now I’m Instagram-famous for being scammed.
