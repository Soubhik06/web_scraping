\[ Hi everyone, before you ask---yes I've talked to the fraud dept, and yes they're sending me a new card as we speak. No charges were made and I didn't hand over my SSN \]

PSA: **If you feel even a little weird about a customer service phone call, it is always okay to hang up and reassess.** I let myself get caught by the classic subconscious "I don't want to be rude to this complete stranger, so I will not push back." Don't let that be you!

\---

I called BofA today to talk about an issue I was having with a wire transfer. I called the number **on the back of my card** (NOT from Google or an ad). The conversation that followed was straight up strange, and alarm bells were ringing from the get-go. I'll list them below so people reading this will know what to look for, but basically the guy was scamming me under the guise of "verifying my information" to generate a PIN so that next time I call BofA, I don't have to go through this process. Even after so many gut feelings of "this isn't quite right", the scammer did manage to get my credit card number, CVV, and expiration date, as well as my address and phone.

🚩🚩Red flags 🚩🚩

1. No phone tree. The computerized voice just said "hang on while we reach a representative" and did not ask me for any info, nor why I was calling, nor even say "welcome to bank of america" now that I think about it.
2. The guy started asking for my information without explanation--I really had to press him for why he needed *all* of my card information.
3. He didn't know anything about me. No "so this is the account ending in 7890?" or "can you verify your phone number starting with 802?"
4. **He was pushy.** When I asked him why he needed info, or if he was sure something was necessary, he got kind of huffy.
5. I hate to say it, but he was Indian. A lot of these spam call centers run out of India, and while I know that many legitimate customer service centers run out of India as well, I do always have that initial suspicion.
6. 🚩While generating the "verification code", he (a) read/mumbled some 'terms and conditions' very quickly, with what sounded like a website that was NOT bankofamericaDOTcom, and (2) the "PIN generation system" had ads in it, that he told me to "just ignore, please, do not be alarmed". Why would Bank of America use a system with ads? Why would he assume I'm alarmed?
7. 🚩**He gave me a new telephone number to call back** in the future. HUGE red flag, and that's when I knew I 100% had to shut down my card.
8. 🚩The new number he gave me was hella suspicious. According to its wikipedia article \[not sure if I'm allowed to link it here\], the number "is an American service offering advertising-supported directory assistance". Again, why in the world would such a huge corporation as BofA use a free directory assistance service with ads?
9. When I asked the fraud department lady if this was a scam, she said YES.

After that phone call, I called a *different* number that I found on the back of my *debit* card, to continue with the wire transfer question. The woman I connected with asked if I had any other questions, and i hesitated before saying, "Yeah, actually.... so I just had a weird conversation with someone who may have been a scammer...." She helped me cancel my card and get a new one, and filed a report within their system. Problem solved, I think?

And after all that, I still haven't solved my wire transfer issue. :-(

\---

Looking back, it's clear I should have hung up and reported this to the fraud department around step 4. I didn't, because of my inclination "not to be rude" and "I don't know how X works, therefore I should assume the best". But, mostly, **I ignored my gut feelings because I had called the right number**, so I thought there was no way it was a scam. I feel quite dumb right now, but I'm telling y'all this so that you don't have to, too.

\---

**Questions for those with experience:**

* **Is there anything else I should be on the lookout for** now that the scammers have my (old) CC information, address, name, and phone number? And presumably recordings of my voice saying numbers and "yes" and "no", etc.
* **How did a scammer intercept Bank of America's own phone number??** I know that it is common for scammers to buy up adjacent phone numbers (ones that are similar to the one you want to call), hoping to snag victims who misdial. But I checked my recent calls and I 100% had called the correct number. So how did this happen?

\---

Edit with some great (but wrong) solutions already suggested:

* **Your phone probably has malware.** Great suggestion, but I happened to be calling from my parents' landline.
* **You probably misdialed.** I definitely didn't. The landline is one with a screen and a menu that allows me to scroll back and see previous numbers dialed. This is how I am 100% sure I called the correct number.
* **The number on the card might be worn away.** The card's only from 2019 &amp; besides, it matches the number on the site.
* **A scammer probably called earlier, your parents picked up, &amp; the scammer stayed on the line to redirect your call.** Also a valid idea, but the last caller was my brother, so nope.

Keep 'em coming, y'all! I really want to know how this happened.

UPDATE: Y'all it happened to my mother calling Wells Fargo on her cell phone. The plot freaking thickens. Now signs are pointing towards a problem with our phone provider.
