The Government of India has recently issued a directive that mandates every smartphone manufacturer and importer to pre-install the government-owned app, Sanchar Saathi, on their devices before selling them in India.

While the Indian government mandating global and Indian private entities to forcefully install an application is an issue, Apple, which has nearly 10% smartphone share in India, became the first smartphone manufacturing company to speak against the directive and said that it will not comply with the directive and will submit its concerns very soon.

Pemmasani Chandra Sekhar, Minister of State for Communications, claims that Apple was not part of the working group, which the DoT reportedly formed to address concerns from smartphone manufacturing companies. It is also important to note that there is no publicly available information about any such working group committee to date, raising serious transparency questions regarding the legitimate stakeholder consultations that were not made public.

Before we reason out why mandating Sanchar Sathi on every smartphone can be a nightmare for privacy and security-related concerns, let us briefly list out the problems with the app:

After the furore, Telecommunications Minister Jyotiraditya Scindia lied before the media, saying that the app can be uninstalled. According to the official directive issued by the DoT on November 28, 2025, point 7(b) states that manufacturers should pre-install the app in a manner that ensures the app’s functions are not disabled or restricted. This means that the application must be permanently installed (for it to function), making the uninstallation of the application infeasible. Otherwise, it is a non-compliance and invites consequences from the Ministry.

It is essential to note that the November 28 notice remains in effect unless the government revokes or amends the directive, or a court strikes it down, regardless of how many statements politicians or ministers make.

Referring to the comments made by Scindia, Mishi Choudhary, the founder of Software Freedom Law Centre, India (SFLC.in), said, “Minister statements are just statements”, and she said the press notes and the official notifications should also reflect the same. “In any case, every tech decision toggles between mandatory and voluntary in India, making it impossible to have user autonomy,” she said in a written statement to MediaNama.
