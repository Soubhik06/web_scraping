https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/banking/rbi-against-dropping-card-storage-clause-in-new-rules/articleshow/85473789.cms

"Under the new norms, millions of cardholders -- both debit and credit -- making payments online in 2022 may have to enter their 16-digit card numbers every time they make a payment online as opposed to just authenticating these transactions through the CVV"

RBI opposes this on the grounds of third party vendors storing your card data and it being prone to breach (if only there was a Data Protection Bill or something that needed to be passed).

Of course, if RBI does this, people, especially elderly, will try to use other payment methods that are easier to remember, like UPI. Which would be great if UPI didn't have [failure rates](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/surging-upi-failure-rates-worry-banks/articleshow/78779204.cms?from=mdr) that don't even allow you to get refunds in some cases (everyone who uses UPI regularly will experience failure every few days - a rate you NEVER face with cards) or [network transaction limits](https://inc42.com/buzz/all-you-need-to-know-about-npcis-30-cap-on-upi-transactions/) that penalise you for using the most popular UPI apps like Google Pay or PhonePe.

If there was a gold medal for unnecessary regulation India would win it every year and all other countries would just get relegated from the league . At which point India would probably introduce a regulation to stop over regulation since we apparently have a problem with being effective at anything.
