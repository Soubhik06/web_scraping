Tl;Dr my girlfriend signed a rent lease agreement which turned out to be fake. She lost the "rent" for the first and last month, the security deposit and "insurance" money, which she paid upfront. Totals to around $7000.

* My gf, a Neonatal ICU nurse from Florida recently got a job in a hospital in Manhattan. She looked up housing through the website nakedapartments (well reputed, they apparently pre screen landlords and brokers for security) and found one she liked. The apartment listing has the broker's name, license number, contact number and e-mail. She double checked his licensee number in the NY Gov database of brokers and called him at the phone number provided+mailed him.

* He emailed her a lease agreement and account number for wire transfer. She sent the money and the signed lease this week, and upon showing up at that address today was told that her lease is not recognized, nor do they use a broker. The money stolen was almost all of her savings.

* She called her bank and filed a claim. She met the cops, who told her that she was stupid to decide on a place online, although they did file a case. But I am told that hers is an established method many people, particularly those who live a long way away, use. The cops told her she should have had a friend in NY do this for her, but she didn't have any. They offered her no sympathy, said it was under Florida jurisdiction, not NY, since she paid the money while in Florida, and that they couldn't do much. They wondered if she will learn a lesson from this "mistake".

* She is now alone, young, in the big city, nearly broke with no permanent place to live in, and terrified. She might or might not get her money back. She will have to find another apartment tomorrow and show up at work on Monday. Meanwhile, a guy who either is, or claims to be a government certified broker, has all her money.

Addendum: Strangely, that apartment listing still exists in the website if I use the saved link, but when I start at the homepage and narrow my price and location down to zone in on it, it's not there.
