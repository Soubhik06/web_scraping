Union telecom minister Jyotiraditya Scindia has said that the decision to allocate satellite spectrum administratively will offer consumers more choices, as per a Reuters report. This statement from the minister came amidst a lack of common ground between Elon Musk’s Starlink and Indian telecom major Reliance Jio over spectrum rights for satellite services.

Earlier on October, 2024, Scindia had confirmed that India will allocate satellite internet spectrum through an administrative process. Government’s  decision to allocate satellite spectrum instead of auctioning it has drawn criticism from telecom operators, particularly Reliance Jio, which has argued that auctioning spectrum would ensure a level playing field.

Unlike terrestrial spectrum which operates at lower frequencies and is auctioned due to its exclusivity, satellite spectrum functions at higher frequencies and is inherently shareable. Scindia had earlier clarified that the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) would determine the pricing for this spectrum to ensure fairness and transparency. This decision has been codified in the Telecommunication Act, 2023, which mandates administrative allocation for satellite communication services.

Scindia defended the move by citing the unique nature of satellite technology. “You can’t auction something that is shared,” he stated, adding that global norms also follow administrative allocation for such spectrum. He further explained that the technology behind satellite communication is evolving requiring companies to adapt rather than rely on traditional business models.

For consumers, the move promises more choices in accessing broadband, especially in remote and underserved areas where terrestrial networks struggle to reach.

However, the policy has faced pushback from domestic telecom operators like Reliance Jio and Bharti Airtel. These companies argue that administrative allocation could disadvantage them after they invested billions in auctioned spectrum for terrestrial networks. Reliance Jio, for instance, spent $19 billion in recent auctions and fears losing customers to satellite-based services offering potentially lower-cost alternatives.

Foreign companies like Starlink and Project Kuiper have already submitted applications to launch satellite broadband services in India but their approvals are still pending. However, the government’s approach has not been without controversy. Critics have drawn parallels with past allocation practices that led to allegations of favoritism like 2G scam.

In an opinion piece on November, 2024, Medianama critiqued the administrative allocation of satellite spectrum, drawing parallels to the contentious 2G scam. As per the analysis, the discretionary power granted by administrative allocation could lead to corruption and lacks the transparency offered by auctions, which the Supreme Court of India has endorsed for the allocation of scarce natural resources.

The report also highlighted the potential for administrative allocation to become a bargaining chip especially in high-profile negotiations like those involving Starlink and Tesla, suggesting this could compromise the integrity of spectrum allocation.

The analysis also opposed differential pricing for various use-cases, advocating instead for uniform internet connectivity with obligations for rural service coverage. The report called for criteria that enable broader participation including startups, to foster competition and innovation in the Indian telecom sector, potentially leading to lower data costs similar to the impact of Jio’s entry.

Additionally, it raised security concerns regarding foreign control over satellite internet particularly data privacy and neutrality, given past instances where connectivity was politically manipulated.
