Truecaller entered Q2 FY25 with ongoing investments in AI, a growing base of premium and business users, and a steady push to expand recurring revenues. At the same time, the company faced pressure from rising tax obligations in India and weaker ad demand tied to geopolitical tensions. It posted 21% net sales growth in constant currency while keeping Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) margins at 43%, excluding incentive costs. Currency fluctuations, especially the strengthening of the Swedish krona (SEK) against the Indian rupee, had a visible impact on reported financials.

Truecaller’s user base continued to grow, reaching 427 million monthly active users (MAUs) and 367 million daily active users (DAUs), reflecting high engagement levels across key markets.

“While our financials were impacted by the strengthening of the SEK, the organic growth, underlying was strong across all our revenue streams,” said CEO Rishit Jhunjhunwala.

CFO Odd Bolin said the company’s effective tax rate is rising gradually as more of its profits are now being taxed in India.

“We are managing our tax exposure within the limits set by the transfer pricing regulations that we have to adhere to in order to ensure that we fulfill the expectations of both the Indian and the Swedish tax authorities”, Bolin stated.

He further added, “The tax rate is a combination of the Swedish corporate tax rate and the Indian tax rate. We see the effective tax rate gradually increasing from historical levels due to more business being recognized in India.”

“The balance between India and Sweden is slowly moving upwards as more profits are taxed in India, which will increase our tax rate over time”, Bolin added.

These remarks build on commentary from Q1 FY25 , when Bolin said the 25–26% tax band would remain steady, but “with worsening quarters.” The company had also previously confirmed a transfer pricing-related visit from Indian tax authorities, though it did not expect material liabilities. The Q2 statements indicate a structural shift in tax exposure driven by India’s growing contribution to Truecaller’s top line.

Truecaller’s leadership repeatedly underscored that artificial intelligence and machine learning remain fundamental to its platform. These technologies drive everything from spam and fraud detection to caller identification and personalisation.

Jhunjhunwala stated, “We’re heavily reliant on machine learning and AI to improve our spam detection, fraud prevention, and user experience .”

He added that “Our models are continuously upgraded, and we incorporate community feedback to enhance our AI-driven fraud and spam detection systems.”

He also said AI supports a wide range of user-facing functions.

“We are using AI to power a lot of our features — whether it’s our spam detection, caller identification, or personalized recommendations. AI helps us deliver more relevant and safer experiences to our users.”

Bolin also reinforced the company’s commitment to AI as a core defence mechanism.

“AI and machine learning are at the core of our efforts to combat communication-based fraud, and we are making significant investments in these areas.”

Bolin further stated that improving the accuracy of these systems directly affects user perception and retention.

“One of our key focuses is to leverage AI to improve the accuracy of our caller ID and spam detection, which directly impacts user trust and engagement.”

These remarks align with earlier product moves such as AI-based fraud logo detection and Message IDs and confirm that Truecaller continues to invest heavily in safety and trust through AI.

Truecaller reported stronger advertising performance in Q2 FY25, led by direct ad sales and the introduction of new ad formats aimed at improving engagement and monetisation.

“Now, let’s talk a bit about AdTech, a very critical area for us for long-term monetization and growth,” said Jhunjhunwala. “Our relentless focus remains on delivering highly valuable and attention-grabbing properties for our advertising partners.”

Jhunjhunwala introduced Truecaller Masthead, a new ad unit placed at the core of the user experience — the moment an incoming call appears.

“This is a truly premium advertising property, strategically positioned right to the heart of the Truecaller experience, which is in connection with an incoming call… With Masthead, we are offering advertisers an unparalleled placement, reaching billions of brand moments every single day.”

He said the format had already generated strong early interest and would begin hitting the market during the current quarter.

The company also launched Truecaller Play, a productised version of its video ad inventory.

“It’s designed to highlight our innovative, engaging elements and is exceptionally well positioned to capitalise on the upcoming festive demand periods,” he said. “Play will also begin its phased rollout during this quarter.”

Jhunjhunwala described both formats as part of a longer-term strategy to increase market share in advertising and improve gross margins.

“They are concrete demonstrations of our strategic dedication to capturing greater market share in advertising. These formats enable us to offer deeper commitments to our partners, leading directly to higher gross margins for Truecaller.”

Truecaller acknowledged that external conflict affected Indian ad revenue in Q2. Bolin was direct about the impact:

“The most important geopolitical uncertainty during the second quarter was the India–Pakistan conflict, which decreased revenue and revenue per DAU because most of our users and revenue come from India.”

“It materially slowed down the Indian ads market for some time, influencing revenue and revenue per DAU.”

He further added, “The underlying ads market was quite stable compared to last year, although we did see some impact in terms of demand softness in relation to the geopolitical uncertainty with the India-Pakistan conflict that we had some time ago, the Middle East situation, and from the continuing global tariff discussions. We did, however, not see any major price changes in the ad segment.”

Despite these headwinds, the company said the overall ad market was relatively stable.

Truecaller for Business suite expanded in Q2, with new feature rollouts and stronger relationships with existing customers.

“Truecaller for Business, we had a great quarter… we continue to expand our suite of offerings, while expanding on our existing customer relationships as well,” said Jhunjhunwala.

One notable product update was Secure Calls, which verifies the identity of businesses and reduces the risk of spoofed or impersonated calls.

Although Truecaller did not revisit it in Q2, the company had earlier flagged potential regulatory constraints under the Telecom Commercial Communication Customer Preference Regulation (TCCCPR), 2018, which prohibits platforms from tagging or restricting calls from designated government and commercial numbers. These restrictions could limit how far Truecaller can scale its verified caller products.

Premium offerings are gaining traction, especially on iOS. In Q2, Bolin said:

“For subscriptions, all underlying metrics are pointing in the right direction and it continues to be a good revenue driver for us. We grew the number of subscribers by 28% and the average revenue per subscriber by 10%. iOS subscriber growth accelerated during the quarter and the number of subscribers grew by 25% on iOS, in Q2 alone, by more than 50% compared to the same period last year. “

He further added, “iOS adoption in India started out slightly slower than some other markets, but gradually during the quarter, we started to see an increased adoption of iOS subscriptions in India, too.”

This builds on momentum from Q4 FY24 and Q1 FY25, when the launch of real-time caller ID and spam blocking for iPhone users began lifting iOS revenue. In Q4 FY24 , iOS users generated over 40% of total subscription revenue, despite making up a much smaller share of the overall user base compared to Android.

Truecaller is increasingly prioritizing recurring revenue through subscriptions and business tools to drive more stable growth.

“Recurring revenues now make up about a third of our total revenue, and they have doubled over the past two years’ time,” said Jhunjhunwala.

“We continue to deliver on the strategy that we’ve set out, which contains user growth in all revenue streams with an extra focus on growing our recurring revenues.”

The company is also looking to deepen its international presence through localisation, hiring, and sales expansion.

“Growth continues in India, but we also grow in many other markets globally. We are increasing our efforts in those markets… by putting boots on the ground, localising our product more, and increasing our sales efforts in those regions too.”
