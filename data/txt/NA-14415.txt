Instagram, owned by Meta, has suspended ( Archive ) the account of political satirist Shamita Yadav for copyright violations. Yadav is widely known as The Ranting Gola on social media.

The account was deleted on May 28, 2025, as shown in a screenshot in a short video she shared, which informed about the suspension of her account. “180 days left to appeal, or we’ll permanently disable your account,” reads the rest of the notice from Instagram.Â

The satirist claims that Meta has suspended her account without prior notice, calling it bullying and harassment. “They are silencing both my voice and yours in the name of copyright violations,” she said in a post on X (formerly Twitter). She added that she wants her account restored.

The controversy around the usage of copyrighted content by the content creator was intensified following the Asian News International (ANI) vs YouTubers case , particularly after Mohak Mandal spoke against ANI’s demands. ANI has led a campaign against YouTubers, charging licensing fees of up to Rs. 45 lakh for clips used without prior permission. MediaNama has independently verified the claims that were first reported by The Reporters Collective.

An anonymous YouTuber told MediaNama that ANI demanded Rs. 18 lakh for its content in two videos, ranging from 10 seconds to over a minute. “In my opinion, this is a form of blackmail,” said the editor of another YouTube channel, which paid the settlement amount to remove copyright strikes from ANI.

Content creators on YouTube, Instagram, and other social media platforms primarily depend on advertisements and sponsorships for revenue. Arbitrary removal of social media handles and YouTube channels impacts the income of content creators.

According to Section 52 of the Copyright Act, personal use, criticism, reviewing, or reporting of current affairs doesn’t fall under copyright violations. However, content creators face restrictions from copyright holders like ANI despite the constitutional provision. This raises severe questions on the fair use of publicly available copyrighted content.

Under The Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, intermediaries must inform users if they remove content under clause (b) of sub-rule (1) of rule 3, which addresses the usage of copyrighted content on a user’s account without permission. However, as per Yadav’s claims, Meta has not sent a prior notice or any specific justification for the suspension. This raises a few more questions.
