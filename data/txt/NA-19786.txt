TITLE: Why Are Consumer Rights Taken Less Seriously?
DATE: 2025-03-07
SCORE: 1
URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/india/comments/1j5olmv/why_are_consumer_rights_taken_less_seriously/

--- POST TEXT ---

It honestly blows my mind how little consumer rights are taken seriously in many places, especially in countries like India. People are constantly getting scammed, and the system for protecting consumers is weak, to say the least.

Take e-commerce in India, for example. Many consumers end up receiving faulty, damaged, or even counterfeit products. When they try to get a refund or replacement, they face an endless cycle of poor customer service where no one takes responsibility. Even if you escalate the issue, you often just get a generic response that delays the process even further, and at times, nothing is done at all. It’s a huge hassle to even get a simple issue addressed, and far too many people are left with no solution.

In India, while we technically have laws like the **Consumer Protection Act (2019)**, they’re often ineffective in practice. There’s little enforcement, and companies continue to get away with misleading practices. Major e-commerce platforms like Flipkart and Amazon frequently sell products that don't match their descriptions, are of poor quality, or even counterfeit. Refunds or exchanges are often delayed for weeks or denied altogether. In fact, many people are forced to settle for partial refunds or no resolution at all, despite the law being on their side. The lack of meaningful follow-up from these platforms shows just how little they respect consumer rights.

India also faces issues beyond just e-commerce. Misleading ads and shady financial schemes are rampant. People are often tricked into signing up for services or products that don’t exist, or that don’t work as promised. Telecom companies, for instance, often overcharge customers, and despite the **Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)** setting rules, enforcement is weak and complaints are often ignored.

Contrast that with Europe, where things are very different. The **EU Consumer Rights Directive** guarantees that consumers can return products within 14 days for a full refund, giving people peace of mind when shopping. And when it comes to faulty goods or services, the process is clear and often handled with ease. There's no endless back-and-forth or endless hold times. Companies are legally obligated to take responsibility, and they face serious consequences if they don't.

Then there's **GDPR**, which gives consumers in Europe full control over their personal data. It's a major step forward in data protection, something India is still lacking. There is no robust data protection law in India, which leaves people vulnerable to having their data misused or exposed in leaks.

Additionally, Europe’s **European Consumer Centres Network (ECC-Net)** provides a solid support system for cross-border disputes. If you buy something from another EU country and run into problems, the ECC-Net helps resolve the issue. In India, however, even resolving disputes within a single state can be a nightmare, especially when the companies involved have no incentive to do the right thing.

The problem in India isn’t that there aren’t laws; it’s that the enforcement is weak and the system is often set up to benefit businesses over consumers. Strong consumer protection laws with real consequences for companies would make a massive difference.

Europe has shown that consumer rights can be protected through strong laws and proper enforcement. If India took steps to implement similar systems and held businesses accountable, it could drastically improve the consumer experience and prevent people from being constantly taken advantage of.
