What Is a Savings Account?

savings account

Key Things to Know Before Opening a Savings Account
Before you open a savings account, it’s important to review the key features that affect your daily banking, overall costs, and long-term savings growth.
Minimum Balance Requirements
Most savings accounts require maintaining a fixed minimum balance. Banks set different thresholds for different customer segments. If your balance falls below the required level, then the bank may levy a penalty on it.
Check the requirement of the monthly or quarterly balance so that you will be in a position to choose an account in accordance with your regular usage.
Interest Rates on Savings Accounts
Interest rates determine how much your savings grow. Banks calculate interest based on the daily or monthly balance, credited at scheduled intervals. Rates vary across institutions and across account categories.
Some banks offer much higher rates on premium variants. Compare the interest rates and the calculation method to estimate how much you could earn.
Fees and Charges to Be Paid
Service charges can dent the net value of your savings. Some important fees include non-maintenance charges, ATM withdrawal fees, issuance or renewal charges for debit cards, costs regarding fund transfers, cheque book charges, and SMS alert fees.
It is always better to discuss the fee schedule with them before opening the account to avoid unwarranted expenses.
Digital Banking Features
At the core of modern banking is digital access. Look for mobile and internet banking platforms that support fast transfers, bill payments, account tracking, and secure authentication. A digital savings account is a preferable option, especially for those who would rather manage their finances online.
Debit Card Facilities and Limits
Savings accounts come with a debit card for withdrawal from an ATM and everyday spending. All cards have limits on daily withdrawals and transactions.
Some cards come with benefits, like rewards or contactless payment. Check out the limits, features available, and what, if any, annual fees relate to the card.
Access to Funds and Withdrawal Regulations
Banks impose limits on ATM withdrawals, branch withdrawals, and online transfers via IMPS or NEFT.
Some deposits have very short holding periods before the funds are available. Knowing these helps you project how quickly you can access the money if need be.
Safety, Security and Deposit Insurance
Fixed regulatory and security norms are followed in savings accounts. The fraud monitoring tools, authentication options, and other digital security features provided by the bank should be evaluated.
Deposits up to INR 5 lakh are insured by DICGC, adding an extra layer of protection. You should consider the reputation of the bank as well as the available security measures while selecting any account.
Documents Required to Open a Savings Account
Opening a savings account requires you to submit basic KYC documents that establish your identity, residence, and tax details. Banks accept a host of valid documents, and the process is quite seamless.
Identity Proof:
You can provide any government-issued document, containing details of the applicant’s name and address
Passport
Driving Licence
Proof of possession of Aadhaar number
The Voter’s Identity Card issued by the Election Commission of India
Job card issued by NREGA duly signed by an officer of the State Government.
The letter issued by the National Population Register.
Address Proof:
If the applicant isn’t able to provide an updated Identity proof, the following documents can be submitted.
Utility bill which is not more than two months old of any service provider (electricity, telephone, post-paid mobile phone, piped gas, water bill)
Property or Municipal tax receipt
Pension or family Pension Payment Orders (PPOs) issued to retired employees by Government Departments or Public Sector Undertaking, if they contain the address.
Letter of allotment of accommodation from employer issued by State Government or Central Government Departments, statutory or regulatory bodies, public sector undertakings, scheduled commercial banks, financial institutions and listed companies and leave and licence agreements with such employers allotting official accommodation.
It is important for the customer to submit ID proof with current address within a period of three months of submitting the documents.
PAN or Form 60:
You need to provide your PAN. In case you don’t have a PAN, you may submit Form 60 to fulfil your tax reporting obligation as defined in Income Tax Rules, 1962.
Additional Documents:
Other documents in respect of the nature of business and financial status of the customer, or the equivalent e-documents thereof, as may be required by the Regulated Entity.
How to Open a Savings Account Online
Opening a savings account online has become quick and requires only a few basic information and documents. You will be able to apply online for the whole process from your phone or laptop in just a few easy steps.
Step 1: Download the bank’s mobile application or visit the official website.
Step 2: Ensure you have your PAN and Aadhaar card. Complete the Video KYC from your phone or computer.
Step 3: After a successful verification from the bank, your account will be activated. You can now use the online banking features, with the welcome kit delivered soon after.
