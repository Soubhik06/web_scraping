 dispute, thereby re-litigating issues cognate to a civil proceeding3. 11. In order to constitute an offence under Section 420 IPC, as explained

 indispensable elements: (i) deception of a person; (ii) fraudulent or dishonest
 inducement to deliver property; and (iii) dishonest intention at the very

 Club (1940) Ltd. v. State of U.P.5 , where the Court emphasised that mere
 failure to keep a promise or a subsequent breach of contract, absent
 dishonest intent at inception, does not constitute the offence of cheating. The

 genuine and voluntary instrument. In light of these findings, the essential

 a "false document" or one procured by initial deception. Hence, the offence
 of cheating is not made out. 13. The findings in the civil suit also undermine any suggestion of
