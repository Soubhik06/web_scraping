Check out this link posted yesterday - [Gujarat government bought solar power at Rs 9.13-15 per unit from private players](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/gujarat-govt-bought-solar-power-at-rs-9-13-15-per-unit-from-private-players-7261958/). You probably know that solar energy contracts are being sold for 2 rupees or so today, so they appear to be overpaying by 6-7x (remember this figure). 

Clicking the article, your fears are confirmed. Yes, seems like a money transfer from the BJP government to Adani.

&gt; During this five-year period, the maximum solar power has been bought from Adani Power Limited, which sold 328 million units at Rs 15 per unit. There was a 10.2 per cent rise in the quantum of power bought from the Adani Group.

For instance, in an auction for 500 MW of solar projects conducted by GUVNL in December 2020, Rs 1.99 per unit was the lowest bid. GUVNL officials said the state government is paying Rs 15 per unit as per the Power Purchase Agreements of 2010-11.

Something the article doesn't mention is why the prices are Rs 15 per unit. A quick google finds this article from 2011 - [Adani Power to start two solar projects](https://www.livemint.com/Companies/qWgZoqHQHuz4AEd9aNdkqN/Adani-Power-to-start-two-solar-projects.html) which says 

&gt; In January 2009, Gujarat announced a policy to promote solar energy, fixing a tariff of ₹ 15 per kWh for developers using solar photovoltaic technology, and ₹ 13 per unit for utilities running on a solar thermal platform—both for 12 years.

But why on earth would they be promising a price more than 6x what it costs in 2021? Another link shows why - [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/solar-pv-prices?time=2009..latest) - between 2009 and 2019 the prices of solar panels fell by more than 6x ($2.39 -&gt; $0.38). 

A price of Rs 15 per unit in 2009 was equivalent to a price of Rs 2 in 2021 taking into account price of solar panels.

---

One thing to remember about solar plants is that unlike coal or gas plants, they cost very little to operate. Once they are built, they generate electricity for a minor maintenance cost. This is how they're able to quote low prices - they negotiate a low fixed price and get a contract for 10+ years. They make back their investment and then some. This promise of a return is why they invested in the first place.

This is a win-win. They get a return and we get electricity at Rs 2 per unit. Of course solar panel prices will continue to fall. In 2025 there might be an article asking "why are we paying Rs 2 per unit when new contracts are being given out at Rs 1?" Well, because they bought the panels in 2021, when it was more expensive.

---

I say all this not to defend or attack anyone. This is perhaps shoddy reporting from Indian Express, but likely not malicious. Clearly the Gujarat government hasn't done anything wrong by merely sticking to their 2009 policy (that will expire this year anyway). Clearly Adani and others haven't done anything wrong by providing power at the rate the government said it would buy.

I'm not criticising the people on that thread. They read the article, which heavily implied (but didn't outright state) that there was corruption going on. It did not give any further details that would have made it a non-story.

I say all this to point out - all of us are susceptible to fake news. We don't verify and investigate further when we read a story that confirms our biases. In this cases, those biases are politicians are corrupt, crony capitalists steal taxpayer money. Our biases are confirmed and we are even more certain about what we thought.

My only request - let's try to be less certain.
