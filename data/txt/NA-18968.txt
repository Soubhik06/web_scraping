In India right now, many scams are being carried out in the name of trading, targeting innocent people. These victims often lack the necessary knowledge, and some are in a rush to become rich. This makes them fall for schemes promising returns of 20-30% per month, which is impossible in the financial market. No one can consistently earn more than 5-10% through trading.

What's shocking to me is learning that Abhishek Kar earned crores through trading schemes. He presented himself as a skilled trader, and people believed him. Many paid large sums for his tips – reportedly as much as one lakh per tip – which is how he made a fortune. I can't confirm the credibility of the source, as I heard this from other YouTubers who exposed his actions. Surprisingly, despite these allegations, he continues to run his YouTube channel, podcasts, and discussions about trading, with no action from SEBI.

This kind of situation isn’t unique to him. Recently, in Assam, scams have become rampant. One 22-year-old scammed people by promising 30% returns every 60 days, amassing around 2200 crore rupees. Many victims were doctors who invested their untaxed income in hopes of making it white or earning more. However, they are hesitant to report these scams, possibly out of embarrassment or fear of revealing their involvement in such schemes.

Another massive scam involved DB Stock Brokers in Guwahati, with claims that the head, Dipankar Barman, scammed over 5000 crore rupees. Shockingly, there are videos of him counting his money to the beat of music. It's baffling how people, who hesitate to part with small amounts, blindly invest crores in these scams, believing they can double or triple their money quickly.

The new generation's obsession with getting rich fast, no matter the means, is worrying. This growing culture of quick money schemes could lead to a dangerous future for India.
