The Enforcement Directorate (ED) has filed a chargesheet against Fairplay betting app operators, according to a Hindustan Times report . The chargesheet revealed that the promoters of the Curacao-registered online casino laundered over Rs. 4,000 crore from India through dummy bank accounts, fraudulent companies, and inflated import bills. Viacom18 Media initially filed the case against Fairplay for illegally streaming Indian Premier League (IPL) matches on its platform.

Fairplay is an illegal offshore sports gambling and online casino app that allows users to bet on different sports tournaments, most popularly on IPL matches. The company is reportedly registered in Curacao, an island nation. Several offshore online casinos and gambling platforms, like Stake and Fairplay, obtain their gambling licenses from this Caribbean island nation.

According to the report, “Krish [Laxmichand] Shah, believed to be operating Fairplay from Dubai, established entities like Fair Play Sport LLC and Fairplay Management DMCC to manage operations in India. He even shifted the appâs domain registration to Dubai-based Anil Dadlani to conceal ownership.”

The ED has filed a chargesheet against Krish Laxmichand Shah, Chirag Shah, and Chintan Shah under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) . The three reportedly used a network of 400 dummy bank accounts and fake import transactions to funnel money outside India. In February 2025, the agency arrested Chirag and Chintan Shah, while Krish Laxmichand Shah remains at large.

In 2024, the app also allowed users to reportedly bet on the General Elections of India, as uncovered by the ED probe.

Jay Sayta, a gambling and technology lawyer, told MediaNama that other illegal betting platforms usually use mule bank accounts. Interestingly, Fairplay was using dummy accounts and bogus and inflated import bills here for money laundering.

Dummy accounts are bank accounts that an entity uses without the knowledge of the account holder. Fairplay could have been using these through forgery, Sayta added. “It is basically a fake account, unlike mule bank accounts, where the owner knows that their bank account is being used by another entity. They are quite similar.”

Fairplay was also using fake import transactions for money laundering. Sayta said, “The company, through fake invoices, showed that they were importing goods from outside India, when that was not the case. Even if there was, they have been accused of producing inflated invoices for the goods that they have imported. Fairplay was allegedly using cryptocurrencies as well.”

As per the report, Fairplay operators used the funds they funneled through 400 dummy accounts for investing in businesses and buying properties across the country

Then, the operators routed the money through shell companies before they transferred it to offshore entities in Hong Kong, China, and Dubai. As per the ED chargesheet, the list of shell companies included NS Online Services, Dynamic Services, and Signox Overseas, among others. Some proceeds were reportedly also pushed through pharmaceutical companies that were engaged in fake billing.

Fairplay was also allegedly using Beffy Finserv and Truefund Innovations India, payment gateways that helped the illegal betting app disguise transactions and payouts worth Rs. 3,000 crore, “by misusing arrangements with Axis Bank,” the report added.

Illegal sports betting and gambling websites in India continue running with the help of different internet domains. The company has migrated from Fairplay24.in, the URL, which is not operational anymore, to the Fairplay.live internet domain, which allows players to gamble after signing up. This is a common practice that various offshore betting platforms like Stake, PariMatch, Fairplay, and Mahadev Online Book follow.

In December 2024, the government, in a reply to a question in Lok Sabha, revealed that between 2022-24, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) blocked 692 online betting/gambling sites.

Such actions have little impact as these illegal companies start operating under a different internet domain as soon as the government cuts usersâ access to them.

During the investigation, the ED reportedly found a link between Fairplay and the Mahadev Online Book platform, another illegal gambling platform that the ED is probing. As per the chargesheet , Krish Laxmichand Shah sold a 70% ownership stake of Fairplay to Saurabh Chandrakar, promoter of Mahadev Online Book. Following this, Krish Laxmichand Shah moved Fairplay to a new app, and Chandrakar started managing the payment gateway for the platform.

The Mahadev Online Book platform and its “sister concerns” were generating Rs. 450 crore monthly with the help of a massive network of call centers.

In April 2023, Viacom18 Media filed a complaint with the Maharashtra Cyber Cell against Fairplay for allegedly streaming IPL matches. Viacom18, which had the exclusive streaming rights to the tournament, alleged that piracy of the illegally streamed matches caused a loss of about Rs. 100 crore. Later, in February 2025, the ED arrested the Shah brothers – Chirag and Chintan. The brothers were managing the software operations of the illegal betting app.
