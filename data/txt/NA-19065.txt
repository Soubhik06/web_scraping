Dear Fellow Citizens,

I am writing to you not just as a student, but as someone desperately trying to take control of their future, yet feeling helpless against a system that seems indifferent to my plight. I graduated in 2023 with a B. Tech degree from MAKAUT in West Bengal. Since then, I worked as a Fullstack Developer in Kolkata, but my dreams pushed me to make a bold decision — to pursue a master’s degree abroad.

For a long time, I had planned to delay my studies for 2-3 more years. But the suffocating, toxic work environment and the grim reality of the society around me made it clear that I needed to escape. I longed for a place where I could thrive, both personally and professionally.

Then came the day that felt like a dream. After months of waiting, 7 rejections, and self-doubt, I received an email — the one I had been hoping for. I had been accepted into a prestigious university abroad for my master’s program! After 7 painful rejections, I had finally made it! My heart swelled with excitement and hope as I imagined the new chapter ahead.

However, the very next day, my excitement turned into fear.

As I started gathering documents for the university, I realized that I was missing a critical one: my Degree Certificate. At first, I thought the provisional certificate I had would suffice — after all, it’s valid for a year, according to university regulations. But I quickly learned that the university abroad needed the actual degree certificate by the start of the semester. If I failed to submit it in time, my admission would be revoked.

I panicked.

The very next day I reached my University MAKAUT, and spoke with the department of examination. Entered Room 110, got redirected to Room 103, further redirected to Room 109 and then to 102 and finally back to 109. After bouncing all around the hallway, I was told to submit an application to issue a bona-fide certificate which would cost 1000 rupees. I didn’t think much. Went the next day with the application and after 5 days got a letter on the university stating and confirming that I was the student of the university and I graduated and that the university guarantees to provide the degree certificates as soon as the administrative issues are cleared. I wasn’t very convinced with the letter. Nevertheless, completed my enrollment to the masters program with the provisional certificates and the letter. I went to my bachelors college the next week. I spoke to the Controller of Examination there.

This is when I got to know what the administrative issue was.

There was no Vice-Chancellor at MAKAUT to sign the degree certificates. Why? This is not a recruitment scam but something much more shameful. The Governor of West Bengal, who is also the Chancellor of the universities appointed the Vice-Chancellor of MAKAUT without discussing it with the Government of West Bengal. But this time that did not happen. The Chief Minister, Mamata Banerjee did not like it because it runs on the money of the state government and she wanted her party member to take the seat instead of some BJP member appointed by the Governor. The argument from the Government of West Bengal is – “Since the state-aided universities are funded and supported by the state government, it is obvious that the state government headed by the chief minister will have a say in the selection process”. Now the Government of India wants to place a RSS aligned person to the seat of VC and the Government of West Bengal wants to place someone who will follow the Chief Minister’s orders. The supreme court has decided to not intervene in this and let both parties decide mutually. Additionally, the court had in the July 8 order said the court-appointed search-cum-selection committee for the appointment of VCs would recommend a panel of names to the chief minister, who will forward it to the governor, the ex-officio chancellor of the state-aided universities. The governor has to give his consent to a name on the panel. If there is any dispute, the Supreme Court will intervene. But the chancellor in a petition sought a modification to the July 8 order. Thus, a legal tug of war is on display. Who suffers in between all of this? Obviously, the middle-class taxpayers. What’s worse? No one is willing to take responsibility.

I have reached out to everyone I could — from university officials to government representatives, even the Governor’s office — but I have been met with silence. Emails go unanswered. Calls are ignored. In the middle of this mess, the only people who suffer are those like me: the hardworking students, the middle-class citizens, who have no voice in this political chaos.

I don’t know if I will ever receive my degree certificate in time to continue my studies abroad. I am living on the edge of uncertainty, with no guarantee of success. I’ve invested thousands of rupees, and yet I might find myself sent back home, empty-handed, without the opportunity I had worked so hard for.

All I want is a chance — a chance to follow my dreams, to get the education I deserve, and to create a better future for myself. But it seems that my future is being decided by politics and inefficiency.

I am not a political figure. I am just a regular person, trying to build something for myself in a world that doesn’t seem to care. I’m reaching out to you, the readers, to help amplify my voice. I need your support to bring attention to this issue, to hold those responsible accountable, and to make sure no other student has to go through this.

My story is not unique. Many students in similar situations are suffering in silence. This is a cry for help, a plea for justice.

Please help me get this message out. Help me ensure that I, and many others like me, are not left behind due to the failures of a broken system.

Thank you for listening.
