I’m writing this to bring attention to a recent scam that has victimized two of my friends. One lost ₹46 lakhs, and the other nearly ₹2 crores.

# How the Scam Operates:

Scammers approach individuals through various platforms like Instagram, Twitter, WhatsApp, and LinkedIn. They impersonate representatives from reputed trading companies such as Motilal Oswal or Indiabulls, claiming to offer profitable trading advice. Here’s how they manipulate their victims:

1. **Fake Trading Platforms:** They provide a platform that looks similar to legitimate trading platforms. Once you sign up, you’ll see your account with detailed information, creating an illusion of authenticity.
2. **WhatsApp Groups:** Victims are added to WhatsApp groups that appear to include other investors and a supposed expert from the company. These groups are designed to build trust by showcasing “profits” earned by other members.
3. **Initial Trades:** The scammers start by encouraging small investments (₹5,000–₹25,000). You might even see potential profits in your account and successfully withdraw smaller amounts, reinforcing trust.
4. **Fake IPO Opportunities:** They then introduce the concept of applying for IPOs, with guaranteed profits. Unlike legitimate IPOs, where money is deducted only after share allotment, these scammers allow you to “subscribe” without payment upfront.
5. **Larger Deposits:** Once the IPO “releases,” they claim you’ve been allotted a large number of shares and ask for significant deposits (often in tens of lakhs). After you deposit the amount, the shares appear in your account, complete with buy and sell transactions.
6. **Withdrawal Tricks:** When you attempt to withdraw your funds, they demand a commission. If you pay, they invent additional fees like taxes or other charges, continuing to drain your money until you can’t pay anymore.

# Red Flags to Watch Out For:

* Contact initiated via Instagram, Twitter, or LinkedIn.
* Operations conducted mainly through WhatsApp.
* Fake platforms shared via suspicious links or apps. Use [https://www.whois.com/whois/](https://www.whois.com/whois/) to check when a domain was registered to verify its legitimacy.
* WhatsApp groups filled with “investors” boasting profits to create a false sense of credibility.
* Random bank accounts provided for fund transfers.
* Demands for commissions or taxes before allowing withdrawals.
* Unusual IPO subscriptions where no payment is required upfront or no choice of the number of shares is given.

# If You’re a Victim:

Contact **1930** immediately to report the scam and seek guidance on what can be done.

# Advice for Investors:

If you’re planning to invest, ensure you engage with professionals by visiting their office for a face-to-face appointment. Never rely on random online contacts or unverified platforms.

This post is purely informational, with the hope of preventing others from falling prey to such scams. Protect your hard-earned money by staying vigilant and informed.
