About 3 days ago, a cab driver shared an incident where he seems to be a victim of this scam. His bank (Kotak) wrongly charged him 50k, and his bank balance was around negative 25k. Since I didn’t know much about the banking sector, I couldn’t help him much but had taken his number to get back to him if I found some help.

Today, when I saw an [article](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/cyber-crime-wing-of-tamil-nadu-police-warns-of-new-upi-scam/article68955688.ece) in The Hindu and a Reddit post describing a similar scam, it immediately reminded me of his case. I thought of sharing the incident here so that people working in the banking sector or those familiar with such cases might be able to help him.

Here’s what happened: The driver’s account was credited 50k by someone, and the person who deposited the money pleaded, cried, and constantly pestered him to return it. After checking his bank statement and confirming the deposit, the cab driver made the UPI payment to that person. However, later on, his bank balance went into the negative because of a fraudulent request filed by the scammer. I have personally verified the driver’s bank statement.

Now, he’s wandering from one bank branch to another, but the staff keep telling him they can’t do anything. They’ve said his only option is to ask the scammer to withdraw the request, even though the driver has all the necessary details—the scammer’s contact number and transaction information.

This highlights a serious flaw in the bank’s transaction processing system. Honestly, if someone pulled the same trick on me, or on around 80–90% of UPI users, we would probably have fallen for it too.

As the article suggested, victims should contact the cybercrime police station, so I have advised him to do the same. If anyone working in the banking sector has any knowledge or suggestions on what else he can do, please share your thoughts.
