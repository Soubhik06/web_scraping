An Advisory on Cybercrime:

	1.	If someone calls saying that TRAI will disconnect your phone, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	2.	If you get a call from “FedEx” about a package asking you to press a number, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	3.	If someone claiming to be a police officer calls about your Aadhaar, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	4.	If you’re told you’re under “digital arrest,” do not respond. It’s a scam.
	5.	If someone says drugs have been discovered in a package linked to you, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	6.	If they say you can’t tell anyone, don’t listen. Report it to Cyber Crime Police at 1930.
	7.	If you’re contacted via WhatsApp or SMS, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	8.	If anyone calls saying they sent money to your UPI ID by mistake and want it back, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	9.	If someone claims to be from the army or CRPF wanting to buy your car or furniture and shows an ID card, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	10.	If a “Swiggy” or “Zomato” rep calls to confirm your address by pressing a number, do not respond. It’s a scam.
	11.	If they ask you to share an OTP to cancel an order or ride, do not respond. It’s a scam. Never share an OTP with anyone over the phone.
	12.	Never answer calls on video mode.
	13.	If confused, turn off your phone and block the number.
	14.	Never click on links sent in blue.
	15.	Even if you get a notice from Police, CBI, ED, or IT Department, always verify offline.
	16.	Check if letters are from authorized government portals.

Digital Hygiene Tips:

	•	Do NOT share your address, location, phone number, Aadhaar, PAN, date of birth, or any personal details over the phone or through messages.
	•	Don’t confirm your own name on a call. Tell them that since they called you, they should already know your name, number, and any details they want you to “confirm.”
	•	Even if they seem to know your details, do not confirm, deny, or engage. Just hang up and block.

In all these cases, the solution is simple: cut the call, note the number, and block it. Don’t press any numbers, don’t listen—just disconnect and block.

If they’re pressuring, intimidating, or forcing you to act quickly, it’s a scam. Cyber fraudsters are coming up with new ways to trap and defraud people. Stay alert!
