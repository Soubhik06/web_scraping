Telangana police have uncovered one of India’s largest movie piracy networks after a four-month investigation, revealing how pirate groups operate and who finances these websites. In a press conference , Hyderabad Police Commissioner C.V. Anand announced the arrest of several key individuals across multiple states for their alleged involvement with piracy portals such as TamilMV, TamilBlasters, and Movierulz.

He also said offshore online betting apps like 1xBet, PariMatch, RajBet, ForaBet, and 4RABET are funding these piracy websites through advertisements and monthly salary-like payments. “While watching pirated movies online, viewers are also getting habituated to online betting. It has become a social crime,” said the Commissioner. “Our investigation revealed that illegal piracy is encouraging online betting.”

Referring to another popular Telugu piracy website, iBomma, Anand said four people have been arrested, but the main operator remains free. Investigators believe he may be based abroad.

However, at the time of writing, all the piracy-related websites mentioned in this report, including TamilMV, TamilBlasters, Movierulz, and iBomma, remain active and accessible through a simple Google search, highlighting the arduous task search engines face in delisting domain-hopping websites that infringe copyrights.

According to the EY report The Rob Report , jointly released by EY and the Internet and Mobile Association of India (IAMAI), the Indian entertainment industry lost an estimated Rs 22,400 crore to online piracy in 2023. In 2024, the Telugu film industry alone lost about Rs 3,700 crore, police said.

India is the world’s second-largest market for pirated content, according to the Piracy Trends and Insight Report 2024 by MUSO , a data-driven content protection service provider.

On June 5, the Telugu Film Chamber of Commerce filed a complaint against several piracy websites, including TamilBlasters, Movierulz, and TamilMV, whose operators the Telangana police later caught. The Chamber said film leaks on these platforms were causing significant financial losses to the industry.

Anand explained digital piracy as “the illegal copying or distribution of copyrighted movies through torrents, websites, and Telegram channels.”

For context, in India’s theatrical digital cinema distribution business, UFO and Qube maintain a duopoly with market shares of 40% and 48% respectively, according to data provided to the Competition Commission of India (CCI) by the Central Bureau of Communication during an anti-competitive investigation . Both companies deliver digital content to theatres using a unique Key Delivery Management (KDM) key for each show to ensure secure and authorized playback.

Hyderabad-based Jana Kiran Kumar was one of the individuals involved in in-theatre piracy. Anand said Kumar recorded films at Cinepolis in Mantra Mall, Attapur, Hyderabad. Police monitored CCTV footage in theatres for suspicious activity and cross-checked it with ticket bookings.

Investigators said Kumar and his associates booked central corridor seats for an unobstructed view and used high-end smartphones to record films. They relied on third-party applications that allowed recording to continue while the phone screen was off, concealing devices in shirt pockets or even inside popcorn tubs.

Cyril Infant Raju, a B.Tech computer science graduate from Karur, Tamil Nadu, was identified as a key operator. He had been active since 2020 and, according to screenshots shown by police, one website linked to him had 7,169 guest visitors and 23,063 registered members.

Raju supplied camcorder-captured films to piracy sites, acting as a handler for operatives like Kiran Kumar. Police said Raju admitted that he arranged for at least 40 movies to be captured with camcorders.

Technical analysis revealed that Raju managed clusters of domains and Telegram channels, distributing pirated content before uploading it to torrent sites. To mask his activity, he routed IP addresses through the Netherlands and operated virtual machines with servers in Paris. “He was shocked that police could reach him,” Anand said, adding that a Delhi-based Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C) team assisted in unmasking the masked IP addresses of Raju.

Police said he uploaded about 15 movies per month, roughly 550 films in five years, and maintained 10 cryptocurrency wallets and three bank accounts. Titles found on his drives included #Single, Hit 3: The Third Case, and Shekar Kammula’s Kubera.

Patna-based Ashwini Kumar, 21, a 12th-grade graduate, is a central figure in the non-theatrical piracy network. He allegedly hacked the servers of digital cinema distribution companies like UFO and Qube to access content even before its theatrical debut.

For instance, police investigators said they found a file labeled Hit 3: The Third Case on his hard drive, which had surfaced on the TamilBlasters website about 18 hours before its theatrical release in high-definition quality. Similar to others, police allege Ashwini also used a Telegram account to distribute pirated films.

In a related case, authorities also arrested Harshavardhan Goud, a software engineer, for leaking high-definition content from the OTT platform ETV Win. Police said Goud uploaded the film Constable Kanakam on Telegram channels even before its official premiere.

Anand revealed that Ashwini operated from his home in Patna with a high-speed internet connection and an extensive camera setup for monitoring his surroundings. When officials approached his residence, he attempted to delete everything from his smartphone, but he could not erase the material stored on his hard drive, which provided evidence of several pirated films he had distributed.

He hacked theatre websites to obtain the unique KDM keys and used them to watch HD-quality movies out of curiosity, wanting to view them before anyone else. Dara Kavitha, Deputy Commissioner of Police for cybercrimes, said Ashwini’s actions escalated quickly: “He started hacking into servers out of curiosity to watch movies before anyone else. Later, he got involved with Telegram groups that offered him money for the pirated copies.” Police say he began his piracy operations in 2022.

Investigators added that the 21-year-old learned debugging, Python, and Java through YouTube tutorials, and used these skills to obtain theatrical KDM keys and other original prints before official release. Technical experts working with police said Ashwini even had the ability to breach government websites and e-commerce platforms. “It is a very complicated process, but he had the expertise,” Anand said.

According to police, Ashwini charged about $800 per movie in cryptocurrency and pirated around 120 high-definition films directly from servers, earning more than $100,000 overall.

Anand explained that investigators conducted a cryptocurrency analysis of his financial transactions and found that he used a ByBit wallet. These transactions were linked to Pune-based crypto trader Asmit Singh, who allegedly converted the cryptocurrency into U.S. dollars.

Investigators also said Cyril Infant Raju, another piracy operator, received payments in cryptocurrency and used platforms such as DCX and ZebPay to convert funds allegedly paid by online betting apps. Depending on the movie’s popularity, he charged between $150 and $500 per film.

Police estimated the scale and revenue of the operation were substantial. They said Raju received about $10,000 per month, roughly Rs 9 lakh, from online betting platforms such as 1xBet, PariMatch, RajBet, and ForaBet, which effectively paid him a monthly salary.

Raj Kumar Akella, head of the Telugu Film Chamber of Commerce’s anti-piracy cell, said about 70% of other online crimes originate from digital piracy.

Police noted that tracing the locations of online betting platforms such as 4RABET is nearly impossible. “Their entire strategy is to lure viewers into betting,” said the Commissioner. He added that Indian celebrities have promoted these apps, which also collect users’ personal information for fraudulent cybercrimes, including digital arrests, sexual harassment, loan scams, and investment frauds. “It’s a big operation,” Anand said.

Authorities further alleged that Ashwini’s hacking activities extended beyond the film industry. Police said their probe found evidence that he had accessed government websites, including state portals in Bihar and Jharkhand, as well as private company sites.

He allegedly extracted employee records and salary-related information. Investigators also claimed he obtained login credentials for elected officials, including MLAs and MLCs, by penetrating state assembly servers.

Police technical lab officials said weak security practices often enabled these breaches. Many accounts used simple passwords such as “[username]@123,” making it easy for him to execute automated scripts that extracted login credentials. Investigators also flagged servers running on outdated versions of Windows and lacking regular security patching as factors that made breaches easier.

Police urged digital cinema distributors such as UFO and Qube to strengthen their firewall mechanisms and improve cybersecurity to prevent piracy, especially through server-level hacking.
