ready and willing to perform the essential terms of the

agreed that, he will pay further advance of Rs.1,00,000/-

Rs.2,00,000/- is payable within two years from the date of this

Inspite of such notice dated 8.4.2000, plaintiff not at all filed

exactly on the last day of 3rd year, probably to overcome the
limitation aspect. This documentary evidence Ex.P.7 and P.8 establishes

pay another Rs.1,00,000/- on or before 31.3.2000 and
remaining Rs.2,00,000/- within two years from the date of
Ex.P.3 i.e., 17.8.2001. Whether plaintiff was having either
Rs.1,00,000/- on or before 31.3.2000 or remaining balance
amount of Rs.2,00,000/- on 17.8.2001 is another vital issue,
which required to be discussed at this stage. Money admittedly
does not exist in vacuum. Money has to be necessarily in the
form of physical object. It is in the nature of document. Money
is deposited in banks in the nature of securities. Money is
capable of being raised from borrowing. When a person claims
that he is possessed of sufficient funds, he has to produce
some documentary evidence, which proves his capacity to raise
the funds or he possess the funds. Such documentary evidence
may be his passbook issued by bank, where he has kept the
balance sale consideration ready for payment. If he has
invested his money by way of securities, he has to produce this
security before court to show that any time, he encash the 21 OS. No.2476 of 2003 same and capable enough to pay the balance consideration.
Similarly, if plaintiff has kept such money in F.D in a bank,
the F.D receipts to prove his ability and readiness to pay the
agreed amount as per the contract Ex.P.3. Whereas plaintiff
absolutely not produced any such documents. P.W.1
categorically admitted in his cross-examination that he has not
produced any such document. Mere stepping into the witness
box and saying on oath that, he is ready with the balance sale
consideration or that he is going to borrow money from any
financial institutions or that he has got sufficient funds in his
bank accounts, without that oral evidence being supported
by documentary evidence will not prove the plaintiff's readiness
to pay the balance agreed amount. It is immaterial whether
such oral evidence is challenged in cross-examination or not.
The plaintiff has to prove to the satisfaction of the court that,
he possessed the requisite funds at the relevant point of time,
which would enable the court to come to the conclusion that,
plaintiff is ready with the requisite balance sale consideration
to complete his terms of contract. In the absence of any such
documentary evidence being produced, it is a case of plaintiff's
case being not proved. Inspite of reply notice as per Ex.P.8 by
husband of 1st defendant herein stating that he is going to

plaintiff not at all bothered to initiate legal action against said
B.Puttappa immediately or during his life time and he has filed 22 OS. No.2476 of 2003 this suit almost after lapse of 3 years from the date of receipt of
such notice. That apart, he has absolutely not produced any
such documents discussed supra to prove that he actually
possessed sufficient amount either on 31.3.2000 or on
17.8.2001. All these circumstances establishes that, plaintiff
not being ready and willing to perform the essential terms of
the contract only managed to file the suit after death of
B.Puttappa against his L.Rs and sisters just to evade
execution proceedings initiated by said B.Puttappa for
executing the judgment and decree in RFA No.147/1990. This
conduct on the part of plaintiff establishes that, he has not all
approached this Court with clean hands nor established his
readiness and willingness to perform his terms of contract
under Ex.P.3. If plaintiff is actually ready with requisite funds,
there was no scope for him to wait all along for 3 years inspite
of receipt of Ex.P.8 from deceased B.Puttappa. However, a
person on oath states in the witness box that he is ready with
the requisite funds, he must produce some evidence to prove
his possession of requisite funds at the relevant point of time.
Whereas plaintiff absolutely not placed any such documents. The learned counsel for plaintiff asserted that in the
mean time, during the pendency of the suit, compromise came

plaintiff settled the matter for Rs.7,00,000/-. Accordingly, 23 OS. No.2476 of 2003 said amount is deposited before the court. All these

2011 i.e., almost after lapse of 8 years from the date of
institution of the suit. Possession of funds at the requisite
