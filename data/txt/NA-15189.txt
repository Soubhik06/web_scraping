The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has imposed a monetary penalty of Rs 29.60 lakh on Fino Payments Bank Limited for not complying with certain regulatory directions related to the operation of payments banks.

According to the central bank, the penalty was imposed through an order dated June 6, 2025. The action follows findings from RBI’s Statutory Inspection for Supervisory Evaluation (ISE 2024), which examined the bank’s financial position on March 31, 2024.

During the inspection, RBI noted that the bank had breached the regulatory ceiling on end-of-day balances in certain accounts on multiple occasions. This ceiling is part of the operating guidelines for payments banks.

After issuing a notice and reviewing the bank’s written reply and oral submissions made during a personal hearing, the RBI concluded that the bank had indeed not complied with its directions. RBI imposed the penalty using its powers under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

RBI clarified that the penalty is related to deficiencies in regulatory compliance. It does not question the validity of any transactions or agreements the bank may have entered into with its customers. The central bank also noted that this action is without prejudice to any further steps it may take against the bank.

This is not the first time Fino Payments Bank has come under regulatory scrutiny. In October 2018, the RBI had imposed a penalty of Rs 1 crore on the bank for opening new accounts despite explicit directions to halt the practice, citing violations of licensing conditions and operational guidelines.

This is not the only recent case in which the RBI has acted against banks for compliance lapses. In May 2025, the RBI had fined Deutsche Bank AG Rs 50 lakh for failing to report certain borrower data to its central credit reporting system. The violation related to RBI’s rules on maintaining a repository of large exposures and reporting credit information under the CRILC framework.

In a separate order, the RBI fined Yes Bank Rs 29.60 lakh for inaccurately disclosing customer complaint data in its annual financial statements for the financial year 2023–24.

In March 2025, it fined four P2P lending firms, Faircent, Finzy, Financepeer, and Rang De, a total of Rs 76.6 lakh for breaching norms under the NBFC-P2P guidelines, including credit risk exposure and improper disclosures.

Payments banks are subject to strict rules, including low deposit limits and no lending, to promote financial inclusion with minimal risk. The end-of-day balance cap is integral to this model. While Rs 29.6 lakh is a modest amount, the penalty signals RBI’s zero tolerance for even technical non-compliance, reinforcing that adherence to operational norms is mandatory. As competition intensifies in the fintech and payments landscape, such regulatory actions could dent stakeholder confidence and pressure payment banks to bolster their risk-control frameworks.
