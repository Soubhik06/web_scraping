1. The instant one is the second petition, filed under Section 483 of

Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, for grant of regular bail to the

petitioner in FIR No. 85 dated 24.05.2025, registered under Sections 318(4),

319, 319(2) and 3(5) of BNS (Section 241 of BNS added later on) at Police

Station Cyber Crime, Nuh. 2. As per the allegations, on 24.05.2025, ASI Raj Kumar, along with

other police officials, was present in front of Police Post Chandaka, when a

secret information was received that the petitioner and co-accused Robin were

engaged in cyber fraud activities. It was alleged that the petitioner had created

fake Facebook IDs to advertise pencil packing jobs, while co-accused Robin,

posing as a girl on the SUGO video-voice chat App, used to contact individuals

and obtain their WhatsApp numbers, manipulate their videos and then

There is no private complainant/victim in this case. The police itself is a

complainant in this case. No incriminating material has been recovered from

him. The recovery of the mobile phone from him cannot be stated to have any

link with any alleged cyber frauds. The petitioner is in custody since

24.05.2025. Investigation has since been completed and challan has been filed.

Conclusion of trial is likely to take considerable time. Co-accused Robin has

already been granted concession of regular bail by the learned trial Court. On

involvement in other cases cannot be made a ground for denying him the

benefit of bail in the given circumstances. No useful purpose would be served

by keeping him in custody anymore. Therefore, it is urged that the petition

deserves to be allowed and the petitioner deserves to be released on regular

counsel has argued that there are serious and specific allegations against the

petitioner. He is a habitual offender as several other complaints regarding cyber

fraud have been received against him from different persons. There are chances

of the petitioner's absconding or committing similar offences, if released on

bail. It is, thus, argued that the petition is liable to be dismissed. 5. This Court has heard the rival submissions. 6. A perusal of the record reveals that there is no private

complainant/victim in the case and the FIR is primarily based on secret

information. The petitioner has been in custody since 24.05.2025. Investigation

has since been completed and challan has been filed. The trial is likely to take

considerable time to conclude. Co-accused Robin has already been granted

concession of regular bail by the learned trial Court. The principle of parity

weighs in favour of the petitioner. Keeping in view the aforesaid facts and

circumstances, this Court is of the considered opinion that no useful purpose

would be served by keeping the petitioner in custody anymore. Accordingly,

the present petition is allowed. The petitioner is ordered to be released on

regular bail, subject to his furnishing personal/surety bonds to the satisfaction

of the trial Court/Duty Magistrate concerned. However, it will be open for the
