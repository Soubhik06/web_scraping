Tonight around 8 PM ICT (6.30pm IST), chaos erupted at the Jinbei 4 compound in Sihanoukville, Cambodia. Reports from Twitter user u/pelops_huang indicate that over 100 Indian nationals have rioted against scam companies that have allegedly held them captive.

The Cambodia-China Times has also reported on the story.

Cambodia-China Times: [Police rushed into a park in Sihanoukville to carry out law enforcement operations](https://cc-times.com/posts/24945) (20 May 2024; Chinese language; use Google Translate)

**Key details**:

1. [**First Tweet**](https://x.com/pelops_huang/status/1792581958305829094): At 8 PM, more than 100 Indians began rioting at Jinbei 4, fighting against the scam companies (video included).
2. [**Second Tweet**](https://x.com/pelops_huang/status/1792582510607757742): Videos show people smashing a car, with some Chinese individuals present (video included).
3. [**Third Tweet**](https://x.com/pelops_huang/status/1792583084828717263): Video of the protest (video included).
4. [**Fourth Tweet**](https://x.com/pelops_huang/status/1792583474173399488): Video of the protest (video included)
5. [**Fifth Tweet**](https://x.com/pelops_huang/status/1792583637948416323): Military police have arrived at the scene to control the situation.

The Indian Express, in an expose published on 29 March 2024, revealed that over 5,000 Indian nationals may be trapped in Cambodia in such scam compounds. 

**Indian Express**: [5000 Indians forced into cyber-slavery in Cambodia, MHA discusses rescue strategy](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/5000-indians-in-cambodia-forced-into-cyber-scams-mha-takes-note-9239156/)

Cambodian authorities have downplayed the scale of the problem. They have stated that many who claim to be victims are illegal immigrants seeking to return home without facing consequences.

**Khmer Times** - [Scammers utilising local social media​ to draw Indian victims](https://www.khmertimeskh.com/501474119/scammers-utilising-local-social-media-to-draw-indian-victims/) (18 April 2024)

This incident is part of a broader issue affecting many Indians trapped in scam operations in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar. These individuals are often lured with promises of jobs and then forced into cybercrime activities. The Indian government and local authorities have been slow to respond effectively to these growing human trafficking networks.
