Act is concerned, the equipment of telephone exchange would
squarely for within the definition of Section 3 (1AA) of the Act
and thus, the provision of the said act would be applicable to this
case also. The allegations against the accused are that he entered
into criminal conspiracy with unknown persons who allow them
unauthorize access to ISD facility by illegally tampering with the
telephone lines where this facility was available. Merely because
accused alone was named in the charge-sheet, it cannot be said
that Section 120B IPC is not made out against him. The accused
had entered the RLU 578 unauthorizedly by using a duplicate
key without any permission from any officer of MTNL. The said
room was used as a place of custody for CSED equipment and
thus, Section 448 IPC was also made out against the accused. 61. So far as offence under Section 380 IPC is concerned, the
Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of 'Avtar Singh vs. State of
Punjab ', AIR 1965 SC 666' has held that electricity is not a
movable property, however, by way of a legal fiction created in
terms of Section 39 of the Electricity Act, 1910, the prosecution
for theft of electricity was permissible under Section 379 IPC.
Subsequently, theft of electricity has been made punishable under Section 135 of the Electricity Act, 2003 which is triable by the
Special Courts constituted under the said Act. Thus, for the
purpose of Section 379 IPC, electricity cannot be considered as a
movable property and hence, the accused cannot be held guilty
for theft of electric signals under Section 380 IPC. 62. Accordingly, accused Ashok Kumar Rai is held guilty for
