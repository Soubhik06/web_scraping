 Order on Board
18-08-2023 1. The petitioner has filed this petition under Section 482 of the Cr.P.C.

 challenging the FIR No. 627/2015 registered at Police Station Mungeli, -2- District Mungeli (C.G.) on 23.12.2015 for the commission of an offence

 punishable under Section 420 of the IPC. 2. Facts of the present case are that, one Ganesh Dewangan lodged a

 report to the effect that the petitioner executed an agreement to sell in

 favour of the complainant with respect to land bearing survey No.627/3

 admeasuring 0.25 acre, situated at Mungeli, Pa. Ha. No. 6, R.I. Circle

 Mungeli, District Mungeli for consideration of Rs. 10,00,000/- and Rs.

 8,00,100/- was given in advance. It is further stated that Smt. Bela Singh

 and the petitioner were aware of the fact that the actual survey number

 of the plot is 627/2 even then they sold the property bearing incorrect

 survey No.627/3 to the complainant. On such complaint police registered

 the offence as stated above. It is stated by learned Counsel for the

 petitioner that the petitioner purchased the plot bearing survey No. 627/3

 admeasuring 0.25 acre from one Bela Singh on 5.10.2012. In the

 revenue records name of the petitioner was entered, thereafter he sold

 that plot to the complainant through a registered sale deed on

 11.11.2013. When the original owner, i.e., Smt. Bela Singh came across

 the fact that correct survey number of the plot is 627/2 and not 627/3,

 she moved an application for correction of the revenue records before

 the Tehsildar on 5.10.2015. A report was called from the revenue

 inspector who submitted the report on 10.06.2016, wherein he

 categorically stated that survey No.627/2 total area 0.40 acre was

 recorded in the name of Ganesh Prasad who sold a plot of 4000 s.f. to

 Gurjeet Singh on 20.10.1989 and that plot was recorded as survey No.

 627/3 in the revenue records. It is further stated in the report that in the

 name of Ganesh Prasad survey No. 627/2 admeasuring 0.25 acre was

 recorded, due to clerical mistake in the sale deed dated 18.05.1992

 instead of 627/2, survey No.627/3 was mentioned and this can be -3- rectified. 3. Learned counsel for the petitioner would further submit that from a

 careful reading of the above stated facts it is apparent that the mistake

 was bonafide and even in the sale deed survey No.627/3 was recorded

 and he executed the sale deed of the same land without knowing the

 fact that survey No.627/3 is recorded in the name of Gurjeet Singh. He

 would pray to allow the petition. 4. On the other hand, learned Counsel for the State and learned Counsel

 for the complainant would submit that the petitioner as well as Bela

 Singh were aware of the fact that correct servey number of the plot is

 627/2 even then the petitioner executed the sale deed of survey

 No.627/3 knowing very well that said plot is recorded in the name of

 Gurjeet Singh. They would further submit that area of survey No.627/2

 and 627/3 are different. They would argue that the petition deserves to

 be dismissed. 5. I have heard learned counsel for the parties and perused the

 documents. 6. Section 415 of the IPC reads as Under:- 7. In the matter of Vijay Kumar Ghai and Others Vs. State of West

 Bengal and Others , reported in AIR OnLine 2022 SC 380, it was

 observed that except for the allegations that on account of the act of

 cheating the complainant had suffered a wrongful loss and the same

 resulted in a wrongful gain for the accused, there was no intention on -4- the part of the petitioner and/or others to cheat and/or to defraud the

complainant right from the inception, therefore, the offences punishable

under Section 420 of IPC would not be made out. The relevant

paragraphs 33, 35 and 36 are reproduced herein below: 35. In Vesa Holdings Pvt. Ltd. & Anr. Vs. State of Kerala &
 Ors. this Court made the following observation:- -5- 36. Thus, we are of the considered view that the finding
 returned by the High Court suffers from an error of law. It has
 delved into the field of appreciation and evaluation of the
 evidence which is beyond the jurisdiction, either revisional or
 inherent, of the High Court under Sections 397 and 482 of the
 Code." In the matter of Vesa Holdings Private Limited Vs. State of

Kerala and Others reported in (2015) 8 SCC 293 the Hon'ble Supreme

Court while dealing with the ingredients of Section 420 of the IPC in

paragraphs 12 & 13 held as under: In the matter of Satishchandra Ratanlal Shah Vs. State of

Gujarat and Another , reported in AIR 2019 SC 1538, the Hon'ble

Supreme Court held that a mere breach of contract cannot give rise to

criminal prosecution for cheating unless the fraudulent or dishonest

intention is shown right at the beginning of the transaction that is the

time when the offence is said to have been committed. In the matter of Wyeth Limited and Others Vs. State of Bihar

and Another reported in AIR OnLine 2022 SCC 1408, the Hon'ble -7- Supreme Court quashed the FIR on the ground that prima facie offence

of cheating was not made out. It was held in paragraph 15 as under:- In the matter of Binod Kumar and Others Vs. State of Bihar

and Another , reported in 2014 AIR SCW 6310, the Hon'ble Supreme

Court held the mere fact that the appellants did not pay the money to the

complainant does not amount to a criminal breach of trust. The relevant

paragraphs 18 & 19 are quoted herein below: With regard to the inherent power of the High Court under Section

 482 of Cr.P.C., the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the matter of State of

 Haryana and Others Vs. Bhajan Lal and Others , reported in 1992

 Supp (1) SCC 335, in paragraph 103 held as under: In the matter of Amit Kapoor Vs. Ramesh Chander and

 Another , reported in (2012) 9 SCC 460, framing of the charge has

 been discussed and it is held that if in the opinion of the Court, there is

 ground for presuming that the accused has committed an offence, it

 shall frame the charge. 8. Now, coming to facts of the present case in light of the judgments

 rendered by the Hon'ble Supreme Court it is quite vivid that the

 petitioner purchased a plot bearing survey No.627/3 from Smt. Bela

 Singh through a registered sale deed dated 05.10.2012. Thereafter, the

 same was sold to the complainant. The fact of incorrect survey number

 was not within the knowledge of the petitioner and bonafidely he

 transferred the plot to the complainant. To establish the ingredients of

 cheating there must be ill intention of an accused from very inception to

 cheat any person. In the present case there is no material to show that

 the petitioner deliberately and knowingly sold the property with the -9- knowledge that he is not the owner of survey No. 627/3. Further, an

 application was moved by Smt. Bela Singh before the Tahsildar for

 correction of the revenue records when the fact of incorrect survey

 number surfaced. The petitioner has purchased the plot on 05.10.2012

 and sold it on 11.11.2013, whereas, the application before the Tahsildar

 was moved by Smt. Bela Singh on 05.10.2015 and thereafter, the

 complainant lodged the FIR on 23.12.2015. The above stated facts

 would show that Smt. Bela Singh, the petitioner and the complainant

 were not aware about the fact that incorrect survey number is mentioned

 in their sale deeds. The ingredients as stated in the matter of Satishchandra Ratanlal Shah (supra) are not satisfied in the present

 case, therefore, FIR No.627/2015 for the commission of an offence

 punishable under Section 420 of the IPC registered at Police Station

 Mungeli, District Mungeli is hereby quashed. Interim order granted

 earlier is vacated. 9. The petition stands allowed to the extent indicated hereinabove. Sd/- (Rakesh Mohan Pandey)
