Types of Data That Loan Apps Collect
An online loan app requires data to control risk, evaluate your worthiness, and ensure regulatory compliance. The different types of data it needs include the following:

Financial Data:
Lenders need information about your income, credit history, bank statements, tax returns, and existing debts to evaluate your lending risk. This data helps them determine your loan amount, interest rate, and other terms and conditions.
Personal Data:
Lending institutions require personal data to check your eligibility and mitigate the risk of fraud and identity theft. This data includes your full name, date of birth, address, employment information, contact details, etc.
Transactional Data:
The lender builds your borrower profile based on your transactional data, which they use to assess your lending risk and determine your loan offering. Typical data they gather include your payment history, bank account activity, loan applications, and purchase patterns.
Significance of Data Protection in an Online Loan App
Data protection in an online loan app is important, as it safeguards your personal and sensitive information and maintains confidence and trust. Factors highlighting the significance of data protection include the following:
Protection Against Fraud and Identity Theft:
An
instant loan app
handles vast amounts of financial and personal data, making it an attractive target for cybercrime. Sensitive information like your bank account details, credit history, and PAN number can get exposed to fraudulent activities and identity theft.
Consumer Trust and Confidence:
Trust is the cornerstone of any relationship, especially in the lending industry. You must feel safe when sharing your personal and financial data on a digital lending platform.
Regulatory Compliance:
The loan app you choose must follow its regional data protection laws when collecting, processing, and storing customers’ data.
Financial Risk Mitigation:
A data breach can lead to considerable economic loss for the lending platform, including regulatory fines, legal fees, compensation, and remedial measures. Moreover, it exposes your data to cybercrime and potential fraud.
Operational Efficiency:
Secure data handling practices streamline the loan application procedure, reducing the risk of fraud and errors. They also enhance credit assessment accuracy, leading to better customer service, faster loan processing, and a seamless user experience.
Ethical Data Usage:
When you share your personal and financial information with the loan app, you expect them to handle it responsibly and use it for legitimate purposes only. Highly ethical data protection standards ensure your privacy.
Privacy Concerns When Borrowing Through a Loan App
Digital lending platforms require access to your personal and financial information. Here are a few privacy concerns when borrowing through an online loan app:
Manner and Extent of Data Collection:
One primary concern about privacy is the manner and extent to which the app collects and shares your data. The app you choose should securely collect and store your data and follow regulations about who can access and use it.
Data Sharing:
Another privacy concern is data sharing with third parties. Lending institutions usually collaborate with several service providers to verify your identity, assess your credit, and fulfil other operational needs. The privacy risk increases each time it shares your data with a third party. You may not know how they will use your data and who accesses it, leading to unauthorised access or potential misuse.
Data Misuse:
The privacy concern also extends to potential data misuse by the
personal loan
app or its partners. They may use your data for purposes beyond loan application and approval. Inadequate data security measures lead to cyberattacks where criminals may exploit your sensitive data for fraudulent activities.
Transparency and Control:
You may be concerned about your level of control and transparency over your personal data. You may not be fully aware of what data you are submitting, how the lender intends to use it, and with whom they will share it.
