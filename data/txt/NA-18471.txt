Every startup, along with a good idea and strategy, needs money to grow. But how will you, a budding entrepreneur, raise funds without knowing the right people?

One of our clients, a start-up founder, faced this problem too! This is what happened with him:

1. Let's call our client Mr. V. He approached a 'Startup Investment Consultancy,' seeking investors for his company.
2. The consultancy connected him with an investor, to whom Mr. V pitched his idea.
3. The investor offered INR 4,00,00,000 to Mr. V, even though the demand was for INR 40,00,000.
4. Mr. V agreed to go forward with the deal. However, before releasing the funds, the consultancy asked Mr. V to sign an agreement.
5. As per the agreement, Mr. V was required to put in a **refundable security deposit** of INR 4,00,000. The consultancy issued a post-dated cheque, which Mr. V can encash in case the deal does not go through.
6. Mr. V was oblivious to a termination clause in the agreement which stated that in case ANY party backs out of the deal, the security deposit will be forfeited by the consultancy.
7. To put it simply, even if the investor decides to back out of the deal, the complete security deposit will go to the consultancy.
8. As expected, the deal did not go through, and Mr. V deposited the cheque to the bank to claim the deposit.
9. The cheque bounced due to insufficient funds. The consultancy stopped responding to calls and attempts to communicate thereafter.

The case is going to court. Such consultancies and scammers prey on the desperation of startup founders to make it big.

**Here's what to do if you are approaching a startup consultancy to raise funds:**

1. Always conduct due diligence on the consultancy. A quick Google Search with the keyword &lt;consultancy name+scam+fraud&gt; will give you what you are looking for.
2. Without proper documentation, a sound idea, and a transparent term and balance sheet, no investor will give a huge chunk of his money to you. If someone seems too eager to provide you with cash, it is too good to be true.
3. Always do a background check on the history of the investor.
4. Get agreements and contracts vetted by a lawyer before signing. Every mischief is hidden in the fine print.

Grow fast, but grow safe!
