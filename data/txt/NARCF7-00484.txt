--- POST TEXT ---

I received an unexpected call from an Indian number, and normally I avoid answering calls from foreign numbers because I know they are often scams. However, this call seemed different. It was an Interactive Voice Response (IVR) supposedly from the Delhi police. The message stated that there was a pending document in my name at the police station and asked me to press any button to be connected. 

Curious, I complied and was indeed transferred to a police station in Delhi. They provided legitimate details such as the station's address and the name of the inspector. What surprised me, even more, was that they had my official email address. The caller then proceeded to threaten me, claiming that my name was involved in a money laundering case with XYZ Bank. 

I immediately explained that I couldn't possibly be associated with such a case, as I don't even use the services of that particular bank and I am just a salaried individual. In response, they warned that they would transfer my call to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), and I would have to prove my innocence to them in order to be acquitted of the charges. 

At that point, I became suspicious and decided to end the call. However, the calls from that number continued persistently, and I even received abusive messages on WhatsApp from a random foreign number, urging me to answer the call. Thankfully, I took quick action and informed the police, reporting the numbers involved. It's astonishing that these scammers managed to obtain my official email address, impersonate the CBI, and even have a Skype account under the name 'cbi india.' This incident serves as a reminder to all residents of Delhi to stay vigilant and protect themselves from such scams. I can only imagine the extent of damage and potential identity theft they could have caused if I had actually engaged with them on Skype. Stay safe, everyone!
