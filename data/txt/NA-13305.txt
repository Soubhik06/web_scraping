Minister of State (MoS) for Finance Pankaj Chaudhary revealed bank and state-wise data on total instances of financial fraud, losses incurred, and the amount recovered, in a written reply to a question in the Lok Sabha on July 21, 2025. Indian National Congress (INC) Member of Parliament (MP) Anto Antony, and Bharatiya Janta Party (BJP) MP Baijayant Panda had raised the query.

Both these lawmakers asked:

(a) whether the Government has any data regarding the number of cyber fraud and financial fraud cases reported in the country (b) the details of the total number of such cases and quantum of money lost and recovered in the country during the last five years, year-wise, bank-wise and State-wise including Odisha (c) The number of financial/cyber crime complaints reported and lodged from Odisha in 2024 compared to those in 2023 (d) And the steps taken by the Government to protect account holders from cyber frauds

The Lok Sabha response highlights several initiatives and measures taken by the government to combat cyber fraud in the digital lending sector. Mulehunter AI, which aids in the identification of money mules, as well as awareness campaigns were mentioned, along with the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) providing AI fraud monitoring systems for banks, among others.

The answer also pointed out that ‘police’ and ‘public order’ are state subjects. Therefore states and union territories (UTs) are “primarily responsible for the prevention, detection, investigation and prosecution of crimes including cyber crime through their Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs)”.

Meanwhile, “The Central Government supplements the initiatives of the States/UTs through advisories and financial assistance under various schemes for capacity building of their LEAs”.

India’s mounting bank fraud cases reveal a deeper rot in how we define digital trust. Despite rising fintech adoption, fraud reporting remains fragmented, opaque, and customer-unfriendly. Regulatory bodies publish aggregate numbers, but there’s no granular public data on where fraud hits hardest, how quickly victims are reimbursed, or how effective preventive tech actually is.

This opacity fosters both consumer helplessness and institutional complacency. Fraud isn’t just a security issue, it’s a transparency issue. The time is ripe for a central, public-facing fraud registry that discloses resolution timelines, successful redressal rates, and anonymised modus operandi patterns.

Without such infrastructure, innovations like AI-led fraud detection or UPI 2.0 risk become optics, solutions built on shaky public confidence. The next step in digital banking reform isn’t just better tech, it’s structural accountability through transparent, accessible fraud governance. Only then can trust be earned, not assumed.

The total loss for FY 2024-25 stood at Rs 101.82 crore. This is less than FY 2023-24, which saw record losses at Rs 189.96 crore. The gap in the amount recovered is a cause of concern here. For context, Rs 48.37 crore was recovered in FY 2024-25, while Rs 151.85 crore was recovered in FY 2023-24.

ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, and Nainital Bank recorded the highest losses in FY 2024-25 at Rs 31.56 crore, Rs 17.37 crore, and Rs 16.02 crore respectively (add figures for these banks) . Nainital Bank lost the above amount in a server hacking case between June 16 and June 20 this year.

HDFC Bank faced the highest instances of fraud with 35,074 cases resulting in a Rs. 3.43 crore loss incurred in FY24-25. Airtel Payments Bank recorded 19,697 cases resulting in a Rs 24 lakh loss, followed by Axis Bank with a total of 17,325 cases.

Banks in Maharashtra reported the highest fraud losses, at Rs 27.44 crore. Uttar Pradesh (UP) followed, with banks there losing Rs 19.08 crore, and Haryana saw losses worth Rs 10.26 crore. Maharashtra’s capital Mumbai is the financial nerve centre of India, indicating why it is in the first position for amount lost to cybercrimes. It is followed by the most populous state UP. Haryana has also grown significantly as an economic hub with a high per capita income.

Banks in Maharashtra recovered a substantial Rs 25.12 crore, while those in Haryana and UP only managed to recover Rs 64 lakh and Rs 39 lakh respectively.

Tamil Nadu witnessed the highest quantum of cases in the last financial year, with 37,497 cases leading to a loss of Rs 5.54 crore. Maharashtra and Haryana followed with 31,658 cases and 23,861 cases respectively.

As digital payments continue to grow across India, the latest data from the Ministry of Finance highlights ongoing challenges in preventing financial fraud. In FY 2024–25, banks lost Rs 101.82 crore to fraud, while they recovered only Rs 48.37 crore. ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, and Nainital Bank saw the highest losses, while such incidents were concentrated in states like Maharashtra, UP, and Haryana.

While the RBI and the NPCI have introduced AI-based tools and issued security guidelines, the gap between losses and recoveries points to the need for further improvements in fraud response mechanisms.

The data also shows that some banks reported higher recoveries than losses, suggesting that recoveries from previous years may skew year-wise figures. As digital transactions become more widespread, the ability of banks and regulators to respond to cyber fraud will remain a key area of concern.
