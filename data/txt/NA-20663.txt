--- POST TEXT ---

Hey guys, I’m Arko, a freshman at a college in India. I like writing about my life—just a usual Delhi boy with usual dreams, lol. I have a younger sister, loving parents, and a close-knit family. Not gonna lie, I’m probably the most pampered one, even more than my sister. And I have the best friends, especially my best friend Adi, whom I love to the moon and back.

College life started last August (2024), and I made amazing friends—Dia, Ganika, and of course, Adi (thank God we ended up in the same college!). But it wasn’t all a bed of roses. The thorns were there, sharp and unexpected.

I met a guy, Farhan. He wasn’t much—just someone who made a habit of spilling secrets and spreading rumors. People caught on quickly; he became nothing more than a psychopathic troublemaker to most. I didn’t care. Another issue? The fragile egos of some boys who faked masculinity, deepening their voices when talking to girls, rolling their eyes when I said I loved Taylor Swift. “Are you gay?” they sneered. I laughed—was that supposed to be an insult? The same people had no issues when I said I liked The Weeknd too—funny how that works. But I didn’t care. I found my group in the hostel.

Eventually, I found my hostel friend group: Shweth, Dheeraj, Aparna, Unnayani, and Shraddha. Dheeraj was my dorm mate, my go-to guy. I didn’t know how to cook or do laundry; he helped me out. We had midnight food adventures, sneaking out in the freezing Jaipur winters to grab deliveries after the hostel gates shut. We explored the city together—Hawa Mahal, Jal Mahal, and more. It was fun. But deep down, I knew I wasn’t truly close to them. My absence often went unnoticed.

And then came the party that never happened—at least, not for me.

The First Blow

The tech fest had just ended. I returned to the hostel, excited for our plan—egg curry night with Sahil and Dheeraj. But Dheeraj was missing. I called—no response. Hours passed. Finally, at 11 PM, he walked in, grinning.

“Oh Arko, you missed it all!” he beamed. “Shweth’s brother threw a party after the fest. It was insane! The seniors were looking for you!”

I asked, '' Who all went?"

He responds, ''me , shweth and Aparna.''

I blinked. They were looking for me, right?, do we live in medieval India that you need to send a bird for the message to get delivered?, i thought to my self

I stood there, stunned. \_We all went.\_ Not a single message, not even a dry text. Nothing.

I swallowed my anger. Maybe it was an impromptu plan. Maybe I was overthinking. Maybe... just maybe, I didn’t matter enough. Anyways unnayani and shraddha didn't go too right...

The Final Cut

Sunday, 9:30 AM. Aparna’s birthday was the next day. She called Dheeraj while I sat nearby.

“Hey Dheeraj! Everyone’s coming, right?”

“Yeah, me, my girlfriend, Shweth, Unnayani, and Shraddha.”

“What about Arko?”

“Oh, why don’t you ask him yourself?” he said, handing me the phone.

“Hey Arko! I know you’ve been busy with studies, but tomorrow is my birthday. Would you please come?”

Her voice was cheerful. I forced a smile. “Of course! Why not?”

She lit up. “Great! We’ll visit Amer Fort in the morning and a café in the evening.”

"Of course! Why not?" I said, shoving aside the fact that I was conveniently forgotten just the other day. It was her birthday, after all.

That afternoon, I had lunch with Adi, returned, and dived into my studies. If I was going to spend the whole day out tomorrow, I needed to be productive now. Hours passed. The clock struck 10:30 PM. Dheeraj still wasn’t back. Odd. He usually returned by 9.

Finally, he strolled in.

“Dude, today was amazing! We went to Amer Fort!” he announced.

I froze.

I stared. Sahil shot me a glance—he had seen what happened before and knew. My face remained neutral, but my hands curled into fists.

He pulled out his phone, scrolling through photos, showing them to Sahil, who glanced at me, visibly uncomfortable.

I asked quietly, “Did Unnayani and Shraddha go too?”

“Yeah.”

I waited for Sahil to leave, as he did soon enough reading the room.

I inhaled sharply. “Dheeraj, are you or the others mad at me?”

He frowned. “No.”

“Then why didn’t anyone ask me?”

A long pause. “Honestly… no one remembered.” A casual shrug. “I didn’t bother to say anything.”

His words hit like a slap. Don’t care about it, he added.

Sorry, but I actually cared. Where I come from, we don’t treat people like afterthoughts.

I did because I was raised better. 'Cause where I come from, friendship isn’t this disposable.

I walked out before I could break.

I ran to Adi’s hostel, knocking frantically. He opened the door, half-asleep, but the moment he saw my face, he knew. No words needed.

Ganika picked up my call on the first ring. I told them everything—every ignored message, every excuse, every realization that hit like a knife to the ribs. By the time I was done, my voice was shaking.

“Arko, this is not your fault,” Adi said firmly. “You need to stop being a pathological people pleaser.”

I let out a bitter laugh with tears flowing out my eyes. “Easier said than done.”

That night, Adi and I sat under the sky, eating momos and chowmein.

While they had commenced with spinning the narrative, as Aparna calls me infront of Adi.

“Hey, Arko! We’re going trekking and then to a café. You have to come!”

I smirked at the irony. “I won’t wake up that early. Count me out.” Huh..i don't even know how to respond to such lows.

I was so desolate i got candy floss like we were kids again. For a moment, I almost forgot.

Almost.

The Aftermath

The next morning, no one even tried to wake me up. They left to trek. I wasn't surprised, I went to college.

During my break, I told Dia everything. She was shocked, like Ganika and Adi were last night. By the time I returned to the hostel that evening, Dheeraj and Shweth were quietly leaving the room as I walked in. I stood there, watching life turn the pages, realizing just how easily people discard others.

By evening, Dheeraj and Shweth walked past me, silent. I watched them go, like strangers.

Later, Sahil told me Dheeraj had been preparing for the trip for hours. He knew all along. I never had a place in their plans.

Turns out, they even had a separate group chat without me, according to my friends.

Turns out, my friendship and innocence was just being tolerated, not loved.

Where Did It Go Wrong?

I scream, cry, but the wound won’t close.

I trusted them. That was my crime.

I gave them my faith, my kindness—was that a mistake? Was I too naïve? Too eager to belong? If they never valued my friendship, why not tell me sooner? If I was a thorn in their perfect little circle, why not pluck me out from the start?

People say, “Don’t think about them.” But how?

How do I erase memories of laughter, of midnight adventures, of stolen moments that meant everything to me but nothing to them?

I feel mucky. Betrayed.

I miss who I used to be.

I just want my childhood trust back. My innocence. But it’s gone, stolen by the ones I called my friends.

Maybe I was never meant to belong.

Maybe I should’ve just walked alone.

Because at least then, I wouldn’t feel this hollow. Oh! Please lord clean this malice off me...
