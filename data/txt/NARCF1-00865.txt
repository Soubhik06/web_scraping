Scene: An imaginary customer service chat from real experience
Guest (Mr. Genius): Hey folks, let me tell you about my recent adventure with Booking.com. Spoiler alert: it’s a comedy of errors. So, I booked this swanky apartment in D####, right? $1000! I thought I was getting a palace. Turns out, I got a broom closet with a view of a brick wall. Construction noises everywhere. I mean, I’ve seen better views from a prison cell! And I heard less loud shouts and cries in jail. I stayed there for 45 minutes before I ran away for the hills. And I immediately contacted Booking.com, “Help, help, is that what you promised me guys? I am a genius, you can say Einstein blood runs in me” or at least that’s what you convinced me I am, right? Mr. Smarty Genius wants his money back!
Booking.com (The Genius Robber): Oh, Mr. Genius, we’re so sorry to hear that. But remember, we just provide the platform, not the travel experience. It’s the service provider’s fault. Probably, it’s your fault because you are super genius, you trusted the photos we host (hahaha). Do you think we rob enough people to match the pictures with the real property? Hey genius, grow up, man!
Guest (Mr. Genius): Oh, come on! You’re like the middleman who takes a cut but doesn’t want any responsibility. You should make sure the services meet the advertised standards. It’s like selling a Ferrari and delivering a tricycle! Also, you still have my money. You never shared my bank account with the property. You charge me the money, then you pay using your own credit card after my stay ends. Come on, you have it, man. I stayed only 45 minutes, don’t give it to him. I sent you the real picture, just give me my money back. Even if he has a no refund cancellation policy, his place lacks suitability, and your policy mentioned we can ask for a refund if the place is unsuitable, right?
Money Matcher: We understand your frustration. Our platform allows users to compare bookings from various service providers. When you book, you enter into a contract with the service provider. You might say it’s unsuitable, but all the reviews said it’s suitable. So, do you think all other reviewers lie and you are the only honest one? Or do you think you are better than others and deserve higher standards? See how people live in other undeveloped parts of the world. Be glad we helped you find a place to put your ass under at night. Or would you prefer we match you next time with a nice, newly built bridge to sleep under, ha?
Guest (Mr. Genius): I’m sorry, I didn’t mean that. It’s just 3 meters long and 2 meters wide. It was not furnished for someone not my size. I’m 179 cm tall, and if I sleep on the bed, from the knee down, I’ll be in the air. Also, the balcony faces construction, and the window is floor-to-ceiling. If I open the curtain, my wife and I will be exposed to the workers. I believe you should help resolve issues, not just pass the buck. It’s like saying, “Here’s your tricycle, now pedal away and good luck!”
Policy Pete: Only providers with a contractual relationship with us are displayed. We don’t own any accommodations. We didn’t build that apartment, and I will repeat what my love, Mr. Money Matcher, said: be glad, others don’t have a place to stay in.
Guest (Mr. Genius): So, you’re saying you’re just the matchmaker? “Hey, here’s your date. If they turn out to be a serial killer, not our problem!” Great business model.
Money Matcher: I wasn’t mistaken when I called you genius. That’s why we offer travel insurance, but you didn’t buy it. Your idea is great. “Mmm!! Hey Policy Pete and Genius Robber, let’s hold an urgent meeting to discuss Mr. Genius’s idea about coffin insurance.” Sorry Mr. Genius, we barely make enough money by earning a very, very, very little commission from service providers after the stay (hahaha). This commission buys our lunch. Some listings marked as ‘Ad’ are paid placements. We only use them just to pay the bill for the phones we use to help you. You know something, Mr. Genius, I didn’t buy a single shirt for a full year. Do you have an extra one you don’t need, please?
Guest (Mr. Genius): Ah, the old “pay to play” scheme. So, the more they pay, or the more they don’t ask for money when a guest cancels, the higher they will appear in your genius list. You can provide them with double booking: one you know is genius enough, and from his previous history, he will most likely cancel the booking, and another newbie who might stay or not. If he stays, the prperty will get the money. Possibly, you will not take commission from the host, as the other booking was canceled and you will keep all that money, more than the commission anyway? It’s like a beauty pageant where the judges are blindfolded and bribed.
Booking.com (The Genius Robber): Don’t say that, Mr. Genius, please. We work for your best interest. You were in a rush, and all the preferences you like were hidden. I mean, they were not available during the time you made the booking. Honestly, it’s your fault. You should book one or two nights first, so you do not lose a lot as usual. You didn’t learn from before? We use recommendation systems to display information on our platform to help users discover properties they might like. If you booked it, you liked it. That’s it, no discussion. These systems include trending destinations, homes guests love, and properties that may appeal based on similar searches by other guests. If you want to dislike it and play the role of black sheep, I will suggest listening to the baby song, “Baa Baa Black Sheep” (hahahaha).
Guest (Mr. Genius): Oh, I see. So, it’s like a dating app that shows you matches based on your exes. “Hey, you liked that disaster? Here’s another one just like her!” . Hey, you put it at the top of preferred list and there were only three 5-stars rating reviews on that place. I booked it only because it was listed on your preferred genius list. There must be bad reviews; it is really bad.
Policy Pete: Sorry, man. Our review system includes a review submission window of 3 months after checkout. Reviews may be removed after 36 months or if the accommodation changes ownership. Whispering to Mr. Genius Robber, “He doesn’t know we ask them to change the owner name and open another account to erase the history and advise them to post a few fake positive reviews if they are willing to give us a good commission (hahaha).” Hey Mr. Genius, reviews are sorted by factors such as recency, language, and content. External review scores from other websites may also be displayed. Whispering again, “To move them again to the top of our preferred genius list, we need to buy shirts.”
Guest (Mr. Genius): Limiting reviews to 3 months? That’s like saying, “You can only complain about your ex for 3 months, then it’s all sunshine and rainbows!” And removing older reviews? It’s like erasing history. “Oh, that never happened. Move along, nothing to see here.”
Booking.com (The Genius Robber): We apologize for any inconvenience. According to the policy of the reservation, it is a non-refundable rate, and we cannot perform the modification without the property’s approval. It is not us, I swear Mr. Genius, if we make enough money, we will give you your money back. I can barely put gas in my Lamborghini.
Guest (Mr. Genius): Non-refundable? So, you’re saying, “Sorry, you bought the tricycle, no returns!” When I contacted the host directly, they were willing to adjust my dates. But you misled me and tried to convince me to make another booking as spaces run out quickly, till you advocate for me to get my refund back. The host told me that he sent you an email about his approval for cancellation before you sent me this email. So you knew the host is okay with that, and you still say he is uncooperative, and you will fight to get my money back, but I have to pay for another booking first. It’s like saying, “Oh, you want a refund? How about you buy another tricycle instead?” or if you need to marry after divorce, “go marry your ex, at least you know her bad side.”
Money Matcher: We understand your concerns. Our Genius membership program is designed to offer exclusive deals and savings. At least we saved you one free breakfast and $300 after you spent $10,000. Isn’t that a good saving?
Guest (Mr. Genius): Genius membership? More like “Genius” at taking my money. It’s like a loyalty program where the only reward is more disappointment. The more I booked, the more I exposed my preferences, and you used that against me. It’s like saying, “Oh, you like tricycles? Here’s a unicycle, good luck balancing!”
Policy Pete: We strive to provide the best service possible, man. If there’s anything else we can do, please let us know. Do you want me to match you with a place you don’t like again? Yes, there will be no refund, but at least you will get a free breakfast. And you know what? We will promote you to Genius Level 2, so you can save 10-15% when you book. Then if you cancel it, we will save it for us. See, it’s a win-win, man.
Guest (Mr. Genius): Oh, you’re right, but there’s more. Let’s talk about your recommendation systems. You show me properties based on my search history. It’s like saying, “Hey, you disliked that haunted house? Here’s another one with extra ghosts!”
Booking.com (The Genius Robber): No, no, no, man. You are our commodity. Our systems are designed to enhance user experience by showing properties that might interest you. But sometimes, because we need money, after you book, we show you a date modification button. But no worries, whatever date you put, you will get, “no rooms available on selected dates.” So either suck it up, or we will suck your money. You choose to allow us to suck your money.
Guest (Mr. Genius): Suck my money to enhance user experience? More like enhance your profits. You prioritize properties that pay you more, not necessarily the best ones for me. It’s like a restaurant recommending the most expensive dish, not the tastiest one. And companies that gift their product to get 5-star ratings, deceiving other customers to buy the product, and the true buyers get disappointed.
Money Matcher: We aim to provide a balanced view through our review system, which includes a review submission window and sorting by various factors. We know some are faked, but it is what the customers demand to see. We show them what they want, and we get what we want, man. Did you get it?
Guest (Mr. Genius): Balanced view? More like a funhouse mirror. You limit reviews to 3 months and remove older ones. It’s like saying, “Only recent complaints matter, and you reserve the right not to make the review public. Only the one who put the negative review can see it, but if he uses another account, his review is not there? Or you might decide not to publish it at all. That’s what your policy says: Old ones? Forget about them!” And sorting reviews? It’s like arranging deck chairs on the Titanic. The ship’s still sinking!
Policy Pete: We apologize for any inconvenience caused. Our policies are designed to protect both the service providers and the customers. (Whispering, mainly protecting us).
Guest (Mr. Genius): Protect? It feels more like exploitation. When I needed to cancel my reservation, I was hit with non-refundable charges based on the stringent cancellation policy enforced by the property host. Yet, and let me say that again: when I contacted the host directly, they were willing to adjust my reservation dates. You misled me into making another booking, benefiting you while obstructing my ability to communicate directly with the host.
Booking.com (The Genius Robber): We strive to mediate between the customer and the service provider to find a satisfactory solution for us, opps, sorry man, I mean for you. We do not want our customers to dive into the negotiation headache; we will do that for them. (Whispering to Mr. Money Matcher, “Hey man, put my email as the property owner, and when he contacts me, I will behave uncooperative.”)
Guest (Mr. Genius): Mediate? More like manipulate. Your Genius membership program, which initially seems like a great way to save money, ends up exposing users’ preferences and habits. This data is then used against them, presenting misleading offers designed to fail, knowing the likelihood of canceling is high. Each cancellation means more money in your pocket.
Money Matcher: We value your feedback and will take it into consideration to improve our services. Just suck it this time. (Whispering, “and most likely next time, hahaha.”)
Guest (Mr. Genius): In conclusion, Booking.com is a profit-oriented platform and not client-oriented, or at least fairly oriented between client and profit. They manipulate what users see and read to maximize profits. They play a deceptive game, positioning themselves as advocates while treating both members and hosts as mere pieces on a chessboard, moving them as they see fit. My personal experience reveals the grim reality of using this platform, and I strongly urge others to consider alternative booking methods to avoid falling into your trap.
