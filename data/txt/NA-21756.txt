--- POST TEXT ---

During my time in college, I had a friend who was already a professional trader. His success piqued my interest, and I decided to try my hand at trading. Initially, I saw some profits, but soon after, I suffered a significant loss of ₹4,000 out of my initial capital of ₹5,000.

Hoping to improve my skills, I reached out to this friend, believing he could provide the guidance I needed. He agreed to teach me but requested a fee of ₹5,000 for his services. Despite my attempts to negotiate a lower price, he remained firm. I eventually borrowed the money from my father and made the payment on Saturday, 14th September 2024. In return, he promised access to pre-recorded videos and live trading sessions.

However, things did not go as expected. He delayed sending the videos until Sunday night, and when Monday arrived, I was anticipating a Google Meet link for the first live session. Instead, he merely sent me a trade recommendation, which resulted in a ₹700 loss. The same pattern repeated on Tuesday, with no live session, and after multiple calls, he informed me that he had overslept. When I expressed my dissatisfaction and asked for a refund, he assured me that he would conduct a session the next day.

On Thursday, the scheduled session was supposed to begin at 10 AM but didn’t start until 11 AM. Even then, after just 30 minutes, he abruptly ended the session without explanation. By Friday, he still had not delivered the promised training.

When I requested a refund, he flatly refused, stating that he would not return the money, regardless of any actions I might take.

I am aware that he operates a company where he provides trade recommendations to clients, earning a percentage of their profits. Additionally, he trades on behalf of his clients by using their account credentials.

Given this situation, is there a legal course of action I can pursue to recover my money? Would filing a police complaint or reporting the matter as cyber fraud be advisable?
