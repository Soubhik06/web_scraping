This Criminal Petition is filed under Section 482 of

Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (for short 'BNSS')

for grant of pre-arrest bail to the petitioner, who is arrayed as

accused No.13 in Crime No.1786 of 2025 of Cyber Crime

Police Station, Cyberabad Commissionerate. 2. The brief facts of the case are that on 18.07.2025 a

complaint was received from A. Hareesh, SI of Police,

Bachupally PS, reporting that, based on credible information

from local residents, a gang was suspected of illegally

operating a fake call centre from Villa No. 29, SRR Pride,

Bachupally. Upon verification, the SRR Pride Welfare

Association observed the villa for 2 to 3 days and confirmed

suspicious activities, noting that a group was secretly residing

there and frequently making phone calls during night hours.

Further inquiry revealed that the gang was involved in serious

cyber offences, including luring victims with promises of high

returns on short-term investments, impersonating 2 SKS,J Crl.P.No.10552 of 2025 representatives of reputed e-commerce and financial

companies, using fake mobile applications, websites, and

emails to defraud victims, extorting money through threats,

abusing or intimidating victims who questioned them, and

falsely representing themselves as law enforcement officers.

Acting on this information, the police visited the location and

found nine individuals actively running the fraudulent call

centre. 3. Heard Smt. Madhavi Priya Manena, learned counsel

appearing on behalf of the petitioner as well as Sri Syed Yasar

Mamoon, learned Additional Public Prosecutor appearing on

behalf of the respondent - State. 4. Learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that the

FIR contained allegations only against A1 to A12, and the

petitioner was falsely implicated as A13 without any evidence

and that the petitioner did not know any of the accused but

had assisted a relative of one accused by engaging an

advocate to pursue their bail application. He signed the

memo of appearance as an interested person, and the

investigation officer then falsely named him as A13. He 3 SKS,J Crl.P.No.10552 of 2025 further submitted that A4 to A12 were granted bail on

05.08.2025, and the petitioner had no involvement in the

alleged offences and that the investigation officer also

threatened the petitioner for information regarding A1 to A3.

The petitioner was a law-abiding citizen, had no criminal

record, worked as an advocate clerk, and was the sole

breadwinner, taking care of his wife's medical treatment.

Therefore, he prayed the Court to grant pre-arrest bail to the

petitioner by allowing this criminal petition. 5. On the other hand, learned Additional Public Prosecutor

the Police verified the villa and found nine individuals running

the fraudulent operation, which involved luring victims with

promises of high returns, impersonating representatives of

reputed companies, using fake applications, websites, and

emails, extorting money through threats, abusing victims, and

misrepresenting themselves as law enforcement officials.

During the investigation, statements of witnesses were

recorded, and A4 to A12 were arrested on 19.07.2025, while

others remained absconding. The name of petitioner/A13

emerged during additional confessions of the arrested

accused, and he was informed to the court. The prosecutor 4 SKS,J Crl.P.No.10552 of 2025 submitted that A1 to A13, including the petitioner, formed a

gang to commit cybercrimes, using internet tools, software,

APK files, VOIP applications, and phishing emails to cheat

large numbers of innocent people and collect their personal

information. A2 and A13 provided accommodation, language

assistance, and protection to the main offenders, while A4 to

A12 were merely employees. Therefore, he prayed the Court

to dismiss the criminal petition. 6. In the light of the submissions made by both the

learned counsel and a perusal of the material available on

record, it appears that there are serious allegations against

the petitioner. The investigation so far suggests that he may

have helped the main accused by providing accommodation,

language support, and protection, which points to his active

involvement in the alleged cyber crimes. Even though the

petitioner claims he only helped someone with legal support,

his name came up during the statements of other accused

who were already arrested. As seen from the record, it

appears that the role of the petitioner is that he helped some

others who were just working under the main accused.

Moreover, the investigation is not yet completed. Some of the 5 SKS,J Crl.P.No.10552 of 2025 main accused are still absconding, and the police are still

collecting key evidence like digital records and financial

transactions. Therefore, this Court is not inclined to grant

pre-arrest bail to the petitioner and the criminal petition lacks

merit and the same is liable to be dismissed. 7. Accordingly, this criminal petition is dismissed. Miscellaneous applications, if any pending, shall stand

closed. _______________
