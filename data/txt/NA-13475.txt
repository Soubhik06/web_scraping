The Delhi Police has uncovered a major cybercrime racket that was cheating people across India through fake bank loans, sextortion, and online scams. The gang used advanced tools to trick victims and steal their money, officials said.

The Crime Branch began investigating the case after receiving a tip-off on May 24. The alert was about a suspicious package in New Ashok Nagar, New Delhi, believed to carry fake banking tools known as “synthetic account kits.” Criminals created fake identities and carried out online fraud using these kits without leaving a trace.

“These kits were reportedly being used in various high-tech crimes such as sextortion, cyber fraud, and debit or credit card cloning,” said Aditya Gautam, Deputy Commissioner of Police (Crime Branch).

Following the lead, Delhi police carried out a raid near Mayur Vihar Phase-1 Extension and arrested three men — Ujjawal Pandey (30), Gaurav Barua (24), and Yug Sharma (19).

During the operation, police recovered a large amount of digital and financial equipment used in these crimes. This included 28 mobile phones, 30 SIM cards, two laptops, eight cheque books, 15 debit cards, and many fake bank account details. They also recovered videos and photos that the gang used to blackmail people, along with a money trail of over Rs 5 crore that the fraudsters moved through different bank accounts.

According to police, these tools allowed fraudsters to create fake banking profiles that looked real. With these fake identities, they could move money, apply for loans, and scam people without going through proper ID checks. The criminals sold or passed on these kits to other fraud networks.

The investigation led police to another location in Mundka , where a fake call centre was running. The gang set it up to cheat people by pretending to offer easy bank loans. Police caught seven people from this centre.

Police identified Dilshad Ali as the main operator of the call centre. He worked closely with another man named Amit, who is currently missing. While Amit arranged fake SIM cards and bank accounts, Dilshad handled the day-to-day scam operations.

Young telecallers working at the centre would contact people with tempting loan offers. Once the victim agreed, they were asked to share personal documents through WhatsApp and pay a “processing fee” via a QR code. Once the payment was done, the fraudsters cut off all contact — their phones went off and numbers were changed.

In another part of the investigation, police identified a group of men who were running a sextortion scam by blackmailing victims with fake sexual videos.

The criminals would first send friend requests on Facebook. After chatting, they would ask for the person’s mobile number. Then, during a WhatsApp video call, the scammer would play adult videos and secretly record the victim’s reactions using screen-recording software. The victims were then threatened — if they didn’t pay money, the video would be posted on social media.

Police arrested three young men from Rajasthan — Jahid (20), Raheesh (21), and Sohil (18) — for their role in the sextortion scam. Police said Jahid was the group leader. A woman named Jayshree, a former BPO employee, also helped the gang by calling victims and making them comfortable enough to fall for the trap.

The gang used different tricks to scare victims, including pretending to be police officers and threatening legal action. Many victims gave in to fear and paid money to avoid embarrassment.

Officials say the arrested people came from different states and backgrounds . While some had studied in colleges, others had worked in regular jobs before turning to crime. Some were running internet cafes, while others were computer-savvy youths earning small salaries.

Police believe that the network was spread across Delhi, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh.

The investigation is still going on, and police are now looking for others involved in the racket, including the main operator, Amit.
