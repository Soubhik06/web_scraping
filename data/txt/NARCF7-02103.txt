The Indian Anti-Copying Act, 1992, was a significant legislative measure aimed at curbing the widespread malpractice of cheating in academic examinations. Enacted by the state of Uttar Pradesh, the Act imposed stringent penalties, including fines and imprisonment, on individuals caught engaging in or facilitating cheating. This law was a response to rampant copying and fraudulent practices that undermined the integrity of the education system.

Today, the relevance of the Anti-Copying Act remains significant. In an era where academic credentials are increasingly crucial for professional opportunities, ensuring the credibility of examination results is paramount. The advent of digital technologies has made cheating more sophisticated, with methods ranging from unauthorized internet access during exams to the use of wearable devices. Therefore, a robust legal framework is necessary to deter such activities and uphold the sanctity of educational assessments.

Making the Anti-Copying Act a national law could provide a uniform approach to tackling academic dishonesty across India. Currently, the measures to prevent cheating vary from state to state, leading to inconsistencies in enforcement and effectiveness. A national act would standardize penalties and procedures, ensuring that all students are subject to the same rules and consequences, regardless of their location. This uniformity would help maintain a level playing field and enhance the overall credibility of the Indian education system.

Furthermore, a national Anti-Copying Act could incorporate modern provisions to address new forms of cheating facilitated by technological advancements. It could mandate the use of secure exam technologies, regular monitoring of exam centers, and stringent verification processes for both online and offline exams.
