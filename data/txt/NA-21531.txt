What is a P2P Scam?

Many people misunderstand P2P scams. In most cases, it's not just a scam but involves money laundering and complaints filed against your bank account through Cybercrime. Less than 1% of buyers file complaints themselves.

Most complaints come from buyers who unknowingly invest in crypto scams (10x/100x mining schemes) and later report the seller—either due to ignorance or to recover losses, often with police guidance. Many law enforcement officers lack crypto knowledge, leading to unfair targeting of sellers.

✅ If the buyer files the complaint themselves, it is a true "P2P scam."
✅ If authorities flag your account for money laundering concerns, it’s not a P2P scam.

---

Bank Freeze &amp; Common Terms Explained

Debit Freeze – Funds cannot be withdrawn.

Debit &amp; Credit Freeze – Entire account is blocked (no transactions).

Shadow Freeze – Bank freezes the account due to income mismatches and unusual transactions.

PAN Freeze – All bank accounts linked to the PAN card are frozen.

Lien – A specific amount is blocked by Cybercrime (NCRP), but the account remains functional.

Hold – A certain transaction amount is withheld for court recovery.

LEA – Law Enforcement Agency.

MHA – Ministry of Home Affairs.

---

Understanding "Layers" in a Bank Freeze

Each layer represents a stage in money movement:

Layer 1 – The scammer (the person who first received fraudulent funds).

Layer 2 – The next person who received money from Layer 1.

Layer 3+ – Each additional recipient before reaching the final destination.

---

Complaint Tracking Numbers

Acknowledgement Number – A 14-digit number from Cybercrime Portal or 1930.

FIR Number – A 4-digit case number + the year of the offense.

Common Questions &amp; Answers on P2P Scams &amp; Bank Freezes

1. Why is my bank account frozen after a P2P crypto trade?

✅ You likely received scam funds—either directly (via third-party transactions) or indirectly (from a buyer who unknowingly received scam funds).

2. Can my bank freeze my account without informing me?

✅ Yes. Due to the high volume of fraud cases, banks must comply with cybercrime notices and freeze accounts to prevent money laundering.

3. How do I check if my account is under a lien or a full freeze?

✅ Try making a small transfer or withdrawing money at an ATM. If your balance is lower than the lien amount, visit your bank and request details from the manager.

4. My account is frozen. What should I do first?

✅ Don’t panic. Visit your bank and ask your manager for details. Request an official letter or email containing:

Acknowledgment number/FIR number

Transaction date

UTR number

Layer details

Cybercrime officer’s contact details

This information is crucial for resolving your case.

5. My bank won’t provide details about the freeze. What should I do?

✅ Visit the nearest cybercrime office and ask an officer to check your account in the NCRP portal. They can provide at least the acknowledgment number and complaint location.

6. I have freeze details. What’s next?

✅ Contact P2P platform support (e.g., Binance) via chat or email, attaching the bank letter and requesting them to freeze the buyer’s exchange account. If their assets are blocked, they may cooperate and help resolve your case.

❌ DO NOT inform the buyer directly—they may empty their wallet and disappear. Always contact the exchange first.

7. How long does a bank freeze take?

✅ There’s no fixed time—it depends on the complaint. Cases can be filed within 48 hours of the last scam transaction, but some accounts get frozen even after a year. A freeze will not be removed automatically unless authorities process it in bulk.

8. My account is frozen. Am I an accused?

✅ No, unless:

You received money directly from the scammer.

You have multiple complaints against your account.

You engaged in third-party transactions using victims’ funds.

If you have proof of legitimate transactions, you can prove your innocence.

9. If I unknowingly received scam money, am I responsible?

✅ Yes, especially if the amount is large or the complaint is from your state. Many people fall into this trap by trading at high rates without verifying sources.

10. How can I prove I’m not involved in money laundering?

✅ If you have only 1-2 complaints, it’s easier to prove innocence. But if you regularly buy/sell crypto at high rates without considering transaction limits, audits, and taxes, proving innocence becomes difficult.

11. Will the next account in the chain get frozen too?

✅ Yes, if the money is transferred further, other accounts may also be frozen or placed under a lien.

12. Can I file a counter-complaint if falsely accused?

✅ Yes, but it depends on your case:

If your buyer filed a false complaint, you can file a counter-complaint.

If you’re caught in a money laundering chain, ensure your investigating officer is cooperative before taking legal action.

If authorities refuse to help, you may need a legal notice or court intervention.

13. What documents should I provide to the police or bank to prove my innocence?

✅ Keep these documents ready:

Transaction receipt

Bank statement

PAN card &amp; Aadhaar card

Handwritten explanation letter

Local police verification (in some states)

14. Can I still use my account if there’s a lien on a certain amount?

✅ Yes, but it’s risky. If the lien amount is small, request your bank to close the account. If it’s high, work on resolving it first.

15. What happens if my account is under Law Enforcement Agency (LEA) investigation?

✅ It can affect:

Government &amp; salaried employees (salary accounts may get blocked).

Passport renewals &amp; visa applications.

In most cases, there’s no immediate impact, but it’s crucial to stay informed and resolve issues quickly.

Each state and city police handle cases differently—this is based on real experiences in India.
