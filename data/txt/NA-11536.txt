Argued By:- Appellant/Complainant-Saroj Bala has filed the present appeal under Section 41 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (as amended upto date) 2 F.A. No. 175 of 2021 against the order dated 17.03.2021 passed by the District Consumer

Disputes Redressal Commission, Mansa (in short "the District

Commission"), whereby the complaint filed by the complainant was

dismissed. 2. It would be apposite to mention that hereinafter the parties will be

referred, as have been arrayed before the District Commission. 3. Briefly stated, the complainant had opened a Savings Bank A/c No

65150792205 with OP Bank (State Bank of India) and had been paying the

necessary charges as per the Terms and Conditions. On 19.09.2017 the

complainant was asked to get her bank account updated with her mobile

number whereupon the officials got recorded her mobile number

9878677751. There was a credit of Rs.95,207.50 as on 29.08.2017 and

there was no further transaction from the bank account from 29.08.2017 to

19.09.2017. She had also obtained debit card, but the same was not used

by her or her representative. 4. However, on 20.09.2017, an amount of Rs.50,000/- was transferred

from her account in three different transactions and debit balance was

shown as Rs.45,207/-. On getting a call from the bank the complainant was

shocked that an amount of Rs.50,000/- had been transferred from her

account, whereas, she had not made any such transfer. According to her,

OP-Bank was responsible for the same and amounts to 'deficiency in

service' on the part of OP. The complainant met the Bank Manager on

21.09.2017 and complained about the incident. She also withdrew the

remaining amount from her account after leaving a nominal amount, to

save herself from any further loss by any unauthorized transfer. Alleging to

be a case of 'deficiency in service' and 'unfair trade practice', the 3 F.A. No. 175 of 2021 complainant filed the complaint against OP Bank for refund of Rs.50,000/-

alongwith interest @12% from the date of debit, besides Rs. 50,000/- as

compensation. 5. Upon notice, OP filed its written reply and admitted that the

complainant had opened the Savings Bank Account and also issued ATM.

She was also made aware from time to time about RBI guidelines, that no

information be shared with any official of the bank and as such she was

responsible for her own lapse. The ATM-cum-Debit Card had always

remained in the custody of the complainant and the password also was in

her knowledge. The complainant had disclosed her password of her ATM-

cum-Debit Card to some person and thereafter, online shopping was done

on 20.09.2017 for a sum of Rs.50,000/- through this card. This transaction

had taken place due to the mistake of the complainant herself and Bank

cannot be held not responsible for the same. No employee of the Bank had

informed the complainant to share any such information. Therefore, the

complaint deserves to be dismissed. 6. The complainant as well as the OP, Bank Manager Sh.Rajnish

Chawla filed their affidavits and closed their evidence. After considering the

whole case, the District Consumer Commission finding no deficiency in

service on the part of the OP Bank, dismissed the complaint. 7. Aggrieved by the order dated 17.03.2021 passed by the District

Commission, appellant/complainant has filed the present appeal before us,

but at the time of hearing of the appeal none has appeared on behalf of the

appellant/complainant. F.A. No. 175 of 2021 8. We have heard the learned counsel for the respondent and have also

gone through the documents available on record, alongwith the written

arguments filed by counsel for the respondent. 9. As per grounds of appeal filed by the appellant/complainant Saroj

Bala that she updated her bank account with mobile number on

19.09.2017. Online shopping was done for a sum of Rs.50,000/- through

ATM-cum-Debit Card on 20.09.2017. The appellant/complainant

immediately informed the bank official about the fraud but he did not take

any action. The appellant/complainant reported the matter to the Bank as

well to the local police vide letter dated 21.09.2017 but the Bank did not

acknowledge the letter. It has further been averred in the appeal that the

District Commission has not taken into consideration the same. The

appellant/complainant in the appeal has further averred that the Bank failed

to abide by the RBI Guidelines i.e. Master Circular

No.DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18 dated 06.07.2017. It has further

been averred that the official of Bank did not take any notice of the oral as

well as written complaint. The District Commission has wrongly dismissed

the complaint. 10. As per version of learned counsel for the respondent/OP that

the District Commission passed a well reasoned order and held that it was

the mistake of the appellant/complainant who shared her password with

some unknown person and due to which online shopping of the alleged

amount has been done and there was no role of any kind of any officials of

the respondent/OP. Moreover, the ATM-cum-Debit Card always remain in

possession of the account holder and he can change the password at any

time. Time and again the RBI and Banks send messages to the customers

not to share their password ATM-cum-Debit Card and Credit Card to any 5 F.A. No. 175 of 2021 other person and to the bank employees. Learned counsel for the

respondent/OP submits that no interference is required in the order passed

by the District Commission. 11. From the scrutiny of the Bank Account of the complainant it is

evident, that all the three transactions, total amounting to Rs.50,000/- were

through OTHPG made on 20.09.2017 through ATM-cum-Debit Card. The

card was issued by the Bank to the complainant for her convenience and it

was her responsibility to keep it in safe custody, without sharing the

necessary information with any other person. In view of above discussion,

we are of the considered opinion that the appellant/complainant failed to

prove any 'deficiency in service' and 'unfair trade practice' on the part

of the respondent/OP and the District Commission has passed the

impugned order after properly appreciating the facts, documents and

evidence available on the file. 12. Consequently, we do not find any merit in the present

Appeal and dismiss the same by upholding the well-reasoned Order

dated 17.03.2021 passed by the District Commission. 13. The appeal could not be decided within the statutory period due

to heavy pendency of court cases and the pandemic of COVID-19. (JUSTICE DAYA CHAUDHARY)
