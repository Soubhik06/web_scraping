accused has been falsely implicated in the present case and he has no
concern whatsoever with the offence in question. 8. It is fundamental principal of criminal jurisprudence that an
accused is presumed to be innocent and, therefore, the burden lies on
the prosecution to prove the guilt of the accused beyond reasonable
doubts. The general burden of establishing the guilt of accused is
always on the prosecution and it never shifts. It is settled preposition
that the prosecution has to prove the guilt of accused beyond all
reasonable doubt and that too by leading independent, reliable and
unimpeachable evidence. There is no controversy to the proposition that
the accused is entitled to the benefit of every doubt occurring in the
prosecution case. 9. It is the case of the prosecution that accused made several threat
calls to the complainant for extortion of Rs.2,00,000/- failing which
complainant was threatened of his life. The threat calls include video

is pertinent to mention that no certificate u/s.65B of Indian Evidence
Act had been filed either by the complainant or along with the charge-
sheet to support and substantiate the authenticity of the screenshots
Mark-X (colly). Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in Anvar P.V. Vs. P.
K. Basheer & Ors. (2014) 10 SCC 473 has categorically held that an
electronic record by way of secondary evidence shall not be admitted in
evidence unless the requirement u/s.65B of Indian Evidence Act is
satisfied. Non-filing of certificate u/s.65B of Indian Evidence Act
renders the secondary evidence inadmissible. In the case in hand, no
such certificate u/s.65B of Indian Evidence Act has been filed,
therefore, the screenshots Mark-X (colly) ought to be eschewed from
the from the array of evidence. 11. It is pertinent to mention that complainant had brought his mobile
phone during his cross-examination, however, he was unable to show
any detail/ log of any threat call allegedly received by him from the
accused. He could only show a screenshot but not the actual call detail/
log. Moreover, he has categorically deposed that he did not furnish the
details of threatening video call to the IO by way of the screenshot. __________________________________________________________________________________________

of making threat calls to the complainant. 14. In view of above said discussion, this court is of the considered
