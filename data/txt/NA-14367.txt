When telecom companies all launch their own spam-detection systems, how will Truecaller change their model to keep up with this competition? We spoke to Truecaller’s Chief Strategy Officer and Co-Founder Nami Zarringhalam about recent developments in the Indian telecom landscape, such as Airtel launching its artificial intelligence (AI) based spam detection system and BSNL following suit soon after announcing that it is in the final stages of launching its own spam detection service. The remaining telcos are also likely to launch the same service, given the fact that the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) issued directives instructing them to do so in June last year .

Here are some notable excerpts from our interview with Nami Zarringhalam. Note that we have edited them for clarity and brevity. Please watch the full interview to get a complete idea of Zarringhalam’s views.

Zarringhalam acknowledges that telecom networks have a clear advantage over OTT (over-the-top) apps like Truecaller due to their complete coverage of their customer base. However, he emphasizes that spam is not isolated to any one network and requires a broader solution. “It’s not an isolated issue where it only happens on the Airtel carrier. For this to be solved on a society level, you need to allow private entities that can have access to the complete user base of all carriers to actually try to address the issue.”

MediaNama’s Editor Nikhil Pahwa told Zarringhalam that based on the TRAI directive from last year, all telcos are likely to launch spam detection services. “I think the regulatory requirement is interesting because, you know, what are you trying to protect the consumers from? Is it really the spam part or is it mainly the fraud part?” he questioned.

“I would say it only allows for Truecaller to stand out more because we will already have that correct information and we will already have your preference set up if you become a Truecaller user. And over 300 million users in India currently with Truecaller, and these users are going to have already set their preferences,” Zarringhalam argued. He emphasizes that Truecallerâs user base and established preferences give the platform a significant edge over network-based solutions.

Zarringhalam  questions whether network-based solutions will have a net positive effect, highlighting, “We have yet to see that these network-based solutions actually have a net positive effect on people’s ability to choose between taking the right opportunity and taking up that phone call… when the credit card company that you’ve applied for is calling.” He mentioned that just like Airtel in India, telcos in other countries (like the US and countries in the Middle East) have also launched similar solutions.

Truecaller changed its approach after receiving feedback from Indian users who were missing important calls, like those for credit cards, due to automatic spam blocking based on community-based reporting. Zarringhalam mentioned that when Truecaller first came to India they spoke to a user who explained that he had ended up missing a call from his bank because of auto-blocking.

“We realized that we were actually completely mistakenâ¦ something that was important for him to run his business, his life, basically with a credit card, was taken away from him because of the choice that Truecaller had made. That day, the first thing we did when we arrived at our then office in India was to actually turn that feature off like that. We don’t take the decision for the users.” This feedback led to a pivotal shift, with Truecaller enabling users to make their own decisions regarding spam, rather than relying solely on community-based blocking.

Zarringhalam explains the importance of allowing users to customize their preferences for handling spam, rather than relying on one-size-fits-all rules. “If you’re just trying to… read the signals without understanding the preference of each individual in that community, I think you could actually be raising potential value for the community.” He adds that Truecallerâs model combines community signals with individual user preferences to create a more tailored experience, “The community generates much more value than each piece individually.”

So weâve actually enabled that with our Truecaller for Business product suite. And we have partnered with all of those businesses that both the companies that are offline generally by nature, but also the ones that are digital natives, because calling and messaging is part of the workflow for all of these companies.”

Pahwa brought up TRAI’s recommendations on caller IDs during the interview and asked Zarringhalam about his views on this as a solution for spam. “I don’t think that business identification resolves fraud. I think fraud detection resolves fraud.” He stresses that fraudsters operate differently from legitimate businesses, adding, “Just because you can identify businesses, you will [not] have lower amounts of fraud happening because fraudsters are not… working as a bank does in terms of like that there’s rules and regulations for how they should behave.”

Zarringhalam highlights that Truecaller has been investing in fraud detection for several years, separate from its spam detection efforts. “We’ve been investing in detecting fraud for the past four or five years… the specific ones when it comes to fraud and scam are like, you need to track that type of history for a very long period before you can start to build models to detect them.”

Zarringhalam emphasizes the importance of aligning incentives when discussing the role of regulators. He states, “The problem is when you have companies that have, for business reasons, an incentive to… increase the amount of calls that they make or increase the amount of leads that they generate, then naturally the solution is going to be hampered by their own business objectives.” He highlights Truecaller’s approach, noting, “Our verified business customers… still have the spam markings despite being a customer at Truecaller. We’re not taking away what the community feedback is just because they are paying for the verified product.”

Truecaller is focused on maintaining a balance between user protection from spam and enabling legitimate business communication. Zarringhalam says, “We think about that in all parts of our product so that we donât make these mistakes… enabling [businesses] and making sure that gets identified properly for the consumer, and that they have the choice to still mark that as spam or let this pass through.” He emphasizes that this system allows businesses to conduct genuine outreach while giving users the power to manage their communication preferences.
