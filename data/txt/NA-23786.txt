--- POST TEXT ---

I had recently created an [OLX](https://www.olx.in/) account, which is a platform for buying and selling your used products.

I wanted to sell my old college preparation textbooks on sale for second-hand and in total, I had uploaded 3 books for sale. After an hour of posting, I was contacted by someone with the name of Praveen Kumar on the OLX messaging feature. It was my first time using OLX and I had shared my contact number with him when he asked me to share it with him along with the buying offer.

&amp;#x200B;

In our phone conversation - He told me that he was a second-hand bookseller and was buying books in bulk from OLX customers for his shop. In our conversation, he told me that he was interested in taking all my books. On our agreement, he decided to buy a total of 15 books (including those 3 already posted on OLX) for for a grand of Rs. 5000.

&amp;#x200B;

**Here comes the scam!!**

In order to share photos of my other books, we had changed our communication medium to WhatsApp.

He told me that he will pay via UPI payment and he will send one of his workers to my address to collect the Books. Due to this, I had shared my Apartment Location with him as well. Along with this, he told me that he will do the Payment instantly and was using a direct debit card transaction called Swipe (I think it is a made-up name) where he would send me the refund QR code. Currently, he has deleted all the QR codes he had sent but I had managed to store a few of them (Not all).

I was using my Paytm gateway -

In order to earn my trust and confirm credentials - he told me that he will be sending Rs 1 from the debit card machine. I would have to scan this QR code and enter my UPI PIN. It will get refunded.

In total, I had lost Rs 8000 to him. I am a student and had a total of Rs 8500 in my bank account prior to his scam. The last Rs 8000 had failed because I didn't have enough balance. Although dumb up to this point, I knew that I had nothing left to lose and I had tried processing that last Rs. 8000 refund request as well. I have the Rs 8000 code as well, but I'm not going to upload it as it looks just like the rest of it.

Seeing me getting furious about the incident-

He told me that he will send one of his workers, who will pay me through cash and along with that will also pick up my books. In the meanwhile, I started asking him about his shop and address. I'm currently residing in Bangalore, he sent me a picture of a random Shop photo pulled up from google(with an address written on it). On enquiring further, I came to know that he knew only one famous location in Bangalore (Indranagar) and his shop wasn't located there as he had mentioned. At this moment, I knew that I was fully scammed and lost Rs 8000.

&amp;#x200B;

I called him up again and was demanding the money. In the end, I told him that I will go to the Police Station to file a complaint but he made a small threatening statement that "If you go to the police I will make your life Black " (All conversation in Hindi). Although I wasn't too afraid about the threatening statement, on consulting with friends they told me that I must be concerned as he has my Full name(from OLX) along with my apartment address. On talking further with him he said that he will send the money but he needs my Aadhar and PAN card number, but I didn't give him.

**Complaint -**

I have filed a complaint with Paytym along with proofs and told me that they will forward it to the security team. Along with this, they also advised me that for future reference, if you ever have to type your UPI pin for any transaction it means that money will be debited. Along with this, I had tried filing a complaint with the Bank but they told me that they can't do anything about handling third-party transactions (Paytm here).

I'm still yet to file a police complaint, but for my own safety, my friends told me that it might be the best idea not to lodge a complaint. If some of the readers think otherwise, please let me know. I'm mainly here for advice,

**I agree that it was being dumb and stupid on my part, and I really hope scammers like this don't target any other old-age or technologically weak citizens like me. I hope my experience is a lesson for everyone out there-**

Here is the list of my mistakes which I should have avoided and do let me know if there any more points to add -

1. It's easy to succumb to greed, the main reason I had gotten this far with that scammer was that the agreed-upon Selling price was a good deal for me and I knew that I wouldn't find many buyers. If a deal is too good to be true, it's probably a scam.
2. This beautiful country is filled with a lot of scammers. Last decade we might have been worried that people are losing money falling prey to internet hacking. Hacking of this kind requires a lot of skill and the current trend is emotional/situational hacking, where these scammers get on a call with you and put you in a tough situation. Explained in this video[ here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VrKW58MS12g).
3. Worst happens only during your most stressful periods, I wasn't able to think clearly during my incident (I had to put an all-nighter the previous night before the call). It's important to handle situations like these in a calm and composed manner. In my case, I should have stopped after that first Rs 2000 was lost, but in order to compensate for the lost money, I continued with the conversation.
4. Do not trust anyone from the internet. In my case, there were many red flags when I should have stopped engaging in a conversation with the scammer, and here they are-
 1. When the deal was too good on OLX and his willingness to buy all my books without a proper bargain (he did try customary bargain but wasn't legitimate)
 2. For using a different unknown payment method called "Swipe" and making an assumption that the debit statement on Paytym was a technological glitch.
 3. Further continuing the conversation after losing Rs 2000.
