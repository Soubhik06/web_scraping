20/08/2025 1. The jurisdiction of this court has been invoked by way of

filing an application under Section 439 CrPC at the instance of

accused-petitioner. The requisite details of the matter are

tabulated herein below: 29.01.2023, the Station House Officer of Police Station Basni,

while on blockade duty along with his team and acting upon

specific intelligence input, intercepted a truck-tanker bearing

registration No. RJ09-GD-2612, which at the relevant time was

being driven by one Om Prakash. Upon conducting a search in

accordance with law, the police recovered as many as 111 gunny

sacks containing a total of 2240.517 kilograms of poppy husk.

After completing the requisite formalities under the NDPS Act and

the Code of Criminal Procedure , the driver Om Prakash was duly

interrogated and arrested, and an FIR came to be registered for

the offence under Section 8 / 15 of the NDPS Act.

2.1. It is significant to note that no recovery has been effected

from the petitioner himself, and his alleged complicity rests

entirely upon the disclosure attributed to the co-accused. The

petitioner had earlier approached this Court by way of a bail

application being S.B. Criminal Misc. Bail No. 11355/2024, which

came to be dismissed vide order dated 25.02.2025. 3. It is contended on behalf of the accused-petitioner that no

case for the alleged offences is made out against him and his

incarceration is not warranted. There are no factors at play in the

case at hand that may work against grant of bail to the accused-

petitioner and he has been made an accused based on conjectures

and surmises. 4. Contrary to the submissions of learned counsel for the

petitioner, learned Public Prosecutor opposes the bail application (Downloaded on 05/09/2025 at 10:13:14 PM) [2025:RJ-JD:37214] (3 of 20) [CRLMB-8048/2025]

and submits that the present case is not fit for enlargement of

the parties and perused the material available on record.

5.1. Upon a careful perusal of the record, it emerges that on

29.01.2023, Jitendra Singh, SHO, along with his team, acting on

secret information, intercepted a truck bearing No. RJ09-GD-2612,

which was being driven by one Om Prakash. During the search of

the vehicle, a huge consignment of contraband, namely poppy

husk weighing about 2240 kilograms, was recovered.

Consequently, Om Prakash was apprehended and a case under the NDPS Act was registered. 5.2.During custodial interrogation, accused Om Prakash stated

that one Arjun, resident of Khanarkhedi, had instructed him to

unload the contraband at the premises of Satyanarayan, resident

of Pal Balaji, Jodhpur. However, the record indicates that Om

Prakash never made any direct reference to Satyanarayan in his

disclosure, and the insertion of his name appears to be a later

development in the investigation. No call detail records, nor any

other independent evidence have been brought on record to

establish any communication or nexus between the petitioner and

the intercepted consignment. It stands to reason that if the

vehicle was indeed intended to deliver contraband to the

petitioner, some form of telephonic or personal contact between

the petitioner and the driver would necessarily have existed. In

the absence of such corroboration, the allegation appears highly (Downloaded on 05/09/2025 at 10:13:14 PM) [2025:RJ-JD:37214] (4 of 20) [CRLMB-8048/2025]

tenuous. 5.3. Further, serious inconsistencies are revealed with regard to

the place of interception and seizure. According to the

prosecution's own version, the truck entered Jodhpur from the

Dangiyawas side and was allegedly destined for the petitioner's

premises at Pal Road. In such a natural route, the vehicle would

not have come in front of Basni Police Station. Yet, the seizure

memo and Parcha Kayami curiously reflect proceedings as if the

vehicle was intercepted at Basni. No plausible explanation has

been furnished by the investigating agency for this deviation. This

unexplained diversion of the vehicle, unsupported by any cogent

material, renders the prosecution's version suspect and casts a

serious shadow on the fairness and transparency of the recovery

proceedings. 5.4. The record also discloses that the name of the petitioner's

son, Mukesh alias Montu, was mentioned in the disclosure

statement of Om Prakash. However, it is an admitted position that

the said Mukesh @ Montu has already been enlarged on bail by

this Court. On the principle of parity, therefore, the present

petitioner stands on an identical footing. 5.5. It is also significant that no contraband or incriminating

article has been recovered from the petitioner himself. The solitary

material against him consists of the custodial disclosure of co-

accused Om Prakash and the uncorroborated statement of Arjun

Ram. In the absence of independent corroboration, such (Downloaded on 05/09/2025 at 10:13:14 PM) [2025:RJ-JD:37214] (5 of 20) [CRLMB-8048/2025]

statements cannot be treated as substantive evidence sufficient to

fasten criminal liability under the stringent provisions of the NDPS

Act . 5.6 At this stage, it is also pertinent to examine the applicability of Section 29 of the NDPS Act, which deals with punishment for

abetment and criminal conspiracy. On a careful perusal of the

record, there appears to be no material to suggest that the

present petitioner had either abetted the commission of the

offence or entered into any form of criminal conspiracy with the

co-accused. 5.7. This Court had earlier directed submission of an explanation

regarding the alleged lapses in the course of investigation. In

compliance, factual reports have been furnished by the concerned

Inquiry Officers and have duly been placed on record.
