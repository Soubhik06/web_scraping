 by the Supreme Court in T. Takano v. Securities and Exchange Board of

 relevant material emerging during investigation must be disclosed. 10. Having heard learned Senior Counsel for the Petitioners, I find merit
 in the contentions raised. It is strange that a request for grant of opportunity
 of personal hearing can be termed as 'unreasonable'. Principles of natural
 justice embody the doctrine of audi alteram partem. Insofar as non-supply
 of material documents is concerned, learned Senior Counsel is right in his
 submission that in T.Takano (supra), the Supreme Court has held that as a
 default rule, all relevant material must be disclosed as the purpose of
 disclosure of information is not merely individualistic, that is to prevent
 errors in the verdict but is also towards fulfilling the larger institutional
 purpose of fair trial and transparency. The Supreme Court observed that
 principles of fairness and transparency of adjudicatory proceedings are the
 corner stones of the principle of open justice. Keeping a party bereft of the

 process. It denies the party concerned and the public at large the ability to
 effectively scrutinize the decision of the authority since it creates an
 information asymmetry. The purpose of disclosure of information is not
 merely individualistic, that is to prevent errors in the verdict but is also
 towards fulfilling the larger institutional purpose of fair trial and
 transparency. Since the purpose of disclosure of information targets both the
 outcome (reliability) and the process (fair trial and transparency), it would
 be insufficient if only the material relied on is disclosed. Such a rule of
 disclosure, only holds nexus to the outcome and not the process and
 therefore as a default rule, all relevant material must be disclosed. 11. Petitioners have made out a prima facie case for grant of interim
