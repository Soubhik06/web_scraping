**Situation**

I have a corporate health insurance policy with NivaBupa and they use mediassist as there medical administration service provider. I filed an OPD claim with a valid doctor's prescription clearly stating the names of the products/medicines and the bill from 1MG as a proof of my purchase. I submitted the same within the stipulated time frame as well. But, my claim was partially approved with one of the products rejected stating that it was a 'soap' and therefore a consumable.

I argued and said that it was a medicated soap that was prescribed by a doctor for a treatment I am taking but they rejected my claim stating it is their policy to not reimburse soaps and lotions. Next, I asked them to provide me policy document stating this exception but they forwarded me a prospectus which is an year old. That is basically a benefits slides PDF stating what we get as employees from the health insurance. There were certain exceptions mentioned like registration fee, day care treatments, consumables during a medical procedure, but no where was it mentioned that a prescribed product from a doctor is not covered.

When I pointed it out that the document doesn't mention anything like that, mediassist dropped a one liner stating it is their policy terms and therefore my claim would not be approved. I escalated this and the next point of contact in the escalation matrix told me that this policy was covered in a virtual session that niva bupa had with the company. But I pointed out that it is not a documented evidence of exception. Zoom sessions cannot be taken as official documented evidence as if the situation was opposite and I would have claimed something which a Nivabupa guy promised over a call but not really in their policy, my claim would have been rejected too.

But, niva bupa stopped responding after that.

**TLDR of situation**

I filed an OPD claim with NivaBupa, my corporate health insurance, but one product was rejected as a "soap" despite being prescribed by a doctor. Mediassist refused to provide a policy document stating this exception, only citing a virtual session as evidence. I escalated the issue, arguing that undocumented policies should not affect claim approvals.
