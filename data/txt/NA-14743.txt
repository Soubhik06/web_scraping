The Competition Commission of India (CCI) on Thursday dismissed a complaint against BookMyShow, which alleged that the platform abused its dominant market position in the online movie ticketing space, creating an entry barrier for new players while also jacking up the price of movie tickets for end-consumers.

What were the allegations against BookMyShow? The case dates back to 2022 and stems from a complaint filed by Vijay Gopal, founder of online movie ticketing portal Showtyme. He made the following allegations against BookMyShow:

1. The complaint alleged that BookMyShow has entered into “ exclusive and refusal to deal ” contracts with cinemas for a period ranging from two to five years, forcing cinemas to sell their movie tickets only through its platform. The complaint also accused the platform of charging unreasonable and exorbitant fees under the guise of a convenience fee. This practice allegedly led to an increase in overall movie ticket prices and was “detrimental to consumers.”

2. As per the complaint, BookMyShow levies a convenience fee of Rs 25 per ticket sold on its platform, out of which it shares 50% with multiplexes and 25% with standalone theatres as commission. Gopal claimed that Showtyme, on the other hand, charges a commission of Rs 11 per user, out of which it shares 50% with cinemas. Despite the considerable difference, cinemas were unable to work with Showtyme, as BookMyShow allegedly gave loans and deposits to cinemas at “zero interest” .

Further, Gopal claimed that he approached more than 30 cinema and theatre managers in person, as well as all multiplexes in Hyderabad, via email. Some of them confirmed they could not sell tickets through BookMyShow’s platform because they had taken monetary deposits and signed exclusive contracts with BookMyShow. This allegedly created a barrier to new entrants like Showtyme and increased prices for end consumers .

3. The complaint said that BookMyShow holds at least a 90% market share in India’s movie ticket booking industry. It alleged that the platform is abusing its dominant position in violation of Section 4 of the Competition Act, 2002 by imposing “unfair and discriminatory conditions” on cinemas through exclusive deals that require them to sell all tickets only on BookMyShow. By entering exclusive agreements with cinema chains such as INOX PVR and Cinepolis, BookMyShow allegedly discouraged fair competition . This, the complaint claimed, was in breach of Section 4(2)(a), 4(2)(b), 4(2)(c), and 4(2)(d) of the Act.

Reliefs sought by Showtyme: In the wake of the aforementioned allegations, the complainant sought the following directions from India’s antitrust regulator:

a) Declare the exclusive agreements between BookMyShow and cinemas as “illegal and anti-competitive.”

b) Impose a penalty under Section 27 of the Competition Act, 2002.

c) Direct BookMyShow under Section 28 of the Act to sell no more than 25% of tickets for any cinema in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, and not more than 50% of the tickets in the rest of India, on its platform.

d) Order BookMyShow and its partner multiplexes and theatres–including Asian Multiplexes, Indra Cineplex, Cinepolis India and INOX PVR–to discontinue the practice of “not dealing with others” and abuse of dominant position.

What are the findings of CCI’s investigation? India’s antitrust watchdog agreed that BookMyShow has a significant lead in the online movie ticketing market due to its early-mover advantage , “significantly higher market share combined with its vertical integration, dependence of consumers on the enterprise, high entry barriers and lack of countervailing buyer power.”

“Entry into the market is challenging due to high financial costs for infrastructure and cinema onboarding, with several players like Fastickets and Justdial exiting in recent years,” the CCI noted.

However, the regulator said that the existence of online movie ticketing platforms like Paytm and Justickets evidences that BookMyShow does not have a monopoly in the market and the space remains wide open for new players.

In the absence of any evidence to the contrary, the CCI said it was unable to conclude that BookMyShow abused its dominant market position by imposing terms and conditions that could have market-wide repercussions and potentially deny access to new entrants.

“Opposite Party (BookMyShow) enjoys dominant position in the … ‘market for online intermediation services for booking of movie tickets in India’, however, no case of contravention of the provisions of Sections 4(2)(a)(i), 4(2)(b)(i) and 4(2)(c) of the Act is made out against OP in the present matter. Accordingly, the matter is directed to be closed forthwith,” the order read.

Further, the CCI found that BookMyShow’s exclusive contracts with certain cinema chains have not created entry barriers in the online movie ticketing space, noting that companies often use exclusivity clauses in commercial agreements to ensure business stability and efficiency.

The regulator also dismissed allegations of discriminatory revenue-sharing conduct against BookMyShow as “unsubstantiated” . It highlighted that in some instances, the platform shared a commission of up to 55% of the convenience fee with single-screen cinemas and up to 77% with multiplexes other than PVR. The antitrust watchdog said that revenue-sharing arrangements with different cinemas cannot be identical, as they depend on several factors, including the costs associated with a particular model and the size and scale of the business.

The CCI also rejected allegations that BookMyShow imposed conditions on cinemas, stating that cinemas reserve a portion of seats specifically for BookMyShow to avoid overlapping in ticket bookings , especially in Tier II and III cities where they lack real-time integration. It also noted that unsold seats are released to theatres before the show, which they can sell at their own discretion.

Why does it matter? BookMyShow has been the go-to platform for audiences to book movie tickets for well over a decade now. Although some competitors such as Eternal’s District, Zoonga and Justickets have emerged over the years, they have yet to reach BookMyShow’s scale of operations. Back in 2013, when BookMyShow became PVR’s “preferred e-ticketing channel partner,” MediaNama wrote about its market dominance .

After more than three years of investigation, the CCI has concluded that the platform held a dominant position due to competitive merits, just as the BookMyShow founder and CEO Ashish Hemrajani had previously claimed in an interview with MediaNama. While the competition regulator dismissed allegations of abuse of dominance against the company due to insufficient evidence this time, it may not always be the case.

“When an aggregator becomes the dominant force, it begins to put the squeeze on the margins for partners […] As online ticketing grows, theatres will realise that they are becoming increasingly dependent on BookMyShow, easily the single largest player in the space, because that is where they will source customers from. From a theatre’s perspective, they need to tie up with more online ticketing sites, because an increased dependency on a single player will create a monopoly. Think about it this way – if BookMyShow (hypothetically) decides not to allow tickets for a theater, what will they do?” — Nikhil Pahwa wrote from MediaNama back in 2013
