Something unsettling is unfolding in Chennai.

Veteran journalist **Omjasvin M D** of *The Times of India*—known for consistently exposing systemic rot in the **Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC)**—has been reassigned from the civic beat to the State Secretariat.

The timing is difficult to ignore.

His transfer came just days after he uncovered a **₹2 crore tender scam**: tenders were floated *after* the civic work was completed, allowing contractors to be paid retroactively through fabricated paperwork. A textbook case of financial misappropriation using public funds.

But this isn't a one-off. Omjasvin has methodically documented a long trail of civic malpractice:

* **Illegal Parking Fee Collection**: GCC continued collecting fees at Marina and Besant Nagar even after the contract expired in 2023—activists called it “organized loot.”
* **Unauthorized Constructions**: The Madras High Court reprimanded GCC for inaction—only 1,966 of 4,115 notices enforced.
* **Backdated Tenders for Completed Work**: ₹2 crore released fraudulently by manipulating documentation.
* **Intimidation of Whistleblowers**: Residents reporting violations in Ward 54 faced threats. GCC remained passive.
* **₹486 Cr Road Projects Stagnant**: Key stretches like Royapuram and T Nagar remain neglected despite a ₹700 Cr budget.
* **₹430 Cr Toilet Privatization Scam**: Cost per seat increased 115 times; facilities remain unusable.
* **Encroachments on Koladi Lake**: 250 illegal homes built with no meaningful enforcement.
* **Environmental Violations on Beaches**: GCC and CMRL dumped debris at Besant Nagar beach—no cleanup to date.
* **₹17 Cr Smart Cycle Project Collapse**: Docking stations defunct, bikes stolen or unused.
* **Streetlights Installed on Lakebeds**: ₹2 Cr wasted lighting up Puzhal and Korattur lakes.

He even reported on a 2019–20 government audit that flagged crores in misused funds—no action was taken.

Now, just as his reporting gains momentum, Omjasvin is suddenly moved off the beat. Publicly, he called it a “new start.” But that doesn’t mask the larger question:

[The closer one gets to the seat of power, the farther they move from journalism](https://preview.redd.it/uu32cf19lyye1.png?width=1170&amp;format=png&amp;auto=webp&amp;s=25d22dbb1f143c23e13284c836e05891d309670b)

&gt;

This shift—dressed up as routine—feels more like a **strategic sidelining** of a journalist who has spent years holding civic power accountable. One who didn’t flinch while exposing corruption under both AIADMK and DMK regimes.

It also lays bare a deeper hypocrisy.

Just recently, the Tamil Nadu CM tweeted about India’s declining press freedom under the BJP. But here in Chennai, reporters face a quieter threat—not raids or arrests, but displacement.

If this exposé had involved a BJP-run city, the “attack on journalism” headlines would’ve written themselves. But when it's within DMK’s fortress, we call it an “administrative routine.”

Let’s not pretend.

This isn’t about beats—it’s about boundaries. And Omjasvin crossed one too many.

**So,**the real question is not just *why* he was transferred—but what it says about the state of journalism in Tamil Nadu:

🔹 Who gets protected when journalists are removed? 
🔹 Who benefits when civic reporting is muzzled? 
🔹 And how do we ensure reporters like Omjasvin are not rewarded with silence for doing their job?
