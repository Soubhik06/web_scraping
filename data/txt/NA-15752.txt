A few weeks ago, I stumbled upon a distressing story about Indians trapped in scam operations in Myanmar. These victims are lured with false job promises, find themselves in a nightmare, forced to partake in elaborate scams under severe duress. One prevalent scam, known as "[pig butchering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_butchering_scam)," involves impersonating personas online to deceive victims into investing in fraudulent cryptocurrency schemes, leading to substantial financial losses.

CNN: [Billion dollar scam](https://edition.cnn.com/interactive/2023/12/asia/chinese-scam-operations-american-victims-intl-hnk-dst/)

The most alarming aspect, however, is the response from the Indian embassy in Myanmar when these victims sought help. The embassy's reaction, or rather lack thereof, raises critical questions about the protection and assistance provided to our nationals in dire straits abroad.

&gt;Months into his confinement, Rakesh called the Indian embassy in Yangon for help. With a group of friends clamoring around the phone they told an officer at the embassy that they are being held against their will and tortured. In a phone call verified by CNN, the official told them: “You people have landed in trouble by your decision, so you have to wait.” 
Rakesh pleaded with him. The fighting in Myanmar had gotten close to Gate 25 and they could hear shelling nearby. “There is one guy there who can help you, but he will charge something,” the official said finally, giving Rakesh the number of an alleged smuggler. 
CNN reached out to the Indian embassy in Yangon and Indian foreign ministry but did not hear back.

This situation begs the question: Why isn't this a national security concern for India? Our citizens are being held captive, possibly defrauding Indians back home out of millions. Yet, there's a deafening silence from the government. No public statements at the national level, no clear actions or insufficient communication. Moreover, the confiscation of passports upon arrival at these scam compounds leaves victims with no means to return home, even if they manage to escape.

There are similar stories coming out of Cambodia, where desperate job seekers are lured by unscrupulous agents in India and sold off to Chinese triads.

Al Jazeera: [Indian workers rescued from Digital Job Scams in Southeast Asia](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/10/8/indian-workers-rescued-from-digital-job-scams-in-southeast-asia) 
Times of India: [Tamil Nadu woman made to work 16 hours a day locked up, tortured in Cambodia](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chennai/tamil-nadu-woman-made-to-work-16-hours-a-day-locked-up-tortured-in-cambodia/articleshow/94258363.cms) 
Orissa Post: [International cybercrime syndicate busted by Rourkela police, five held](https://www.orissapost.com/international-cybercrime-syndicate-busted-by-rourkela-police-five-held/) 
Phnom Penh Post: [Kingdom, India explore new tactics against online crimes](https://www.phnompenhpost.com/national/kingdom-india-explore-new-tactics-against-online-crimes)

(As you can see from the last two stories, there have been some steps taken but a complete lack of acknowledgment as regards the scale of this problem or a conversation at the national level.)

Why is this issue not garnering the attention and action it desperately needs? What can we, as a community, do to raise awareness and prompt a response from the authorities? Looking forward to hearing your thoughts and insights on this critical issue.
