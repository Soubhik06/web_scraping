PhysicsWallah Limited filed its Updated Draft Red Herring Prospectus (UDRHP-I) with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) on September 6, 2025, for a proposed initial public offering (IPO). The offer, aggregating up to Rs 3,820 crore, comprises a fresh issue of equity shares worth up to Rs 3,100 crore.

In addition, the offer also includes an offer for sale (OFS) of equity shares aggregating up to Rs 720 crore by the company’s founder and CEO Alakh Pandey, and co-founder Prateek Boob. Specifically, each of the promoters is offering shares aggregating up to Rs 360 crore each.

The company proposes to list its equity shares on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) and the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE). Notably, Kotak Mahindra Capital Company Limited, J.P. Morgan India Private Limited, Goldman Sachs (India) Securities Private Limited, and Axis Capital Limited are acting as the book-running lead managers to the offer. To explain, these managers are companies that assist the DRHP company in the pre-issuing process in the specific context of IPOs.

For context, Physics Wallah Limited is an Indian ed-tech firm developing a variety of online and offline learning platforms and providing a wide range of educational courses and services, primarily to students preparing for competitive examinations in India. Significantly, it will be the first Indian ed-tech unicorn to go public in the country.

PhysicsWallah plans to use the funds raised from its IPO primarily for business expansion and growth initiatives, as detailed in its UDRHP-I. The company has earmarked a substantial portion of the fresh issue for physical infrastructure, allocating approximately Rs 460.5 crore for new offline and hybrid centre fit-outs, and Rs 548.3 crore for lease payments for such existing centres. Meanwhile, a sum of Rs 710 crore will support marketing initiatives to enhance brand presence.

Furthermore, the company will also invest in its subsidiaries to strengthen its market position. This includes a Rs 47.16 crore investment in Xylem Learning for new centres, and lease payments for hostel facilities, as well as Rs 33.7 crore for lease payments for Utkarsh Classes & Edutech Private Limited.

Additionally, PhysicsWallah plans to allocate Rs 200.1 crore for server and cloud infrastructure, and a further Rs 26.5 crore to acquire a greater stake in Utkarsh Classes. Notably, the company has stated that a portion of the funds will be used for future acquisitions and general corporate purposes.

PhysicsWallah’s revenue from operations stood at Rs 2,886.64 crore in the fiscal year ending March 31, 2025, a significant rise from Rs 1,940.71 crore in FY24. The company’s EBITDA reached Rs 193.19 crore in FY25, following a negative EBITDA of Rs 829.34 crore in FY24.

Elsewhere, the company’s net losses narrowed to Rs 243.25 crore in FY25, an improvement from a loss of Rs 1,131.13 crore in FY24.

In addition, the company’s net worth stood at Rs 1,945.37 crore as of March 31, 2025, improving substantially from a sub-zero figure of Rs 861.79 crore in FY24.

The company also reduced its total borrowings to Rs 32.7 lakh as on March 31, 2025, significantly down from Rs 1,687.3 crore in FY24.

A rise in its user base accompanies this financial performance, as the company now serves 4.46 million paid users, up from 3.63 million users in FY24.

The company reported losses in previous financial years, despite generating revenue growth. Consequently, it cannot guarantee that it will achieve or maintain profitability in future periods. Continued losses may restrict reinvestment in technology, expansion of offline centres, and development of new products.

The business model depends on acquiring new students and retaining existing ones across online and offline platforms. Rising competition or dissatisfaction with course quality could lower enrolments. And a decline in student base would reduce subscription renewals and overall revenue.

The company’s ability to deliver courses depends on attracting and retaining qualified faculty. High attrition rates or inability to recruit suitable replacements may disrupt course delivery and affect the learning experience for students.

The company depends substantially on the leadership of its founders and promoters: Pandey and Boob. Therefore, their exit or reduced involvement could negatively affect brand value and business continuity.

Franchisees operate 33 PhysicsWallah offline centres as of March 31, 2025, and their compliance with laws and contractual obligations directly affects operations. Defaults in timely payments, non-adherence to exclusivity clauses, or quality lapses at franchise-operated centres may lead to disputes, reputational damage, and legal costs.

The company has grown its offline presence since 2023, which increases exposure to risks of securing properties, obtaining local permits, and managing higher employee and infrastructure costs. It has not finalised all sites for proposed centres, making the roll-out vulnerable to delays, unplanned expenses, or regulatory non-approvals.

One of the company’s subsidiaries, iNeuron, faced procedural non-compliances such as not maintaining a separate bank account as required under the Companies Act, 2013. Such issues could result in penalties and affect integration with the PhysicsWallah conglomerate.

The company must ensure the safety and security of students at its offline centres. Any incidents of injury, harassment, or inadequate safety measures could lead to reputational damage, legal claims, and regulatory scrutiny.

The company has not declared dividends during the current fiscal year, as well as the last three fiscal years. Future dividend payments will depend on profitability, capital needs, and regulatory requirements, and as such, investors cannot assume regular payouts.

The company is subject to Indian competition law, and any finding of anti-competitive conduct by the Competition Commission of India (CCI) could lead to penalties and restrictions. Such outcomes could affect operations, brand reputation, and growth strategy.

For context, PhysicsWallah is currently involved in CCI proceedings, wherein the National Restaurant Association of India (NRAI) has alleged thatcertain activities conducted by the edtech company are in contravention of the Competition Act, 2002.

After public listing, the company’s shares may fall under SEBI surveillance frameworks such as Additional Surveillance Measure (ASM) or Graded Surveillance Measures (GSM). As such, these restrictions could reduce liquidity and impact share prices.
