Miyapur police in Hyderabad have booked 25 celebrities and social media influencers for allegedly promoting betting and gambling apps.

The FIR names actors like Rana Daggubati, Vijay Devarakonda, Prakash Raj, Manchu Lakshmi, Praneetha, and Nidhi Agarwal for endorsing betting platforms through pop-up ads and social media posts. It also lists 19 influencers for directly promoting multiple gambling platforms through promotional videos and advertisements.

The police filed the case under Sections 318(4) and 112 read with 49 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), along with Sections 3, 3(A), and 4 of the Telangana Gaming Act, and Section 66(D) of the Information Technology Act. Police have initiated an investigation into the financial networks supporting these platforms and are assessing the extent of celebrity involvement in the alleged gambling racket.

The FIR highlights the involvement of celebrities and influencers with both legally recognized platforms like A23 and Junglee Rummy, as well as widely known illegal entities such as PariMatch and FairPlay. However, all these platforms remain banned in Telangana, as the states of Telangana and Andhra Pradesh prohibit all activities related to real-money online gaming, including skill-based gaming. However, Andhra Pradesh, in an effort to augment tax revenue, may consider permitting lotteries and online gaming.

“They are games of skill, but in Telangana, they have been banned since 2017. Unlike in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, where the High Courts overruled similar bans, there has been no stay or striking down of the law in Telangana,” explained Jay Sayta, Technology and Gaming lawyer.

“Gambling & betting is a state subject, and hence one has to examine whether a particular game is legal or not as per a particular state’s gaming law. While there is fragmentation in state laws, all the names mentioned in this case are Telugu stars or Telugu influencers. This has been going on for the last few weeks. For example, Bollywood stars have also been associated with similar promotions, yet they haven’t been named—only Telugu stars have been,” he further added.

For instance, Shah Rukh Khan promotes A23, while Ajay Devgn endorses Junglee Rummy. Recently, Urvashi Rautela also came under scrutiny for promoting 1xBet, a widely controversial and illegal offshore entity.

The FIR charges the actors under various sections.

“Whoever cheats and thereby dishonestly induces the person deceived to deliver any property to any person, or to make, alter, or destroy the whole or any part of a valuable security, or anything which is signed or sealed, and which is capable of being converted into a valuable security, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, and shall also be liable to fine.”

“Whoever, being a member of a group or gang, either singly or jointly, commits any act of theft, snatching, cheating, unauthorised selling of tickets, unauthorised betting or gambling, selling of public examination question papers or any other similar criminal act, is said to commit a petty organised crime.”

Simply put, the complainant invoked these sections in the case, alleging that promoting betting apps deceives people into risking their money (Section 318(4)), involves participation in unauthorized gambling as part of a larger network (Section 112), and aids these illegal activities (Section 49).

Moreover, Section 66(D) of the Information Technology Act, states:

“Punishment for cheating by personation by using computer resource: Whoever, by means of any communication device or computer resource, cheats by personation, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years and shall also be liable to a fine which may extend to one lakh rupees.”

In this context, the argument stems from the claim that celebrities and influencers promoting betting apps indirectly mislead users, making them liable for punishment.

However, Sayta pointed out that these are non-bailable offences and questioned whether the charges truly amount to cheating.

“Offshore gambling websites have been a problem for a while, so cracking down on them is a good step. But instead of just using the Gambling Act, they are being booked under cheating and other serious charges under the BNS and IT Act—that’s my concern. These provisions don’t really apply here; they’re just being used to make the offences non-bailable, making it harder to get bail,” he stated.

“Who has been cheated, and how they have been cheated—that hasn’t been established. Secondly, even if someone loses money on the platform, how does that make the endorser or celebrity guilty of cheating? That’s something that remains unclear,” he added.

States are ramping up restrictions on real-money gaming and gambling, signalling a tougher regulatory landscape. Haryana recently introduced a new gambling bill, while Tamil Nadu has further tightened its rules surrounding real money gaming. At the same time, legitimate gaming businesses are struggling with the burden of 28% GST, pushing back against the taxation framework. In response to growing scrutiny, industry bodies have introduced a code of ethics to self-regulate and address concerns, intending to mitigate the ongoing tension between regulation and the gaming industry’s efforts to find a middle ground.
