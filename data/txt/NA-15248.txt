If you use a mobile number to verify users as part of your business, you may fall within the scope of the draft amendment to the Telecommunication Cybersecurity Rules, 2024. The draft introduces a new category of services covered under the rulesâTelecommunication Identifier User Entity (TIUE). These are defined as entities (other than a licensee or an authorised entity) that use telecommunication identifiers to identify users or to provide/deliver services.

TIUEs must fulfill several obligations, such as collecting data on how they use telecom identifiers, sharing it with the government, and suspending the use of certain identifiers when ordered.

Besides including a new entity category under the rules, the amendment also introduces a new platform. The draft amendment suggests that the government, by itself or through its agencies, intends to establish a Mobile Number Validation (MVN) platform to prevent cybersecurity incidents. It will issue directions to authorised and licensed entities regarding the form and manner in which they must participate in the platform. TIUEs can, either by themselves or based on directions from the central/state government or government agencies, place a request to verify telecom identifiers or their users/customers.

A strict reading of the definition of TIUEs under the Act implies that anything from a food delivery service like Swiggy or Zomato, to WhatsApp and other communication services, or even a store at the local mall that messages you the e-receipt of your purchase, could fall within the scope of TIUEs.

TIUEs have a range of responsibilities that the original version of the rules limited strictly to telecom companies. These include:

In the interest of ensuring telecom cybersecurity, the central government (or its authorised agencies) can ask TIUEs to submit data related to the telecommunication identifiers that they use.

They can also ask TIUEs to provide this data from designated points through digital means, to enable processing and storage. The government may analyse the data collected from TIUEs to take measures to enhance telecom cybersecurity. It may also share this analysis with law enforcement agencies, telecom entities, TIUEs, or users. No data disseminated to these entities may be used for purposes other than ensuring telecom cybersecurity.

Every TIUE must ensure compliance with the standards and directions that the government releases to prevent misuse of telecommunication identifiers, equipment, networks, or services.

When the government considers immediate action necessary or expedient in the public interest, it can pass an order directing the TIUE to temporarily stop using a specific telecom identifier to identify customers or deliver servicesâwithout issuing prior notice. It can also ask telecom companies to suspend the use of that identifier.

The government will provide a copy of the suspension order to the affected TIUE or person. Within 30 days of the order, the TIUE must submit a written representation explaining why it should not suspend the identifier. After providing the affected entity with a chance to be heard, the government may revoke, modify, or uphold its order, recording its reasons in writing. The modification may include further directions to prohibit or limit the use of such identifiers.

If necessary, or on a TIUEâs request, the government may share a list of telecom identifiers that it has acted upon under these orders.

Once the platform receives a verification request, it passes it on to the licensed/authorised entities, who will then process the request and send a response in a government-specified format. The platform will provide mobile number validation services only for validating customers or users associated with a telecommunication identifier for services linked to such an identifier. TIUEs and authorised entities must comply with data protection laws while doing so.

If a TIUE chooses to verify identifiers by itself, it has to pay three rupees per request. Of this, the government/agency will keep one rupee and send the rest to the relevant authorised entity or licensee undertaking the mobile number validation. However, if the TIUE is carrying out this verification based on government directions, it has to pay only one rupee and fifty paisa per request. Of this, the government will keep fifty paisa and send the rupee to the relevant authorised/licensed entity. If the central or state government department/ministries use the platform for verification, they do not have to pay any fee.

The MVN gives platforms the option to verify each individual user. This means that users can no longer rely on services that generate temporary phone numbers or services like Google Voice, which allow people to create free phone numbers. This is because TIUEs will not be able to verify these numbers against the records of licensed telecom operators.

With no option but to provide service providers with their actual phone numbers, one could argue that this raises concerns about user identification at scale.
