What is wrong with electoral targeting?
&gt;Targeted Marketing: Good for Elections, Bad for Government
https://www.themuse.com/advice/targeted-marketing-good-for-elections-bad-for-government

Basic premise is that you can tailor specific messages for each individual while campaigning. While there is nothing wrong in it and a door-to-door campaigner could do the same when he gets to know about me. Amazon and Flipkart advertise products to me all the time based on my interests and I largely like them and ‘purchase’ the offering and the product is ‘delivered’. But in elections, my ‘vote’ is the purchase, but delivery in policy of what I was promised becomes tricky. Because the party promised variations of the same policy, often contradictory, to everyone. It becomes politics of appeasement with no delivery in the end.

&gt;An administration that must repay its (not “the,” but “its”) electorate by keeping specific promises to specific groups will have a very difficult time pushing forward big ideas or sweeping reforms or comprehensive agendas. And the fundamental role of government, in the modern transaction, gets lost.

**Persistent and the other govtech companies**

How does aadhar really solve de-duplication in welfare schemes? If it is just used as a card which you show someone to get service, it doesn’t help. The idea is that you really know it’s the same person when he presses his fingerprint. And this needs to be done before the service is delivered to the beneficiary. *Comment's I can't quote someone on* [3]. For now it’s only functioning as a paper card, the same as BPL census in all practicality. The push is to get people enrolled. *To be fair, the biggest case study of Aadhaar actually being used successfully is JIO and not a welfare scheme as were promised and sold the idea to*. Yes, there are rough links on saving through DBT, but the exact role of Aadhar verifications and their continuous usage in those schemes is guess work. It’s more episodic, a change in IDs will lead to reduction in fake names based on previous IDs until the new IDs fakes start flooding the market.

The push for verifications being done for service delivery is as important if the government wants to push this as a welfare scheme. But there hasn't been much visible movement by the government on it. 

Given the government will come to pushing verifications too one fine day, one has to realize it’s not just software that is needed. It’s hardware too. 

Who stands to benefit most from these pushes? Big players like Persistent Systems. Persistent has been banking on Aadhar technologies from the early start (Some early email verified signature product et al - can’t find a link now). They are already one of the top players in government tech projects and procurements and stand to reap the most out of all this. They are already building aadhar based apps for the government etc (*apart from the thousand other apps which usually don’t get used or data updated after first couple of months* )

More subject comments in appendix on thumbprints floating in the market [3]

**Conflict of Interest and what’s wrong with it?** 
Basically an entity who is deemed to make monetary or otherwise profits out of the favourable decision of a policy can’t be expected to be fair and unbiased about exploring and debating the rights, wrongs and ramifications of it.

Most schemes have only been told to collect Aadhaar cards as a new entry in their MIS (as a number -- same as BPL). There is little or none talk on getting it verified or having processes around it in policy*

[3] *Also, if the government does go around pushing for verifications, the welfare demographic will be giving a new swipe every other day. Giving thumb impressions will be normalized for Aadhar and non-Aadhar products. My password will be floating around in multiple devices and securing it is going to be difficult. Even now a lot of people new to the internet end up being phished. When aadhar is my password, phishing is hardware devices saving my thumbprints all the time. There will be breaches and in the worst case scenario, it will lead to thumbprints being useless as a measure of security. Will then the government own up to its mistakes and let this craze pass? Likely not, given the capital and infrastructure being spent on it and the politics we will live in bliss ignorance while the government works on strenthening its security further which it should have done before rolling it out. Are we being pushed in an insecure future?*
