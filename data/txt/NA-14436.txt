Florida Attorney General (AG) James Uthmeier has escalated his office’s investigation into Roblox Corporation’s child safety issues by issuing criminal subpoenas to the company. In a video shared on X, Uthmeier accused Roblox of profiting from children while failing to protect them, stating, “They enabled our kids to be abused.”

We are issuing criminal subpoenas to Roblox, which has become a breeding ground for predators to gain access to our kids. pic.twitter.com/vcyTVnkrxU

The subpoenas aim to gather information on suspected criminal activity within the platform, including evidence related to both predators and victims. This move follows previous criticisms of Roblox’s child safety measures, including a report from Hindenburg Research in October 2024. In response, Roblox introduced new safety features such as limiting under-13 users from accessing unrated experiences, social hangouts, and free-form user creation. Furthermore, the company also introduced age-checks and parental controls in 2025.

However, despite these efforts, scrutiny continues, with lawsuits in Louisiana and San Francisco alleging that Roblox inadequately protects children from online predators. Additionally, Iraq recently banned the platform due to concerns that it enables direct user communication, exposing children and adolescents to potential exploitation or cyber-extortion.

In his statement, Uthmeier emphasized that authorities will hold companies accountable if they act carelessly and expose children to harm. He asserted, “Companies like Roblox have become breeding grounds for predators to get to and have access to our kids.” Uthmeier concluded, “Companies that act carelessly and expose them (children) to harm will be held accountable”.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier’s office, which launched an investigation into Roblox earlier this year over child safety concerns, has now escalated it sharply with a criminal subpoena seeking a wide array of company records. The subpoena, issued by the Office of Statewide Prosecution in October 2025, compels Roblox to hand over an extensive set of documents and data that could reveal how it has managed user protection, abuse reports, and internal oversight mechanisms.

Specifically, the state has demanded all business records connected to safety and moderation operations, including internal emails, memos, and policy documents detailing how Roblox identifies and handles reports of grooming, harassment, or other criminal behaviour. It also calls for logs of communications with law enforcement, records of complaints from users or parents, and reports of disciplinary actions taken against accounts suspected of endangering minors.

Furthermore, the Attorney General’s office has asked for documentation of Roblox’s moderation algorithms, escalation processes for abuse cases, and evidence of any audits or reviews of its safety systems.

A recent study documents how Roblox’s immense volume of user-generated content makes real-time moderation extremely challenging. The research notes that both automated systems and human moderators struggle with “managing real-time user interactions and the sheer volume of user-generated content”.

Meanwhile, a rapid-analysis report by the Australian Research Council Centre of Excellence for the Digital Child observes that although Roblox boasts millions of under-13 users, the platform’s safety controls are under-developed relative to its scale. The report states that the in-platform virtual currency (Robux) and user-created experience model raise monetisation and exposure risks for children.

In a further investigation published by Revealing Reality , researchers created test accounts representing users aged five, nine, 10, and 13, and discovered that those accounts could access adult-coded or sexually suggestive environments, overhear explicit voice-chat, and engage with older users without effective age-verification barriers. Roblox has since introduced age verification measures and age-gating. However, a subsequent investigation found that adults could still interact with children using fake accounts.

Hindenburg Research’s October 2024 investigation accused Roblox of fostering an unsafe environment for children, alleging that predators have repeatedly exploited the platform’s design and moderation failures. The report described Roblox as an “X-rated pedophile hellscape”, claiming that its chat and group features enable predators to “efficiently target hundreds of children, with no up-front screening to prevent them from joining the platform”. It documented cases where adult users allegedly groomed minors through in-game experiences and private channels, often using suggestive avatars and explicit virtual spaces to initiate contact.

Furthermore, Hindenburg said that thousands of users accessed sexually explicit groups and games such as “Adult Studios”, which remained online despite containing overt sexual content and child pornography. The firm asserted that Roblox’s content filters and parental controls were ineffective, allowing even under-nine users to encounter such content.

It also cited examples of organised communities trading child sexual abuse imagery and of predators allegedly using Roblox’s private servers to communicate with minors away from public moderation.

Additionally, Hindenburg interviewed former moderators, who said that heavy workloads and low pay left them unable to review harmful content promptly. The report concluded that Roblox’s safety systems were ineffective, enabling predators to operate with limited oversight while the company profited from children’s engagement.

Louisiana and San Francisco became focal points this year for legal action accusing Roblox of exposing children to sexual predators. In August, Louisiana’s attorney general sued Roblox, arguing the company allowed predators to operate on its platform by failing to enforce effective age verification, moderation, and parental controls.

Additionally, the complaint contends Roblox prioritised growth and revenue over child safety and even distributed child sexual-abuse material, and it seeks injunctions and damages under state consumer-protection law.

Earlier in 2025, a San Mateo County suit , brought on behalf of a minor, accused Roblox and Discord of furnishing a “hunting ground for child-sex predators”. The complaint alleges a repeated pattern: predators meet children inside Roblox, coax them to other chat apps, then groom, coerce, or exchange explicit material, with the platforms’ features and lax safeguards enabling abuse.

Similarly, another lawsuit from parents in Iowa alleges that their child was kidnapped by an adult whom she met and communicated with online on Roblox.

Moreover, these high-profile actions sit alongside a growing cluster of lawsuits. For example, Bay Area teenage plaintiffs and other families have filed separate lawsuits this year that repeat the same core claims: insufficient in-game protections, inadequate monitoring, and failures to prevent adults posing as children from contacting minors.

The lawsuits and investigations surrounding Roblox in 2025 have drawn attention to the broader issue of child safety in digital platforms used by millions of young people. As one of the world’s largest online gaming spaces, Roblox has become a focal point for questions about how effectively tech companies safeguard minors from harm while maintaining open, user-generated environments.

The legal actions in Louisiana, San Francisco, and Florida are significant because they test the boundaries of existing child-protection and consumer laws in the digital sphere. They raise concrete questions about whether platforms can be held responsible when predators exploit communication features or when safety systems fail to prevent abuse. Moreover, these cases may help clarify what constitutes adequate moderation, reporting procedures, and parental oversight in large-scale online communities.

Consequently, Authorities in multiple states are investigating, and the rising number of complaints highlights a broader pattern of public and legal scrutiny over children’s online safety.

Taken together, the current wave of litigation matters because it could influence how companies design, monitor, and account for safety within digital environments that attract young audiences. Regardless of the eventual legal outcomes, the proceedings highlight the tension between accessibility, creative freedom, and the responsibility to protect children online.
