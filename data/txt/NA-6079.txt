 and the fact that they cohabitated is admitted. 15. It is relevant to note that Section 23 of the DV Act
 empowers the Magistrate to grant interim orders if the
 application prima facie discloses that the respondent is
 committing an act of domestic violence, has committed an act of
 domestic violence or may commit an act of domestic violence
 against the aggrieved person. It is settled law that marriage is not
 necessary for the maintainability of an application for relief
 under the DV Act and a relationship akin to a marriage is
 sufficient to attract the provisions of the DV Act . It is trite law
 that having children is a strong indication that a relationship
 between the parties is in the nature of marriage. 16. The learned Family Court in order dated 17.08.2022 has
 explicitly noted that although there was heavy discord between
 the parties, their marriage was solemnised on 24.02.2003. A bare
 perusal of the said order indicates that the respondent had stated

 that the parties cohabitated for almost half a decade after the
 respondent's separation from her first husband and they had a
 child, this Court is of the opinion that prima facie their
 relationship was in the nature of marriage and the subsistence of
 the respondent's marriage to her first husband, ispo facto, will
 not hinder her right to claim relief under the ambit of the DV Act .
 It is also relevant to note that even though the marriage between
 the parties was declared to be null and void by the learned Family
 Court, however, they clearly socialised in public as a couple as
 well. 18. The defences along with the allegations and counter
 allegations, would be the subject matter of the trial, and would
 have to be decided after the parties have led their evidence. 19. It is not disputed that the impugned order is only an order
 of interim maintenance. The learned MM would pass a final
 order in regard to the maintenance after considering the evidence
 on record. 20. Even otherwise, the petitioner is an able-bodied man and a
 maintenance of ₹4,000/- per month, in the opinion of this Court,
 is reasonable at the interim stage. 21. The learned MM is directed that the final order be passed
 in the case uninfluenced by the findings made in the impugned
 order dated 30.11.2022 or this order. 22. In view of the above, this Court finds no reason to interfere
 with the impugned orders and the petition is dismissed in the
 aforesaid terms. AMIT MAHAJAN, J
