Once again pooram is here. it’s a cultural event that belongs to all of Kerala. And just like every year, thousands will gather — from across the state and even from abroad — to experience this cultural festival.

But before we jump into the celebrations, here’s something we all need to hear.
Because every year, the same problems repeat. And every year, we forget that the way we behave during Pooram reflects not just on Thrissur, but on all of us — as Keralites.

Here are few things we desperately need to fix this year:

1. Stop Coming Drunk
Yes, it’s a celebration. No, it’s not a reason to get drunk in public and ruin it for others.

Every year, there are groups who show up completely wasted — reeking of alcohol, yelling, picking fights, or just creating discomfort for the people around them. It's not just shameful — it's dangerous. There are families, children, elderly people, and foreigners in the crowd.
If you truly love Pooram, experience it sober. Respect the people around you.

2. Respect the Tourists
We all take pride in seeing foreigners show up to watch Pooram — it feels good, right? But what’s the point if some of us can’t behave properly around them?
No staring, no sneaky pictures, no inappropriate comments, and absolutely no harassment. If they need help, offer it. If they look confused, guide them. Let’s actually mean it when we say “Athithi Devo Bhava.”

This festival is our chance to show the world what Kerala is all about — don’t turn it into a shame story someone posts online later.

3. Stop Treating Thekkinkadu Maidan Like a Dustbin
Every Pooram, it’s the same scene. By the end of the day, the whole place is buried under plastic bottles, ice cream wrappers, and disposable visharis.

And what’s worse? There are bins placed all over. So clearly, the issue isn’t infrastructure — it’s us.

Take a second to dispose of your waste properly. This isn’t just about looking clean for tourists — it’s about basic self-respect as a citizen. Let's not let our pride become polluted.

4. The Vishari Throwing Trend — Let’s Kill It
Last year, it started with one person. Then another. Then a whole crowd began throwing their visharis into the air and onto the ground, as if it was some sort of ritual.

Let’s be honest — it was nothing but pure herd mentality. And it turned into a national embarrassment.

This year, don’t be the one who starts it. Don’t be the one who follows it. Be the one who stands up and says, “Enough of this nonsense.”

Thrissur Pooram is a celebration of Kerala’s cultural identity. It’s loud, vibrant, and glorious — and it deserves to be celebrated with pride, not chaos. Whether you’re from Thrissur or any other part of the state, this festival is yours too.

Let’s show the world what a real festival looks like — one with heart, culture, and yes, civic sense.
