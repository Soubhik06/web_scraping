YouTube removed an episode of the talent-comedy show ‘India’s Got Latent’ on February 11, in accordance with a government directive. Kanchan Gupta, Senior Advisor at the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, announced the development on X. YouTube confirmed to MediaNama that they removed the video following a government request, but did not specify the department or ministry that issued the order or the laws and provisions that it invoked.

The takedown followed an allegedly offensive statement made by podcaster and influencer Ranveer Allahbadia that drew sharp criticism from various quarters.

Priyank Kanoongo, a member of the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC), had written a letter to Google directing them to remove the offending videos from YouTube. The NHRC felt that the content was prima facie in violation of “various provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, the Information Technology (IT) Act and other applicable laws.”

However, lawyers MediaNama spoke to questioned the NHRC’s jurisdiction over content removals online. Pointing out that the NHRC’s powers come from The Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 , Raman Jit Singh Chima, Asia Pacific Policy Director at Access Now, stated that the Act itself did not provide the NHRC with the power to take down content online. “…The NHRC does not have the legal powers to legally compel any takedown of content or web censorship,” he said, adding, “That is a power that is currently granted under the Information Technology Act, specifically under section 69A.”

He added that while section 69A allows the government to block web content, it does not allow them to do so on the grounds of morality or obscenity. “In the past when government orders at the state or central level have blocked content in relation to morality and obscenity, they’ve actually had to argue that it is a threat to public order. It would be therefore important in this case to understand if the Ministry has blocked this content stating that it is causing a public order issue, otherwise technically the IT Act does not allow for blocking solely on concerns related to morality or obscenity under section 69A,” he said.

Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma announced that the Guwahati Police had filed FIRs against Allahbadia and various influencers present during the show, including Ashish Chanchlani, Jaspreet Singh, Apoorva Makhija, Ranveer Allahbadia, Samay Raina and others. Raina and Allahbadia are facing the heat in another state too, as the Mumbai Police have reportedly summoned the duo for an inquiry into their comments. Samay Raina is the creator of the ‘India’s Got Latent’ show, which is at the centre of the controversy. The show follows the format of the popular ‘Got Talent’ franchise, and involves contestants lining up before a panel of judges to perform.

The Guwahati police filed cases under sections 79, 95, 294, 296 of the BNS, which deal with insulting the modesty of a woman, hiring a child to commit an offence, sale of obscene material and public obscenity respectively. Allahbadia had made the allegedly inappropriate comments on an episode of ‘India’s Got Latent,’ where he asked a contestant if they would rather watch their parents have intercourse every day for the rest of their lives or join them once and stop it forever. He later released an apology video on February 10, attributing the comment to a “lapse in judgement” on his part.

The controversy surrounding the comedy show is reminiscent of the furore that followed a celebrity roast show organised by comedy group All India Bakchod (AIB) in 2015. The Mumbai Police booked the organisers and many of the celebrity attendees over obscenity and criminal conspiracy charges, after a complaint alleged that the participants had used “vulgar, obscene and pornographic” language.

While no arrests were made in the case as the Bombay High Court stopped the Mumbai Police from doing so, AIB soon took down the video and offered its apologies to those offended by it.
