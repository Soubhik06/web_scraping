A heartbreaking case from Karnataka highlights the devastating impact of cyber fraud on senior citizens. **On March 28, 2025, an elderly couple from Belagavi, Karnataka, died by suicide** after losing over **₹50 lakh** to scammers impersonating government officials. The fraudsters used **fear tactics, fake legal threats, and digital extortion** to siphon off the couple’s savings, ultimately driving them to despair. 

**How They Were Targeted** 
- The scammers **video-called** the couple, pretending to be officials from a telecom company and the Crime Branch. 
- They accused the 82-year-old victim of being linked to a **SIM card misuse case** and demanded a **"settlement fee"** of ₹5 lakh. 
- Over time, the criminals extorted **₹50 lakh+**, forcing the victim to take **a gold loan and sell his wife’s jewelry** to meet their demands. 
- The couple left behind a **suicide note**, expressing their helplessness and requesting that their **bodies be donated for medical research**. 

**Why Delhi Should Be Concerned** 
Cybercrime is a growing menace in **Delhi**, with senior citizens being among the **most vulnerable targets**: 
- **In the first six months of 2024, cybercriminals stole ₹452 crore from Delhiites**—a **158% rise** compared to the same period in 2023. 
- Fraudsters are increasingly **laundering stolen money through multiple digital wallets and cryptocurrency**, making recovery difficult. 

**Cyber Fraud Targeting Delhi’s Elderly** 
Delhi Police recently arrested **Priyesh Giri**, a man who scammed elderly citizens by **posing as an insurance agent**. 
- He approached victims under the pretense of **renewing health insurance** and convinced them to transfer large sums of money. 
- A **74-year-old woman lost ₹95,000** before realizing she had been issued a **fake policy**. 
- Investigators found that he had **scammed multiple elderly victims** over the past year. 

**Other Ways Cybercriminals Operate** 
- **Digital Extortion &amp; Fake Arrests**: Like in the Karnataka case, criminals pose as law enforcement to extort money. 
- **Banking Fraud &amp; Investment Scams**: Cybercriminals **lure victims with fake investment opportunities**—nearly **63,000 investment scams** were reported in India in early 2024. 
- **Use of AI &amp; Deepfakes**: Scammers impersonate **family members or officials using AI-generated voices** to manipulate victims. 
- **Misuse of Bank Accounts**: Fraudsters **bribe poor and illiterate individuals** to open bank accounts, later using them for money laundering. 

**Key Takeaways** 
1. **Elderly citizens are prime targets** for cyber fraud, with criminals exploiting their limited digital literacy and social isolation. 
2. **Cybercriminals use fear tactics** like "digital arrests" and fake legal threats to trap victims into making repeated payments. 
3. **Scam tactics are evolving rapidly**, with criminals using AI, cryptocurrency, and decentralized payment methods to avoid detection. 
4. **Prevention is crucial**: 
 - **Be cautious of unsolicited calls** claiming to be from banks, police, or government agencies. 
 - **Verify investment offers and insurance renewals** before making payments. 
 - **Report cyber fraud immediately** via the Cyber Crime Portal (1930) for potential recovery.
