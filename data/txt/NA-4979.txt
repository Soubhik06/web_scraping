The learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that the petitioner, who is

 the defacto complainant, filed an application before the Trial Court seeking the

 return of a sum of Rs.32,56,325/-, which was frozen by the respondent police

 during the course of investigation. The said amount was frozen from the

 accounts of unknown accused persons involved in an online trading scam

 operated through a WhatsApp group named “VIP 50 Market Guides.” 2. It is stated that the petitioner had transferred a total sum of

 Rs.51,20,100/- through RTGS transactions during the year 2025. Subsequently,

 he came to know that the assurances given by the accused were fraudulent in

 nature and that he did not receive any benefit from such investments. Therefore,

 he lodged a complaint against the accused persons for cheating and fraud.

 During investigation, the police froze a sum of Rs.32,56,325/- lying in the

 accounts of the unknown accused. Hence, the petitioner sought for return of the

 said amount, claiming that it rightfully belonged to him.

 petitioner failed to establish a clear nexus between the amount frozen and the

 amount transferred by him. The Trial Court further observed that the frozen

 money constituted case property and, therefore, must remain under police

 custody. Aggrieved by the said order, the petitioner has filed the present

 revision. 4. The learned counsel for the petitioner further submitted that the Trial

 Court failed to properly consider the particulars furnished by the petitioner at

 the time of lodging the complaint, which included all relevant RTGS details and

 bank statements evidencing the transfer of Rs.51,20,100/-. Despite the

 production of sufficient documentary proof establishing ownership over the said

 amount, the same was not considered by the Trial Court. Hence, he prayed that

 the findings of the Trial Court be set aside and the frozen amount be returned to

 the petitioner. 5. The learned Government Advocate (Criminal Side) raised an objection,

 by transferring the said amount through RTGS to the joint account of one

 David, Madhankumar, and Susan Joseph, maintained at the State Bank of India,

 transaction details prima facie tallied with the bank statements furnished by the

 defacto complainant, which clearly established that he had transferred the said
