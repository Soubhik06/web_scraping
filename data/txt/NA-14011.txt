The Software Freedom Law Centre ( SFLC.in ) India wrote to the Railway Ministry, urging the reconsideration and suspension of mandatory e-Aadhaar authentication for Tatkal ticket bookings. The organisation claims that the move expands the scope of Aadhaar usage beyond the law’s bounds without addressing the real, systemic issues plaguing user bookings.

This follows Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw’s recent announcement about Indian railways deploying e-Aadhaar authentication for Tatkaal bookings to help genuine users secure confirmed tickets. Users without such authentication would have to wait three days after account creation before they can book.

SFLC.in argued that such authentication fails to meet the three state-mandated requirements for Aadhaar use—legality, necessity, and proportionality—set out in the Justice K.S. Puttuswamy vs Union of India judgment. As per the court decree, there should be a clear legislative mandate authorising Aadhaar use, the measure should be the least intrusive mechanism for achieving a legitimate state aim, and it should balance individual rights and state interests.

Legality: The organisation explains that the Aadhaar Act or other laws lack any explicit statutory provision authorising Aadhaar use for Tatkal bookings. A government notification alone doesn’t satisfy the legislative backing requirement as mandated in the Puttuswamy and subsequent judgments.

Proportionality: The measure also lacks proportionality, as it places an undue burden on travellers who have not linked their Aadhaar details to their IRCTC accounts. Consequently, this move could risk the exclusion of vulnerable populations facing digital or infrastructural barriers.

Necessity: Finally, the test of necessity falls flat because such verification fails to address the root causes of the issues faced by the platform. The letter references several instances and reports highlighting the technical incapacity and systemic shortcomings of the Indian Railway Catering and Tourism Corporation (IRCTC) website. These problems include ticket unavailability due to the platform’s inability to handle high traffic, server crashes, and the recovery of the website only after tickets have vanished.

The organisation explained that while the Centre’s move aims to curb fraudulent practices, it doesn’t address the technical challenges faced by genuine users. Instead, SFLC.in requested that the Railway Ministry commission a technical audit of the IRCTC booking platform to identify the causes of these booking failures and continue strengthening bot detection and related solutions.

The government’s notification functions as yet another display of the expanding scope of Aadhaar authentication. In March 2025, the IT Ministry allowed private entities to use Aadhaar for face authentication, extending its usage beyond government organisations. Previously, the government also expanded this scope, allowing private entities to use authentication for e-commerce, tourism, healthcare, and hospitality. However, given the sensitive nature of Aadhaar data, the move raised privacy and data security concerns.

In its push to further Aadhaar usage, the government also launched a revamped application with facial recognition-based authentication in April 2025. Alongside this, the IT Minister directed the UIDAI (the operator of the Aadhaar project) to harmonise the Aadhaar Act with India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023.

In a different vein, cases of Aadhaar fraud have increased multifold. Earlier this year, four men in Uttar Pradesh were caught tampering with the system and manipulating the biometric data of over 1,500 cardholders. Previously, the Gujarat police had flagged the use of websites to create fake Aadhaar and PAN cards, aiding identity fraud. Besides this, the Ministry of Finance had disclosed that Aadhaar breaches through land records were a prominent reason behind the surge in Aadhaar-enabled payment system (AePS) fraud. For context , the AePS enables online interoperable financial inclusion transactions at micro-ATMs through business correspondents of any bank.
