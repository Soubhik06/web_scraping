These are actual events that happened with me in Hyderabad.

**Scenario A**

1. You ask for fuel worth Rs. 1000/-. 

2. Attendant fills till 300 and pulls out. Cashier tells him loudly that 'Arre, 1000 ka daalna hai'. 

3. Attendant goes on to set the meter at 700 and smiles apologetically.

4. Starts pumping petrol again BUT the meter isn't set to 0. It is still at 300. 

5. Pumping auto stops at 700. 

6. If you've paid 1000 already and go on your way, Congratulations, you've just done the equivalent of watching a Tushar Kapoor movie in a Mumbai multiplex on a weekend.

Chances are, the cashier had you distracted when the attendant resumed fuelling at the 300 mark. I was lucky enough that my partner hadn't stopped observing and protested instantly. Personally, I had a 'doubt' when he stopped because I was making the payment at the restart mark but if it weren't for an additional witness, I would not have protested.

**Scenario B**

This is similar to the first scenario but its execution was rather impressive.

1. You arrive at an empty petrol pump. Attendant tells you to wait because there is no power and that they need to start the generator.

2. You say that you want Rs. 1000 worth of fuel. Attendant *yells* for the cashier and says Rs. 1000 worth of fuel is required.

3. At the 210 mark, the meter stops. 

4. Attendant tells you that the power is back on and that they are shifting from generator mode to normal power.

4. Cashier in the meanwhile comes and asks you to pay Rs. 10 extra. You ask him why and he smiles at you warmly and says that they will fill the remaining 800 as round figure.

5. While you are still talking to the cashier, the power is back on and the attendant resumes filling. You don't see him zero-ise the meter and he stops at 800.

6. If you've paid 1000 and left, well, you just got robbed off Rs. 210 courtesy of some amazing team work by 3 people (The attendant, the cashier and the off-screen 'generator' operator)

I've only recently started driving a car and have faced the above events in a span of 2 weeks. When on bike, the amount of refuelling is not much and due to your proximity to the meter, it is not as easy to scam bikers. That being said, it is common knowledge that you have to personally visually ensure that the meter is set to 0. The following are some of the red flags/do's and dont's that I discovered while browsing around.

1. Always. Always. ALWAYS get off your lazy ass and stand outside your car with your head fixed towards the meter. 

2. Do not get distracted. Do not entertain anyone. Distractions can come in the form of a cashier or beggar or a stranger asking where a particular address is.

3. If the meter is stopped for any reason, end the transaction right there.

4. Always pay at the end. Even if the cashier asks you for the money, do NOT look at him while responding that you'll pay at the end.

5. If you are the first owner of your car, always choose to refuel closer to home. Your registration number gives a good indication of your area of residence and the attendants are less likely to scam you if you live near by. The above two incidents happened far away from home.

6. If you are going to pick someone up, choose to refuel after they've boarded. It's always good to have more sets of eyes.

7. If the hose at your petrol pump is extremely lengthy, it works in favour of the petrol pumps because the pumping stops when the petrol leaves the pumping machine NOT when it leaves the nozzle.

8. If you have a lot of time, you can shortlist a couple of petrol pumps by putting into practise /u/petty86 's [educational comment](http://www.reddit.com/r/india/comments/11isaf/ama_petrol_pump_owner/c6n7bzo)

The above list is not exhaustive. Please share your experiences and suggestions too. 

At the end of it all, you can also report these frauds to the consumer court, but the objective here is to try and compile a list of precautionary steps.
