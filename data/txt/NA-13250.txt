The Financial Intelligence Unit – India (FIU-IND) has made cybersecurity audits compulsory for all Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) service providers before they can register or continue as reporting entities under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA).

In a circular dated September 15, 2025, FIU-IND directed all the concerned directors, principal officers, and chief compliance officers of VDA service providers, including cryptocurrency exchanges, to submit a cybersecurity audit certificate from CERT-In empanelled auditors. The order states that compliance is mandatory with immediate effect.

VDA service providers include entities involved in exchange between cryptocurrencies and fiat currencies, crypto-to-crypto trading, transfer of digital assets, safekeeping or administration of digital assets, and financial services related to the sale of digital assets. For context, the Ministry of Finance had brought these activities under the PMLA through a notification dated March 7, 2023.

As per FIU-IND, VDA service providers must follow all obligations under the PMLA, including customer due diligence, record keeping, internal controls, employee training, and filing of suspicious transaction reports. Notably, failure to register or comply can lead to action under Section 13(2) of the PMLA.

The directive follows a series of major crypto thefts in India. In July 2024, WazirX lost about $235 million from a wallet hosted on Liminal, while CoinDCX lost around Rs 368 crore this year in a cyberattack on its internal operational account.

Experts say the cybersecurity audits could help improve user safety and aid probes into stolen funds. Harshal Bhuta, Partner at CA firm PR Bhuta & Co., told the Economic Times that the introduction of cyber security audits is because of recent crypto thefts.

Notably, he remarked that strict compliance, such as log maintenance and retention of subscriber data for prescribed period, would aid investigative agencies in tracing funds obscured through crypto transactions.

Elsewhere, Purushottam Anand, Advocate and Founder of Crypto Legal, told the Economic Times that FIU-IND has replaced the earlier ‘Fit & Proper’ certificate, required from new applicants, with a ‘Partner Accreditation for Compliance & Trust’ (PACT) certificate.

India has had an uneasy relationship with cryptocurrencies . The Reserve Bank of India banned banks from offering crypto-related services in 2018, a move struck down by the Supreme Court of India in 2020. While the government has since taxed crypto income and brought it under the PMLA, it has not introduced any dedicated law to regulate the sector. In May 2025, the Supreme Court flagged the lack of legal clarity and regulatory measures for crypto transactions.

Notably, there are currently around 55 registered VDA service providers in India. However, a report by crypto platform Giottus said that crypto-related crimes made up nearly 20–25% of all cybercrime cases in India in recent years.

Notably, between FY 23 and FY24, the Centre collected Rs 705 crore in taxes from VDA income. Raids and surveys also uncovered an additional Rs 630 crore of unreported crypto income, Pankaj Chaudhary, Minister of State (MoS) for Finance, informed Parliament last month.

Around the same time, the Income Tax Department sent nearly 44,000 notices to individuals for not reporting their crypto transactions in their FY25 income tax returns.

The cybersecurity audit move signals the government’s intent to tighten oversight of India’s fast-growing crypto ecosystem, but also raises questions about the balance between innovation and regulation.

Cybersecurity audits could strengthen investor protection, boost the trust factor, and make it harder for bad actors to launder money or siphon off user funds. They may also improve the traceability of crypto transactions, which is a major challenge for enforcement agencies today.

However, mandatory audits and the new PACT certification regime could raise compliance costs, especially for smaller or newer players, potentially driving them out of the market or pushing activity underground.

Imposing such obligations without a comprehensive crypto law could lead to regulatory overreach, creating uncertainty for legitimate businesses.

Therefore, India’s crypto industry may continue operating in a grey zone – under heavy scrutiny, but sans the legal clarity that long-term investors and innovators seek – until a clear legislative framework emerges.
