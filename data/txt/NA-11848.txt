1. Prosecution case is that on 28.02.2008, SI Rajender Kumar
was on patrolling duty along with HC Yashpal and Constable Ashok Rana. FIR No. 47/08 1 of page 10
 At about 7.30 pm when they reached at Taj Enclave near Geeta Colony, SI
Rajender Kumar received a secret information that two boys would come at
Thokar No. 14, Pushta Road, Geeta Colony from U.P. to deliver the
counterfeit notes and can be apprehended. SI Rajender Kumar passed the
information to SHO telephonically who directed him to take the necessary
action. SI Rajender Kumar prepared a raiding party. 3-4 passers by were
requested to join the raiding party but they went away expressing their
inability. They then reached at Thokar No. 14, Pushta Road, Geeta Colony
where on the pointing out of secret informer, two boys whose names on
enquiry came to be known as Akram and Arif were apprehended. On the
formal search of accused Akram, 63 counterfeit notes of Rs. 50/- were
recovered from the right pocket of his pant. Three notes were bearing the
same serial number i.e. 6SS 631672, 26 notes were of serial No. 6SS
631673 to 6SS 631678, 10 notes were of serial No. 6SS 631680 to 6SS
631685, 12 notes were of serial No. 6SS 631688 to 6SS 631690 and 12
notes were of series 6SS 631693 to 6SS 631699. The total value of
counterfeit notes was Rs. 3150/- The notes were kept in a pullanda. On the
formal search of accused Arif @ Pappu, 131 counterfeit notes of Rs. 50/-
denomination were recovered. Two notes were bearing serial No. 6SS
631600, 5 notes were bearing serial Nos. 6SS 631604 to 6SS 631608, 6
notes were of serial No. 6SS 631610 to 6SS 631612, 6 notes were of serial
No. 6SS 631614 to 6SS 631617, 5 notes were of serial No. 6SS 631619 to
6SS 631621, 9 notes were of serial No. 6SS 631623 to 6SS 631627, 1 note
was of serial No. 6SS 631630 and 97 notes were of serial No. 6SS 631634
to 6SS 631671. The total value of the counterfeit currency notes recovered
from accused Arif was Rs. 6550/-. The said notes were also kept in a cloth
pullanda. Both the pullandas were sealed with the seal of RK and were

 numbered Mark A & B. Seal after use was handed over to Constable Ashok
Rana. Raiding team waited at the spot but no one came to take the delivery
of the notes. SI Rajender Kumar prepared the rukka Exbt. PW-4/A and
sent the same at police station through Constable Ashok Rana. FIR was
registered under Section 489-B / 489-C / 34 IPC. Both the accused were
arrested. Their disclosure statements were recorded. IO obtained the police
remand of accused but co-accused Sharif named in the disclosure
statements could not be arrested. The recovered counterfeit currency notes
were sent to Currency Note Press, Nasik. As per report from Nasik Press,
notes were counterfeit. Charge sheet was prepared under Section 489- B/489-C/34 IPC and both the accused were sent to court for trial. 2. After compliance of Section 207 Cr. PC, case was committed to
Sessions Court. Charge under Section 489-B / 34 IPC and under Section
489-C IPC was framed against both the accused to which they pleaded not
guilty. 3. In order to prove its case, prosecution examined 6 witnesses.
PW-1 is HC Birpal, Duty Officer. He recorded the FIR Exbt. PW-1/A.

 PW-2 is HC Yashpal, PW-3 is HC Ashok Rana and PW-4 is SI
Rajender Kumar. They are the members of the raiding party. They had
apprehended the accused and had recovered the counterfeit currency
notes Exbt. P-1. Their testimonies are more or less the same as stated in
Para 1 of the judgment and are therefore not being repeated again for the
sake of brevity. FIR No. 47/08 3 of page 10
 PW-5 is Sh. R.B. Jadhav, Assistant Works Manager, Currency
Note Press, Nasik. He had examined the currency notes and found that the
same were counterfeit. His detailed report is Exbt. PW-5/A.

 PW-6 is HC Satender, MHCM, PS Geeta Colony. He proved
the relevant entry of Register No. 19 with regard to deposit of two pullandas
containing fake currency notes. 4. Statements of both the accused were recorded under Section
313 Cr. PC wherein they stated that they were innocent. They refused to
lead any evidence in their defence. 5. The learned counsels of accused have mainly argued that there
is no public witness of recovery and the case of prosecution is based on
the testimonies of the police official who are only interested in the success
of their case and therefore without corroboration, their testimonies should
not be accepted. It is further argued that no departure entry has been
placed on the record, Constable who took the sealed pullandas to Currency
Press Nasik, has not been produced. It is submitted that complainant
himself is the IO of this case and therefore there is no possibility of fair
investigation. The learned APP, however, has argued that it is a matter of
common knowledge that public persons are not interested in joining the
investigation and therefore on account of the same, prosecution case
cannot be disbelieved. It is further argued that there is no reason to
disbelieve the testimonies of police officials who have proved the recovery
of counterfeit currency notes from the accused. FIR No. 47/08 4 of page 10 6. I have considered the submissions made by the Additional PP
and the learned defence counsels and have carefully perused the records.
Accused have been charged for the offence under Section 489-B and 489-
C IPC. Section 489-B provides punishment for using forged or counterfeit
currency notes and Section 489-C provides punishment for possession of
forged or counterfeit currency notes. As per prosecution case, the information
was that two boys had come from U.P. at Thokar No. 14 to give delivery of
fake currency notes. Both the accused were apprehended and from their
possession, counterfeit notes were recovered. Admittedly, accused were
apprehended before they could deliver the fake currency notes to anyone.
There is no investigation whatsoever about the persons to whom the notes
were to be delivered. Admittedly, no one came at the spot to take the delivery
of the notes. Thus, there is no evidence that accused had used forged or
counterfeit currency notes and hence ingredients of Section 489-B IPC are not
proved. 7. Undoubtedly, it is not a case of chance apprehension and if the
prosecution story is to be believed, PW-4 SI Rajender Kumar had received an
advance information about the accused coming at Thokar No. 14 to make the
delivery of fake currency notes. PW-4 SI Rajender Kumar deposed that the
information was received at 7.30 pm and thereafter accused were
apprehended. Admittedly, no public person was joined in the raiding party. SI
Rajender Kumar tried to give explanation by deposing that he had requested
2-4 passers by to join the raiding party but they refused and expressed their
inability. Admittedly, their names and addresses were not noted and no notice
was given to them to join the raiding party. As per arrest memos, accused

FIR No. 47/08 5 of page 10
 were arrested at about 11.10 - 11.15 pm from the spot. In cross examination,
PW-4 admits that the passers by were passing from the spot but he did not
ask any of those passers by to join the investigation. Thus, no sincere effort
was made to join the public persons in the investigation. The explanation
given by the IO does not inspire confidence. In the case of State of
Rajasthan Vs. Teja Singh 2001 (II) AD (SC) 125, the Hon'ble Supreme Court
held: In the case titled State of Punjab Vs. Gurdyal Singh 1992 (1) RCR
(DB) 646, Roop Chand Vs. State of Haryana 1989 (2) RCR 504 and State
of Punjab Vs. Sukhdev Singh 1992 (3) RCR 311, it was held by the Hon'ble
Court that: 8. Statutory desirability in the matter of search and seizure is that
there should be support from unbiased and neutral corner. The search before
an independent witness imparts much more authenticity and creditworthiness
to the search and seizure proceedings. Such safeguard is intended to avoid
criticism of arbitrary and highhanded action against police officers. This is to
lend credibility to the procedure relating to search and seizure. In the present

FIR No. 47/08 6 of page 10
 case, the explanation for not associating independent witnesses is not
satisfactory more so when they were available right at the elbow. 9. As per prosecution case PW SI Rajender Kumar was on patrolling
duty with Constable Ashok and HC Yashpal when he received the secret
information and therefore that being so, the relevant record containing arrival
and departure should have been produced that they were on patrolling duty by
producing DD entry for the same as per Chapter 22 Rule 49 of the Punjab
Police Rules which are reproduced as under:- 10. None of the prosecution witnesses has deposed as to by what
entry in register No. II, they were patrolling in the particular area. The relevant
entries regarding the arrival and departure of the police officials have not been
proved on the record. It has been held in Rattan Lal Vs. State 1987 (2)
Crimes 29 that: FIR No. 47/08 7 of page 10
 "if the investigating agency deliberately ignores to comply
 with the provisions of the Act the courts will have to approach
 their action with reservations. The matter has to be viewed
 with suspicion if the provisions of law are not strictly complied
 with and the least that can be said is that it is so done with an
 oblique motive. This failure to bring on record, the DD entries
 creates a reasonable doubt in the prosecution version and
 attributes oblique motive on the part of the prosecution." 11. There are also contradictions in the testimonies of the police
witnesses, inasmuch as, PW-3 HC Ashok Rana stated that he was not having
any weapon but PW-4 SI Rajender Kumar stated that he himself was having a
pistol while the other staff were having dandas. PW-2 stated that accused
persons were apprehended at a distance of about 3-4 meters from the main
road at Thokar No. 14 but PW-3 stated that accused were standing at 2-4
paces from the road. PW-3 HC Ashok Rana stated that IO was having a jeans
cloth bag but IO stated that IO bag was made up of leather. PW-3 stated that
they came at the police station in a government vehicle which was called from
the police station but PW-2 deposed that they went to police station from the
spot in a TSR. PW-3 could not tell as to who apprehended first of all and to
which accused. He could not tell which paper was prepared by the IO first of
all. Thus, PW-3 has given evasive replies which make his presence at the
spot doubtful. 12. PW-4 SI Rajender Kumar, at whose instance the FIR was
registered, is the complainant of the case. Normally, a person who makes the
complaint, should not conduct investigation of the case but in this case after

FIR No. 47/08 8 of page 10
 registration of the FIR, investigation was entrusted to SI Rajender Kumar
himself. Thus, there is a doubt whether SI Rajender Kumar had conducted the
investigation fairly and properly. In the case of Megha Singh Vs. State of
Haryana AIR 1995 Supreme Court 2339, the FIR was lodged on the
complaint of Head Constable who arrested the accused and recovered a
pistol and cartridges from him. It was held that being the complainant, he
should not have proceeded with the investigation of the case. The Supreme
Court held that such practice, to say the least, should not be resorted to so
that there may not be any occasion to suspect fair and impartial investigation. 13. PW-4 SI Rajender Kumar deposed that sealed pullandas were
sent to Currency Note Press, Nasik through Constable whose name he did
not remember. PW-6 HC Satender, MHCM, PS Geeta Colony deposed that
pullandas of currency notes were sent to Currency Note Press, Nasik through
Constable Rajender. The report from Currency Note Press, Nasik Exbt. PW-
5/A mentions that the seal on the pullandas was intact but the initials of the
seal are not mentioned in the report. PW-5 Sh. R.V. Jadhav, Assistant Works
Manager from Currency Note Press Nasik also could not tell the initials of the
seal by which the two pullandas were sealed when they were received in their
office. Admittedly, Constable Rajender who deposited the pullandas, has
neither been cited nor examined by the prosecution. Thus, there is no
evidence that the pullandas when deposited in Currency Note Press were
bearing the seal of RK and were not tampered. 14. Hence, in the totality of facts and circumstances, I am of the
opinion that prosecution has failed prove its case against both the accused
beyond doubt. Accused are accordingly acquitted. Their bail bonds are
