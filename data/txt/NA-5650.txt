Heard learned counsel for the applicant, learned Additional Government Advocate representing the State and perused the record. By means of this application under Section 483 of BNSS, applicant Rajjak Ansari, who is involved in Case Crime No. 16 of 2025, under Sections 66B , 66C , 66D I.T. Act and Section 3(8) , 317(3), 317(4), 318(4), 319(2), 336(3), 338, 340(2) BNS, police station Raunapar, district Azamgarh seeks enlargement on bail during the pendency of trial. As per prosecution case, in brief, complainant Shivanand Verma got a first information report lodged on 17.01.2025 against an unknown person under Section 66D I.T. Act alleging inter alia that he is having a saving bank account at State Bank, Belkunda. From the said bank account, he was using PhonePe app and for the last some days, his PhonePe app was not working. In that regard, he searched the customer care number of PhonePe online and talked to the customer care representative but that phone number belongs to some unknown fraudster. Thereafter, aforesaid fraudster made him whatsapp call and got the screen shared of his phone and committed online fraud of Rs. 45,000/- and Rs. 20,000/-, total Rs. 65,000/-. Thereafter, he made complaint on the Cyber Helpline No. 1930 and got his complaint registered at No. 33110240127585. It is contended by learned counsel for the applicant that the applicant is not named in the first information report and has been falsely implicated in this case by the police. It is further submitted that the alleged offence is compoundable in nature and the applicant is willing to pay Rs. 65,000/- to the complainant. The applicant does not have any criminal history to his credit. Lastly, it is submitted that the applicant is languishing in jail since 08.04.2025, therefore, he may be released on bail. Per contra, learned A.G.A. for the State opposed the prayer for bail of the applicant by contending that the mobile number which was used in the commission of crime was obtained by the applicant by fraudulently committing theft, regarding which a first information report being Case Crime No. 67 of 2024, under Sections 3(5) , 111(2) , 317(2), 317(4), 318(4), 319(2), 336(3), 338, 340(2) BNS and Section 66B , 66C , 66D I.T. Act, police station Cyber Crime, district Jamtara (Jharkhand) was lodged. It is also submitted that in para 15 of the affidavit, the applicant has mentioned that he has no criminal history whereas prior to lodging the instant F.I.R., he has criminal history of aforesaid Case Crime No. 67 of 2024. The applicant is resident of district Jamtara, Jharkhand. In case he released on bail, there is every possibility of his absconding. Hence, bail application of the applicant is liable to be rejected. Having heard learned counsel for the parties and examined the matter in its entirety, I find that nowadays cyber crime is on rise globally fueled by sophisticated technologies causing financial loss to the innocent persons and they are hazardous for the society at large. They are constantly developing new and more complex methods to by-pass security measures. The district Jamtara is a hub of cyber crime. One F.I.R. against the applicant is already registered at district Jamtara. Considering the overall facts and circumstances of the case as well as keeping in view the submissions advanced on behalf of parties, gravity of offence, role assigned to applicant, criminal history of the applicant and severity of punishment, I do not find any good ground to release the applicant on bail. Accordingly, the bail application is rejected. It is made clear that the observation contained in the instant order is confined to the issue of bail and shall not affect the merit of the trial.
