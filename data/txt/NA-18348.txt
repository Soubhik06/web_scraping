TITLE: What does the UN report on "cyber-violence" say about India?

--- POST TEXT ---

Recently, the UN Women and the UN Broadband Commission presented a report called **[Cyber Violence Against Women And Girls: A World-Wide Wake-up Call](http://www.unwomen.org/~/media/headquarters/attachments/sections/library/publications/2015/cyber_violence_gender%20report.pdf?v=1&amp;d=20150924T154259)**. It essentially equated cyber-violence to [physical violence](http://time.com/4049106/un-cyber-violence-physical-violence/) and argued that something needs to done as a lot of women and girls are victims of it. 

It was criticised and mocked everywhere. The [UK Telegraph](http://www.telegraph.co.uk/technology/social-media/11902140/The-UN-proposal-on-cyberbullying-is-a-blueprint-for-censorship.html) published an article which said *"UN is now a joke organisation completely detached from its noble past aims"* and *"it has now adopted another issue which is completely irrelevant to millions of women across the globe"*. The [NY Magazine](http://nymag.com/scienceofus/2015/09/uns-cyberharassment-report-is-really-bad.html) said cyber-bullying was a serious problem, but criticised the report itself by calling it *"extremely slipshod, careless document that doesn’t offer anything approaching a sophisticated understanding"*. 

What this got to do with you? Given the current government's stance towards censorship and ending anonymity on the internet, under the guise of protecting women and children, I think you should know what it says about India and what wordings were used. Below, I have analysed any mention of India, both the good and the bad.

* The reports argues that cyber-crimes are not a [first-world problem](/r/firstworldproblems) using India's example. "*Online crimes are not a ‘first world’ problem; they seamlessly follow the spread of the Internet. The use of WhatsApp instant messaging, for example, has become, the latest harassment tool of choice in countries like India and Malaysia, and increasingly around the world.*"

* The reports mentions India's low penetration of internet and its fast growth. "*Among the top 20 countries, India has the lowest penetration rate; a mere 19 per cent, but commands the highest yearly growth rate.*"

* Again Whatsapp was mentioned. "*Some 500 million people use mobile devices and the WhatsApp mobile text messaging application. In India alone WhatsApp users reached 70 million per month.*"

* The report claims that most female victims of crime-bullying in India don't reported it. "*A report from India, for instance, suggests that “only 35 per cent of the women have reported about their victimization, 46.7 per cent have not reported and 18.3 per cent have been unaware of the fact that they have been victimized ... women prefer not to report about their victimization owing to social issues.”*" Here is the [survey](http://www.cybervictims.org/CCVCresearchreport2010.pdf) (Debarati Halder and K. Jaishankar, 2010) they quoted. The sample size of the survey was 60 women and 13 men.

* A 2014 [BBC World and GlobeScan poll](http://www.bbc.co.uk/mediacentre/latestnews/2014/ws-freedompoll) was cited to claimed that in India 67% people feel safe to express their views online. 17 countries were survey, only in 6 countries the majority felt safe to express their views online. All 6 were developing countries, meaning this has nothing to do with cyber-bullying, more about state surveilance. The BBC article says so but the UN report twisted it.

* Now, the report quotes former Chief Justice K. G. Balakrishnan, "*The widespread circulation of such content is particularly
harmful for women. The pervasive gender discrimination in our society is further heightened since the digital medium provides the convenient shield of anonymity and fake identities. Errant persons become more emboldened in their offensive behavior since it is presumed that they will not face any consequences."* The quote is real, I checked it. It was part of [2010 National Seminar on the 
Enforcement of Cyber Law](http://supremecourtofindia.nic.in/speeches/speeches_2010/national_seminar_on_cyber_law_8-5-10.pdf). Among other things, he had criticised internet anonymity in that speech.

* The UN report mentions a presentation on ["Using social media for the prevention of violence against women: Lessons learned from social media communication campaigns to prevent violence against women in India, China and Viet Nam"](http://www.partners4prevention.org/resource/using-social-media-prevention-violence-against-women-lessons-learned-social-media) by the group called "Partners for Prevention", which had used social media to promote awareness about violence against women in India, China and Vietnam.

* The UN report mentions some groups and projects from India: [Let’s Talk Men 2.0](http://www.livemint.com/Politics/19QfBCcON7fxzQR65JjYdJ/Preview--Lets-Talk-Men-20.html), a documentary series from the sub-continent; and [Centre for Cyber Victim Counselling](http://www.cybervictims.org) an Indian NGO started in 2009 to counsel victims of cyber crime, they published the 60 women survey above.

* It mentions that one college-educated and working woman in India wants women-only cyber cafes. *"More women-friendly content would drive women online according to Egyptian high-income women who did not use the Internet but called for “new websites only for women” or “dedicated to women,” or women-only chatting. 
Somewhat in the same vein, a high-income, university educated Indian professional who does use the Internet wanted cybercafés that are open only to women."* The reference is a Intel, Dalberg and GlobalScan study called "[Women and the Web](https://www-ssl.intel.com/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/pdf/women-and-the-web.pdf)" The study quotes one Gayatri Buragohain, who founded the Feminist Approach To Technology, saying that there should women-only cafes similar to women-only rural bank branches, "*Stakeholders could do something similar with Internet cafes in rural areas: train women to operate and manage them, and let them make a profit.*"

* It blames Whatsapp for not monitoring the messages instead relies on a reporting system and says that in India harassers take advantage of the policy, as most people in India don't know how to report. *"WhatsApp excuses itself from monitoring of contents or messages by putting the burden of safe practices on the users and directing the victims to report the matters through takedown reports or self-protection mechanisms. Harassers or perpetrators take huge advantage of this situation since the level of awareness regarding safe practices in the WhatsApp is low in countries like India."*

* The report mentions the *Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act 1986* under which porn and erotica is banned in India, but not online. (Note: There are also laws banning erotica. But, this is the main one.) *"Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act 1986 (IRWA), is currently under consideration for amendment by Parliament to include virtual spaces. Indian laws regarding data privacy, offensive communication through Internet and digital communication technology and jurisdictional issues in cases of crimes committed through ICT and DCT still need to be developed."* The report makes it seem like a good thing. The report makes no mention of the IT Act, which criminalises revenge porn, child porn and until recently "offensive messages". (Remember Section 66A?).

* The mentions the "[Feminist Principles of the Internet](http://www.genderit.org/node/4097)" and claims that the Indian government, among others, is adopting parts of it.
