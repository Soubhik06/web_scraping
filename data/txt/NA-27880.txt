My job makes ignoring unknown numbers difficult. I'm a teacher and I get calls from coworkers, volunteers, or parents all the time. Yay for quarantine forcing me to give out my private number. 

Anyway, I'm also fine with answering scam calls. Sometimes I'll play with the scammer to waste their time so they can't call an actual vulnerable person. 

Today, though, I got a call that did have me question if it wasn't a scam. I am like 98% sure that it was, but still I'd love confirmation. 

I got a call from one of the two local power companies, it happened to be the one we don't use funnily enough. I entertained it, because it just seemed like a good idea and I'm a bit bored today so why not? 

The hallmarks were there: 
- thick Indian accent
- they had my name and address probably from one of those records/background check websites
- they had my maiden name, which changed almost two years ago 

But the scam was them "sending out someone for free to check my HVAC system". Now they were trying to schedule an appointment with me, though I told them I'd moved and their address wasn't right (I gave them the address of our local police department hoping they'd Google it and realize I was fucking with them). Because I couldn't figure out where the scam was going, she ended up telling me she'd call me back in two weeks. 

I'm mostly familiar with tech scams, especially those that want to connect to your computer with TeamViewer or anydesk or some other program like that. 

If anyone can confirm this is a scam for me, do you know what their script is? Where does the actual scam come into play? I wouldn't obviously schedule anything, but I want to know for future reference how to play the game they're trying to play.
