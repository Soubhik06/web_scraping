In a major development, the Karnataka High Court (HC) has directed the formation of a Special Investigation Team (SIT) to investigate a serious case of data theft from a Bengaluru-based drone company, according to a report by The Indian Express . The court took this decision after finding gaps and irregularities in the ongoing police probe.

The case involves NewSpace Research and Technologies Pvt Ltd, a private company that designs and supplies drones to India’s defence sector. The company has accused three of its former employees of stealing important data and using it to help a rival firm, Lenviz Technologies.

According to the complaint, Lenviz used the stolen data to build similar drone products, win contracts, and place a competitive bid in a defence-related drone competition. The court took serious note of the fact that the data involved could impact national security, especially since organisations like the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) were among the company’s clients.

The Karnataka HC’s decision came after NewSpace filed a petition asking for a fair and specialised investigation. Their lawyer informed the court that the investigators had mishandled the initial investigation.

They said that although the police traced one of the accused, namely Prabhat Sharma, the former vice-president of NewSpace, to Noida, they did not arrest him. Instead, they used Section 35(3) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) to issue a notice, a provision typically invoked when arrest is not considered necessary. The court questioned why Sharma was not subjected to custodial interrogation, after denying anticipatory bail to the accused.

The petitioner also revealed that authorities had suspended the original investigating officer for allegedly taking a bribe. While the state government appointed a new officer, the court said this alone could not fix the larger problem of an unfair investigation.

Justice Nagaprasanna noted that the case had the characteristics of cyber espionage , which requires a blend of technical expertise and forensic knowledge. He stated that an ordinary investigation would not be enough and could lead to a miscarriage of justice. It, therefore, ordered the formation of a three-member SIT to ensure a proper and thorough probe.

The SIT will be led by Karnataka DGP Pranab Mohanty, along with IPS officers Bhushan Gulab Rao Borase and Nisha James. The court has asked the team to begin work immediately and submit its findings within three months. The court also directed that the copy of its order be sent to the Chief Secretary and the Principal Secretary of the Home Department for compliance.

While passing the order, Justice M Nagaprasanna also highlighted the rising threat of cybercrime and data theft in the country. He said that Karnataka must build strong institutions like a Cyber Command Centre (CCC) or a dedicated Cyber Crime Bureau to handle such crimes effectively.

He remarked , “If a CCC is established to combat cyber crimes and strengthen cyber security, it would usher a new beginning of tackling new-age crime with new-age investigating centres.”
