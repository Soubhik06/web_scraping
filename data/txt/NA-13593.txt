The Enforcement Directorate (ED) questioned former cricketer Shikhar Dhawan over his appearance in advertisements for the illegal betting platform 1XBet, according to a report by the Hindustan Times. The agency reportedly interrogated Dhawan for eight hours, asking about the nature of his promotional activities, financial transactions, and compliance with tax obligations.

The Indian government had blocked 1XBet in 2023 under Section 69A of the Information Technology (IT) Act, along with numerous other gambling apps.

The ED had also summoned fellow ex-cricketer Suresh Raina last month for allegedly endorsing 1XBet. Investigators reportedly questioned him about his links with the app, any endorsement fees he might have received, and the mode of communication used between both parties.

Dhawan came under the spotlight earlier this year after his image was featured in multiple advertisements for 1XBet.

For example, an X (formerly Twitter) user pointed out Dhawan’s presence on a 1XBet advert on an ATM screen.

However, in a report by G2G News, Dhawan denied endorsing 1XBet. His representative clarified that the cricketer had previously partnered with a sporting merchandise brand called 1xBat Sporting Lines, which was entirely separate from 1XBet. According to a report by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI), offshore betting advertisements accounted for 43.5% of reported ad violations, with 318 influencers promoting such ads on social media. More than 31% of influencer-related violations came from the betting segment.

The Department of Consumer Affairs (DoCA) and ASCI highlighted the consumer harms caused by surrogate advertising of illegal products during a stakeholder consultation in February last year. ASCI noted that, out of 1,085 cases of illegal advertisements, 765 were related to betting.

The advisory also cautioned online intermediaries that safe harbour protection under Section 79 of the IT Act would not apply if they failed to remove illegal content expeditiously.

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) had also issued an advisory before that, highlighting that surrogate advertisements regarding betting or gambling, considered to be illegal, have been on the rise. According to the CCPA, betting platforms are employing celebrities and influencers to endorse and promote such activities, which ultimately creates an impression that indulging in such activity is acceptable, even though it prohibited under the Public Gambling Act of 1867 and remained illegal in most of the country.

Further, Clause 9 of the Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements , 2022 explicitly prohibits advertisements for illegal products and services.

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Bill, 2025 has outlawed all forms of online money games, whether skill-based or chance-based. Crucially, the law also bans advertisements and promotions of such games across all mediums. The provision covers both direct and indirect endorsements, including through electronic communication and influencer or celebrity promotions.

Violating this provision can lead to up to two years in prison, fines of up to Rs 50 lakh, or both.
