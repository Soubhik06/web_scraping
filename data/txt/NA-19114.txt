Let me start with - No, I am not depressed with my hair loss. I'm not the quintessential bald man in hair transplant TVC that Indians assume when they see a bald/balding man. I've lost hair aggressively for past 4 years, going from Jude Law [this](http://static1.purepeople.com/articles/0/52/87/0/@/387506-jude-law-lors-des-empire-film-awards-a-637x0-2.jpg) to [this](https://s-media-cache-ak0.pinimg.com/236x/03/50/94/0350942f24eb3569ff28d072d1762270.jpg). 

In my twenties, I am close to a Norwood 4 (Norwood is the scale for measuring male pattern baldness). I buzz my hair and am generally well groomed. Sure, I look different than since I lost hair, and I probably went from an 8 to a 7.5, but I don't dwell on this much for it's very superficial. Note to feminists - My approach is not too superficial towards women either. 

Life is good, and the hairloss doesn't bother me any more. I am used to a few cracks now and then, no problem. I went through my trouble with hairloss in the initial days; but I buzzed my hair and never looked back. I focus on my career, travelling and making friends. I do okay with girls, bar hookups but no dates in past 2 years. I should also note that I lived in Rotterdam for past 6 years, and my contract brings me to India now (I am Indian, born and brought up, including all schooling). So that's my background. 

I've come to conclude that India doesn't suit me very much. Of course its not India's problem, but *my* problem. Its root is in the fact that as an introvert, I can not fit myself well in Indian society. Firstly, there are too many people in every place I go. Add to it the fact that most people are too nosy, and socially and audibly noisy. I am used to living a calm life in my own space, without drawing attention. 

This is where hairloss becomes hard to tackle in India. India doesn't seem to acknowledge or respect personal space and privacy AT ALL. Its common for random people to stare and ponder on my buzz cut, starting from the security guard at customs who said "aajkal ye ganje hone ka style chal raha hai bhaiya ji! (it's in style to be bald nowadays)". Not sure why he feels the need to make small talk in my personal domain, but okay. I chuckle and carry on because I know the worst is yet to come - familia!!! **clenches teeth)* 

Parents and relatives are the absolute worst. Being an eerie nation where the comb-over is more acceptable than buzzing/shaving, relatives ogle at my head like kids at Disneyland. With complete and utter disregard to how that might make me conscious or piss me off. To all those people who undermine hairloss by saying "there are worse things like cancer that could happen to you", yeah I know all about it. I'm centred enough to not place too much importance to it, but if it hurts sometimes, it hurts. I don't need to justify it. Men can be very sensitive about their hair, and let me tell you the transition is extremely painful because it quite transforms your appearance drastically. Especially at a young age, where you're not prepared or anticipating all of this. 

So anyway, coming back to nosy relatives - there's the obvious staring. This happens especially with relatives I met after a long time. Its understandable to be taken aback, my looks have changed a bit. Its also okay to acknowledge and react with a compliment like "hey, your hair looks different. nice look though!" or a non-compliment like "hey, wow you look different with that hair". 

But it doesn't end there. My hairloss must now be the topic of discussion in the hall while we drink chai (tea). 
Nosy relative 1: "Toh ye aisa kya style kar liya tumne? (So what's with this look of yours?)" 

Me: "Haan aunty wo thode baal kam ho gaye, toh chhote rakhta hu (Yeah I lost some hair, so I keep it cropped nowadays)" 

Nosy relative 2: "Lekin beta ye tumhari age mein achha nahi dikhta, baal badhao abhi iss age mein, hain? (But such hair isn't appropriate for your age. You should grow out your hair, yeah?)" 

Me: "Hehe (awkward laugh)" 

(My mother tells me after they're gone that one aunty expressed her concern for my marriage. My mother was actually serious when she told me that, and wanted to use this as an excuse to start discussing the topic with me. Something she has been wanting to take up at every opportunity, and I've been snubbing her) 

Half an hour later, just as I breathe a relief that my hairloss is out of the spotlight, it comes right back. So we're discussing politics (favourite family passtime) and Sharma uncle remarks I am quite the Kejriwal when it comes to my political views. Before I can even react, witty Gupta uncle jumps in, "Haha more like Amit Shah he is!". Everyone laughs and I too awkwardly chime into their laughter with, "Haha good one". A little part of me just died though, do people really associate the 20-something me to Amit Shah already? I decide its enough socializing for me, so I head to my room and enjoy my drink alone, the best way I know how to. 

These digs from relatives are too common. Some of you might say I am over reacting or being too sensitive, but that's just me. I dont like personal invasions and I absolutely dont like explaining or justifying anything in my personal boundary to anyone. Let's talk about bigger things than you and me. 

So anyway, that was relatives. I take those cannons once or twice in a month. Hard to avoid relatives when living in India, they just SHOW UP. But anyway, let's come to parents now. Indian parents are conservative, and if I may add - very very extremely superficial. They care too much about societal perception, and a huge chunk of perception is looks. There are textbook rules for what is good looking for guys - fair, tall, neatly combed hair like [this](http://s3.india.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/new-jism-2-stills-sunny-leone-sizzles-in-red-2.jpg). Of course, I fail on the latter, and that adds a big concern to my parents. They've decided that the solution is - do NOT accept that our son is losing hair. So they constantly try to 'guilt' me into growing my hair with photo albums of class 2 and that one photo where I had grown my hair kinda like [this](http://www.thebengalurean.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/377232282.jpg). Everytime I buzz my hair, I get the silent treatment from my mother for 2 days. The overall attitude is - I lost my hair because of my 'bad' habits like drinking and eating non-veg. I can regrow my hair but I dont do it just to hurt parents. Also, I brought the hairloss on myself and its a disease. 

Ridiculous as it is, I cannot change it. I hope to move back away soon, because my hair is such a big deal in the house. It causes unhappiness regularly, and its a topic discussed too often beyond my comfort and privacy. I also get joked around as being a terrorist and a thug (Indian television often holds the thief or creepy guy roles exclusively for bald men, thereby positively reinforcing society's perception). Parents of course say this jokingly, but nonetheless I've lost tolerance to hearing it too often in India. I also know my parents, and things aren't going to flatten out, but only worse as I soon plan to sport a shiny pate like [this](http://vignette2.wikia.nocookie.net/dexter/images/3/3e/Ramon3.png/revision/latest?cb=20131022234817). 

So that's a bit about the uneasiness I face in family and friends. Its not so much as derailing my life or anything, but it makes me angry how people take the liberty to just comment on your appearance or personal choices. Very ill mannered I would say, and irritating. You lose patience over time. 

There was another incident just today that triggered me to take up the topic. So at the gym, you ID yourself at the entrance. Now, my photo is from 3 years back, I decided to utilize it for gym, instead of wasting a new photo. I didn't have a buzz so looked somewhat like [this](http://i.huffpost.com/gen/1111702/thumbs/o-JERRY-SEINFELD-HOUSE-facebook.jpg), as opposed to how I do [now](http://s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/blogs-prod-media/us/uploads/2015/08/29143751/Jerry-Seinfeld-kids.jpg). The security guard fails to ID me and says, "ye kiska card de raho ho?". I say "Thats me". He stares at me while I sheepishly look in all directions, meanwhile 4-5 uncles nearby take notice and take it upon themselves to stare at my card photo and back at me with a remark, "Bachpan ki photo hai lagta hai hahahah (Looks like a photo from childhood hahahah)". The security guard is not having it, and says "Ye aap nahi hai sir, koi ID dijiye". I mean WTF! My eyes, my ears, my nose have not changed. Its so insulting to have to do that, while being discussed in public like that. Ruined my whole fucking day. Ridiculous I know, but people's words can sometimes get to you. Even though I know I don't look much older than my age (I have ample assurances that I best gained 2-3 years), its like a bullet when someone says - ye tumhare bachpan ki photo lagti hai (this appears to be a photo of your childhood). 

Why can't Indian people mind their own business and not be so judgemental? Be a little sensitive to what someone might be going through, please.

Edit: I'd also like to educate folks that there is no known cure for male pattern baldness. Sure, you can try your 'nuske (home remedies)', but that's for your own satisfaction. If there was a cure for hairloss, people would be shouting about it from the roof tops, and Satya Nadela or Jeff Bezos would NOT be bald. Think about it! 

There are a few common FDA-approved drugs that might slow down the process (NOT reverse it), such as Rogaine or Propecia. But these carry side effects. And in my experience, these do more harm than good. They may slow down your loss for a while, but once you stop using it - it will cause rapid hair loss. So these at best will let you hold onto your hair for 3-4 years. Give it a try if you have to, these are OTC drugs if you wish to commit. Just ensure you use them regularly everyday for the next few years, otherwise you're fucked. If you are dedicated enough to apply this disgusting-smelling greasy liquid to your head before going to work everyday, good. Otherwise, don't start. 

All the TVC commercials, paper ads and Dr. Batra are looting off of people's insecurities. Please do not go to these and waste you money. Worst of all - they give you false hope. Please don't go for these oils and creams and what not. The manufacturers know that people with hairloss can be insecure, and will jump to buy almost anything that they market with a fake before-after photo. Be wary! 

Hair transplant is the only viable option, that is IF hair loss bothers you too much. Salman Khan got it, Virender Sehwag and Shane Warne did too. It involves literally plucking the hair from the back of your head (where hair loss is pretty dormant) and grafting on your crown and towards forehead. Its a painful costly process, and can only be done in the initial stages of hairloss. Upto a NW3 I'd say. 

So choose your options carefully, and do not waste money on scams. And for God's sake, don't pick the newspaper with a random hair treatment advertisement and approach your bald friends/sons/relatives about trying it! They know much better than you, they have done a PhD on it.
