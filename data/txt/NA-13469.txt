On 31 August 2025, the Enforcement Directorate (ED) provisionally attached properties worth over Rs 394.42 crore in connection with the Bike Bot scam under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002.

Several educational and charitable trusts, including Kamakhya Educational and Social Welfare Trust, Kamakhya Educational Society, Guru Nanak Charitable Trust, Alpine Technical Education Society, AP Goyal Charitable Trust, and one Smt. Meena Anand, held the assets. The case stems from investment schemes launched by Garvit Innovative Promoters Ltd (GIPL) and its promoter Sanjay Bhati, who promised returns through a bike taxi service that was later found to be a multi-level marketing operation.

These diverted funds were utilized to purchase immovable properties in Meerut and release previously mortgaged properties from banksâ.

The agency has previously attached assets worth Rs 220.78 crore and filed prosecution complaints against 27 accused before a special PMLA court in Ghaziabad, which has taken cognisance.

The Bike Bot scheme began in 2017 when GIPL and its promoter, Sanjay Bhati, launched what they presented as a two-wheeler taxi service. The company invited investors to purchase packages of 1, 3, 5, or 7 motorcycles, claiming it would operate these bikes as taxis and pay monthly returns in the form of rent, EMIs, and bonuses. Investors were also offered incentives for enrolling new participants, giving the scheme the structure of a multi-level marketing operation.

In practice, the promised taxi service barely materialised. Investigators confirmed that although franchisees were allotted across several cities, the bike taxis were never meaningfully deployed. Instead, the scheme circulated funds collected from investors through related companies and layered them into charitable trusts, educational societies, and individual accounts.

By 2019, the operation collapsed. Reports in June 2019 noted that roughly 2.5 lakh people had invested in the scheme, mostly across Uttar Pradesh but also in other states.

In December 2020, the ED estimated that the Bike Bot scheme had duped 2.25 lakh investors of Rs 3000-4000 crore . Separately, a Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) FIR in 2021 referred to potential losses of up to Rs 15,000 crore, citing the breadth of investments uncovered.Â

Between mid-2020 and late-2021, the ED conducted systematic actions against the Bike-Bot scheme. In July 2020, ED attached movable and immovable assets worth over Rs 103 crore , including 26 properties and bank balances across 22 accounts, GIPL, and related entities.

Furthermore, in October 2021, ED issued another provisional attachment worth Rs 112.36 crore. These assets comprised flats in the Saga Habitat project, land and buildings of Arni University in Himachal Pradesh, and under-construction towers in projects such as Amadeus and White House, as well as fixed deposits. At the same time, ED initiated search operations in December 2020 and July 2023, recovering digital evidence and other incriminating documents.

Meanwhile, in the same month, the CBI began probing the Bike-Bot scam, registering a case against GIPL, its promoter Sanjay Bhati, and 14 others on charges of defrauding investors of approximately Rs 15,000 crore through a fraudulent investment scheme.

The agency consolidated at least 11 FIRs across Uttar Pradesh, bringing them under one central investigation, and noted that Noida authorities and police officials had suppressed complaints against the scheme.

The Bike-Bot case highlights the vulnerability of investors to fraudulent financial schemes disguised as legitimate business models. Between 2017 and 2019, GIPL persuaded more than two lakh people to put money into a bike taxi service that investigators later confirmed never operated as promised.
