TITLE: Stop Language Policing. Live and let live.

--- POST TEXT ---

Language is a beautiful part of culture, a bridge that connects us to our roots. But when it becomes a tool of division, harassment, and misplaced pride, it loses its essence. Recent incidents of people being forced to speak Marathi under the pretense of protecting regional identity raise many uncomfortable questions.

If Marathi means so much to you, why are your own children studying in English-medium schools? Why not enroll them in Marathi schools and have English as just a subject? The truth is, education is about securing a future, and every parent wants the best for their child. Language should be a tool for growth, not a barrier to opportunities.

The harassment is conveniently directed at the weakest sections of society—auto drivers, delivery personnel, waiters—people struggling to make ends meet. Would you dare demand the same from your surgeon before a critical operation? No, because when it’s a matter of life and death, only skill and knowledge matter, not where someone is from or no what language they speak.

If you cannot tolerate people speaking Hindi, then why allow Hindi movies in theaters? Why enjoy Bollywood, cricket commentary, or business ventures that thrive on Hindi and English? Hypocrisy surfaces when selective enforcement benefits personal gains.

With countless pressing issues in society—farmer suicides, rape cases, corruption, unemployment—why is so much energy being spent on language policing? Shouldn’t true leadership focus on the welfare of people, the betterment of society, and justice for the oppressed? Instead of intimidating common workers, why not fight against frauds, scams, and systemic failures?

Will you treat Bollywood stars, cricketers, industrialists like Ambani, Tata, Patel, and Mehta the same way? No, because power is not used against the powerful. It is the weak, the voiceless, the daily wage earners who bear the brunt of these so-called cultural crusades. And that is what makes it all the more painful.

The harassment needs to stop. No one is disrespecting Marathi or any language. People are simply trying to survive, to put food on their tables. You provoke them, humiliate them, and then take action as if they committed a crime. What crime? The crime of working hard in a city that provides them livelihood? The crime of doing an honest day’s work?

There are those who benefit from these regional tensions—perhaps this is the only achievement they will ever have. Ironically, the same people send their children to English-medium schools, knowing very well that their divisive tactics are just a show. They themselves failed in their careers, and now they seek relevance by creating strife.

A politician’s duty is to uplift society, to create jobs, to ensure safety, to fight corruption, and to provide opportunities for growth. Leadership is about unity, not division. Maharashtra is a land of resilience and strength, built by the sweat of millions—Marathis and non-Marathis alike. It is not language but hard work, honesty, and perseverance that make a state great.

It is heartbreaking to see people who wake up before dawn, work tirelessly in the scorching heat or pouring rain, just to earn a dignified living, being humiliated for something so trivial. They don’t ask for handouts. They don’t expect privileges. All they want is the right to live, work, and speak in the language they are comfortable with.

Live and let live. Respect cannot be forced—it is earned. If you truly love your language, promote it with pride, not with intimidation. Let Maharashtra be known for its strength, not its divisions. Let people breathe without fear. Let them work without harassment. Let them live.
