Cybersecurity firm Kratikal Tech Limited has filed its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) for its proposed initial public offering (IPO) on the Small and Medium Enterprises (SME) platform of the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE).

The issue is a 100% book-built offering, i.e. the entire share issuance price is determined solely by investor demand, and comprises a fresh issue of up to 3 million equity shares with a face value of Rs 10 each, with no offer for sale component. Consequently, the entire proceeds from the issue will flow to the company. Beeline Capital Advisors is acting as the book-running lead manager, while KFin Technologies has been appointed as the registrar to the issue.

Founded in 2013 by Pavan Kumar, Paratosh Kumar, and Dip Jung Thapa, Noida-based Kratikal operates as a cybersecurity solutions provider offering AI-driven, SaaS-based security products and services.

Notably, the DRHP states that Kratikal is CERT-In empanelled and is also authorised by the National Stock Exchange (NSE) to conduct system audits for trading members. Furthermore, its subsidiaries include proprietary platforms such as Threatcop and AutoSecT, which focus on people security management, penetration testing, and vulnerability management.

Kratikal Tech Limited plans to deploy the net proceeds from its proposed public issue primarily to scale its overseas subsidiaries, strengthen sales and marketing, and invest in workforce and product development, according to its DRHP. The company states that a substantial portion of the funds will be invested in its wholly owned subsidiaries, Threatcop FZ LLC in the UAE and Threatcop AI Inc in the US, to support sales and marketing initiatives and the development of workforce resources.

Specifically, the DRHP highlights marketing as a critical growth lever, noting that targeted spending will help Kratikal expand its presence in the global cybersecurity market, improve brand visibility, and accelerate customer acquisition across regulated and enterprise-heavy sectors.

In addition, the DRHP links the proposed investments to the development and enhancement of its AI-driven, SaaS-based cybersecurity offerings, noting that the proceeds will support product development alongside workforce expansion. The company emphasises that these investments are intended to strengthen its proprietary platforms while supporting the delivery of integrated cybersecurity solutions across people, process, and technology layers.

In addition, a portion of the issue proceeds will be earmarked for general corporate purposes, which includes meeting operational expenses, strengthening internal systems, and supporting strategic initiatives. However, the DRHP clarifies that spending on general corporate purposes will be capped at 15% of the issue size or Rs 10 crore, whichever is lower, in line with regulatory limits.

Kratikal Tech Limited reported Rs 6.47 crore as revenue from operations for the three months ended June 30, 2025, representing the company’s disclosed FY26 performance to date. During the same period, the company generated an EBITDA of Rs 1.05 crore, resulting in an EBITDA margin of 16.22%. Profit after tax (PAT) for the quarter stood at Rs 73.13 lakh, while the PAT margin stood at 11.29%. In addition, operating cash flows for the corresponding period amounted to Rs 1.07 crore.

Meanwhile, Kratikal reported revenue from operations of Rs 20.85 crore for the financial year ended March 31, 2025, compared with Rs 13.01 crore in FY24 and Rs 9.14 crore in FY23. EBITDA for FY25 stood at Rs 5.51 crore, up from Rs 3.87 crore in FY24, while the company had reported an EBITDA loss of Rs 11.74 lakh in FY23. PAT followed a similar trajectory, rising to Rs 4.23 crore in FY25 from Rs 3.20 crore in FY24.

From an operational standpoint, the DRHP shows that Kratikal generated export revenue of Rs 1.81 crore during the quarter ended June 30, 2025, compared with Rs 2.75 crore for the quarter ended March 31, 2025, reflecting contributions from overseas clients. Meanwhile, the company has maintained a non-existent debt–equity ratio across all reported periods. Furthermore, the current ratio stood at 1.78 as of June 30, 2025, indicating its liquidity position during the period under review.

To explain, the debt–equity ratio is a financial metric that reveals how much debt a company uses to finance assets versus its own funds, indicating financial leverage and risk. And the current ratio shows a company’s ability to pay short-term debts (due within a year) with its short-term assets (convertible to cash within a year).

Kratikal operates under multiple data protection, privacy, and cybersecurity laws, including India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, which impose obligations around consent, data security, retention, and user rights. As these frameworks continue to evolve, the company may need to modify its business practices and technical systems.

Any actual or perceived non-compliance, including under laws with extraterritorial application, could result in regulatory action, penalties, litigation, or reputational harm.

The company derives revenue from both government and non-government customers, with non-government clients contributing over 96% of revenue in FY25.

Government contracts remain subject to budget approvals, procurement processes, and policy changes, which may delay or disrupt revenue recognition.

Meanwhile, enterprise demand depends on broader economic conditions and technology spending trends. Adverse developments in either segment could lead to revenue volatility and working capital pressures.

The company’s operations depend heavily on its Threatcop and AutoSecT platforms. Any failure to adequately test, upgrade, or maintain these systems can cause service disruptions, security vulnerabilities, or contractual breaches.

Moreover, delayed upgrades or insufficient quality assurance may impair customer satisfaction and renewal rates. As cyber threats and compliance expectations evolve rapidly, platform underperformance could weaken Kratikal’s competitive position and financial results.

Kratikal faces operational risks if its data backup, disaster recovery, or business continuity measures prove insufficient. System failures, cyber incidents, or natural disruptions could lead to data loss or prolonged downtime. Such events may delay project execution, disrupt billing and reporting, and expose the company to contractual penalties as well as regulatory scrutiny.

Kratikal’s growth depends on the reliability and scalability of its internal IT infrastructure, including cloud environments and internal systems. Failures arising from capacity constraints, configuration errors, or third-party outages could disrupt operations and service delivery. Inadequate investment or misjudged capacity planning may also limit the company’s ability to scale, affecting customer confidence and long-term growth prospects.

As a cybersecurity provider handling sensitive customer data, Kratikal remains exposed to risks of data breaches, fraud, or insider misuse. The company also relies on third-party tools for secure data transmission and authentication, reducing its control over certain security measures.

A significant breach could cause financial losses, invite regulatory action, trigger termination of customer contracts, and result in lasting reputational damage, even though the company has reported no such incidents to date.
