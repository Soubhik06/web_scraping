We missed this earlier: Tata Technologies Limited has informed that its systems suffered a ransomware incident, in a regulatory filing on January 31. The company said that the incident affected “a few” of its IT assets, but added that its client delivery services have “remained fully functional and unaffected throughout.”

The company did not disclose details on the extent of damages caused due to the attack, the identification of the attacker, and if they demanded any particular amount of currency as ransom.

A ransomware attack usually includes threat actors deploying malware that encrypts files and prevents the user from accessing them. Hackers subsequently grant users access back in exchange for ransom, by handing them a decryption key.

Tata Technologies further said it was working with experts “to assess the root cause and to take remedial action as necessary,” and that it was taking all possible steps to address any potential risks.

Bump in malware, ransomware numbers

This assumes importance in light of findings from a study conducted by the Data Security Council of India (DSCI) and cybersecurity firm Seqrite in 2024, which discovered that India had faced around 370 million malware attacks throughout the year at a rate of 702 detections per minute.

The study, titled ‘India Cyber Threat Report 2025’ analysed data from 8.44 million endpoints. Healthcare, hospitality, business, financial services, and insurance (BFSI) were the most targeted industries, contributing over 58% to the total attacks.
