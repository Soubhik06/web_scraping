In a recent development that has raised several eyebrows and questions about India's diplomatic engagements, unnamed Indian representatives have issued an apology to Cambodian authorities. 

This was reported by the Khmer Times, a government-aligned media outlet in Cambodia, which has been notably skeptical of claims made about the plight of Indian nationals in the region.

**Khmer Times:** [**Scammers utilising local social media​ to draw Indian victims**](https://www.khmertimeskh.com/501474119/scammers-utilising-local-social-media-to-draw-indian-victims/) (18 April 2024)

&gt;Bun Eng, however, is angered by the report in the Indian Express, as it has caused damage to Cambodia’s reputation and will also have an impact on the two countries’ relations. 
&gt; 
&gt;“**Indian representatives have approached us and apologised, and I really respect that**,” she said. “But if the source who spoke to the newspaper is real, it is still from an Indian official.” 
&gt; 
&gt;“While we are working hard on preventing and cracking down on the real criminals, we need the Indian authorities to raise their people’s awareness on the issue and prevent them from falling for the scam before they fly to our country.”

The backdrop of this apology involves [distressing allegations reported by the Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/5000-indians-in-cambodia-forced-into-cyber-scams-mha-takes-note-9239156/) earlier, which claimed that around 5,000 Indian nationals were forced into cyber scams in Cambodia. In response, MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal [stated that 250 Indians have been rescued](https://www.livemint.com/news/world/250-indians-cambodia-crackdown-fake-job-scam-ministry-of-external-affairs-randhir-jaiswal-cyber-activities-11711855092692.html) as of 31 March 2024. Further complicating the narrative, the Indian Ambassador to Cambodia, Devyani Khobragade, [stated to the Khmer Times](https://www.khmertimeskh.com/501465552/cambodia-probes-unverified-report-of-5000-trafficked-indians/) that only 300 Indian nationals had actually reached out to the embassy seeking help. Notably, the official statement from the MEA does not specify the total number of Indians who have sought assistance, focusing instead on the number successfully rescued. This discrepancy highlights the need for clarity in the data presented by the Indian officials.

Chou Bun Eng, the Permanent Vice-Chairwoman of the National Committee for Counter Trafficking (NCCT) in Cambodia, acknowledged the apology from Indian representatives but questioned the credibility of the initial reports.

The nature and details of this apology remain unclear and are shrouded in diplomatic vagueness. Who exactly were these "Indian representatives" who extended the apology, and what exactly were they apologising for? Was it for the misinformation spread by the media, or was it an attempt to smooth over broader issues affecting bilateral relations?

These developments bring to the forefront significant concerns regarding India's foreign policy execution and its impact on international relations, especially concerning the welfare of its citizens abroad.
