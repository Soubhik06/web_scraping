Kerala’s Cyber Operations Wing has registered a case against the X account “Laxmi N Raju ( @valiant_Raju )” and issued a legal notice to X over an AI-generated video. The video allegedly depicted Prime Minister Narendra Modi and the Election Commission of India (ECI) in a misleading and defamatory manner during the ongoing Kerala Assembly elections, The Hindu reported on March 26, citing an official release.

What happened: ECI inputs flagged the video through official channels, and Kerala Police registered the case at the Cyber Crime Police Station in Thiruvananthapuram City against the account and unidentified others under the Model Code of Conduct (election conduct rules that come into force once polls are announced). The video has around 400 views .

Why it matters: Kerala Cyber Police’s action reflects a growing trend of state forces using election-period content enforcement against platforms without transparency on how they make these decisions. When platforms fail to act on government removal notices, they lose safe harbour protection under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act .

Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)’s February 2026 IT Rules amendments introduced India’s first formal framework for deepfake takedowns, but authorities are not required to inform users of the reasons for removing content, a gap X Corp has challenged before the Karnataka High Court via its case against the Sahyog portal (MeitY’s centralised content takedown portal).

Investigation: The probe will examine the tools used to create the deepfake, the amplification network, and whether anyone coordinated an attempt to influence voters.

Second notice to X in days: On March 24, Kerala Cyber Police directed X to remove posts about an ECI circular bearing the BJP Kerala unit’s seal, which the Chief Electoral Officer had described as a “clerical error,” under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act and Rule 3(1)(d) of IT Rules 2021. The Wire reported that journalists and former officials criticised the notice, questioning why police framed an admitted clerical error as a communal harmony issue. Facebook and Instagram took down the posts; X did not comply.

Broader pattern: Maharashtra Cyber Police issued a takedown notice to X on March 25 seeking the removal of Rajya Sabha MP Sagarika Ghose’s account and a post, alleging she circulated fabricated AI-generated audio and video targeting the Prime Minister. Ghose said her post contained no images or videos and cited Article 19(1)(a) (constitutional provision guaranteeing freedom of speech) in response.
