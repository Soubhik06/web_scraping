Have you come across real estate ads promising 18% assured returns on property investments? It sounds tempting, doesn't it? But before you get swayed by these bold claims, it's crucial to understand what assured returns really mean and whether they are a legitimate investment strategy or a well-disguised scam. In this blog post, we'll dive deep into the world of assured returns in Indian real estate and uncover the truth behind these schemes.

What are Assured Returns in Real Estate? In simple terms, **assured returns** refer to a scheme where a real estate developer promises to pay a fixed return to property buyers, in addition to the ownership of the property. Usually, these returns range between 12-18% per annum and are offered on under-construction properties until the time of possession.

Here's how it typically works:

1. You purchase an under-construction property from a developer.
2. The developer commits to paying you a fixed return (e.g., 12% per annum) on the amount you have paid until the property is ready for possession.
3. Once the construction is complete, the assured returns stop, but you own the property.

The Origins of Assured Returns: The concept of assured returns started with commercial real estate to attract investors who were hesitant to buy under-construction properties. By offering fixed monthly returns, developers made their projects more appealing, as investors could earn an income even before the property was ready to be leased out or used for business purposes.

This scheme later extended to residential real estate, where developers used assured returns to:

* Help buyers manage their home loan EMIs during the construction period
* Arrange low-cost funding for their projects by collecting money directly from buyers

How Developers Market Assured Returns: Developers often market assured returns in two ways:

1. Committing a fixed percentage return (e.g., 12-18% per annum) on the amount paid by the buyer until possession.
2. Promising a fixed monthly rent on the property during the construction period.

In both cases, the effective returns usually fall between 10-18%, which is higher than typical home loan interest rates of 7-10% and commercial property loan rates of 9-12%. This makes investing in under-construction properties with assured returns seem like a lucrative option.

The Risks and Controversies Surrounding Assured Returns: Despite their apparent benefits, assured returns have been marred by controversies and legal issues:

1. SEBI's Stance: In 2016, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) declared assured return schemes as **Ponzi schemes**, deeming them fraudulent and illegal. SEBI's reasons were twofold:

* The high interest rates promised were unrealistic.
* The money was being invested in under-construction properties that generated no immediate income.

1. RERA's Perspective: The Real Estate Regulatory Authority (RERA) also takes a strict stance against assured returns. If a developer is found launching such a scheme, buyers can file a complaint with RERA, and action will be taken against the developer. However, if a buyer has invested in an assured return scheme after SEBI's ban in 2016, RERA may not provide assistance, as the buyer knowingly participated in an illegal scheme.
2. Developer Defaults: Many developers have defaulted on their assured return payments, leaving buyers in a lurch. Recovering the promised returns becomes a lengthy and challenging legal battle, with limited chances of success.

How Developers Continue to Offer Assured Returns: Despite the legal restrictions, some developers still find ways to offer assured returns:

1. Portraying Returns as Discounts: Some developers market assured returns heavily but portray the total return amount as a discount when buyers approach them. For instance, if a property costs Rs. 1 crore and the developer promises 12% returns for a year, they might offer a Rs. 12 lakh discount instead of monthly payouts.
2. Word-of-Mouth Marketing: Other developers may not actively promote assured returns but continue to offer them through word-of-mouth and broker networks. They rely on the lack of awareness about SEBI and RERA guidelines among buyers to push these schemes.

Lessons for Property Buyers: As a property buyer, it's essential to be cautious and well-informed when considering investments in assured return schemes. Here are some key lessons:

1. Be Wary of High Discounts and Offers: If a developer is offering substantial discounts or assured returns, it could be a red flag. It may indicate that the developer is struggling to sell the property or facing cash flow issues. Reputable developers rarely offer large discounts or assured returns.
2. Avoid Developers Promoting Illegal Schemes: If a developer is blatantly promoting assured return schemes, it shows a disregard for the law. Such developers may not hesitate to default on payments or engage in other unethical practices.
3. Understand the Legal Implications: Investing in an assured return scheme after SEBI's ban in 2016 could be considered a criminal offense. If the developer defaults, you may have limited legal recourse and face a lengthy, expensive legal battle.
4. Conduct Thorough Due Diligence: Before investing in any real estate project, conduct extensive research on the developer, their track record, and the project's legal status. Consult with real estate experts, lawyers, and financial advisors to make an informed decision.
5. Focus on Fundamentals: Instead of being swayed by assured returns, evaluate a property based on its location, price, quality, and growth potential. Invest in projects by reputable developers with a proven track record of timely completion and delivery.

Assured returns in real estate may seem like an attractive investment opportunity, but they come with significant risks and legal complexities. As a smart property buyer, it's crucial to look beyond the lure of high returns and carefully assess the developer, project, and legal aspects before making a decision. By staying informed and cautious, you can safeguard your investments and make wise choices in your real estate journey.

Remember, if an offer seems too good to be true, it probably is. Focus on fundamental factors and work with trusted developers to build a solid real estate portfolio. Stay informed, stay vigilant, and make decisions that align with your long-term financial goals.
