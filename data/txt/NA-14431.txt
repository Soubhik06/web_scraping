“My concern is disparagement, and these videos are, on the face of it, disparaging,” said Justice Amit Bansal to Senior Advocate Chander Lall, who was representing YouTuber Mohak Mangal against ANI’s defamation lawsuit.

The news agency had sued the YouTuber after he published a video accusing ANI of extortion. He claimed that he had received multiple copyright strikes from ANI after using small sections of their news reports in his YouTube video.

ANI had taken umbrage at specific sections of Mangal’s video. “They are calling me a thug, extortionist, goonda, kidnapper, and the like. And much worse which I can’t say in court,” said Senior Advocate Amit Sibal, representing ANI.

After listening to the video, Justice Bansal recommended that Lall take instructions on removing certain portions of it. “You should have put it in a more civilised manner,” he said.

“I’ll give a red-lined version, with the portions that I will remove,” Lall offered.

Advocate Chander Lall argued that ANI’s conduct qualified as extortion, as they did not follow due process of law and Mangal’s use of ANI’s clips did not amount to infringement.

“In a 30-minute video, if I use eight seconds, it’s de minimis,” he contended. (De minimis refers to something too trivial to merit consideration)

Lall described the process of YouTube copyright strikes: typically, a copyright holder issues strikes against a channel for infringement, and after three strikes, YouTube takes down the channel. ANI had sent two strikes to Mohak Mangal and asked him to pay Rs 40 lakh, including a yearly license fee and infringement penalties.

“If my channel gets blocked, then I’m stuck. Then I have to pay,” said Lall. “There’s a legal way of dealing with copyright infringement if they have a grievance against me. They can’t call me and extort money from me, saying that they will block my channel if I don’t pay. That’s the crux.”

However, the court questioned why Mangal chose to make a public video on the topic instead of seeking relief through the courts.

“If YouTube is blocking your channel, your remedy is to move the court, no? The cause of action would be perhaps YouTube, no? YouTube is allowing their strikes, no?” said Justice Bansal.

“Let’s say their demand is wrongful. So you’ll call it extortion?” he asked.

ANI accused the YouTuber of disparaging the news agency’s trademark, citing Sections 29(8) and 29(9) of the Trade Marks Act. Chander Lall argued that those sections were not applicable.

“Disparagement falls under the Trade Marks Act Sections 29(8) and 30, where I’m permitted to use a registered trademark. It is not in the course of trade. I’m not their competitor. So a trademark infringement suit will not lie,” he said.
