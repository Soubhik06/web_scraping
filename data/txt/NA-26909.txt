My guess is yes, but here’s the short of it:

She tried to buy health insurance online, found an option through a random website. Put her phone number in, received a call. Man with foreign accent answers (we’re in the US), connects her to an American guy in New Jersey. He essentially picks a plan for her, over the phone, and gets everything. Email, phone, SSN, address, former/parents address, birthday, debit card number.. she even willingly gave the guy her Instagram name. 

The “insurance” was billed through a third party billing company with 1.3/5 stars on the BBB website, although they have a B+ rating. This was billed immediately for $50 more than agreed upon, when the “insurance” doesn’t go into effect until April 1st.

The guy even suggested himself that she put herself on a do not call list because of the amount of spam calls she’s going to get. 

The insurance company itself has a half-baked template website with Lorem Ipsum in spots, unclear contact info, an address in Florida. They do seem to be registered as an LLC in Florida, and I was able to find a job listing for the company which showed that they make 35% commissions on insurance premium sales. Looking into the confirmation email, it shows a parent company that’s actually an Indian (Delhi) insurance company with pretty much no other info online. 

When I realized what she did, we promptly froze her credit on all three bureaus, cancelled her debit card and got a new one, filed a fraud report for the $315 they charged her, and now we’re just hoping this was cheap insurance and not a full-on fraud. 

Thoughts?
