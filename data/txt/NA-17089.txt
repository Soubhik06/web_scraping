Just back today from India, not my first trip, so I more or less was expecting some of the issues I faced, but from time it really got on my nerves to a breaking point (last day was pretty much "fuck it, I'm done" and this post is the result).

* What is it with *all* being transactional when you are a visitor ? I am not a millionaire, I don't shit money every morning. As a consequence, if someone has a service to offer, then we ought to first talk about the price, most of the time it will be expensive-ish for what it is, but maybe worth it. After it's done, I'll pay what I said I would. However if someone offers a service, without mentioning anything about payment, I will naturally assume it's out of kindness (you know, like normal human interactions...). So when someones starts asking for money because he took a picture, opened a door, showed me directions, you name it, but AFTER the deed, he won't get anything. If he gets upset about it (sometimes VERY, VERY upset, and SCARILY upset), too bad. 
* Linked to the point above, but thanks to many, many similar experiences, I am now pretty much assuming the starting point of any discussion while visiting will be to find a way to extorting money, and most "kindness" I first feel will be completely fake. 
* And also, if by some miracle there is no additional money asked for some specific service (hotel, experience, etc.), then each and every time it will be "can you log on googlemaps or whatever and give your opinion". Several times a day. If I feel like giving an opinion, positive or negative, I'll give it, if I want. If you force me, I'll make sure I give a negative opinion.

I have been to many countries, developped, not developped, developping, large, small, rich, poor, and I never faced something THAT intense. There is clearly something that can be done about educating interactions with visitors, because if anyone asks me what I think about visiting India, I'll give him an honest feedback.

Add that I am not a woman. I accompanied a female in a previous trip, this was something else...
