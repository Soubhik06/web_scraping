The Government of India has made it mandatory for the top 300 pharmaceutical companies to print or affix QR (Quick Response) codes on the packaging of the medicines they sell.

These codes, which can be scanned using mobile applications, are meant to help consumers verify the authenticity of medicines and curb the circulation of spurious drugs. The rule came into effect from August 1, 2023, under the amended Drugs Rules, 1945.

This information came to light through a written reply in the Lok Sabha to an unstarred question asked by Trinamool Congress MP Rachna Banerjee on August 1, 2025. The questions were:

(a) The number of pharmaceutical companies that have adopted QR codes under Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 along with the steps taken/proposed to be taken by the Government to ensure compliance from others; (b) Whether the Government plans to extend the mandatory QR code to all categories of medicine in the country and if so, the details thereof; and (c) Whether the Government is planning public awareness campaigns to educate consumers on using QR codes to verify medicine authenticity and if so, the details thereof?

In her reply, the Minister of State (MoS) for Health and Family Welfare, Anupriya Patel, stated that the requirement for QR codes applies to the top 300 brands of drug products listed under Schedule H2. Manufacturers must print these codes on the primary packaging label, or on the secondary label if space on the primary package is limited. They must ensure the QR codes store readable data that enables drug authentication.

State Licensing Authorities (SLAs) must implement and enforce this rule. The Government has directed them to closely monitor compliance and take action in case of violations.

The Minister also informed that the Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB) has agreed to gradually extend the QR code requirement to all vaccine products, as well as antimicrobials, narcotic and psychotropic substances. This QR code expansion will happen in a phased manner.

To inform the public, the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) has made the notification available on its official website. It has also urged industry stakeholders to run public awareness campaigns. Furthermore, CDSCO has published step-by-step guidance online for identifying and verifying spurious drugs using QR or bar codes.

However, despite the rule being in effect for over two years, the Government has not disclosed how many pharmaceutical companies have actually adopted the QR code system.

The absence of concrete figures raises questions about the scale of implementation and the Government’s ability to monitor compliance effectively. Without such data, it remains unclear how far the industry has moved toward meeting the new requirements.

The QR code system aims to empower consumers, but its success also depends on factors the government has not addressed. Digital literacy varies widely across the country, and although smartphones’ use is increasing, the digital divide between rural and urban India remains stark .

According to data from the National Sample Survey Office (NSSO) released last year, only 24% of rural Indian households have access to the internet, compared to a 66% in India’s cities. Furthermore, 14% of rural citizens actively use the internet, as compared to 59% of urban population.

This disparity is rooted in various factors, such as patchy network coverage, affordability issues, and a lack of localised vernacular internet content. And this could affect the utility of QR codes in large parts of the country.

Ultimately, the QR code initiative’s effectiveness will depend on whether the Government launches targeted awareness and accessibility efforts.
