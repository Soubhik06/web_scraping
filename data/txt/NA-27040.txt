Shivam Chopra, 21, had made nearly Rs 50 lakh between April and May this year before Amazon realized that they were being conned and complained to the police.

A 21 year old graduate from Delhi ordered over 166 expensive smartphones from Amazon. He claimed refunds by saying that he received empty boxes. Also, he sold the phones on OLX or in local markets.

First, in March this year, he ordered two phones as a test, asked refunds and he got it. For the next two months, he made a business out of it, ordering Apple, Samsung and OnePlus mobile phones.

A local mobile shop owner supplied him over 140 pre-activated sim cards. He placed the orders using these numbers and gave the incorrect address in his locality. When delivery associate would call him on one of the numbers, he would guide them somewhere near his locality and accept the delivery. He would call up to complain that the box was empty and get a refund.

He repeated this for more than 166 times.

This kind of scams are not very uncommon in India. Last year, in Hyderabad, two persons were arrested who would tactfully open the seal of the packet while the delivery associate was waiting for payment at their doorsteps, replace the item with sand and reject the item to online retailers such as Amazon and Flipkart.

I have seen many people who brag about how they fooled the e-commerce sites.
