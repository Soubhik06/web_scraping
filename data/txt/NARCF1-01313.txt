Corruption is a disease that eats away at the trust we place in public institutions. Recently, I faced an incident at the Ahmedabad RTO (East) that shook my faith in the system. The officer in charge of RC renewal blatantly demanded a bribe to do his job—work that is his responsibility as a public servant. Refusing to pay meant my work wouldn’t be done.

This experience left me feeling helpless, frustrated, and deeply sad about the state of affairs in our country.

The Incident: 
I approached the Ahmedabad East RTO for a routine RC renewal. After submitting all the required documents and completing the formalities, I was hopeful that the process would be smooth. However, the officer in charge outright told me that unless I paid him a certain amount as a "gift," my file would not move forward.

I refused to pay the bribe because I believe in doing things the right way. But my refusal came at a cost—my work remains incomplete, and I am stuck in an endless loop of delays.

As someone who works hard and believes in honesty, this incident hurt deeply. Why should a citizen, who pays taxes and follows the law, have to endure this kind of treatment? Why do we have to beg and bribe for services that are our basic right?

This isn't just about one incident or one officer. It’s about a broken system where corruption thrives unchecked, and ordinary people are left feeling powerless.

During this ordeal, I couldn’t help but think about how different things are in countries like the United States. Their systems are built on transparency and efficiency. Public servants are held accountable, and processes are streamlined to ensure citizens don’t face unnecessary hurdles.

For example, renewing a vehicle registration in the U.S. is often as simple as filling out a form online and paying a small fee. There’s no need to deal with middlemen or endure harassment. The system works for the people, not against them.

I’m not saying their system is perfect, but it’s light-years ahead of ours in terms of integrity and efficiency.

This experience has left me wondering: When will we have a system that truly serves its citizens? When will we see a day where honesty is rewarded, and corruption is punished?

To anyone reading this, I urge you to stand up against corruption. Refuse to pay bribes, report such incidents, and demand accountability. Change starts with us, but it also requires systemic reform and strict action against corrupt officials.

Conclusion: 
I still believe in the potential of our country. We have brilliant minds and hardworking citizens who deserve a better system. Let’s work towards building a nation where integrity is valued and corruption has no place.

Together, we can create a brighter future—one where no one has to endure what I did.
