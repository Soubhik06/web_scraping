--- POST TEXT ---

**Assumption 1: 100 Cr voters equally distributed among 543 constituencies, so \~18.4L voters in each constituency.**

**Assumption 2: Everyone votes**

**Assumption 3: There are only 2 equally popular parties**

So, in a ideal and direct democracy, you would need 50% + 1 votes, that would be 50cr + 1 votes.

But in a representative democracy, you need 272 seats and need 50% + 1 votes in each constituency to form majority.

 272 * ((18,40,000 / 2) + 1) = 25,02,40,272 votes (~25%)

**Lets modify assumption 3 a bit**

**Assumption 3A: The are many parties but only 2 popular parties. Except for top 2, all other parties combined get 20% votes in any constituency**

So, from remaining 80% votes you need 40% + 1 votes to win any constituency

 272 * ((18,40,000 * 4 / 10) + 1) = 20,01,92,272 votes (~20%)

**Now lets modify Assumption 2 a bit**

**Assumption 2A: Only 65% voters vote in any constituency.**

 272 * (((18,40,000 * 65 / 100) * 4 / 10) + 1) = 13,01,25,072 votes (~13%)

**Finally we can modify Assumption 1 as well.**

**Assumption 1A: Population across 543 constituencies is distributed normally.**

Asked ChatGPT for normal distribution of voter count across constituencies. I generated with following parameter

* Mean ≈ 18.17 lakh
* Standard Deviation ≈ 4.9 lakh
* Minimum ≈ 78,415 voters
* Maximum = 30 lakh voters

Now I took average count of smallest 272 constituencies i.e., 14.33L voters

 272 * (((14,33,000 * 65 / 100) * 4 / 10) + 1) = 10,13,42,032 votes (10%)

In a population of 140 Cr people, out of which 100 Cr voters, from whom 65 Cr actually vote,

**You need about 10% of votes to actually form majority government in a representative democracy like India. Which is \~7% of population of India.**

Notes:

* This a general framework, it can be modified and extended to calculate more exact numbers. We can use data from 2024 and even calculate for 2029 or any upcoming state election.
* If you feel, I have made some wildly wrong assumption, you can recalculate and post your numbers.
* This provides an estimation of **minimum** number of votes in different scenarios.
* **There are lots of assumptions in this i.e., mentioned them as 'Assumption'.**
 * I have tried to keep these numbers close to actual numbers from 2024 Lok Sabha elections.
 * **Assumption 2A** needs to better handled. It is applied same to all constituencies. I believe it could create a small difference if actual data is used. As voter participation varies across constituencies and you might have more voter percentage in lower populated constituencies.
 * **For Assumption 1A**, we can either use actual data or maybe reduce standard deviation.
* With this framework, you can analyse impact of
 * Divided opposition
 * Voter fraud
 * Low voter turnout
 * Impact of NOTA
 * Voter participation
 * Voting age, etc
* I hope this would trigger conversation about direct democracy (like Switzerland), two-round system (like France) or any other ways to reduce this disparity.
