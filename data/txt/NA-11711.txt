Seeking the Court to enlarge the petitioner/accused

NO.9 in Crime No.200 of 2024 on the file of the Central Crime

Station, Hyderabad, on bail, the present Criminal Petition is

filed. The offences as alleged against the petitioner are under

Section 403, 406 and 420 read with Section 120-B of Indian

Penal Code (for short ' IPC '). 2. The brief facts of the case are that the complainant

alleges that DKZ Technologies/Dikaro Solutions Private

Limited, its directors (Ashfaq Rahil and Ighal), managers

(Umair, Moiz, Aslam, Nazeer, and Bilal), and employees

(Bhagya, Fatima, Sabiya, Sunita, Rubi, Khaled, Salman, and

Mohammed Abdul Waheed) cheated investors, including

himself, of approximately Rs. 25-100 crores. The company,

established in 2018, promised 8-12% monthly profits through

B2B and B2C businesses, partnerships with Amazon and

BOULT, and hired influencers to promote its legitimacy.

However, after consistent payouts, the July 2024 payment 2 SKS,J Crl.P.No.13243 of 2024 was delayed, and the company cited NBFC certification

processes as the reason. Investors suspect fraud after the

office suddenly closed, and phones went unanswered. The

complainant requests prompt action under Sections 403 , 406 , 420 , and 120-B IPC, citing investors' significant losses,

including Abul Jaish (Rs. 2.72 Cr), Md. Athaur Rahman (Rs.

1.74 Cr), and Syed Salma (Rs. 84 Lakhs). The case (Cr. No.

200/2024) has been registered and handed over to Inspector

E. Jahangeer Yadav, EOW.2. 3. Heard Sri V.Raghunath, learned senior counsel

appearing for Sri Mohammad Imtiyaz Pasha, learned counsel

for petitioner, and Sri Syed Yasan Manoon, learned Additional

Public Prosecutor, appearing for respondent. 4. Learned senior counsel for petitioner submitted that the

Petitioner/Accused No. 9, a housewife and nominal director of

Dikazo Solutions Pvt. Ltd. from 2018 to June 2023, seeks

regular bail in Crime No. 200/2024. She was appointed to

comply with the Companies Act, 2013 , and had no active role

in the company's operations or decision-making. Despite

resigning before the alleged offenses, she was falsely

implicated. He contended that the prosecution case is flawed, 3 SKS,J Crl.P.No.13243 of 2024 lacking substantive evidence of her involvement in financial

mismanagement or fraud. Notably, the complainants omitted

her name, raising credibility concerns. Her resignation in

June 2023 removes her liability for the alleged July 2024

offense. The Petitioner's inclusion is baseless, infringing her

legal rights and violating principles of individual liability, due

process, and natural justice. With no evidence of her

involvement, the court should recognize the absence of

substantive evidence and grant bail, dismissing the erroneous

invocation of Section 5 of the Telangana Protection of

Depositors of Financial Establishments Act, 1999. Therefore,

prayed this Court to allow the criminal petition and enlarge

petitioner on bail. 5. Learned Additional Public Prosecutor on the other hand

contended that the petitioner had actively participated in the

affairs of the company and entered into an agreement with

victims and that his name was stated by the victims in their

statements. The petitioner defaulted to repay amount even

after the maturity period, which would attract Section 5 of the

Telangana Protection of Depositors of Financial

Establishments Act, 1999 and other offences registered 4 SKS,J Crl.P.No.13243 of 2024 against him under IPC and prayed to dismiss criminal

petition. 6. This Court, considering submissions made by both the

learned counsel and reviewing the record, it is noted that the

petitioner's contention that she resigned as director on

01.06.2023, and thus had no involvement in the alleged July

2024 offences. However, the economic offence involves

multiple victims and significant financial losses, predating the

petitioner's resignation. 7. Further, learned counsel for the petitioner relied upon

the judgment of Apex Court in Sheikh Javed Iqbal v. State of

Uttar Pradesh , wherein in paragraph No.27.1, it is held as

under: 8. Reverting to the facts of the case on hand, investigation

is not yet completed, whereas, in the above case observations

are after framing charges during the course of trial, as such,

above analogy is not applicable to facts of the case on hand.

The Court rejects the argument that resignation absolves

culpability, especially the one given by accused No.2 on

17.03.2024, which suggests an anticipatory move. The

investigation remains ongoing, and the petitioner's continued

detention is warranted due to the severity and scope of the

allegations. Therefore, the criminal petition lacks merit and is

liable to be dismissed. 9. Accordingly, the Criminal petition is dismissed. Miscellaneous applications, if any pending, shall stand

closed. _______________
