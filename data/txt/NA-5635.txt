 The factual report dated 18.5.2025 received by the learned

Public Prosecutor from the office of Assistant Police Commissioner, (Downloaded on 22/05/2025 at 09:39:59 PM) [2025:RJ-JD:24692] (2 of 3) [CRLMP-3362/2025]

Cyber Police Station, Commmissionerate, Jodhpur is produced

before this Court and the same is taken on record. The impugned FIR and the factual report dated 18.5.2025

indicate that the petitioners were found involved in economic

offences and financial frauds. They were found misusing the

Phonepe POS machine issued to them by banks and thereby

swindled huge amount from their customers. Having perused the case file, this Court finds that the

allegations against the petitioners prima facie indicate commission

of a cognizable offence. Thus, no case for quashing of the FIR is

made out. It is a settled law that this Court while exercising powers

under Section 528 BNSS cannot minutely go into the correctness

of the allegations levelled against the petitioners. At this stage,

this Court is not expected to either scan the entire material

available on record or to record any definite finding thereon. Hon'ble the Supreme Court in the case of Dinesh Sharma

Vs. Emgee Cables and Communication Ltd. & Anr. (Neutral

Citation : 2025 INSC 751) has been pleased to hold that the

FIRs where economic offences/financial frauds are manifest, the

accused persons in such FIRs are not to be extended the benefit of

quashing of the FIR under Section 528 BNSS. The history of

civil/commercial transactions between the parties is not relevant

as the intention to cheat is manifest. Financial frauds demand a

higher degree of judicial scrutiny, given their far reaching

consequences on the economy, investors in financial institutions.

Thus, when an accused is engaged in conduct akin to financial

fraud, Courts must be conscious and weigh all circumstances (Downloaded on 22/05/2025 at 09:39:59 PM) [2025:RJ-JD:24692] (3 of 3) [CRLMP-3362/2025]

 before exercising their powers under Section 528 BNSS to quash

 the proceedings. In the present case, as noticed above, specific allegation of

 committing financial fraud through POS machine issued to the

 petitioners by the Banks has been levelled against the present

 petitioners. The investigation in relation to the impugned FIR

 conducted by the police prima facie indicates offences under Sections 420 , 406 , 120-B IPC and Section 66C , 66B I.D. Act to be

 proved against the present petitioners. In view of aforesaid discussion and taking into consideration

 the law laid down by Hon'ble the Supreme Court of India in the

 case of Dinesh Sharma (supra), this Court does not find any of

 the conditions for quashing of the FIR to be prima facie fulfilled in

 the present case and thus, this Court is not inclined to exercise

 the powers vested in it under Section 528 BNSS for quashing the

 FIR in question. Accordingly, these instant criminal misc. petitions and stay

 petitions stand dismissed. (KULDEEP MATHUR),J
