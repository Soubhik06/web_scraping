Regular readers and veterans of the stock market, don't need this.

I've never been in receipt of an anonymous stock tip. Today, I was fortunate enough to study a fresh [one](http://imgur.com/Qq6EuaV).

This one is great, because it ticks all the boxes on what to look for in a pump-and-dump scheme.

1. History of lack of liquidity. Tick. [This](http://imgur.com/LxrOizV) volume and price graph should explain itself. Pump-and-dump schemes don't necessarily have a short accumulation phase. Some operators could accumulate stocks for months or years.

2. Promoter shareholding. Tick. Promoter holding is at 2%. Guess who wants to sell off shares in the company? Something very common about a pump-and-dump scheme is that you'll almost always find retail shareholding to be high. Not that all such retail investors are suckers, but it's easy for operators to hide their trading activity behind such accounts.

3. Lack of reason for interest in shares? Tick. Filings to the exchange have to be made by the company on events that materially affect the financials. There have been none in the past month. Apparently, the exchange sought clarification from the company on a new item about a new online app, and [the company responded it had no such plans](http://corporates.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachLive/F70B4882_C75C_4648_AA1B_21A7BAA88059_132512.pdf). I can't seem to find the news in question, but there it's likely to have been planted.
