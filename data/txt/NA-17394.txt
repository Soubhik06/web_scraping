This is my first post in this group, and I am honestly concerned and worried about how scarce the conversation around this topic has been here over the last few weeks. I have grown up watching NDTV among others, when it used to have stellar standards and professionalism set by people like Ravish Kumar. I genuinely felt proud of the media (theoretically supposed to be the fourth pillar of democracy) when I used to watch him along with other great anchors and hosts, venturing into the heartlands of the country, and not just the urban, concrete jungle of the metropolitan cities. They were fearless in their reporting and used to hold the government accountable, or atleast, dare to raise their concerns over the problems that the people has and experienced (https://scroll.in/article/929461/greater-self-censorship-dogged-digital-resistance-how-indias-news-media-have-changed-since-2014&amp; https://frontline.thehindu.com/news/media-freedom-india-south-asia-ifj-report-rings-alarm-bells-democratic-decline/article68153948.ece).

Then came the infamous buyout by Adani, which inevitably turned into a commercial advertisement vehicle (forgive my bluntness), which was more interested in pushing forward government agenda than talking about ground-level issues faced by every community and strata of the society. This is yet another sign of corporate entities infiltrating the crucial infrastructure which supports the smooth functioning of the country (https://www.livemint.com/news/india/adani-group-vs-ndtv-the-tale-of-hostile-takeovers-in-indian-corporate-industry/amp-11661307221684.htm &amp; https://www.aljazeera.com/opinions/2023/1/6/big-money-is-choking-indias-free-press).

Eventually, I shifted to digital media to keep me updated on my country's progress, development and issues. I hope this doesn't sound like I am promoting any specific platform and neither have I been paid by them to do so, but I genuinely feel that YouTube has served as a viable and useful alternative to many people across the country, and which serves to some degree to alienate themselves away from the general rhetoric adopted by the mainstream media. YouTubers like Dhruv Rathee, Mohak Mangal, The Deshbhakt, Ravish Kumar, etc., have played a significant and important role (regardless of what you think of them) in keeping their viewers informed about topics which the big media houses are either too afraid or uninterested to take up. The amount of dedication, planning, effort and passion that they invest in these videos are a testament to their resilience and should be the benchmark that the other news handles should adopt.

However, coming back to the topic at hand, this Bill being proposed by the Central Government seeks to tighten the hold and scrutiny of such individual creators and keep them buried in paperwork due to arbitrary laws. 

Let me also add, no other democracy in the world except for Bangladesh (https://carnegieendowment.org/2021/12/09/how-bangladesh-s-digital-security-act-is-creating-culture-of-fear-pub-85951),
and something which in recent weeks Pakistan has also shown an interest in (https://thediplomat.com/2024/05/pakistan-plans-to-regulate-social-media-through-legislation/), has strived to introduce such a Bill in their country. It also needs to be noted that we do already have existing digital and libel laws in our country which restrict the peddling of illegal and abusive content (https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/technology/how-the-digital-india-act-will-shape-the-future-of-the-countrys-cyber-landscape/article67397155.ece &amp; https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023).

The entire Bill has been kept away from public scrutiny and serves to bring even global personalities such as Mr.Beast into this umbrella and get a clearance certificate from the Government approving their content for Indian viewers. The drastic amendments added to them won't even spare us, the common man and woman of India, if they wish to question the government policies or offer a satirical take on any issues in any social media platform. 

As a youth of this nation, seeing the recent turn of events in our country, this is the last thing that we need. Going forward, maybe even criticising your local PWD or Municipality Corporation in X, will not be allowed by the stringent and authoritative laws and regulations under this Bill. This will also change the digital landscape wherein anyone who voices their concerns against the government may also be arrested and their equipment seized without an approved court order, facing legal quandaries (https://www.giga-hamburg.de/en/publications/giga-focus/digital-surveillance-and-the-threat-to-civil-liberties-in-india).

I apologise if this is too much to read since this is my first time, and I wanted to express my opinion on this matter without any doubt or speculation as to my particular standing or position in this matter. The writing may look lengthy, however, this is a topic which should be discussed with precision and thoroughness to analyse the long-term impact it may have on our freedom of speech. I acknowledge that the writing may seem shoddy and stretched, however, in my view I thought it would be best to attach the supporting material in regards to my assessment of the situation, in case anyone would like to verify these sources.

To conclude, I would love to listen to your comments and opinions on this issue and whether this Bill may have any ramifications in your own life. I do hope this post creates an informed and constructive discussion on this topic and am looking forward to hearing your thoughts on this issue.

P.S - The intention behind this post is not to downgrade the current Central Government or it's performance. It merely serves to critique and question the Bill tabulated by the Government from a strictly ethical, and humane perspective. I would also request everyone to not attach any political or religious connotations since I do not wish to enter into either realm.
