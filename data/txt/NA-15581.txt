The Australian eSafety Commissioner revealed that Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok , and YouTube are under investigation in its first compliance report since the country banned under-16s from social media in December 2025. The regulator said it aims to finalise the probes and decide on enforcement action by mid-2026, based on evidence gathered through ongoing enquiries.

Evidence still being built: The report describes observed practices as illustrative , noting that further evidence is required to establish non-compliance. The eSafety Commissioner continues to gather information through legally enforceable notices, platform engagement, and testing, indicating that the current observations form part of an ongoing evidentiary process.

Reactive improvements by platforms: Meanwhile, the report notes that s ome platforms have made improvements following engagement with the eSafety Commissioner. For instance, certain services have enhanced reporting pathways or introduced limits on repeated age verification attempts. However, these changes appear to have been implemented after regulatory intervention, suggesting compliance efforts have often been reactive rather than proactive.

No reduction in harm signals: Crucially, the eSafety Commissioner reports no discernible drop in complaints involving under-16 users since the law took effect. It continues to receive cyberbullying and image-based abuse complaints from children with accounts on age-restricted social media platforms. This indicates that, despite account removals and restrictions, underage users remain active and exposed to harm.
