Razorpay, a payments and banking platform for businesses, has secured a John Doe Order from the Delhi High Court, attempting to protect its brand identity and customers from financial fraud. Effective immediately, the order mandates that social media platforms suspend accounts found to be infringing on Razorpay’s trademarks and conducting fraudulent activities. Domain name registrars will also be required to block domain names associated with these entities, bank accounts, and UPI IDs.

According to a press release from the company, Razorpay had filed a lawsuit against unknown individuals who were misrepresenting themselves as company recruiters, offering part-time jobs for extra income in exchange for deposits and making other false promises. These scams were primarily perpetrated through platforms such as WhatsApp, Facebook, and Telegram.

The Delhi High Court, observing that a prima facie case was established in favour of Razorpay, issued an order prohibiting the unauthorized use of the “Razorpay” trademark. This decision aims to prevent fraud against the general public, including financial scams. In response, Razorpay has also placed a disclaimer on its website to caution the public against such fraudulent activities.

The government of India has been coordinating with fintech companies to curb cybercrime and fraud in the sector. Last year, the Department of Financial Services (DFS) held a meeting with banks, payment companies, and other entities to discuss online fraud. They discussed the need for coordination between police and financial entities and bringing all financial entities into the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFCFRMS).

Two parliamentary panels have also taken note of this rise in payment fraud and have prescribed various measures to curb it. The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Communications and Information Technology proposed various measures to combat financial fraud, including promoting Indian payment apps and sensitising users via fintech apps.
