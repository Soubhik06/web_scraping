 counsel for the accused have been carefully considered along with the
 evidence on record. 19) It is a settled proposition of law that in a criminal trial, it is for the State
 to prove its case beyond all reasonable doubts by leading reliable, cogent
 and convincing evidence and it is for the prosecution to ensure that its
 case is able to stand on its own legs. The prosecution cannot derive any
 benefit whatsoever from the weakness of the defence of the accused if
 any. Accused is entitled to the benefit of every reasonable doubt in the
 prosecution version. 20) The prosecution's case rests on an alleged General Power of Attorney
 (GPA) and related documents purportedly executed in favour of Bhim
 Singh Sharma in 1980. However, the document presented by the
 prosecution is a photocopy, not an original. The Investigating Officer was
 directed to recover the original, which was not done. The photocopy was
 categorically rejected by the Forensic Science Laboratory (CFSL), which
 refused authentication on the ground that photocopies cannot be
 forensically verified. In absence of the alleged original documents, the
 charge of forgery under Sections 467 & 468 IPC does not stand. The
 prosecution failed to bring forth even a shred of admissible documentary
 or oral evidence of forgery, fabrication, or cheating by the accused. 21) The assertion by the complainant that Municipal Corporation of Delhi
 (MCD) officials visited and inquired about unpaid house tax in the names
 of Lalita and Yogesh is unsubstantiated. No house tax demand notice has
 been placed on record, and there is no public document or witness to
 support this version. 22) Upon examination of the testimony of PW1, Kalawati, it is apparent that
 her statements before the Court are materially contradictory on essential
 issues. On 05.03.2013, she claimed that the property was divided into
 two equal shares between her sons Amarnath and Yogesh through a
 family settlement, but on 18.05.2013, she denied any such division.
 These conflicting statements cast serious doubt on her credibility as a
 witness and challenge the veracity of her claims. Furthermore, PW1
 contradicted herself on the paternity of Yogesh, denying he was her son

 fraudulent documents) were not met due to lack of inducement,
 conspiracy, original documents, or reliable evidence of forgery or fraud.
 The prosecution side failed to submit original or certified copies and
 relied instead on testimonies that were inconsistent or contradicted by
 prior statements or legal documents. 27) Coming to final conclusion, GPA in question was never subjected to
 forensic examination (FSL), and the original document was not
 recovered, allowing only a photocopy with no verification of
 authenticity. This means no scientific or expert analysis was performed to
 support allegations of forgery or use of fraudulent documents. No
 handwriting specimen of Kalawati was compared, nor was any expert
 opinion obtained, leaving essential procedural and evidential
 requirements under IPC Sections 467 , 468 , and 471 unfulfilled. 28) There are persistent contradictions in witness testimony and obvious
 procedural lapses, undermining the reliability of evidence presented by
 the prosecution. The investigation and the prosecution's evidence failed
 to link the accused persons Yogesh Kumar Sharma and Lalita Sharma to
 any alleged forged documents. The complainant (Kalawati) remained
 silent for decades, did not object to property transactions previously
