--- POST TEXT ---

In 2021, I registered for a course with UpGrad EdTech. Initially, they asked me to pay Rs. 10,000/- to reserve a seat in the upcoming batch. I borrowed the money from my father to make the payment. However, upon conducting some research, I discovered that their services were subpar. I requested a refund of the Rs. 10,000/- that I had paid to reserve the seat, but they refused and pressured me to take the course. To cover the remaining amount, they connected me with their financial partner, Liquiloans. Liquiloans obtained all the necessary identification documents from both me and my father. Initially, they informed me that it was a personal loan at a 10.25% interest rate. However, without my consent, they converted it into an educational loan or consumer loan and disbursed the entire amount to UpGrad. When I questioned them about this, they claimed it was an educational loan that would be disbursed to the relevant party. I was shocked and asked Liquiloans to cancel the disbursement, but they refused and directed me to contact my educational partner. My father sensed something fishy, but I was unsure of what to do. Eventually, I decided to proceed with the course since the funds had already been disbursed.

This marked the beginning of a series of unfortunate events that made my life miserable. The faculty was subpar, and despite complaints from the entire batch, they did not replace the inadequate backend instructor. My main goal in taking this course was to receive job offers from prospective employers. However, the placement services were terrible, and the interview calls they provided were for support roles and one product role interview, which was not in my preferred location. As the course quality and placement services were unsatisfactory, I requested a refund based on the terms and conditions. However, UpGrad stopped responding to my emails and calls. After I posted my issue on LinkedIn, I received an email from UpGrad's Grievance Cell, but the issue was not resolved satisfactorily. We engaged in a heated argument about their policies, services, and placements. Finally, the representative asked me to remove the LinkedIn post, to which I responded, "Let it be," and she hung up the call. Following this, I requested Liquiloans to halt the loan and took the matter to their grievance cell. They informed me that they couldn't cancel it unless UpGrad agreed. However, UpGrad was unwilling to cancel the loan. Consequently, I stopped paying the EMIs, which had a negative impact on both my and my father's credit scores.

Last year, I received a settlement notice from Lok Adalat through Liquiloans. In response, I filed a case against UpGrad and Liquiloans for their manipulative and fraudulent practices at the cybercrime cell. I also hired a lawyer and sent notices to UpGrad and Liquiloans. UpGrad received and participated in the legal proceedings, while Liquiloans received the notice but did not provide a reply.

As a result, my lawyer pursued the case against Liquiloans in their absence. Recently, I received an email from Liquiloans stating that they have assigned our case to a collection agency. However, it has been almost 10 days, and no one has contacted me or visited my house. What does this mean? Did they lie to me about involving a collection agency? If I have a case filed against them in the consumer court, do they have the authority to engage recovery agents and send them to my residence? What are my chances, and what should I do if a recovery agent shows up at my door?
