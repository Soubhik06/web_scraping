Mr. RAJESH K. ARYA, MEMBER
Argued by: Alleging the aforesaid act amounted to deficiency in service and unfair
trade practice on the part of the respondents, the
appellant/complainant filed a consumer complaint before the District
Commission. 3] On the other hand, respondent No.1/opposite party No.1,
in its reply filed before the District Commission, pleaded that on
receipt of the complaint, internal inquiry was conducted, wherein it
was found that the complainant himself shared his login credentials
i.e. OTP for the disputed transaction. Respondent No.1 placed on
record of District Commission, copy of SMS delivery report dated
9.1.2024 proving that the said OTP was successfully delivered to the
complainant and the said transaction was only completed when he
used the said OTP. It was further pleaded that the complainant could
not approach the Commission for its own wrongs as from Exhibit C-1,
it stood proved that it was an IMPS transaction. 4] However, opposite party No.2, despite proper service, failed
to appear before the District Forum, and as such, it was proceeded
against ex-parte by the District Commission vide order dated
16.05.2025. 5] The complainant filed rejoinder before the District
Commission wherein he reiterated all the averments made in his
complaint and repudiated those as stated in the reply of opposite party
No.1. 6] The contesting parties led evidence in support of their
respective cases before the District Commission.
7] The District Commission, after hearing arguments and
considering the documentary evidence on record, dismissed the
consumer complaint as stated above. 8] The order has been assailed by the appellant - complainant
on the grounds that once timely notice of unauthorized transaction
was given to the respondents, the onus squarely lies on the
respondents to restore the funds by following the RBI circulars and
guidelines in letter and spirit; that the District Commission misapplied
the RBI Circular DBR No.Leg.BC.78 dated 06.07.2017, specifically the
concept of "limited liability" and "zero liability" under cyber fraud
situations affecting consumers; that the District Commission
erroneously concluded that mere usage of OTP constitutes
contributory negligence, disregarding the settled principle that online
frauds often employ sophisticated means which induce consumers to
part with OTP by deception and such conduct cannot be treated as
"voluntary sharing" constituting negligence, especially when victims
promptly notify the bank and that the District Commission erred in
relying on the State Commission precedent in case 'Nidhi Gupta v.
Kotak Mahindra Bank Ltd. & Ors.', First Appeal No.72 of 2021, which
is distinguishable on facts as the present case involves impersonation
and phishing through a bank link amounting to third-party breach 4 rather than pure consumer recklessness. The appellant has relied
upon the dictums passed by the Principal Bench of this Commission
in case SBI Vs. Rajesh Garg, First Appeal No.208 of 2024 decided on
30.09.2024 and that of Hon'ble Supreme Court in case 'State Bank of
India Vs. Pallabh Bhowmick & Others ', SLP(C) No.30677 of 2024.
Lastly prayer for setting aside the impugned order and allowing his
consumer complaint has been made by the appellant.
9] On the other hand, on behalf of the respondents-opposite
parties, it has been contended that appeal filed by the appellant-
complainant challenging the impugned order is without any merit and
has rightly been dismissed by the District Commission. It has further
been contended that the appellant-complainant, in his consumer
complaint, remained silent about the receipt and use of OTP and it
was only after filing of reply by the respondents-opposite parties to the
complaint, wherein it was stated that the OTP was successfully
delivered to the appellant-complainant, thereafter, the appellant-
complainant admitted the factum of receipt of the OTP but denied the
fact that he never shared the said OTP received for the transaction in
the rejoinder. It has further been contended that now in Para No.2 in
appeal, the appellant-complainant has further changed his version by
stating that not only he received the OTP but also filled the OTP
because of which the transaction was completed. It has further been
contended that since the appellant-complainant himself admitted the
factum of receipt and sharing/filling of the OTP for the alleged
transaction, hence, the appeal is also liable to be dismissed and the
same be dismissed as such. 10] After considering the rival contentions of the parties and
going through the impugned order, the grounds of appeal, the record
of the District Commission and the written arguments filed by the
parties along with the case relied upon, we are of the considered view 5 that the present appeal is liable to be dismissed for the reasons to be
recorded hereinafter. 11] At the outset, it is significant to note that the factual
matrix is largely undisputed. It stands admitted that the disputed
transaction of ₹49,997/- was effected through IMPS using SBI Internet
Banking and that the transaction was completed only after successful
authentication through One Time Password (OTP) sent on the
registered mobile number of the appellant-complainant. The
documentary evidence, particularly the SMS delivery report dated
09.01.2024, conclusively establishes that the OTP for the disputed
transaction was duly delivered to the appellant at the relevant date
and time and the transaction could not have been completed in the
absence of entry of the said OTP. These facts, which have been
meticulously appreciated by the District Commission, strike at the
very root of the appellant's plea of an "unauthorised transaction".
12] The contention of the appellant that mere prompt reporting
of the transaction shifts the entire onus upon the bank to restore the
funds, is misconceived and contrary to the RBI Circular dated
06.07.2017 itself. The RBI guidelines do not create an absolute or
automatic right of refund merely upon reporting of a transaction.
Rather, they clearly carve out distinct categories of "zero liability" and
"limited liability", depending upon whether the loss has occurred due
to bank's deficiency, third-party breach without customer negligence
or due to the negligence of the customer himself. The District
Commission has correctly held that the present case squarely falls
under Clause 7(a)(i) of the said guidelines i.e. a case where loss has
occurred due to negligence of the customer, such as sharing of
payment credentials including OTP, in which event the customer is
required to bear the entire loss until the unauthorised transaction is
reported. 13] The plea of the appellant that usage of OTP cannot, by
itself, amount to contributory negligence, also deserves rejection.
While this Commission is conscious of the growing menace of cyber
frauds and sophisticated phishing techniques, each case has to be
decided on its own facts and evidence. In the present case, the
appellant has not been able to demonstrate any deficiency, system
failure or security breach attributable to the respondents-bank. On
the contrary, the evidence on record established that the bank's
secured two-factor authentication mechanism functioned precisely as
designed and the transaction was authorised only after successful
OTP validation. The appellant's shifting stand, first denying receipt of
OTP, then admitting receipt but denying sharing and finally stating in
appeal that he filled the OTP himself seriously dents his credibility and
reinforces the finding that the transaction was completed due to his
own act, whether induced or otherwise. Such conduct, even if arising
out of deception, does not absolve the customer of the responsibility
cast upon him to safeguard his banking credentials.
14] The argument that the present case constitutes a "third-
party breach" entitling the appellant to zero liability is also devoid of
merit. A third-party breach, as contemplated under the RBI
guidelines, presupposes absence of negligence on the part of both the
bank and the customer. Once it is proved that the OTP, a critical
payment credential, was used to complete the transaction, the case
travels beyond the scope of a pure third-party breach and enters the
realm of customer negligence. The District Commission has, therefore,
rightly held that the appellant's case does not fall under part (a) i.e.
"Zero Liability of a Customer" of RBI Circular dated 06.07.2017.
Equally untenable is the challenge to reliance placed by the District
Commission on the decision of the Principal Bench of this State
Commission in Nidhi Gupta v. Kotak Mahindra Bank Ltd. & Ors. (supra). The ratio laid down therein, that a bank cannot be held liable 7 where transactions are completed through secured OTP-based
authentication and no negligence of the bank is established, fully
applies to the facts of the present case. The attempt of the appellant to
distinguish the said judgment on the ground of impersonation or
phishing does not advance his case, particularly when there is no
material on record to show that the phishing link emanated from or
was attributable to the respondents-bank or that the bank's systems
were compromised. 15] The reliance placed by the appellant on the decisions in SBI v. Rajesh Garg ( supra) and State Bank of India v. Pallabh
Bhowmick & Ors. (supra) is also misplaced. In SBI v. Rajesh Garg
(Supra), respondent/complainant Sh. Rajesh Garg never shared any
OTP, PIN or bank details and only shared a UPI scan code meant for
receiving money, which does not involve disclosure of confidential
credentials; thus, no negligence was attributable to him. Further in State Bank of India v. Pallabh Bhowmick & Ors. (supra), the Hon'ble
Supreme Court of India held that banks are liable to refund customers
for unauthorized or fraudulent transactions if the customer is not at
fault, reinforcing 2017 RBI guidelines. However, in the present case,
no such lapse on the part of the respondents-bank has been proved. It
is a settled principle that precedents cannot be applied mechanically
without regard to the factual foundation of each case.
16] It is also pertinent to note that the appellant's assertion
that the transferee account was maintained with respondent No.1
stands falsified from the statement of account itself, which clearly
shows that the beneficiary account was maintained with HDFC Bank,
which further weakens the appellant's allegation of any direct
negligence or complicity on the part of respondent No.1, as has rightly
been observed by the District Commission in its order. In our
considered view, the impugned order reflects a detailed analysis of
pleadings, evidence, RBI guidelines and binding precedents. The 8 conclusions drawn by the District Commission are reasoned and
supported by the material on record. 17] For the reasons recorded above, this appeal, being devoid
of any merit, stands dismissed with no order as to costs.
