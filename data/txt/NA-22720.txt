I have been observing a lot of people asking men not to hold the kind of extreme views for women which have surfaced since the Atul Sir's incident. I have a kind reply to you all. I am up for a civilised constructive discussion with you all. Let me know what you think of my thoughts. 

This was originally in response to a Reddit post as a comment. Posting it here now because I need people to read this.

----

What do you take me for? A saint meditating under a tree? 

How am I supposed to not feel hate when these people are handed privileges and validation at every step in life? How can I not be angry when I see special points awarded to them at IIMs for belonging to a specific gender? Or when they’re handed prestigious corporate roles under diversity quotas while much more talented people are rejected? Or when their Instagram posts mock and generalize men with derogatory remarks whenever something happens? Where are the so-called progressive torchbearers in those moments? Silent, as always.

How can anyone not be infuriated when their tax money is siphoned off to these people in the form of freebies—every month—just because they are women? Free bus seats, entire train coaches reserved, half the seats in buses forcibly kept vacant while hardworking men are crammed into congested spaces like cattle. Do you know how gut-wrenching it is to see an entire reserved coach completely empty, and behind it, men, the actual taxpayers, squeezed into the rest of the train?

Let me guess: a feminist will now waltz in to deliver the age-old lecture about how their XX chromosome family is still oppressed and these "freebies" are essential for their empowerment, while the men can just go to hell, right?

I am a general male. I am middle-class, and I wear torn socks. That’s my reality. Without a shred of exaggeration, let me tell you this: I have never benefited from a SINGLE government scheme. Why? Because there are none for me. ZERO. Absolutely nothing. Why? Hey, I am a man dude, I deserve no support dude. 

Meanwhile, women have Sukanya Samriddhi schemes, banks rolling out special discounted loans, and countless other privileges. What’s there for me? Nothing. Tell me, how is my anger not justified? 

Even the armed forces—the last bastion of respect in this country—have fallen victim to this charade. My family, many of whom serve in the forces, share the same plight: lady officers comfortably enjoy desk postings while men are sent to desolate outposts. Fair? Absolutely not.

And how can we forget fake cases? The ones that shatter lives without trial? Isn’t that judge who enabled those false accusations still free? Are the accusers—those parents and that wife—still roaming around as if nothing happened? Meanwhile, the men accused are buried under the weight of accusations like those in the Chandigarh University or the Punjabi man's case. Remember Jasleen?

What’s the excuse now? "Don’t blame the players; blame the game?" If these players were so noble, why aren’t they out demanding the abolition of these absurd privileges? They won’t, because they enjoy switching between "oppressed" and "independent" based on convenience.

We don’t always have to act neutral. Sometimes, we need to take a stand. And no, I will not sit here pretending this is okay. When the horrendous RG Kar Medical College case happened, lakhs of men marched for justice. Yet, the narrative was still about "all men being to blame." Insta stories, news headlines—they all targeted us.

And now? Look at Atul Sir’s case. Where are the candle marches? Are there lakhs of women on the streets demanding justice for men? Nowhere. Yet men were there for them—even in cases where the allegations turned out to be false. Ask this to yourself, is this paragraph the truth or not?

---

Do you see why I am so bitter now? I wasn’t like this. I was once a staunch feminist, genuinely believing in their cause. I empathized with their struggles, and many of their issues were—and still are—valid. But over time, the hypocrisy became impossible to ignore.

My anger didn’t grow overnight. It’s the result of years of watching this unfairness unfold. At some point, I just couldn’t take it anymore.

What do you expect me to do? I am human. I have my own struggles. I can tolerate a lot, but there’s a limit to everything. Don’t ask me to pretend I’m okay with this—because I’m not.
