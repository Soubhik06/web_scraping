This appeal preferred by the Revenue is directed against the order

dtd. 17-2-2011 passed by the ld. CIT(A) - 6, Mumbai for the A.Y. 2004- 05. 2. Briefly stated facts of the case are that the assessee company is

engaged in the business of buying and selling of shares and securities,

investments. The return was filed declaring total income of Rs.

2,07,44,456/-. During the course of assessment proceeding it was, inter 2 ITA No. 3594/MUM/2011 alia, observed by the A.O. that the assessee in the P&L account debited a

sum of Rs. 1,27,17,463/- pertaining to bad debts and sundry

irrecoverable balances written off which included an amount paid to M/s

Arihant Exim Scrip Pvt. Ltd. Rs. 1 crore which was written off as bad

debts by the company as the said company was declared as defaulter

and suspended from the primary membership of Calcutta Stock

Exchange Association w.e.f. 25-9-2001 as there was no chance of

recovering the same. The A.O. further observed that no documentary

evidence was submitted by the assessee with regard to the

correspondence with the Stock Exchange for arbitration in the matter.

He further observed that normally, in such cases, a suit is filed by the

assessee (Investor) with the Investor Protection Fund formed by the

concerned Stock Exchange and the Stock Exchange intervenes for the

protection and refund to the investor. In the instant case, the assessee

has failed to furnish any evidence to prove that efforts were made for

getting its money back through arbitration. In fact, it is interesting to

note that the assessee has not entered into any share transaction with

the said broker M/s Arihant Exim Scrip Pvt Ltd. even though the amount

is stated to have been paid in F.Y 2000-01 as an on account payment for

the purposes of buying and selling the shares. The A.O. further observed

that it is surprising that no complaint has been filed by the assessee

against the matter with the Calcutta Stock Exchange and the assessee

has also not furnished any undertaking with respect to the refund 3 ITA No. 3594/MUM/2011 received or receivable to any extent from the appropriate Investor

Protection Fund. He further observed that the claim of the assessee is

not justifiable being in the nature of capital advance. The A.O. also

considered the amendments made under the provisions of section

36(1)(vii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (the Act) and has observed that

even after the amendment in law, the primary condition still remains

that it should be a 'bad debt' and not 'any debt'. He further observed that

the assessee has not brought any material on record to support the claim

that the debts have gone bad. He further observed that the assessee

company despite being given ample opportunity to justify the claim of

bad debts has not availed of the same, the assessee has also not brought

on record any material to prove that these debts represent amounts,

which have been actually accounted as income in any earlier assessment

year or during the previous year. He, therefore, held that the assessee

company is unable to give any plausible explanation that can justify the

claim of bad debts and accordingly disallowed the claim of bad debts.

The A.O. after making some other disallowances, completed the

assessment at an income of Rs. 3,56,08,756/- vide assessment order

dtd. 30-11-2006 passed u/s 143(3) of the Act. 3. On appeal, the ld. CIT(A) after considering the assessee's
