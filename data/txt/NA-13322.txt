The Supreme Court on Monday issued a notice to the Centre to file a reply to a plea seeking action against betting and gambling websites allegedly operating under the guise of e-sports and social gaming platforms, even after the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act (PROGA) banned online money games . The Public Interest Litigation (PIL), filed by the non-governmental organisation Centre for Accountability and Systemic Change (CASC), sought directions to block unlawful betting sites, restrict UPI transactions to unregistered gaming platforms, and safeguard children’s data collected by such companies.

A Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K.V. Viswanathan heard the matter and subsequently directed the government to file its response. Moreover, the petition urged the adoption of stricter measures, such as provisions from Tamil Nadu’s Prohibition of Online Gaming Act, 2022, at a national level to curb illegal betting and gambling activities.

During the hearing, panel counsel V.C. Bharathi, appearing for the Centre, informed the Court that a new law regulating online gaming, the PROGA had already been enacted but was yet to be notified. Once implemented, he said, it would “squarely cover” the reliefs raised in the petition. Consequently, the Court took note of this submission and tagged the case with a related transfer petition.

The petition alleges that despite the enactment of the PROGA, large-scale betting and gambling operations continue unchecked under the guise of e-sports and social gaming. It presents evidence indicating that these platforms are still thriving and that the government’s enforcement efforts remain inadequate.

According to the PIL, more than 65 crore Indians are engaged in such games, generating an annual business exceeding Rs 1.8 lakh crore, while around 45 crore people have already suffered financial losses, with over Rs 20,000 crore of hard-earned money lost. The petition cites data showing that from 2022 to June 2025, the government issued 1,524 blocking directions under Section 69A of the IT Act; however, illegal betting and gambling apps continue to operate.

Furthermore, it points out that while the NCRB recorded 1.6 lakh to 1.7 lakh conventional gambling cases annually between 2021 and 2023, online gambling offences reported were only 27 in 2021, 37 in 2022, and 87 in 2023, reflecting regulatory inaction against digital offenders.

The petition further argues that the PROGA fails to explicitly prohibit betting and gambling apps or provide safeguards for minors, despite acknowledging the social harm and suicides caused by online money games. It claims that these platforms employ manipulative algorithms, aggressive advertising, and celebrity endorsements to target vulnerable users, particularly children.

Moreover, the petitioner alleges that even after the enactment of the PROGA, around 2,000 betting and gambling apps remain active and accessible to Indian users, continuing to operate through offshore servers and surrogate promotions. The petition also highlights that offshore operators continue to evade taxes and launder money, thereby posing risks to financial integrity, data privacy, and national security. Consequently, it seeks urgent judicial intervention to ensure effective enforcement and protection against these ongoing violations.

The petition seeks a series of wide-ranging directives from the Supreme Court aimed at curbing illegal betting and gambling operations. It calls on the Centre and state governments to harmonise the provisions of the PROGA with state laws, thereby prohibiting betting and gambling disguised as e-sports or social games. Moreover, it requests that the Centre issue blocking orders under Section 69A of the IT Act against all unlawful gambling platforms.

In addition, the petition urges the Finance Ministry, Reserve Bank of India (RBI), and National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) to take measures to prevent UPI or other digital transactions involving unregistered money gaming platforms. It further seeks nationwide adoption of stricter measures similar to those in the Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gaming Act, 2022, to combat organised betting activity. The petition also calls on Google and Apple to comply with the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, and whitelist only licensed gaming apps in their app stores.

Furthermore, it demands the recovery of GST and income tax dues from offshore gaming firms, with assistance from enforcement agencies and Interpol. Lastly, it seeks safeguards for children’s data collected by online gaming platforms and any other directions the Court may deem fit to protect public interest and national security.

The Supreme Court’s intervention comes at a crucial time for India’s online gaming and betting ecosystem. Despite the PROGA being passed earlier this year, the alleged persistence of over 2,000 active offshore and unregistered betting platforms highlights serious enforcement gaps.

These platforms, often operating from tax havens, continue to advertise digitally and process payments. Furthermore, research from March 2025 found that these offshore platforms operate several mirror websites, which further complicate takedowns.

The petition also underscores growing public harm, including addiction, financial fraud, and exposure of minors, issues that existing frameworks have failed to contain. It raises concerns about data privacy, with offshore entities collecting sensitive financial and behavioural information from Indian users.

Therefore, the Court’s decision to seek a government response could push the Centre to clarify how the PROGA will be implemented and whether it will explicitly outlaw betting disguised as e-sports. Ultimately, the case represents a wider test of how India balances innovation in online gaming with accountability, child protection, and digital sovereignty in a high-stakes, fast-growing industry.
