It feels like a lot of problems are common to India and Nigeria. Chinua Achebe's book "The Trouble with Nigeria" details the main problems facing Nigeria and I couldn't help but notice that India suffers from the same thing.

* **Leadership Problem:** Both countries struggle with leadership, where strong leaders can bring positive change, but poor leadership can lead to stagnation.
 * Nigeria's experience with a leader's "ruthlessness" improving public servant punctuality mirrors India's Emergency period where discipline improved. I almost laughed out loud when I saw Achebe mention this.
* **Tribalism/Division:** Deep-rooted divisions (caste, language, region in India; tribalism in Nigeria) are a roadblock to national unity and progress.
 * You can even see this in caste "stickers" on license plates of vehicles.
* **Cult of Mediocrity:** Inefficient public sectors and corruption plague both nations, leading to citizens having to fend for themselves for basic services.
 * The elite in both countries are perceived as benefiting disproportionately from the system.
 * Additionally, the "chalta hai" attitude seems to be common. At this point, I am not even sure if it is an Indian thing or a developing country thing.
* **Indiscipline:** Lack of discipline among leaders and the prevalence of "VIP culture" undermine national progress.
 * The author draws a parallel between Nigeria's "siren mentality" and India's use of flashing red lights on vehicles of government officials to get through traffic.
 * Achebe should really visit India! I wonder what he'd say.
* **Corruption:** Rampant corruption and scams divert resources and erode public trust. Achebe has had the exact same experience with corruption that India has had.
* **Secularism:** Both countries face challenges in implementing secularism due to their complex cultural and religious landscapes. Nigeria professes secularism but doesn't really implement it. It faces many of the same roadblocks to it as India.
