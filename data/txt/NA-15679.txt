X (previously Twitter) has altered the social media platform’s block feature. Users began getting pop-up notifications that said: “Block is changing soon. If your posts are set to public, accounts you have blocked will be able to view them, but they will not be able to engage. For more control over who can see your posts, you can still protect your account.”

While blocked users won’t be able to comment, like, or retweet, they can still observe your activity—effectively making the platform a one-way mirror. The only way to completely stop blocked accounts from seeing your posts is to set your profile to private, which further restricts your visibility to only approved followers.

In the past, blocking an account on X was a straightforward way to tailor your online experience and avoid unwanted interactions. When you blocked someone, they couldn’t see your posts, message you, or interact with your content at all. It served as a vital tool for protecting personal space in a public digital environment. With this significant change, removes a fundamental protection that users have relied on for years to shield themselves from harassment, unwanted attention, and overall toxicity on the platform.

Since Musk’s takeover in ‘22, X has witnessed a wave of structural changes such as removal of content moderation teams and the reinstatement of banned accounts. Previously, Musk had expressed his dislike of the block button, saying that the feature “makes no sense” and that “it needs to be deprecated in favour of a stronger form of mute”.
