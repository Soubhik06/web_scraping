So I'm pretty sure I got myself into some deep dumbness here. Deciding between posting here or TIFU at this point.

&amp;#x200B;

THE SCAM: A "travel agent" purchased $16,000 of flights for me on what I believe are stolen credit cards, and wanted me to pay him $3600, claiming he had special travel agency rates. In the process, he got a LOT of my personal information, and given what I'm pretty sure he does for a living, I'm nervous about that. 

THE STORY: So I'm booking travel for 3 people for 6 different destinations across different places. Some could be round trips. Some couldn't be. I'm going from California to a conference in Italy. My wife is joining me at the end of the conference. She's dropping off our daughter with my parents for the duration of our trip. We want to fly together for all the relevant different legs of that trip. We could choose to have me and wife and baby all fly together to grandma and grandpa's and me leave from there, or we have me fly straight out of CA.

&amp;#x200B;

Long and short - it's a complicated mess with multiple options and multiple things that could ideally run parallel.

&amp;#x200B;

So we were recommended by a friend (who used to be a travel agent) to try to use a travel agent! 

&amp;#x200B;

I went to the American Society of Travel Agents website, clicked the "Look for a quote" button, and got 2 quotes from people within 2 days of my submission. Agent #1 was offering flights for more expensive than I could find on [flights.google.com](https://flights.google.com). Agent #2 was offering flights for about 2/3 the cost I was seeing on google flights - which is cheap, but not crazy. His Indian accent definitely doesn't match his Texas phone number, but I'm quite used to calling places and talking to redirected call centers.

&amp;#x200B;

He then puts together an itinerary for me, and straight-up \*books the tickets for me\* and confirmations are sent to my email. 

&amp;#x200B;

I'm like, "Wait buddy, I need to pay you!" He's like, 'great, let's do that now." He takes my credit card number and asks for my driver's license number - says that they use that for identification. I'm ready to give the dude my passport, so I'm not really weirded out by my Driver's License here.

&amp;#x200B;

He tells me he's going to charge me, and my bank sends me a text saying "We blocked possibly fraudulent activity from [xoom.com](https://xoom.com). Did you initiate this charge? Text YES if you want to approve, and NO if you want to deny." I texted back YES.

&amp;#x200B;

At this point, the travel agent says that he'll try running the charges again and get back to me.

&amp;#x200B;

An hour later, he calls and says that he tried and it was rejected, and so he'd like me to just pay via a 3rd-party system called \*Skrill.\* This is my first warning sign. I simply tell him that I will not be using any sort of program or system where I wire money. 

&amp;#x200B;

He suggests a variety of different things, but tells me he won't be trying to use the credit card again even if I have them on the phone authorizing it, and tells me that he can't use paypay because they have a limited account with paypal, and they've already used all their allowed charges for the time being. 

I'm not interested in anything like this. He gets kinda pressure-y, and then says he'll talk with his risk team and see what he can do. He calls back in 20 minutes saying that I should do the 3rd party thing, and when I refuse, he says that if I send them the first page of my bank statement, that should verify it well enough. I feel a bit surprised, but I look at the first page of my bank statement, and all that has is my address, and I've already given him my address when I submitted for a quote on the travel agent website, so I go ahead and do it.

30 minutes later, he calls and says they want a selfie of me with my ID before they run it again. I say no. 

&amp;#x200B;

I'm feeling suspicious, so I try to investigate some... I click on the links, go to the airline websites, and Lufthansa says "Here is your upcoming trip! It was paid for by &lt;random weird email&gt; and &lt;random weird phone number&gt; and &lt;blanked-out credit card except for last 4&gt; and it costs $5,400 per person!"

&amp;#x200B;

This guy bought over $16,000 of flights for the 3 of us, even though you can find the cheapest flights for around $4.5-5k. But they're ALL REAL TICKETS. Like, they exist! They have my travel information! I called the airline, said that I had been booking with a travel agent that I don't totally trust who had me pay him a lower price than it seems like the flight costs, and they verified that I had real working tickets. They said "naw, looks like everything is good mate!"

&amp;#x200B;

So - here I am with some guy in India who claims to be the business owner of a business located outside Austin, TX, and he has purchased flights for me, has my credit card information but isn't using it to buy flights, and has given me what are apparently real tickets, and he's got enough other information about me to make it feel like a serious identity theft risk.

&amp;#x200B;

So /r/Scams \- What are your thoughts?
