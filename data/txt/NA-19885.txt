Hello /r/india,

I was just thinking about the logistics of the whole 'vaccination for all who're 18+' project that the government announced pretty pompously. 

As everyone already here knows (and keep cribbing about) India is an INSANELY BIG country, both geographically and in terms of population. We've got cities that are so densely packed that it puts slums of Manila to shame. We've got terrible infrastructure in many many parts of the country, especially in states that are seeing a huge spike in cases like Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, MP and West Bengal. We've got woefully inadequate healthcare infrastructure, as the past couple weeks bore witness.

My primary question is: how on earth is the government (both central and the states) planning to accomplish this?

A few follow up questions I have are: 

* Do we have enough doses in the first place to cater to the extreme demand that'll start crushing the system when the registrations open up on the Co-WIN site from 28th April?

* Do we have proper logistical and supply chain management for ensuring the smooth flow of vaccines (while being carefully stored in cryogenic temperature needed for it to be effective)?

* Do we have enough healthcare workers and trained staff to manage to administration at the ground level?

And finally...

* Does the Co-WIN database and cloud have proper security architecture/hardware configuration in place to prevent possible data leaks and/or server outages?

Bottomline -- I feel the first couple weeks of the rollout are going to be a shit show. People are going to frantically signup with few -- if any -- getting the slots and the overcrowding is going to lead to more super spreader events. Leading to more spread and higher casualty numbers.

I don't trust the Indian government and its bureaucrats to pull this off without some major issues. This is what happens when you laze around for almost a year and [[[PANIK]]] suddenly when the second wave sucker punches you right in the face.

/rant.
