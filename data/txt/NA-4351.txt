witnesses on record it is very much necessary to have a glance over

the provisions of section 420 of the Indian Penal Code and section 66 of the Information Technology Act. It is pertinent to note here that, for the offence under section 420 of Indian Penal Code, it must be proved that the complainant parted

with his property acting on a representation which was false to the

knowledge of the accused and that the accused had a dishonest

intention from the outset. It is pertinent to note here that, a person

can be said to have done a thing dishonestly if he does so with the

intention of causing wrongful gain to one person and causing

wrongful loss to another person. It is pertinent to note here that,

dishonest intention which constitutes the men's rea on the basis of

which the dishonest or fraudulent inducement was made is a mental

element and it may not be always capable of direct proof by positive

evidence. It is pertinent to note here that, the onus is on the

prosecution to prove the dishonest intention of the accused.

 It is pertinent to note here that, section 66 of the Information

Technology Act, deals with the phrase Hacking and which

contemplates that, whoever with intent to cause or knowing

that he is likely to cause wrongful loss or damage to the

public or any person destroys or deletes or alters any

information residing in a computer resource or diminishes its 7 C.C.No.4707/2015 value or utility or affects it injuriously by any means,

commits hacking. 12. It is to be noted here that, on back ground of aforesaid

provisions now let us analyze the evidence of the witnesses.
