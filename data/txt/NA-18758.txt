These were my father's words when I was incapacitated by a silent panic attack the day before my Nani died. She was supposed to come home as she was COVID negative but it was her ashes that returned instead.

This was sometime around mid-September, when the cases were peaking in the first wave.

These were the protocols we followed then:

- Only one family member would go outside into the world. Because that one person in my family was a doctor, his exposure alone was more than enough.

- Mask at every moment anyone steps outside. Obviously, it was a necessity that during those times, our hands wouldn't touch our face. **Masks are essentially worn so that if you have the virus, you do not give it to someone else. Snuggly fitting N95s are worn so that you don't contract. Since asymptomatic cases exist with COVID-19, you are never going to know if you are positive until you have been tested. If you are around others who aren't wearing a mask, you are the one at risk.**

- Two people from two different households always stood at a distance of minimum 6 ft from each other, indoors or outdoors. (The girl I have been walking with for a year now; we are each other's only outside contact... I never even touched her.)

- If we went to anyone's house, we would sit 6ft from the other family members and not take our masks out. I have actually visited only one family the entire year.

- Even our Didi, who comes to our house to work, has never taken off her mask, sanitises, washes her hands and feet till foam is formed and stays 4-5 feet away from us at all times. We have a separate nightie prepared for her which she puts on top of her saree and works. She has followed all protocols for her family. 

- Sanitising hands after touching anything which comes from the hands from a member of another household. Including notes. My father goes to the other extreme and washes the notes followed by ironing. Well, his notes come from the hospital...so, that is kinda justified.

- As soon as Dad enters the house, and he knows that he has to go outside, he only sits on a plastic chair. Any surface that he touches is sanitised with Hypochloride solution. Because well, Fomites are real. And that's how COVID has transferred too. (*Edit:* Changed from 'majorly' to 'too')

This is the definition of a Fomite from Wikipedia: *A fomite is any inanimate object that, when contaminated with or exposed to infectious agents (such as pathogenic bacteria, viruses or fungi), can transfer disease to a new host.* 

- The virus stays in the nasal and oral tract. So, when he comes back, he does a warm gargle with salt water. A complete steam inhalation every single day. His clothes are put straight for washing immediately.

- We have gone out 3-4 times in the entire year. And followed all possible protocols. When we come back from a crowded place, we run straight to the bathroom, put our clothes for washing and foam foam foam.

- Sanitising groceries. Sanitising fruits and vegetables with turmeric water. Car seats have been wrapped in plastic covers which is also sanitised on a regular basis. *Sometimes, I feel like the alcohol industry is running because of my family.*

There are a lot more precautions that he takes when he is in a medical facility. One of the most visible ones being wearing a surgical mask on top of N95.

**Immunity, Vaccines and Vaccinations:**

- **Once infected**, the immunity does not last forever. I was told that on an average it's 3.5 months. I am not sure about the exact number, but it's in that range. Which means, **you still stand the chance of getting infected again**, and the second time it could be more severe because your immunity has been lowered after the first infection.

- Vaccines **do not guarantee complete immunity**. Vaccine efficacy measures how well shots prevent disease among vaccinated people in clinical studies, when compared to unvaccinated control groups. With 96% efficacy, it does not mean that a vaccine is going to be 96% effective at stamping out all disease, even the asymptomatic kind. It does not mean that 4% of vaccinated people globally will get sick. And it does not mean that, once you are fully vaccinated, you have a 5% risk of getting COVID-19. 

*At best, it provides a temporary snapshot, a vaccine maker's rough estimate as to how well their vaccine will perform at curbing symptomatic infections in the general public, based on just a few months worth of trial data.* (As taken from the article on Business Insider's *When experts say COVID-19 vaccines are 95% effective it doesn't mean you'd have a 5% chance of getting sick* by Hilary Brueck)

- **Vaccines ensure the virulence of the virus is reduced.** Which mean, if you do test positive, the symptoms won't be as severe as it would have been without the vaccine. You still might get hospitalised, but you won't die. Hopefully.

- Also, it is not known for how long is the vaccine going to give immunity. It idea that people might need booster doses every couple of months is being worked upon. There wasn't enough time for human trials.

- While going for vaccinations, protocols still need to be followed. Social distancing has to be maintained in the queue and waiting room. Masks have to worn properly; not on your chin, not covering just your mouth with your nose outside...and all other ways in which they have been worn through the year.
