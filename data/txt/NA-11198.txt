The Case 1. This is a suit for permanent and mandatory injunction filed
 by the plaintiff Arti Gehlot against the defendant Poonam,

 the said property. 2. The plaintiff claims ownership of the suit property through

 defendant was allowed to stay in the property only as a
 licensee, and despite termination of the license, the
 defendant has refused to vacate. 3. The defendant, on the other hand, denies the plaintiff's

Issue No. 1: 24.The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff. This
 issue requires the Court to determine: 25.The plaintiff claims ownership of the suit property based
 on the relinquishment deed dated 16.08.2019 (Ex. PW1/3)
 executed by her brother Rajesh Gehlot and sister Payal in
 her favor. The plaintiff has produced this registered
 document before the court. The defendant has questioned
 the validity of this document but has not brought any
 evidence to prove that it is fraudulent or illegal. 26. Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908 mandates that a
 relinquishment deed transferring immovable property
 requires registration, and the document in question is duly
 registered. The relinquishment deed prima facie appears to
 be valid and legally executed, bearing the signatures of all
 concerned parties and attested by witnesses. 27.It is a settled principle of law that a registered document
 carries a presumption of correctness and authenticity until
 proven otherwise. The defendant has not adduced any
 evidence that would rebut this presumption. The mere
 allegation of fraud, without corresponding proof, is
 insufficient to invalidate the registered document. CS SCJ 623/22 ARTI GEHLOT Vs. POONAM 12/24
 Nature of the Property and Rights of Legal Heirs: 28.The defendant claims that the property is ancestral and her
 husband could not have relinquished his rights without
 considering her interests. This necessitates an examination
 of the nature of the property and the rights of various
 stakeholders. 29.Both PW1 and PW2 have testified that the property was
 owned by their father, Late Tara Chand Gehlot, who died
 intestate on 25.10.2006. Although during cross-
 examination, PW1 was confronted with the relinquishment
 deed which mentioned that Late Sh. Tara Chand Gehlot
 was the "ancestral owner" of the suit property, this
 terminology alone does not conclusively establish that the
 property was ancestral in the legal sense. 30.Under Hindu Law, a property is considered ancestral only
 if it was inherited by a male Hindu from his father,
 grandfather, or great-grandfather. There is no evidence on
 record to establish that Late Sh. Tara Chand Gehlot
 himself inherited this property from his paternal ancestors. 31.Upon the death of Tara Chand Gehlot intestate, the
 property would have devolved upon his legal heirs: his son
 Rajesh Gehlot and daughters Arti Gehlot and Payal as per Section 8 read with Class I of the Schedule to the Hindu
 Succession Act, 1956 . All three legal heirs have consented

 to the plaintiff becoming the sole owner through the
 relinquishment deed. 32. The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 recognizes the right of
 a co-owner to relinquish his share in favor of another co-
 owner. This is precisely what has occurred in the present
 case, where Rajesh Gehlot and Payal have relinquished
 their shares in favor of their sister, the plaintiff Arti
 Gehlot. Rights of the Defendant as a Wife: 33.The defendant's claim that she has a right to reside in the
 property by virtue of being the wife of Rajesh Gehlot
 merits careful consideration in light of recent legal
 developments regarding women's right to residence. 34.The right of a wife to reside in the matrimonial home is
 recognized under various laws, including the Protection of
 Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA).
 However, this right is not absolute and is subject to certain
 limitations, especially when the rights of third parties are
 involved. 35.It is trite that a wife can claim right to residence in a
 "shared household" under the PWDVA. However, it is
 equally true that this right cannot override the rights of a
 third party who has lawfully acquired the property. The
 protection under PWDVA is primarily against

 dispossession by the husband or his relatives, and not
 against a third party who has validly acquired ownership
 rights. 36.In the present case, the property is not owned by the
 husband anymore, having been transferred to the plaintiff
 through a valid relinquishment deed. There is no evidence
 to suggest that the transfer was made with the intention to
 defeat the rights of the defendant, especially considering
 that PW1 (plaintiff) testified that her brother had been
 facing financial difficulties and she had been supporting
 him financially prior to the execution of the relinquishment
 deed. 37.Furthermore, PW2 (Rajesh Gehlot) testified that he wishes
 to reside with his wife and daughter, but the defendant is
 unwilling to join him. The defendant has not produced any
 evidence to contradict this testimony. Status of the Defendant's Occupation: 38.The plaintiff contends that the defendant was allowed to
 stay in the property only as a licensee, and this license was
 terminated through a legal notice dated 07.04.2022 (Ex.
 PW1/4). 39.A license, in legal terms, is a permission to do something
 on someone else's property which, without such
 permission, would amount to trespass. In the present case,

 there is no evidence of any transfer of property to the
 defendant. The plaintiff's testimony that she allowed the
 defendant to stay in the property out of love and affection,
 coupled with the subsequent issuance of a termination

 indeed that of a licensee. 40.Infact, the defendant admitted during cross-examination
 that she received the legal notice terminating her license
 but did not place on record any reply to the same. In law,
 failure to respond to such a notice can be construed as
 acquiescence to its contents. Defendant's Alternative Accommodation: 41.The defendant admitted during cross-examination that she
 is receiving maintenance from her husband. Thus, her
 husband is discharging his legal obligation towards her.
 While the defendant has alleged that her husband is not
 adequately maintaining her and her daughter, she has
 admitted to receiving maintenance, albeit claiming it is
 only "half the maintenance amount." If the defendant feels
 the maintenance is inadequate, the appropriate remedy
 would be to approach the concerned court which granted
