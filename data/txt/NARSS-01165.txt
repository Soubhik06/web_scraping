April 22: First Waves of Intimidation

1. Anonymous Threats Begin

Kashmiri student associations in Mumbai and Bengaluru log over 50 phone calls and online death-threats, warning students against “sympathizing with terrorists.” 

2. Hostel Notices

In Chandigarh, anonymous posters appear on hostel doors: “Leave India or face consequences.” 

---
April 21–23: Physical Assaults and Trader Attacks

3. Beatings on Campus

At least 12 Kashmiri university students in Delhi and Pune report being slapped or shoved on campus by mobs shouting “Go back to Kashmir.” 

4. Shopfront Vandalism

20+ Kashmiri shawl and handicraft stall-holders in Jaipur and Ahmedabad suffer stone-pelting and glass-smashing, with some traders beaten by vigilantes. 

---

April 24–25: Official Responses and Aid Efforts

5. Helpdesks Opened

Student welfare bodies (JKSA) set up helpline desks in Delhi, Mumbai, and Bengaluru after reports of harassment; they record over 1,000 distress calls in two days. 

6. Bengaluru Solidarity

A Bengaluru resident publicly invites Kashmiri students to stay at his home, calling it a “safe house” amid rising attacks. 

---

April 26–27: Surveys, FIRs, and Lingering Fear

7. Under‐Reporting of Crimes

Delhi Police have registered fewer than 15 FIRs for attacks on Kashmiris, despite dozens of complaints. Victims and local MPs decry the low prosecution rate. 

8. Student Federation Survey

A national student federation survey finds 70% of Kashmiri students now avoid public transport and extracurricular activities for fear of assault.

9. Trader Lockdowns

Several Kashmiri traders in Lucknow and Bhopal temporarily close shops or halt new shipments, citing repeated threats and lack of timely police action. 

10. Continued Intimidation

Right-wing vigilante groups issue social-media warnings targeting Kashmiri students in Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh, urging them to “leave the country.”
